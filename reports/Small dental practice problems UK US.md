# Small dental practices miss calls, rework claims and fear clawback

Small independent dental practices in the UK and the US (one to five dentists; NHS, private and mixed in the UK; fee-for-service, PPO and Medicaid in the US) spend their day on work that is not dentistry: answering a phone that rings while a patient stands at the desk, checking what a payer or the NHS will pay, re-typing the answer into the practice system, chasing patients who did not book the treatment they were shown, and keeping paper and logs ready for an inspector or an auditor. In the US the average general dentist netted **$215,320 in 2025**, about **$60,000 less than in 2010** in real terms, while **65% of practices reported higher overhead** (up about 5% a year) and **one dentist in three says the practice is "not busy enough"** ([ADA HPI, dental practice trends](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dental-practice-trends) [snippet]; [Dental Economics/Levin survey](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/practice/overhead-and-profitability/article/55280684/higher-production-helps-offset-chronic-overhead-increases) [snippet]; [ADA HPI Q1 2026](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/state_us_dental_economy_q12026.pdf)). Vendor call data say **31 of every 100 callers hang up before reaching anyone**, only **21 book**, **4.8 get a follow-up call**, and once a patient is in the chair only **42% to 46% of diagnosed treatment is accepted**, leaving "$1 to $1.5 million" unscheduled per average practice ([Patient Prism, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.patientprism.com/report/dental-patient-access-report/) [vendor]; [Henry Schein One, 22 Apr 2026](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index) [vendor]; [Henry Schein One, 20 Aug 2024](https://www.henryscheinone.com/insights/blogs/average-providers-are-losing-hundreds-of-thousands-in-unscheduled-treatment) [vendor]). Insurance verification alone cost US dentistry **$2.1 billion in 2023**, about **12 minutes per manual check against 4 electronic**, and billing vendors put first-pass denials at **8% to 15%** against a 4% benchmark ([ADA News, 24 Mar 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/march/benefit-verification-drives-increased-administrative-spending-in-dental-offices/); [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/revenue-cycle-management/article/55409403/the-7-revenue-cycle-gaps-most-dental-practices-dont-know-they-have) [snippet]). In England **one NHS contract in ten has been given up since 2017**, only **40% of adults saw an NHS dentist in the 24 months to March 2026**, about **£900 million was clawed back over two years** for under-delivered UDAs, and from April 2026 the NHS Compass portal **cannot show a practice whether it is meeting the new urgent-care target** ([GDPUK, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-ten-practices-have-ceased-general-nhs-dentistry-since-2017/) [secondary]; [The Probe, 1 Oct 2026](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/10/the-system-is-moving-but-is-it-changing/) [secondary]; [Dentistry.co.uk, 5 Mar 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/03/05/nhs-contract-clawback-at-900-million-in-past-two-years/); [GDPUK, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/news-in-focus/compass-without-direction-practices-unclear-on-uda-achievement/)). Staffing is the other wall: **about 90% of US dentists recruiting a hygienist call it very or extremely hard**, **77% of UK owners who tried to hire a dental nurse had difficulty** and **63% of UK vacancies stayed open more than six months** ([ADA News, 27 Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/five-years-later-staffing-shortages-infection-control-since-the-covid-19-pandemic/); [BDA DDRB 2026-27 evidence](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)). The two countries use different words for the same pain, and keyword targeting has to follow the words: the US says "no-show", "breakdown of benefits", "eligibility", "claim denied", "PPO", "office manager" and "HIPAA"; the UK says "FTA", "UDAs", "FP17", "clawback", "exemption", "practice manager", "CQC" and "GDC". Marketing is where the money leaks and the rules bite: agencies charge **$1,000-$2,500 a month** in the US and **£1,500-£8,000+** in the UK, owners say they get "no idea" of return, agencies answer that "your front desk is the problem" with **37% of tracked calls unanswered**, and the FTC review rule, TCPA texting consent, GDC website rules (only **1.8% of audited practice websites** were compliant) and Google's health-ads policy all sit on the same pages that bring in private patients ([RankAI, 7 Dec 2025](https://rankai.ai/articles/dental-seo-pricing-guide-costs-factors-roi) [vendor]; [Dominate Dental](https://cms.dominatedental.com/?p=467) [vendor]; [Dentistry.co.uk, 29 Dec 2021](https://dentistry.co.uk/2021/12/29/marketing-money-down-the-drain/); [Dentaltown, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/26399/your-marketing-isnt-broken-your-front-desk-is) [vendor]; [PubMed, BDJ 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33767368/)). The law fixes what a machine may not do: in the US the clinician's narrative and signature on an appeal, the patient's consent, and the HIPAA business associate agreement that consumer ChatGPT does not offer; in the UK the dentist's FP17 declaration, the patient's signed exemption form, Montgomery-standard consent, and clinical judgement that the MHRA says turns a scribe into a medical device. The service gaps with the strongest problem signal and the weakest vendor coverage are all private-data jobs: reconciling FP17 claims against Compass and the UDA target, tracking unscheduled treatment to a named follow-up, measuring leads to seated patients across the agency's CRM and the practice's system, assembling denial appeal packs inside deadlines, checking practice websites and ads against GDC, ASA, FTC and Google rules, and handling patient record requests within the legal clock. The crowded areas are AI phone receptionists, SEO and websites, review tools, reminder platforms, offshore verification labour and imaging AI. On money, employed dental-AI engineering pays **$145,000-$240,000** in the US (H-1B medians: Overjet $195,000, VideaHealth $175,000) and **£46,000-£88,000** in UK dental software, with single-digit R&D openings per vendor, hubs in San Mateo, Boston, Lahore and Noida, and no stated sponsorship policy; the freelance path is priced against **$600-$700 a month Philippine dental VAs**, **$300-$900 a month AI-receptionist subscriptions** and **$4-$7 an hour** posting gigs, with the only mid-four-figure budgets found in AI-receptionist builds and CRM or PMS integration (**$2,500-$8,000**) ([Ellis, Overjet](https://www.ellis.com/visa-sponsors/overjet-inc/salaries); [Glassdoor UK, Henry Schein One](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salary/Henry-Schein-One-Salaries-E7869334.htm) [snippet]; [OnlineJobs.ph 1335685](https://www.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/1335685); [Upwork AI receptionist builders](https://www.upwork.com/hire/ai-receptionist-builders/) [snippet]). Neither path needs a clinical background; both need PMS integration (Open Dental, Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Dentally, Exact), Python and SQL, insurance or NHS claiming workflow knowledge, and HIPAA or UK GDPR handling, which the ads do not state but the fines and the vendor terms make unavoidable.

## 1. The US dentist nets $215,000 on a 62% overhead; the NHS dentist loses one pound in seven to clawback

The US small practice is still the common unit. **72.5% of dentists owned a practice in 2023** (84.7% in 2005), **DSO affiliation rose from 7.2% in 2015 to 16.1% in 2024**, and about **34% of dentists worked alone in a single location** in 2024, so roughly 85% of dentists sit outside a DSO and carry billing, marketing and HR in-house ([Becker's Dental on ADA HPI](https://www.beckersdental.com/?p=21392) [snippet]; [DrBicuspid on ADA HPI](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/office-management/practice-trends/article/15683050/how-dentistrys-practice-models-continue-to-evolve)). Money is thinner than it looks. Average net income for a private-practice general dentist was **$207,980 in 2024 and $215,320 in 2025**; adjusted for inflation the average is down nearly **$60,000 since 2010** and the median down more than $64,000 ([ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dental-practice-trends) [snippet]; [Becker's Dental](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/good-news-bad-news-for-dentists/) [secondary]). Overhead runs **60-65% of collections** (one vendor analysis of 100+ practices puts general practice at 61.8% in 2024), **65% of practices reported higher overhead** in the latest Dental Economics/Levin survey with a rise of about 5.1%, and **55% named rising overhead the biggest challenge** in 2025 ([Clerri](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-practice-overhead-statistics/) [vendor]; [Dental Economics/Levin](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/practice/overhead-and-profitability/article/55280684/higher-production-helps-offset-chronic-overhead-increases) [snippet]; [Dental Economics, 2025 survey](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/practice/article/55368689/findings-from-the-2025-dental-economics-levin-group-annual-practice-survey)). The payer side has not moved: dentists' verbatim comments to the ADA in early 2026 say reimbursement is "downright abysmal", that "those payers have not increased reimbursement in over 20 years but these asking salaries have now almost tripled", and reimbursement indexes stood at 131-134 against inflation at 138 (2016 = 100); **35% planned to drop some networks** ([ADA HPI Q1 2026](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/state_us_dental_economy_q12026.pdf) [user, 2026]). The demand side has also softened: **one-third of dentists report they are "not busy enough"** (one-quarter in Q1 2024), new-patient wait time fell to 12.4 days, and one in five lack enough administrative staff ([ADA HPI Q1 2026](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/state_us_dental_economy_q12026.pdf)).

Staffing is the problem dentists name first in both countries, and the numbers show it has become a retention problem rather than a supply problem. **Only 60% of US dentists have enough hygienists**, **91% of those recruiting call it very or extremely challenging**, and this persists even though 2025 produced the most hygiene graduates ever and dental-office staff employment has been flat for 18 months ([ADA HPI, Apr 2026](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dentist-workforce/dental-hygienist-shortage)). Owners' own words to the ADA: "Not a single application in 9 months"; "3 months of sponsored advertising... and one tattoo artist applied"; "We now have to use a recruiter and pay 17% of first year's wage"; "I cannot compete against the DSO EFDA... because of all the benefits they pay them" ([ADA HPI Q1 2026](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/state_us_dental_economy_q12026.pdf) [user, 2026]). In the UK the GDC register grew to **131,680 in 2025** and international joiners outnumbered UK-qualified dentists for the first time, yet **77% of practice owners who tried to recruit dental nurses had difficulty**, **63% of owners with vacancies had posts open more than six months** (76% for high-NHS practices), the NHS general-dentist vacancy rate was **19%** in the survey the BDA cites (10% in the December 2025 survey), and **75% of dental nurses who change jobs leave dentistry altogether** ([GDC, 7 May 2026](https://www.gdc-uk.org/news-blogs/news/detail/2026/05/07/internationally-qualified-dentists-outnumber-uk-qualified-dentists-joining-gdc-register-for-first-time) [snippet]; [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf); [Dentistry.co.uk, 15 Nov 2024](https://dentistry.co.uk/2024/11/15/one-fifth-of-nhs-general-dentist-positions-unfilled-data-shows/); [Dentistry.co.uk, 31 Mar 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/03/31/dentistry-is-the-uks-most-loyal-profession/)). A UK dental nurse earns **£14.73 an hour**, within about £2 of a receptionist (£13.92), and nurses describe themselves as "glorified cleaners", "hidden" and "overworked and underpaid" ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/dental-nurse/salaries); [Dentistry.co.uk, 8 Apr 2024](https://dentistry.co.uk/?p=407492) [user, 2024]). Every dentist appointment needs a nurse, so an empty nurse post removes surgery sessions and, with it, UDA delivery.

The UK problem is the NHS contract, not the number of dental premises. NHS general dental contracts in England fell from **about 6,260 in March 2017 to about 5,650 in March 2026** while total practices rose by more than 400, so the share of practices with a general NHS contract fell from **about 65% to 56%** and roughly 1,000 more practices are now private-only ([GDPUK, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-ten-practices-have-ceased-general-nhs-dentistry-since-2017/) [secondary]; [The Probe, 1 Oct 2026](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/10/the-system-is-moving-but-is-it-changing/) [secondary]). Activity is rising but coverage is flat: **76.8 million UDAs** and **37.6 million courses of treatment** in 2025/26, yet **18.8 million adults (40%) seen in 24 months**, the same 40% as the year before; **78% of those who tried to get an NHS appointment succeeded** in the GP Patient Survey, but of those trying for a new appointment only **38% succeeded** ([NHSBSA 2024/25](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/statistical-collections/dental-england/dental-statistics-england-202425); [NHS England GP Patient Survey, Jan-Mar 2025](https://www.england.nhs.uk/statistics/statistical-work-areas/gp-patient-survey-dental-statistics-january-to-march-2025-england/); [GDPUK, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-ten-practices-have-ceased-general-nhs-dentistry-since-2017/) [secondary]). The money mechanism is the UDA: the average contract pays **£36.22 per UDA**, delivery below **96%** of the annual target is clawed back in full, and about **£1 in every £7 paid to NHS dentists was recovered over two years** (£479m in 2024, £457m in 2025) ([GDPUK, corporate vs independent UDA rates](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/news-in-focus/does-size-matter-corporate-versus-independent-uda-rates/); [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01672](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01672/en-us); [Dentistry.co.uk, 5 Mar 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/03/05/nhs-contract-clawback-at-900-million-in-past-two-years/) [secondary]). One owner "slightly under 96%" asked for leeway and was told "no, you'll need to pay the clawback money"; focus-group dentists call the contract a "hamster on the wheel" and say "Financially, it can't do it" ([GDPUK blog](https://www.gdpuk.com/blogs/guest-contributors/entry/2391-nhs-contract-changes-what-they-said-and-what-they-meant) [snippet]; [PMC12065166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12065166/) [user, 2023]). Income on the NHS side is **£83,000** taxable for a self-employed NHS-active dentist in England in 2024/25 (Scotland £99,100), after a fall of almost 35% over the decade to about 2022 ([Dentistry.co.uk, 3 Aug 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/08/03/dentists-taxable-income-rises-across-uk-with-scotland-nearing-100000/); [Dental Tribune UK](https://uk.dental-tribune.com/news/bda-survey-shows-most-dentists-want-to-leave-nhs) [secondary]).

The leakage numbers that marketing vendors sell against are real but all vendor-measured; no regulator or academic body publishes a dental missed-call, lead-response or recall rate in either country. The largest dataset, Patient Prism's 11.55 million calls across 8,280 US locations in 2025, shows **31 of 100 callers hang up before reaching anyone, 21 book, 13 walk away without booking, 4.8 get a follow-up call and 0.7 come back and book**; reasons for not booking were timing 45.2%, money and insurance 34.4% ("insurance outweighs price by more than 11 to 1"), service fit 10.3% and connection failed 9.3% ([Patient Prism, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.patientprism.com/report/dental-patient-access-report/) [vendor]). Three other vendors put unanswered calls at **35%, 37% and 38%** with different denominators, so the figure should be quoted as a vendor range ([DenteMax](https://www.dentemax.com/dentists/blog-articles/2025/Why_missed_phone_calls_are_dental_offices_largest_revenue_loss) [vendor]; [Dentaltown, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/26399/your-marketing-isnt-broken-your-front-desk-is) [vendor]; [Peerlogic, Feb 2026](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study) [vendor]). No-shows run **4%** on Henry Schein One's data, **6.9%** on Planet DDS's 2025 data, **9.1% to 14.3%** in peer-reviewed studies and 15-30% in marketing blogs; the spread is definitions (same-day cancellations counted or not) and payer mix ([Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index) [vendor]; [Ainora on Planet DDS](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-no-show-statistics-ai-reduction) [vendor]). ## 2. Their own words: the US says "no-show", "breakdown of benefits" and "denied three times"; the UK says "FTA", "UDAs", "clawback" and "CQC"

The two markets describe the same desk with different nouns, and the switch matters for every keyword list. The UK employs a "dental nurse", a "practice manager" (often "CQC registered manager"), works a "diary" and a "rota", claims "UDAs" on an "FP17" through "Compass", checks "exemption", records a "course of treatment" in "Band 1, 2 or 3", fears "clawback" and the "GDC", prepares for "CQC inspection", and sells "Denplan" or a "practice plan". The US employs a "dental assistant" and an "office manager", runs a "schedule", verifies "eligibility" and gets a "breakdown of benefits (BOB)", submits "claims" with "CDT codes", works "denials" and "appeals", calls a missed visit a "no-show", pays "in-network" or "fee-for-service", and sells a "membership plan" ([Reed](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/dental-nurse-jobs); [DentalPost](https://www.dentalpost.net/dental-jobs/dental-front-office-jobs/); [NHSBSA activity and payments](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/activity-processing-and-payment-services); [ADA CDT glossary](https://www.ada.org/publications/cdt/glossary-of-dental-clinical-terms)). A UK missed appointment is "FTA" ("failed to attend", "dental fta policy", "nhs dental fta policy"); a US one is "no show fee", "no show policy", "no show code" ([Google autocomplete, UK](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=en-GB&gl=uk&q=dental+fta); [Google autocomplete, US](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=dental+office+no+shows)).

## 3. Every workflow runs on a form, a phone and someone else's deadline

The UK mixed practice runs two front-end flows through one receptionist. For an NHS patient the desk must have the patient complete and sign the **FP17PR declaration before treatment begins**, ask for exemption evidence, note a valid certificate and its expiry on the record, and if no evidence is shown mark "No" in the "Evidence of exemption or remission seen" box, tell the patient that checks are routine, and have them sign; an unsure patient pays and is given an **HC5(D)** refund form ([NHSBSA exemption guide, V3 08/2021](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-03/Patient_exemption_checking_guide_dental_V3%20_08_2021.pdf)). The signed PR form "must be retained by the practice as part of the patient record for a period of two years", an electronic version is accepted only if the signature was captured by a system meeting the NHS anti-fraud electronic-signature standard, and "a practice cannot scan an original paper PR form and destroy the original" ([NHSBSA FAQ KA-01916](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01916/en-us)). The desk collects the band charge (£27.90, £76.60 or £332.10) and NHSBSA nets patient charges off the monthly contract payment, so any mismatch between what reception took and what the FP17 declared shows up on the monthly schedule and has to be chased ([NHS.uk charges](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/dentists/how-much-nhs-dental-treatment-costs/); [UDA Pulse](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/) [secondary]). The clinician completes the clinical data set and signs the dentist's declaration, one FP17 per course, and the claim must reach NHSBSA Compass **within two months of completion** or its UDAs are disallowed and marked "Late Submitted Claim"; corrected claims must also land inside the window; electronic claims need 48 hours to process and "may not show on Compass for a number of hours" ([NHSBSA FAQ KA-01820](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01820/en-us); [NHSBSA schedule programme 2026](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/Schedule-programme-April-2026-to-December-2026.pdf); [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01797](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01797/en-us)). The practice then works from Compass outputs: a monthly payment notification, the DCS129 UDA summary, the DCS127 patient-level activity report, a mid-year review at six months, and the **SD86c year-end statement around July with two months to dispute it** ([UDA Pulse](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/) [secondary]; [ICLR, Balachandra 2026](https://www.iclr.co.uk/document/2026001068/2026ewhc294kb_TNA/html)). Three more calendars sit on top: a **30 September checkpoint** for 30% of the urgent-care requirement, the **DSPT self-assessment by 30 June** each year (version 9 for 2026-27 is due 30 June 2027), and the CQC's expectation of staff files (identity, employment history, hepatitis B immunity, DBS), decontamination logbooks completed daily, weekly, quarterly or annually under HTM 01-05, fire risk assessment, IR(ME)R justification records and audit cycles, with "about two weeks' warning" of an inspection ([UDA Pulse](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/) [secondary]; [DSPT news 169](https://www.dsptoolkit.nhs.uk/News/169); [CQC mythbuster 19](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/dentists/dental-mythbuster-19-staff-recruitment) [snippet]; [Kent Express on HTM 01-05](https://kentexpress.co.uk/blog/the-importance-of-dental-decontamination-record-keeping) [secondary]; [Dental Nurse Network](https://www.dentalnursenetwork.com/news-articles/experiencing-a-cqc-inspection)). The private flow runs beside it: plan membership checks, a **written treatment plan and cost estimate before treatment** (GDC standard 1.6) and a fresh estimate if the plan changes (1.7), a deposit of **50-100% at booking** at one Plymouth practice, finance applications, an up-to-date medical history "each time that you treat patients", and consent discussions that must be recorded and, after Montgomery, must cover material risks and alternatives, since a signature "does not indicate valid consent" ([GDC Standards](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/standards-for-the-dental-team); [SpaDental Plymouth fees](https://www.spadental.co.uk/plymouth/fees/); [Dental Update](https://dental-update.co.uk/content/general-dental-practice/medical-histories-for-the-computerized-practice); [Dentistry.co.uk, Feb 2021](https://dentistry.co.uk/2021/02/15/informed-patient-consent/)). The 2026 contract adds per-clinician funded appraisals (£213 each), a quality-improvement audit on set topics (£3,400), an urgent-care volume to track (8.2% of contract value, 11 courses per £10,000) and complex-care pathway claims, which a consultant summed up as "increased admin, monitoring, benchmarking" for a practice that gains no administrative staff ([Dentistry.co.uk, 2 Feb 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/02/02/what-does-the-2026-nhs-contract-amendment-mean-for-dentists/); [Price Bailey](https://www.pricebailey.co.uk/blog/nhs-contract-changes-cma-review-dental-practices/) [secondary]). The US front desk runs a six-stage loop. Before the day it checks the answering service, email and portals for schedule changes and arrives at the huddle with open slots, overdue accounts and lab cases for today's patients; it **verifies eligibility and benefits one to three days ahead**, by phone or portal, at **5 to 30 minutes per patient** depending on whether the payer has a usable portal or forces a call, with holds "sometimes up to 30 minutes or more" at peak times and at the start of the year ([Burkhart Dental](https://www.burkhartdental.com/practice-guide/front-office-systems/front-office-task-flow-guidelines/); [GoAudits](https://goaudits.com/checklist/dental-office-manager-daily-checklist/918/156/); [Curve Dental, 8 Sep 2025](https://www.curvedental.com/blog/verifying-dental-insurance) [vendor]). The CAQH Index puts the average at about **12 minutes per manual verification, 7 per portal check and 4 fully electronic**, with dental spending on verification up 15% to **$2.1 billion** and a **$580 million** savings opportunity that stays unrealised because providers say the automated 270/271 transactions "often don't return data robust enough to be reliable" ([ADA News, 24 Mar 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/march/benefit-verification-drives-increased-administrative-spending-in-dental-offices/); [StealthAgents on CAQH](https://stealthagents.com/research/dental-insurance-verification-workload-statistics-2026) [secondary]). The day closes on paper: a day sheet, a transaction report, a card settlement report and a deposit slip, with the deposit often not closed until the next morning because card transactions are still pending ([Carestream WinOMS help](https://help.carestreamdental.com/rh/web/server/WinOMS/projects_responsive/WinOMS_CS_OnlineHelp/Managing_Your_Practice/Optimizing_Your_Practice/Performing_End_of_Day_Procedures.htm); [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/front-office/collections/article/16353145/what-happens-at-the-end-of-the-day-in-the-life-of-your-business-coordinator)). ## 4. The marketing market: $1,000-$2,500 a month bought on trust, with the front desk and the regulators in between

Spend is small, vendor-measured and badly defined. The only survey-based US figure is ProSites' 2022 report (110 practices, October 2021): **61% spent $501 or more a month, 35% more than $1,000, under 4% more than $5,000**, and nearly half of $1M+ practices spent $1,000-$5,000; the channels rated important were word of mouth (80%), online reviews (57%), website (46-51%) and SEO (46%), with TV, radio and mailers rated unimportant ([DrBicuspid, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/patient-communication/marketing/article/15379233/marketing-spending-reaches-new-high-in-2022-survey) [vendor survey]). Agencies and consultants recommend **3-6% of gross revenue** (5-8% in growth mode, 8-12%+ for start-ups), which for a $1M practice is $2,500-$5,000 a month, well above what the ProSites median practice spends, so the agencies' addressable market is the top third of practices by spend ([HIP Agency, 16 Jul 2026](https://hip.agency/dental-marketing-budget-guide-investing-the-right-amount-in-growth/) [vendor]; [Bulletproof Dental Practice](https://www.bulletproofdentalpractice.com/dental-marketing-budget-percentage/) [vendor]). Cost per new general-dentistry patient is quoted at **$150-$300** (implant and cosmetic cases $400-$800; SEO-sourced $50-$150 versus Google Ads $150-$350; rural $50-$75), and lifetime value at $5,500-$10,000, all by agencies ([Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-patient-acquisition-cost-statistics) [vendor]; [RankAI, 7 Dec 2025](https://rankai.ai/articles/dental-seo-pricing-guide-costs-factors-roi) [vendor]). The one dataset with treatment-level cost is Delmain's own 187 search campaigns (June 2026, 120-day window): cost per tracked lead, meaning a call or a form and not a booked patient, of **$7.64 branded, $50.60 general "dentist", $77.62 emergency, $87.01 dentures, $107.63 implants, $119.62 cosmetic and $212.63 Invisalign**, with the last three rows "thin and directional" ([Delmain, Jun 2026](https://delmain.co/blog/dental-patient-acquisition/) [vendor]). UK figures come from one self-described research publisher whose numbers are "directional benchmarks": marketing spend **5-10% of revenue** for private-focused practices, 3-5% mixed, under 2% NHS-heavy; monthly budgets **£500-£1,500** for a sole dentist, £1,500-£4,000 for two or three, £3,000-£8,000 for four to six; cost to acquire **£30-£150** from Google search, £20-£80 from social, £0-£50 from referrals; annual new-patient value £300-£500 NHS-heavy, £1,000-£2,500 private, £2,000-£5,000+ cosmetic ([Whito, May-Oct 2026](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-dentist-marketing-data/) [vendor]). UK agencies quote cost per lead by treatment that does not reconcile between them (implants £100-£180, £120-£250 or £180-£350; Invisalign £80-£150 or £120-£200; emergency £25-£40; one agency's 2025 average £62.01 per lead at 9.08% conversion) and an overall **£295 per new patient** (SEO about £170, Google Ads about £270), with Central London 40-60% dearer than Birmingham or Manchester ([Dominate Dental advertising cost guide](https://www.dominatedental.com/dental-advertising-cost-uk-guide/) [vendor]; [Dominate Dental case studies](https://www.dominatedental.com/dental-marketing-case-study-examples/) [vendor]). The UK demand backdrop is NHS overflow: of people without an NHS dentist who failed to get care, **11% went private and 78.5% did nothing**, 17% felt pressured to pay privately when booking, and only 7% in northern England could afford private care against 20% in the south ([The Dentist, BDA](https://the-dentist.co.uk/content/news/dental-association-urges-the-government-to-support-nhs-dental-services) [snippet]; [Healthcare & Protection](https://healthcareandprotection.com/patients-pressured-to-go-private-as-dentists-ditch-nhs-work/) [snippet]).

The leakage from lead to seated patient is the quantitative core of the dispute, and every number in it is a vendor's. Using Patient Prism's 2025 funnel, about a fifth of inbound calls become bookings and under 1% of lost opportunities are recovered by callbacks; **1,445,814 patients walked away without an appointment and 890,621 never got a callback** across 8,280 locations ([Patient Prism, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.patientprism.com/report/dental-patient-access-report/) [vendor]). Booked patients then no-show at 4-7% and cancel at 12.9% on aggregated practice data; seated patients accept 42-46% of diagnosed treatment; and the one AI-lead-response vendor claims (without a source) that 60-70% of paid dental leads are never successfully contacted, that Facebook form-to-booking is 5-8% with manual follow-up versus 15-22% with a sub-five-minute AI response, and that 53% of prospects give up after one missed callback ([Ainora on Planet DDS](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-no-show-statistics-ai-reduction) [vendor]; [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index) [vendor]; [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/lead-response-time-statistics-dental-2026/) [vendor, unsourced]). Every dataset found stops at cost per lead; **no source in either country reports cost per seated patient or cost per registered patient**, which is the metric one agency commentator calls the only one that matters, and no public, comparable data exist on agency contract terms, churn or client-reported ROI ([LinkedIn commentary](https://lt.linkedin.com/in/laurynas-mickus)). The UK leak is older and less measured: the 2017 DenGro survey (two-thirds on paper, 2-3 week gaps, fewer than half checking leads daily) and the 2021 East Sussex mystery shop (13.5% unreachable; practices that "did not log enquiries or keep waiting lists at all"; "frequent and repeated calls... can lead to severe pressure on the staff") are the only primary data, and a 2011 test found 44% of 3,557 calls failed to reach the team ([Dentistry.co.uk, 2019](https://dentistry.co.uk/2019/05/22/half-dental-practices-missing-leads/) [vendor]; [Healthwatch East Sussex](https://nds.healthwatch.co.uk/sites/default/files/reports_library/20220311_East%20Sussex_Mystery%20Shopping%20of%20Dental%20Practices%20in%20East%20Sussex.pdf); [Dentistry.co.uk, 1 Jul 2011](https://dentistry.co.uk/2011/07/01/44-cent-phone-calls-fail-reach-practice-team/) [snippet]).

The rules that bite marketing sit on the pages that bring in private patients, and the enforcement found so far is against large players, with small practices exposed by the same wording. In the US, OCR's tracking-pixel bulletin was partly vacated on **20 June 2024** (the "IP address plus health page visit" theory exceeded HIPAA authority) and HHS withdrew its appeal on 29 August 2024, but guidance on authenticated pages such as patient portals still stands and private wiretap suits continue: Aspen Dental settled a Meta Pixel and booking-data class action for **$18.7 million** covering two million people who booked online between February 2022 and January 2025 ([HIPAA Journal, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.hipaajournal.com/texas-judge-vacates-ocr-website-tracking-technology-guidance/); [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/hhs-ocr-withdraws-tracking-technologies-appeal-in-aha-v-becerra); [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/dentistry/research-and-news/news/55307953/aspen-dental-reaches-187m-settlement-in-data-tracking-case)). The FTC's consumer-review rule took effect **21 October 2024**: fake or AI-generated reviews, review incentives conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews and suppression of negative reviews can draw civil penalties of about **$51,744-$53,088 per violation**; a prize draw for 4-5 star ratings violates it; the first public use was warning letters to ten unnamed companies on 22 December 2025, and no dental case has been found, although a Minnesota dentist's 5-star ratings came under investigation ([FTC, 14 Aug 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [CDA, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/practice-management/promoting-a-fake-or-purchased-review-youre-violating-the-ftc-rule/); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/keeping-it-real-ftc-targets-fake-reviews-in-first-consumer-review-rule); [Fox 9](https://fox9.com/news/fake-reviews-fox-9-investigators)). Under the TCPA, appointment reminders need prior express consent while marketing texts need **prior express written consent**, damages run **$500 per message ($1,500 if wilful) with no cap**, messages must stop after any STOP or verbal revocation, and the FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit on **24 January 2025**, one business day before it would have taken effect ([Appointment Reminder TCPA guide](https://appointmentreminder.com/guides/tcpa-compliance) [vendor]; [Morrison Foerster, 30 Jan 2025](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule)). Google's personalized-advertising policy bars remarketing on health content including invasive and cosmetic procedures, so agencies say implant and sedation pages cannot carry remarketing audiences while general dentistry messaging can ([HawkSEM](https://hawksem.com/blog/healthcare-ad-restrictions/) [vendor]; [Google Ads community, 2019](https://support.google.com/google-ads/thread/8464402/can-i-do-a-remarketing-campaign-for-dental-services?hl=en)). State boards' specialty-advertising rules are in flux: Texas's rule tying "specialist" to ADA-recognised specialties was struck down (Fifth Circuit) and replaced in March 2021, South Dakota's was ruled unconstitutional, and Ohio proposed loosening in 2025 ([DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-business/regulatory-updates/article/15371674/us-judge-rules-for-aaid-over-texas-board-on-specialist-ads); [Becker's](https://www.beckersdental.com/?p=25668) [snippet]). Replying to a review with any patient detail is a HIPAA breach: three dental OCR cases (Elite Dental 2019 $10,000; Igbinadolor 2022 $50,000 for a Google review reply; New Vision Dental 2022 $23,000 for Yelp) and an Iowa board fine were all for review responses ([HIPAA Journal penalty list](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-violation-fines/); [Iowa Capital Dispatch](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/?p=59244) [snippet]).

In the UK the GDC's 2013 advertising guidance is still the current page: a practice website must show the practice name and address, contact details, the GDC's contact details or a link, the complaints procedure (NHS body for NHS care, Dental Complaints Service for private), and the **date the website was last updated**; any named professional must show qualification, country of qualification and GDC number; adverts must say whether the practice is NHS, mixed or wholly private; only dentists on a GDC specialist list may use "specialist" (others may say "special interest in", "experienced in" or "practice limited to", and DCPs may not use "Smile specialist"); social posts on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn or X must state that the treatment "may not be appropriate for every patient" and depends on assessment; and the registrant "is responsible for checking that it is correct", with no mention of agencies ([GDC advertising guidance](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)). A peer-reviewed audit of practice websites in North East England and North Cumbria found **only seven (1.8%) fully compliant**, blamed on registrants not knowing the guidance, and the sweep in this research found no "last updated" date on any of five sites and GDC numbers on no home page ([PubMed, BDJ 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33767368/); [Lund Dental](https://www.lunddental.co.uk/)). CAP's advice says before-and-after images "must be genuine and representative" with "signed and dated proof", "Dr" must not imply a medical qualification (the ASA ruled in 2000 that dentists' use of "Dr" misleads), whitening and brace efficacy claims need evidence such as clinical trials, Botox is prescription-only and cannot be advertised, and an ASA enforcement notice on braces cites rulings against Perfect Smile Surgery, 15 Dental and OrthoAccel for "less painful" and "faster" claims; the only recent dental ruling found is against a Turkish-treatment facilitator (Dental Centre Turkey UK, 16 Nov 2022, three issues upheld), and **no 2024-2025 ASA ruling against a UK dental practice was located** ([ASA/CAP, Dental: General, 16 Mar 2022](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/dental-general.html); [ASA/CAP, 9 May 2024](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/this-is-not-a-drill-dental-ads-wisdom.html); [GDC-hosted ASA enforcement notice](https://www.gdc-uk.org/docs/default-source/information-standards-and-guidance/guidance-documents/asa-enforcement-notice.pdf?sfvrsn=c28bb3a0_2); [ASA ruling A22-1157178](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/dental-centre-turkey-uk-ltd-a22-1157178-dental-centre-turkey-ltd.html)). The ICO finalised its storage-and-access (cookies and pixels) guidance on **29 April 2026** with chapters on consent for advertising tracking and measurement, and the GDC's social-media guidance forbids posting any information that could identify a patient, so a UK dentist "should never respond to patient's complaint directly on line" ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/); [GDC social media guidance](https://www.gdc-uk.org/docs/default-source/guidance-documents/guidance-on-using-social-media); [SD Magazine](https://www.sdmag.co.uk/2019/04/11/social-media-a-blessing-or-a-curse)).

## 5. Security and AI limits: fines land on missing records, ChatGPT has no BAA, and the GDC has no AI rule yet

A US dental practice is a HIPAA covered entity, so any AI vendor that touches protected health information is a business associate and must sign a business associate agreement; the California Dental Association's endorsed compliance partner tells dentists to "never use the free, public version of ChatGPT with patient data", to "avoid vendors that refuse to sign a BAA, including medical AI companies", and notes that OCR guidance on AI "is forthcoming" ([CDA/Abyde, 27 Aug 2025](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/endorsed-services/ai-in-dentistry-what-are-the-hipaa-violation-risks/) [vendor]). OpenAI's help centre, quoted second-hand because the page blocked reading, says "we don't offer a BAA for ChatGPT Business", only sales-managed Enterprise or Edu accounts are eligible, and the API is BAA-eligible with modified retention; ChatGPT for Healthcare (8 Jan 2026) has no self-serve option and BAAs are "prepared case by case, previewed only under NDA"; a health-economics centre's line is that "once you enter something into ChatGPT, it is on OpenAI servers and they are not HIPAA compliant... that is, technically, a data breach", with civil penalties from **$145 to $2,190,294 per violation** by culpability tier ([IntuitionLabs, updated 9 Aug 2026](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/is-chatgpt-hipaa-compliant) [secondary]). The practical reading: a small practice using the consumer app is outside any BAA by default, and a vendor that answers calls and transcribes or books is a business associate, not a conduit, so AI receptionists, website chat widgets (any free-text box "should be assumed to collect PHI") and staff "pasting claim narratives or chart details into unapproved AI tools" are three separate disclosure paths ([My Social Practice, Sep 2025](https://mysocialpractice.com/2025/09/ai-and-hipaa-compliance-in-dentistry/) [vendor]; [Accountable HQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-compliance-do-you-need-a-baa-for-your-website-chat-widget-if-patients-discuss-symptoms) [vendor]; [DeepInspect](https://www.deepinspect.ai/blog/ai-vendor-risk-dental-practices) [vendor]). The American Association of Endodontists adds that general AI tools "lack knowledge of carrier claims histories, state rules, and policy exclusions" and give confident but unreliable coverage answers ([AAE](https://www.aae.org/specialty/why-dentists-should-be-careful-when-using-ai-to-make-insurance-decisions/)).

OCR's record against dental practices is a record of paper and policy failures, not of hacking. HIPAA Journal's penalty list carries **eleven dental entries since 2019, from $5,000 to $140,000**, and the causes repeat: refusing or delaying patient record requests (six cases), disclosing patient details when replying to online reviews (three), and having no privacy officer, policies or training. The latest, Shen Smiles (Pennsylvania), was announced on 8 October 2026 at **$140,000**: a 2020 complaint about unfulfilled record requests led OCR to find records "in plastic boxes, the floor, and a private bathroom", files often missing at appointments, "no formal HIPAA Privacy Rule training" and no breach-notification policies ([HIPAA Journal penalty list](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-violation-fines/); [Paubox, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.paubox.com/blog/dental-practice-agrees-to-140k-settlement-after-records-complaint) [vendor]). The largest dental sum came from a state attorney general: Westend Dental (Indianapolis, about 17,000 patients) paid **$350,000** after a 2020 MedusaLocker ransomware attack it reported two years late and described to the state as "not a ransomware attack"; investigators found usernames and passwords in plain text, no risk analysis, no HIPAA training before November 2023, no BAA with the non-employee managing the practices, servers "unprotected, in employee break rooms and bathrooms" and incomplete backups, and the case began with a patient whose records "could not be provided due to a hacking incident" ([HIPAA Journal, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.hipaajournal.com/indiana-ag-westend-dental-hipaa-violations/)). First Choice Dental (Wisconsin) paid **$1.2 million** to settle a private class action over an October 2023 hack affecting more than 150,000 patients ([LexBlog, 18 Nov 2025](https://www.lexblog.com/2025/11/18/dental-practice-settles-data-breach-lawsuit-for-1-2-million/)).

The proposed HIPAA Security Rule rewrite would raise the floor for every practice regardless of size. Published in the Federal Register on **6 January 2025**, it would remove the "addressable" category so nearly all specifications become mandatory, require **encryption at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication for all access to ePHI systems "with no size exceptions"**, a written asset inventory, network segmentation, **vulnerability scans every six months and an annual penetration test**, written and tested incident-response and recovery plans with restoration of critical systems within 72 hours, and direct obligations on business associates; compliance would be 60 days to effect plus 180 days ([Washington State Dental Association, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.wsda.org/news/blog/2026/04/29/hipaa-security-rule---proposed-rulemaking-update); [Georgia Dental Association, 26 May 2026](https://gadental.org/news/what-dental-practices-need-to-know-about-the-proposed-hipaa-security-rule-changes); [D3RX](https://d3rx.com/sra/guides/hipaa-mfa-requirements) [vendor]). The ADA joined a coalition letter on 17 February 2025 asking OCR to rescind it; status reports conflict (final action targeted May 2026 in one account, July 2027 in the Unified Agenda per another, "final" per one vendor), and the Federal Register and hhs.gov pages blocked reading, so whether it has been finalised is unresolved. What is not in dispute: OCR's position that "the size of a practice is irrelevant when it comes to compliance", that "in 2022, more than half of OCR financial penalties targeted small medical practices", and that compliance costs "can reach tens of thousands of dollars for practices with thin margins"; the Senate HELP Committee voted 22-1 in February 2026 to advance a bill that would codify MFA and encryption and fund grants for small practices ([Physicians Practice, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/small-practices-face-growing-cybersecurity-burden-as-federal-rules-tighten); [Outsource Accelerator, Aug 2026](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/hipaa-security-rule-delay/) [secondary]).

US AI governance is standards and insurer caution rather than rules. The AGD adopted a 2023 statement that AI should not replace the dentist in clinical decisions; the ADA published a white paper (1106:2022), a technical report on evaluating image-analysis systems (1109:2025) calling for an independent validation dataset "kept by a third party that is not an AI manufacturer", and the first ANSI-approved US dental AI standard (1110-1:2025); the FDA's AI-device list shows a steady run of dental radiograph 510(k) clearances for Overjet, Pearl, VideaHealth, Diagnocat, DS Core and DTX Studio through 2025-2026, which makes imaging the one dental AI category with a clear regulatory status while scribes, chatbots and phone AI sit under HIPAA contract rules only ([AGD policy](https://www.agd.org/advocacy/agd-priorities/agd-policies/agd-policies/artificial-intelligence-in-dentistry); [ADA News, 3 Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/what-are-the-standards-for-ai-use-in-dentistry/); [FDA AI-enabled device list](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/list-artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices)). The main dental malpractice insurer tells dentists that "liability in cases of malpractice involving AI decisions is a legal gray area", to "obtain informed consents that explicitly cover AI use", set protocols and documentation, and verify AI output against the standard of care; it does not say which tools are safe and does not mention BAAs ([CDA/TDIC, 27 Jan 2025](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/practice-management/ai-in-dentistry-strategies-for-managing-risks-and-potential-for-malpractice-claims)). California's AB 3030 (1 January 2025) requires a disclaimer on generative-AI patient communications about clinical information unless a licensed provider reviews them, exempts scheduling and billing messages, and does not name dental offices in any source found ([ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/california-requires-disclaimers-health-care-providers-ai-generated-patient)). Adoption is real and uneven: **43.3% of US dentists use AI for at least one task** (imaging 22.8%, insurance verification 13.6%, explaining findings 13.2%, reception or check-in 10.1%), **34.8% plan charting and note-taking** and 32.6% plan insurance verification, 82.6% never plan to use it for treatment recommendations, and the hesitancy is "driven in part by dentists' fears of AI replacing human judgment and the potential for errors leading to overtreatment"; a three-country survey found 32% daily users, 12% who tried and stopped (cost 28%, poor PMS integration 22%, accuracy 18%), and 40% of those quitting within three months ([ADA HPI, Jul 2026](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dentists-ai-usage-and-attitudes); [GDPUK, 1 Jun 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-three-dentists-now-using-ai-tools-survey-finds/)). The reputational limit on imaging AI is public: a Wall Street Journal columnist's AI-read X-ray led to a four-session periodontal plan "costing thousands of dollars" that three other dentists said home care would handle, practice staff said owners asked "Why didn't you drill it?", Pearl's site was quoted claiming "37 percent more disease and deliver 24 percent more care", and a 2026 journal article examined marketing of Overjet, Pearl, VideaHealth and Diagnocat built on "pay for itself" through treatment acceptance; Pearl's CEO replied that clinicians "didn't disagree with the x-ray analysis" ([Futurism, 16 May 2026](https://futurism.com/health-medicine/dentists-ai-unnecessary-dental-work); [Dentistry Journal](https://doi.org/10.3390/dj14060341); [Pearl](https://hellopearl.com/blog/ai-in-dentistry-unpacking-the-upsell-question) [vendor]).

The UK has no profession-specific AI rule. As of 16 October 2026 the GDC has published only a review of 45 international studies ("No UK-based studies were included") and, on **17 September 2026**, a joint statement of intent with the medical, nursing and social-work regulators to develop "high-level principles" with no timeline; Dental Protection asked the GDC on 9 September 2026 to clarify "professional accountability" because "open-ended expectations may create uncertainty"; a parliamentary report warned that clinicians using AI diagnostic software "could face negligence claims even when a defect originated in the AI system" ([GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/5054) [snippet]; [Dentistry.co.uk, 17 Sep 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/09/17/gdc-to-develop-rules-for-ethical-ai-use-in-healthcare/); [GDPUK, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/top-news/dental-protection-calls-on-regulator-to-deliver-clear-guidance-on-ai-use/)). The MHRA, with NHS England, drew the device line on 2 August 2026: tools used only for "transcription, consultation summaries, draft referral letters or suggested clinical codes" for clinician review "are not medical devices under current law", while products supporting "diagnosis, treatment or prevention" or that "take automated action without clinician review" are regulated devices, and "clinicians remain responsible for checking AI-generated transcripts, summaries or outputs" ([Dentistry.co.uk, 2 Aug 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/08/02/ai-scribes-guidance-dental-practices/)). NHS England's ambient-scribe guidance reached version 3 on **29 July 2026** with a separate information-governance guide and template DPIA (24 March 2026); the GP-side summary of it says most scribe products that summarise "would likely qualify as a medical device", practices must complete DCB0160 documentation (safety case, hazard log) and a DPIA, consent "must be explicit and informed", notes and recordings may be disclosable under a subject access request, liability is "a complex and largely uncharted area, with limited case law", and "CQC inspection teams will consider if: Technologies are safely deployed (practices need a DPIA)" ([NHS England publication page](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-enabled-ambient-scribing-products/) [snippet]; [GPCE summary, May 2025](https://www.essexlmc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/GPCE-Guidance-on-the-use-of-AI-enabled-ambient-scribing-products-in-health-and-care-settings-A-Summ.pdf)). The Chief Dental Officer's warning on 9 October 2026 that "the volume of AI notes is getting incredibly scary", with records "the length of textbooks" and clinicians "logging in the evenings", is the first regulator-level signal that note volume itself is now a problem ([GDPUK, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/volume-of-ai-notes-in-patient-records-becoming-incredibly-scary-warns-wong/)). Under UK GDPR a dental law firm warns that "the user terms between a UK dentist and an AI company based in the US, may put the dentist in breach of their own data protection policies", and "pseudonymised data... is still personal data" ([JFH Law](https://jfhlaw.co.uk/ai-and-gdpr-compliance-are-dental-practices-in-breach-of-their-own-data-protection-policies/); [Dentaljuce](https://www.dentaljuce.com/ai-chat-gpt-and-data-privacy)). The DSPT is the NHS data-security self-assessment every practice with NHS patient data must complete yearly (dentists are Category 3; version 9 for 2026-27 is CAF-aligned and due 30 June 2027), and "contracts with the NHS may not be renewed or commenced" without a sufficient submission ([DSPT news 169](https://www.dsptoolkit.nhs.uk/News/169); [Presencis](https://presencis.com/questions/does-my-organisation-need-dspt/) [vendor]). The ICO keeps issuing public reprimands rather than fines to health bodies; no monetary penalty or reprimand against a UK dental practice was found, the nearest small-primary-care case being a GP surgery reprimanded in February 2026 for sending 23 years of records to an insurer that asked for five, because it "lacked written procedures for insurance requests" and regular training ([Mills & Reeve, Jan 2025](https://www.mills-reeve.com/blogs/health-and-care/january-2025/no-fines-just-fine-tuning-the-icos-reprimand-strategy-boosts-data-protection-in-the-health-and-care-sector/); [Freevacy, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.freevacy.com/news/ico/ico-reprimands-gp-surgery-over-excessive-disclosure-of-medical-records/7095)). Record rules add their own clocks: dental clinical records are kept **11 years minimum** (children until the 25th birthday), a subject access request must be answered **within one month**, free, with the clock starting at the request, and at least one practice website still advertises £10 and £50 fees for records that conflict with the free-access rule ([NHSBSA FAQ KA-01913](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01913/en-us); [MDDUS call log](https://www.mddus.com/resources/publications-library/insight-dental/q2-2021/call-log-dentolegal-dilemmas); [Dentistry.co.uk, 26 Jun 2018](https://dentistry.co.uk/2018/06/26/data-protection-evolution-not-revolution/)). On reviews, MDDUS advises that "responding directly to a negative review online could breach confidentiality" and to reply only with an acknowledgement and a link to the complaints policy ([MDDUS, Apr 2022](https://www.mddus.com/resources/resource-library/case-files/2022/april/case-study-adverse-comment)).

Small practices are hit through their vendors as often as directly, and the vendor outages stop the money. The 2019 ransomware attack on the backup firm DDS Safe/PerCSoft encrypted files at about **400 dental practices**, some fearing missed payroll; Henry Schein's September 2023 attack (AlphV/BlackCat, 35TB claimed) notified 29,112 people and cost an estimated 10-15% of customers "went elsewhere"; Delta Dental's MOVEit breach exposed about **7 million** people's claims data; and the 21 February 2024 Change Healthcare attack (190 million people) left dental services "not fully restored" nearly ten weeks later, with dentists mailing paper claims (which "may not work if claims require electronic attachments"), paying for postage and call-centre time, and the ADA asking Congress for prompt-pay rules and cyber support ([KrebsOnSecurity, 29 Aug 2019](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2019/08/ransomware-bites-dental-data-backup-firm/); [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/henry-schein-cyberattack-29k-affected/702036/); [Orthodontic Products, 18 Dec 2023](https://orthodonticproductsonline.com/practice-management/it/delta-dental-hack-exposes-sensitive-data/); [ADA News, 16 Apr 2024](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2024/april/ada-comments-on-congressional-cyberattack-hearing/); [CDA, 12 Mar 2024](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/newsroom-archives/recommended-actions-for-practices-affected-by-change-healthcare-cyberattack/)). In the physician proxy survey, **36% suspended claim payments, 32% could not submit claims, 22% could not verify eligibility** and 90% were still losing revenue two months later ([AMA follow-up survey](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/change-healthcare-follow-up-survey-results.pdf)).

The everyday security state of a small practice is best described by a UK IT provider's analysis of **192 on-site audits at 184 dental and orthodontic practices** (median three surgeries): **48% ran out-of-date Windows or server software** (Windows 7 in 40 practices, a couple on XP, Server 2008 and 2012 still holding live patient data), 45% slow or unreliable internet, 29% no proper server (a desktop "pressed into service"), **25% generic shared logins or weak passwords**, **20% exposed or unmanaged remote access**, 20% a consumer router with no real firewall, **17% backups failing, partial or absent**, 9% Wi-Fi shared with patients, and antivirus "inadequate / none" at 43% of the sites where it could be assessed; anonymised cases include RDP "left open to the entire internet", "Every member of staff logged in with the same password: Password1!... Every user was a full administrator... no multi-factor anything... no backup system at all", nightly backup logs reading "backed up files: 0", a cloud backup "quietly throwing errors since the previous April", and a practice on an NHS national network with "no firewall, just a basic router" ([Dental IT, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.dentalit.ltd.uk/assets/files/state-of-dental-practice-it.pdf) [vendor]). A UK practice adviser says "a huge number of dental practices still aren't using Multi-factor Authentication" although most software offers it free, and the US enforcement files above show the same pattern ([Practice Plan, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.practiceplan.co.uk/blog/cyber-security-in-dental-practices-why-it-matters/) [vendor]). Insurance now enforces what the rule proposes: carriers probe MFA on email, remote access, admin accounts and the PMS, EDR, full-disk encryption and immutable offline backups, "most carriers will not even issue a policy unless a practice proves it has implemented" them, misattesting lets the carrier deny a claim, and premiums run about **$1,000-$2,500 a year for a solo US practice** ($150-$200 a month per office in Florida; a $952 median for healthcare professionals generally) ([Techvera](https://techvera.com/resources/blog/what-dental-practices-need-to-know-before-renewing-cyber-insurance-in-2026) [vendor]; [Florida Dental Association, 17 Jul 2025](https://blog.floridadental.org/2025/07/17/hackers-dont-care-youre-a-dentist-why-cyber-insurance-matters-now/); [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/dental-office-insurance/cost)). Across all UK businesses, 43% identified a breach or attack in the year to April 2025, phishing hit 35% of micro businesses, ransomware rose to 1%, and the average cost of the most disruptive breach was £1,600 (£3,550 where there was a cost) ([DSIT Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/cyber-security-breaches-survey-2025)).

## 6. Verification and claiming: the data pull takes minutes, the phone call takes an hour, and the signature stays human

The US cycle has six stages and the automation sits unevenly across them. Stage one and two, collecting insurance details and verifying eligibility and benefits, are where the money and the hours go: **$2.1 billion** of dental administrative spend in 2023, up 15%, with **1.2 billion verifications**, about 12 minutes each when done by phone, fax or mail, 7 by portal and 4 fully electronic, and **82% electronic adoption for dental against 96% for medical with 15% still fully manual** ([ADA News, 24 Mar 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/march/benefit-verification-drives-increased-administrative-spending-in-dental-offices/); [Deepgram on CAQH CORE](https://deepgram.com/learn/ai-voice-agent-benefits-verification) [secondary]). The HIPAA 270/271 transaction is available inside the PMS (Open Dental sends it to ClaimConnect, EDS or Trojan; Denticon bills it as a flat monthly DentalXChange fee; Eaglesoft's Insurance Suite returns it in one click), but carriers "frequently" send "nothing more than a single yes or no response", so a person "must still interpret the data before importing", decide whether a percentage is the patient's or the insurer's share and choose in- or out-of-network; "30-40% of plans still require phone calls", complex cases (Medicaid, HMO, dual coverage, out-of-network) take 30-45 minutes, and one practice described manual checks taking "30 minutes to 4 hours per patient, depending on what insurance I needed to speak with" ([Open Dental manual](https://opendental.com/manual/ebenefits.html); [Planet DDS](https://support.planetdds.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002085652-How-does-the-Real-Time-Eligibility-feature-work); [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-insurance-verification-ai-statistics) [vendor]; [Weave, 12 Jul 2022](https://www.getweave.com/weave-launches-newest-addition-to-its-all-in-one-platform-insurance-verification) [vendor]). The products that automate it differentiate on portal scraping and breakdown compilation, not on the EDI transaction: Zuub (direct payer connections "bypassing EDI clearinghouses"; Capterra 4.7 from 38; complaints that it cannot search by insurer, name or date of birth and that "sometimes patients don't have their sub ID"), Pearl Precheck (June 2024; pulls clearinghouses, payers and EOBs into a downloadable breakdown; claims "30% more patients" covered), Curve Eligibility+ (batch pulls, "SmartSync" auto-update, "up to 95% faster"), NexHealth Verification (nightly sync of the next seven days, but insurance details are "not written to the EHR"), Weave Insurance Verification (one-click, results into the schedule, "over 50%" time saved), Overjet and Verrific; none publishes a price ([Capterra, Zuub](https://capterra.com/p/200040/Zuub/reviews/?page=2) [user, 2026]; [Pearl press release](https://hellopearl.com/press-release/pearl-launches-groundbreaking-ai-powered-dental-insurance-verification) [vendor]; [Curve Dental, 8 Sep 2025](https://www.curvedental.com/blog/verifying-dental-insurance) [vendor]; [NexHealth help](https://www.nexhealth.com/help/how-does-automated-insurance-verification-work) [vendor]). The human alternative is priced: eAssist verification at **$250 a month per location for under 30 checks, $650 for 31-75, plus $299 set-up**; others "as low as $500 a month" or **$2.50-$3.00 per check**; a DSO employs "insurance verification specialists" at $22-$27 an hour; Upwork sellers offer "Breakdown of Benefits (BOB)" via "Availity, Skygen, Delta Dental portals and many more" at $5-$20 an hour with 50+ proposals per post; Philippine VAs do it at $5-$8 an hour ([eAssist pricing](https://dentalbilling.com/pricing-dental-insurance-verification/) [vendor, snippet]; [Dental Claim Support](https://www.dentalclaimsupport.com/blog/compare-dental-insurance-verification-services-providers) [vendor]; [MB2 Dental job](https://dentalcareers-mb2dental.icims.com/jobs/21339/insurance-verification-specialist/job); [Upwork job](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022067110157780444776) [snippet]). Stage four, the claim, is cheap and electronic: about **$0.51 per claim** through DentalXChange, Vyne Trellis on one flat monthly fee covering claims, attachments, ERAs, eligibility and "denial support", Dentrix eClaims at unpublished bundle prices with free print-and-mail fallback; Change Healthcare is "not recommended" by Open Dental since the 2024 attack ([XLDent, 27 Mar 2024](https://www.xldent.com/whats-new/update-regarding-change-healthcare-and-recommended-options-2-2-2-2-2-2-2); [Vyne Trellis](https://vynedental.com/trellis/) [vendor]; [Open Dental clearinghouses](https://opendental.com/manual/clearinghouses.html)). Stage five, posting, is semi-automatic (ERA auto-match with detached exceptions). Stage six, denials and patient balances, is where the rework lives.

Denial rates have no regulator source and vary by definition. The defensible statement is a **4% benchmark against 8-15% at most practices**, with vendor and podcast figures of 10%, 12%, 15%, 19.3% and "nearly 1 in 5", a podcast's reading of Delta Dental transparency files at **22.5% of 932,795 in-network claims denied in 2024** (17.7% for the rest of the market), and a 2026 survey of 160+ billing professionals in which **nearly 80% of practices reported more denials or payer scrutiny** in the past year ([DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/revenue-cycle-management/article/55409403/the-7-revenue-cycle-gaps-most-dental-practices-dont-know-they-have) [snippet]; [Henry Schein One, 10 Sep 2024](https://www.henryscheinone.com/insights/blogs/the-nightmare-of-eligibility-and-verification-in-dentistry/) [vendor]; [The Dental Billing Podcast](https://thedentalbillingpodcast.buzzsprout.com/1783772/episodes/19634075-27-837-denials-zero-appeals-make-this-make-sense) [secondary]; [Becker's on Zentist](https://beckersdental.com/revenue-cycle-management/78-of-dental-offices-see-uptick-in-claim-denials-report) [snippet]). A study of one large insurer found **72.88% of denials were administrative** rather than clinical, which matches the cause list: bundling (four bitewings with a comprehensive exam), missing or thin narratives (perio, crown replacement inside the frequency limit), frequency limits and calendar-year versus policy-year mismatches, coordination-of-benefits errors (missing primary EOB), and wrong identifiers (date of birth, subscriber ID, NPI) ([DOAJ record](https://oalib-perpustakaan.upi.edu/Record/doaj_f6a7b7d2bd974390a2df2a59c1fa211f/Details?print=1); [Curve Dental, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.curvedental.com/dental-blog/dental-claim-denials-whats-causing-them-and-how-to-stop-the-cycle) [vendor]). Only about **5% of claims reach appeal and about 50% of those are reversed**, mostly periodontal; **54% of denied claims are eventually paid after an average of three resubmission cycles of 45-60 days each**; an appeal needs the original claim, the EOB with the reason highlighted, clinical notes, radiographs or photos and a cover letter, within 90-180 days, with "a new, more detailed narrative" each round, and reviewers "decide only from what was submitted" ([dentalbilling.com](https://dentalbilling.com/?p=31917) [snippet]; [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/revenue-cycle-management/article/55409403/the-7-revenue-cycle-gaps-most-dental-practices-dont-know-they-have) [snippet]; [Periospectives](https://onlinedigeditions.com/article/Dental+Insurance%3A+Managing+The+Maze+Of+Appeals/3263094/551201/article.html) [snippet]). Days in AR run **32-45 on average, 40-60 at solo practices**, AR over 90 days is 18% of total on average against a 5-10% target that practices admit "could seem unrealistic", and a clean-claim rate of 95%+ is "leading" while the majority sit at 75-85% ([Dental Billing Assist](https://dentalbillingassist.com/blog/posts/dental-billing-kpis-benchmarks) [vendor]; [Arini](https://www.arini.ai/blog/days-sales-outstanding-benchmarks-dental-practices) [vendor]; [ACT Dental](https://www.actdental.com/blog/data-snapshot-accounts-receivable-percentage) [vendor]). Outsourced billing costs **4-10% of collections** (most quotes 5-7%; one tiered schedule charges $1,350 a month under $40,000 collected, 3.5% to $100,000, capped at $3,500) or $4-$12 per claim ([MedsDental](https://medsdental.com/how-much-do-dental-billing-services-companies-charge) [vendor]; [Bulletproof Dental Practice](https://www.bulletproofdentalpractice.com/outsourced-dental-billing/)). Upstream of all of this is credentialing: a new dentist assembles licence, DEA, NPI, CAQH, CV, diplomas, board certification, malpractice history, ID, immunisations and references; timelines "vary by payer and state", vendors quote 30-45, 60-120 or 90-120 days per carrier, "Medicaid enrollment in particular can take months", CAQH must be re-attested every 120 days, in-network status resets on a tax-ID change, and **85% of new dentists were not enrolled in (or unsure about) the ADA's credentialing service** ([ADA News, 6 Jul 2026](https://adanews.ada.org/new-dentist/2026/june/credentialing-101-what-new-dentists-need-to-know); [Pearl credentialing guide](https://hellopearl.com/blog/dental-credentialing-a-step-by-step-guide) [vendor]).

The UK cycle is one claim per course against a contract target, and its risks are deadlines and reconciliation rather than per-procedure adjudication. At the desk the patient signs the PR form; if an exemption is claimed one "evidence seen" box must be ticked; for under-18s both the age box and the evidence box; the tax-credit exemption is no longer accepted for courses accepted on or after 6 April 2025 ([NHSBSA FP17 guidance V14, Aug 2025](https://cms.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/Completion_of_form_guidance_FP17-England_20250801_%28V14%29.pdf)). NHSBSA then sample-checks exemption claims after the event: it checks its own databases by NHS number, then DWP benefit records through an API answering within five seconds, about **1.2 million checks a month** across prescriptions and dental, posts an enquiry letter where it cannot confirm, and issues a **penalty charge notice of five times the charge up to £100 plus the charge, with a 50% surcharge after 28 days**; patients confirmed by Real Time Exemption Checking are excluded from the sample ([gov.uk algorithmic transparency record](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/nhsbsa-prescriptions-and-dental-exemption-check); [NHSBSA, understanding penalty charges](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/understanding-penalty-charges)). The volumes are large: **115,463 dental PCNs to Universal Credit claimants alone in the year to 16 May 2025** (60,677 had paid, none taken to court), 96,489 in the first eleven months after checks resumed in February 2024, 8,232 to income-based JSA claimants; the NAO found **about 30% of PCNs (1.7 million, £188m) later withdrawn** because a valid exemption was confirmed after challenge, around 47% of dental treatments claimed as exempt, and £212 million lost to wrongly claimed exemptions in 2017-18; NHS England pays NHSBSA **£7.59 million for six months** of a Dental Exemption Checking Service ([NHSBSA FOI-02776](https://opendata.nhsbsa.net/dataset/foi-02776); [NHSBSA FOI-02518](https://opendata.nhsbsa.net/dataset/foi-02518); [NAO 2019](https://www.nao.org.uk/report/investigation-into-healthcare-penalty-charge-notices/); [Find a Tender 081407-2025](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/081407-2025)). The liability sits with the patient "regardless of whether the FP17PR form is completed by the patient, someone on the patient's behalf, or dental practice staff", the DDU advises staff not to advise patients on entitlement, the BDA objects that eligible people "shouldn't face £100 penalties by default just for ticking the wrong box", and the enquiry letters come back to the desk as calls and complaints ([NHSBSA exemption guide](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-03/Patient_exemption_checking_guide_dental_V3%20_08_2021.pdf); [DDU Journal, 2017](https://ddujournal.theddu.com/issue-archive/autumn-2017/handling-patient-complaints-around-bsa-fines); [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/3201-bsa-slated-for-fining-disabled-person-with-mental-age-of-two)).

The FP17 itself is validated against band rules. Since 1 April 2022 the clinical data set must match the band claimed (Band 1 only Band 1 items; Band 2 needs at least one Band 2 item and no Band 3; Band 3 needs a Band 3 item; urgent only Band 1 or 2 items), failures first produce **comment codes 011-014** on the schedule (no treatment to justify the band; treatment higher than band; Regulation 11 without an appliance; free repair without a repairable item) and NHSBSA said it would warn before these became rejecting errors; error 115 (code not valid for date, region, age or claim type) and 125 (unacceptable combination) reject outright; highest BPE sextant score and NICE recall interval are mandatory on adult banded claims; "Further treatment within 2 months" in the same or lower band carries no charge but an urgent course within two months "must be claimed as a separate course of treatment"; the dentist's declaration "must be completed by a qualified dentist on every form" ([NHSBSA FP17 guidance V11.4, Oct 2022](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-11/Completion_of_form_guidance-FP17-England_202210_V11.4_Final.pdf); [NHSBSA, changes to FP17s](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/changes-fp17s-dental-teams-england); [NHSBSA common errors](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-09/Common_Errors_FP17W_from_1_April_2022_FINAL.pdf)). Year-end reconciliation "is completed by September each year": **below 96% delivery, clawback up to 100% of the shortfall plus a breach notice; 96-100% carried forward; 100-102% over-delivery carried forward; above 102% not paid**; carried-over activity must be completed before any new-year activity counts ([NHSBSA FAQ KA-01672](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01672/en-us)). The April 2026 reforms converted urgent care to a **£75 credit per course (£15 fixed, £60 activity) with a minimum of 8.2% of contract value**, moved fissure sealants to Band 2, added a 2-UDA denture-repair sub-band, a 0.5-UDA fluoride-varnish item, complex-care pathway credits of £284, £709 and £248, and converted all cash items back to UDAs at each practice's own rate for reconciliation, described by an accountancy firm as "no new investment... a redistribution of the current contract base" ([Price Bailey](https://www.pricebailey.co.uk/blog/nhs-contract-changes-cma-review-dental-practices/) [secondary]; [Dentistry.co.uk, 21 Jul 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/07/20/nhs-dental-contract-reform-whats-changed-and-where-do-we-go-from-here/)). The reporting gap followed immediately: Compass's "Unscheduled" figure shows only the £15 element (1.6% of contract), the £60 element "cannot be identified on Compass", a contract can hit its total on Compass and still miss the target, associates cannot see the split because eDEN is limited to commissioners and providers, an LDC motion called it "a fundamental contract management failure", and conversion errors "have tended to favour the NHS BSA" ([GDPUK, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/news-in-focus/compass-without-direction-practices-unclear-on-uda-achievement/)). The practice software does the sending and flags the rejection but not the reconciliation: EXACT sends the FP17 "as part of every claim"; Dentally marks a rejected claim "Invalid" with a notification that must be found through the NHS Claims report filter and fixed by hand, cannot invalidate a claim once an exemption is linked to it, and for Scotland requires each dentist to "manually count teeth present, filled, and extracted for every check-up claim" with its product director admitting "Auto-add fees and GP17 DMF are known gaps"; NHSBSA "are unable to advise on EDI transmission issues" ([SOE knowledge base](https://soeidental.atlassian.net/wiki/x/JgHsVQ); [Dentally help](https://help.dentally.com/en/articles/3563621-dealing-with-nhs-england-claim-problems); [Dentally community, Feb-Apr 2026](https://community.dentally.com/other-54/lack-of-functionality-for-scottish-users-598) [user, 2026]; [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01797](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01797/en-us)). No NHSBSA statistic for the share of FP17s rejected, late or carrying comment codes was found, and no minutes-per-claim figure exists for UK desks.

## 7. The platform audit angle: Open Dental publishes its prices, everyone else quotes, and nobody sells the reconciliation

## 8. Ten handoffs drop the same three things: the owner, the timing and the money

Each handoff in the patient and payment chain is owned by a different product, and none of them owns the step before or after it: the lead CRM stops at "new patient created", the PMS stops at "treatment planned", the analytics layer surfaces "unscheduled" but relies on a human to call, and the comms layer only knows about appointments that already exist. The numbers that practices trust (case acceptance, aging, recall) differ by product because each defines the denominator differently, so the gap is definitional before it is operational ([DI vs Dentrix aging](https://educate.dentalintel.com/en/articles/15481262-why-the-claims-aging-metric-doesn-t-match-dentrix-s-insurance-aging-report) [vendor]). ## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the opportunity in two ways. First, the problem a small dental practice will pay to remove is not dentistry and not even "the phone"; it is the chain of handoffs that each product owns one link of and nobody reconciles: the lead the agency delivered against the patient the PMS seated, the treatment the dentist planned against the appointment nobody booked, the breakdown the add-on produced against the ledger the biller works, the FP17 the software sent against the UDA target Compass can no longer show, the record request the patient made against the thirty-day clock. In 2025-2026 that chain came under outside pressure in both countries at once: the NHS reforms of April 2026 and a reporting system that cannot show the new target; the FTC review rule, the proposed Security Rule and OCR's October 2026 dental settlement; the MHRA's device line and the Chief Dental Officer's warning about AI note volume; insurers that will not bind cover without MFA and backups; and a marketing market in which neither the practice nor the agency can prove what the money bought. The strongest sales language is therefore reconciliation and evidence, not answering or generating, and the vocabulary is split by country: "clawback", "UDAs", "FP17", "FTA", "exemption", "CQC", "GDC" and "Denplan" for the UK; "no-show", "breakdown of benefits", "claim denied", "PPO", "office manager", "HIPAA" and "membership plan" for the US.

The second lesson is about where not to go and what to leave human. AI receptionists, SEO, review tools, reminders, offshore verification labour and imaging AI are where the vendors, the gigs and the venture money already sit, and their users complain about contracts, prices, accents and upselling, not absence. The steps the law reserves to a person (the dentist's FP17 declaration and the clinical band, the patient's signed exemption form, Montgomery-standard consent, the clinician's narrative and signature on an appeal, the review of any AI transcript, the release decision on a record request, the BAA that consumer ChatGPT does not offer) are boundaries to design around, and every one of the thirteen gaps stops at one of them. On money, an employed US dental-AI engineer earns in a month what the visible freelance market pays for one custom voice-agent build, the UK employed figure equals a year or two of a single agency retainer, the seats are a handful per vendor in named hubs with low sponsorship volumes, and the freelance floor is set by a $700-a-month VA who already knows Open Dental; the only budgets above that floor found in the open market are integration and reconciliation work, which is also the work the practices, the agencies and the regulators have all, in different words, said is missing.
