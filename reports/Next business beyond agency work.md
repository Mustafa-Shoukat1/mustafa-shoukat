# Sell the decisions, bill the team

## The short answer

**Go this way, but not as a "CTO" yet. Sell a paid two-week AI check-up to mid-size firms outside Saudi Arabia, then a monthly fee where you decide and your team builds.**

1. This week, ask a Saudi labour lawyer, in writing, if you can do this from Riyadh on your work residency.
2. Until the lawyer says yes, do not sell, sign or invoice in your own name, and sell to Saudi customers only through Navid.
3. Write one client story with one real number and the client's written OK.
4. Sell one offer at one price: check-up $2,500, then $4,500 a month.
5. Message your past clients first, then ten US and UK business owners a day.
6. Put one person in charge of building, and change nothing else for 90 days.

This is research, not legal advice. Everything below is the "why", for when you want it.

## The reasons in one paragraph

The route you chose on 3 October works only in a smaller form than the one you described. People who win part-time executive roles are veterans: in the one survey found, **72.8% had 15 or more years of experience and only 6.4% had under ten**, and 92.8% got clients through people who already knew them ([Fractionus, summarising the Frak 2024 survey](https://fractionus.com/blog/fractional-work-statistics-2025-income-market-data)). You have about five years, an unfinished degree, no written client result, and a team size and income that no file proves. The version that fits you is an outside AI lead who brings his own builders: a fixed-price check-up that turns into a monthly fee, sold first to your past clients and to US and UK firms of about 50 to 250 staff. A fair starting price is **$2,500 for the check-up and $4,500 a month**, the low end of what advisers charge and inside your own rate card. The biggest danger is legal, not commercial: on an employer-sponsored Saudi residency, working "for your own account" brings **a SAR 10,000 fine and deportation on the first offence**, and your employer's permission does not fix it ([Gulf News, 25 August 2026](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-warns-expatriates-of-jail-sr50000-fine-for-working-for-themselves-1.500651242)). On the researchers' reading of the rules, selling to Saudi customers is lawful today only when Navid signs the deal. Done well, this route reaches about three to five monthly clients in a year, roughly $14,000 to $35,000 a month against under $10,000 today (my arithmetic, not a forecast). That is a good step, not "very big money". The big money comes later, from owning a team business that many firms pay every month.

## Part-time AI chiefs are 15-year veterans; you have five

The role you want has a name: a fractional executive (a part-time boss who works for several firms at once). The only survey found, of 250 such people in the US in 2024, says most charge **$5,000 to $8,000 a month per client** and hold two or three clients at 10 to 15 hours a month each. It also says who they are: 72.8% have 15 or more years of experience, 30.4% have 26 or more, and 6.4% have under ten. And it says how they win work: 92.8% through people who already know them, while 59.6% name finding clients as their biggest problem ([Fractionus](https://fractionus.com/blog/fractional-work-statistics-2025-income-market-data)). A June 2026 rate guide puts a light adviser seat at $4,000 to $10,000 a month and an active part-time technology chief at $10,000 to $25,000 ([A.Team](https://www.a.team/talent/guides/fractional-cto-rates)). A seller's guide to part-time AI chiefs says buyers expect someone who has shipped AI products at scale, sold a company or led a large engineering group ([Groovy Web](https://www.groovyweb.co/blog/fractional-ai-cto-cost-usa-2026)).

Your files do not match that profile today, and it is better to hear it here than from a buyer. Your live LinkedIn supports **five years and two months** of experience, while your resumes say six or eight ([decisions file][DEC]). Your degree runs to August 2027. LinkedIn showed 8,471 followers, 202 post views in a week and no current job ([LinkedIn audit][LIA]). No client story with a measured result exists for any project; your own sales pack says "Missing, build it first" ([proposal templates][PT]). Your income ("under $10K a month") and your team ("15 to 20 people") are your own words with no invoice, ledger or staff list behind them. The files show about eight to ten named people around your projects and a company GitHub group with one member, you ([profile][FP]; [position file][PVM]; [GitHub inventory][GI]; [3 October note][MEM]). Staff who do not write code would not show on GitHub, so the team number is unproven, not disproven. The market, though, is moving your way. Buyers of cheap, simple work are leaving: Fiverr's active buyers fell 21.9% in a year, while Upwork's AI-related work grew more than 22% and its "AI strategy and consulting" group grew more than 50%. These come from the companies' own results, seen through summaries because the pages were blocked ([Fiverr Q2 2026](https://www.fiverr.com/news/fiverr-q2-2026-earnings); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/upwork-q2-revenue-falls-17-though-ai-related-work-provides-bright-spot)). Most firms that try AI get stuck. An MIT study found about 95% of company AI pilots showed no measurable return, and that buying from a specialist outside firm worked about 67% of the time, roughly three times as often as building in-house ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)). Gartner expects over 40% of AI agent projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). In the Gulf, 54% of firms are still testing AI and 31% have rolled it out widely ([Enterprise AM, on McKinsey](https://enterpriseam.com/uae/2025/11/19/gcc-firms-ramp-up-ai-adoption-but-only-11-are-realizing-meaningful-value-mckinsey/)). A firm in that position needs someone to say what to build, what to stop, and to get one thing working. That is a real job for an outsider, and you have something most solo advisers lack: builders, and live systems they built.

If the team number is true, the first win is more income per person from the people you already have.

## Riyadh residency decides where you can sell, before price does

The rule is short. A foreigner on a work iqama (a residency permit tied to one employer) may work only for that employer. On 25 August 2026 the Ministry of Interior warned expatriates against working "for their own account": first offence, **a SAR 10,000 fine and deportation**; second, SAR 25,000, a month in prison and deportation; third, up to SAR 50,000, up to six months and deportation ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-warns-expatriates-of-jail-sr50000-fine-for-working-for-themselves-1.500651242)). Your employer's permission does not make it legal ("Permission from your sponsor protects nobody"), and an employer who allows it faces a SAR 10,000 to 20,000 fine of its own ([Saudi Life Guide](https://saudilifeguide.com/?p=1751); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/Saudi-Arabia-updates-Labour-Law)). The Saudi freelance permit is for citizens only ([Motaded](https://motaded.com.sa/blog/saudi-freelance-regulations-guide)). Using a Saudi friend or a Saudi company as cover for your own business is a separate crime, called tasattur (concealment): **up to five years in prison, a fine up to SAR 5 million, and deportation with no return** ([Anti-Concealment Law, Ministry of Investment translation](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Anti-Concealment-Law.pdf)).

On the researchers' reading of those rules, two things are clear and one is not. Clear: pitching, signing or invoicing for your own business to Saudi customers while on Navid's sponsorship is the plainest breach. Clear: selling to Saudi customers is lawful when Navid signs the customer and you sell as Navid's employee, with any commission written into your job contract and paid through payroll. Not clear: whether you may own shares in a foreign company and advise foreign clients remotely from Riyadh in your own time. No source answered that. **It needs a written opinion from a Saudi labour lawyer before any selling starts.** The lawyer must also read your Navid contract, which is not in your files and can be stricter than the law on outside work, on who owns what you build, and on competing after you leave (Saudi law allows such a clause for up to two years if it is written and limited by place and type of work) ([Etqan Law Firm](https://etqanlawfirm-sa.com/en/article-83-saudi-labor-law/)). The research could not find how often professionals are actually punished for own-account work. Treat that as unknown, not as safe. One more caution from the researchers, as their reading and not a quoted rule: being the public face of your own firm on LinkedIn or at Saudi events creates evidence of own-account work even if contracts are signed abroad.

There is a tax trap as well. If you live in Riyadh and you negotiate and sign deals for your foreign company, the Saudi tax office can treat that company as having a taxable presence in the Kingdom: 20% tax on the profit tied to it, plus registration and yearly filings ([Dariba](https://www.dariba.co/?p=264)). Saudi customers must also hold back 5% of fees for technical and consulting services paid to foreign firms, even when all the work is done abroad ([ZATCA circular, January 2025](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Tax-Circular-WHT-Implementation-Under-Double-Taxation-Agreement.PDF)). One commentary says work done fully abroad escapes this ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/withholding-tax/1581596/withholding-tax)); the tax office's own text should win. **This needs a Saudi tax adviser.**

The structure that fits these rules has three layers, in this order. First, a Pakistan private limited company, registered with the Pakistan Software Export Board, employs the team and sends the invoices, with a trusted director who lives in Pakistan, signs contracts and runs delivery. Export income brought in through banks is taxed at 0.25%, and budget reports say that rate now runs through tax year 2029 ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40187354); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/13/federal-budget-fy2026-27-advances-ministry-of-it-telecoms-vision-for-a-digital-nation-pakistan/amp/)). The final law was not read, so **confirm with a Pakistani tax adviser**. Dividends you take out have 15% held back ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/pakistan/corporate/withholding-taxes)). Stripe does not serve Pakistan, so money arrives by bank wire or Payoneer ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/global); [Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/about/pricing/)). Second, add a US company only when a US client needs a US contract or card payments: about $500 through Stripe Atlas, about $400 a year after that, plus one yearly tax form with a $25,000 penalty if it is missed ([Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/doola-vs-stripe-atlas); [Private Law wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/us-llc-non-resident)). Skip a UK company unless UK clients insist, because it is taxed in the UK wherever its owner lives ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/95011)). Third, and later, a UAE free-zone company for Gulf clients outside Saudi Arabia: AED 6,010 to 12,900 a year without a visa, no company tax while income stays under AED 3 million and the small-business relief is claimed (now extended to 2029), with the bank account as the hard part for Pakistani owners ([Avyanco, RAKEZ](https://avyanco.com/rakez-free-zone-cost/); [Avyanco, IFZA](https://avyanco.com/ifza-free-zone-cost/); [Osome](https://osome.com/ae/blog/small-business-relief-extended-2029/); [Flying Colour](https://www.flyingcolour.net/pk/blog/common-mistakes-pakistanis-make-when-starting-a-business-in-uae/)). None of the three makes selling from Riyadh lawful by itself. They only decide who signs and where the money lands.

Navid needs its own deal. The Arabic document platform sits in Navid's code store, and under the Copyright Law in force since 12 August 2026, work an employee creates in the job and related to the employer's business belongs to the employer unless a contract says otherwise ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)). You cannot reuse that code, its prompts or its data in your own business without a written licence. What you can do is bring Navid customers and be paid for it, or have Navid license the system to your foreign company in writing. No source showed how such deals are normally written in Saudi Arabia, so **a Saudi employment lawyer should draft it**.

If the lawyer says remote advising from Riyadh is not allowed, three lawful versions remain. You do the whole thing inside Navid, as its AI adviser to customers, on salary and commission. Or the Pakistan company sells through people who live outside Saudi Arabia while you stay an owner only, which is itself a question for the lawyer. Or you change the basis of your residency. Premium Residency at **SAR 100,000 a year** (about $26,700) lists no degree rule and allows both a job and a business. The talent tracks need a finished degree or an SAR 80,000 monthly wage, so they stay closed until at least August 2027. The startup licence excludes "traditional IT companies" ([Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2024/09/tawaref-series-saudi-entrepreneurship-licence-glance)). The residency figures come from an adviser's summary; the official site could not be read.

In Saudi Arabia, training needs a licensed training facility and single trainers cannot be licensed ([TVTC](https://tvtc.gov.sa/en/ContactUS/FAQs/Pages/default.aspx)), while the state has already trained over a million citizens in AI for free ([Saudi Ministry of Education](https://moe.gov.sa/en/mediacenter/moenews/Pages/news1_09112025.aspx)). Paid online communities show a modelled median of $1,200 a month, and a large Indian creator course business (over INR 100 crore in five years, by its founder's count) closed in May 2026 with the founder calling AI's part in that "huge" ([Profitable.app](https://profitable.app/skool/stats); [Channel iAM](https://en.channeliam.com/2026/05/16/ankur-warikoo-closes-online-courses-business/)). What content does do is put you on the buyer's list early: one buyer study, quoted in a round-up, says 95% of business purchases go to a seller the buyer already had in mind on day one ([Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/b2b-social-media-statistics/)).

**Agency models.** A monthly managed service is the best-evidenced model: digital agencies keep about 13 to 15% of income as profit, and firms that price by value instead of by the hour report about 18% ([Agiled](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics)). Placing full-time developers has the clearest prices, $2,500 to $7,500 a developer a month in Indian sellers' guides, but the least protection, because any low-cost shop can replace you ([Acquaint Softtech](https://acquaintsoft.com/blog/dedicated-development-team-pricing)). Reselling other firms' tools rests on sellers' marketing only. On the case histories found, your own software product takes years and usually stays small; small software firms for sale list at about 2.6 times yearly income ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)). The lesson for your route: monthly fees plus placed builders are the engine, and the adviser seat is the door. Placed builders alone are easy to replace. Placed builders led by the person who decided what to build are not.

## One client story and a true team list come before any pitch

A buyer of advice checks the adviser first. Six things must exist before you pitch, and each is one to three days of work.

**One client story with one number.** The Philadelphia Realty Exchange WhatsApp bot is "in production with a real client". The step "pull one before/after number and write the first case study" was set on 6 September and has not been done ([position file][PVM]; [GitHub findings][GF]). Client names need written permission ([decisions file][DEC]). If the client refuses, publish the story without the name.

**One person who will vouch for you.** One of your six LinkedIn recommendations is from a past client who was a Global Director ([LinkedIn audit][LIA]). Ask him to take one reference call. Ask Navid for a reference letter; none is on file ([position file][PVM]).

**A public record nobody can disprove.** Your files list the weak spots: six or eight years where the record shows five; "air-gapped" for a system whose answers still come from an outside service; "contributor to Mulhem" with zero commits; code contributions called merged that were not; "trained 150+ professionals" with no record; and test scores of 0.87 and 0.91 that were "scripted, never run" ([decisions file][DEC]; [position file][PVM]; [five framings][FF]; [OpenShift proposal][OSP]). LinkedIn shows no current job. An adviser is paid for judgment, so one false line costs more here than in any other route.

**The team on paper.** Names, roles, full-time or part-time, monthly cost, and one named delivery lead. A buyer of "my team builds" will ask who. The research on owners who step back from delivery is thin and mostly stories, but the failure it describes is simple: nobody senior enough to catch bad work before the client does. Your files hold one warning already: a delivered WhatsApp product stopped working for three weeks in July 2026 because a licence you had set expired ([GitHub inventory][GI]).

**The money on paper.** Six months of income, client by client. No ledger, invoice or client list exists in the workspace ([workspace audit][WA]). The lawyer and the tax advisers will ask for it, and it tells you which clients to approach first.

**A sample check-up report.** Two or three pages from a real past project with names removed, so a buyer can see what $2,500 buys. This item is my addition; your rate card already says "the audit is the lead product" ([rate card][RC]).

One asset stays off the list for now. Whoza.ai is live, but its public site belongs to a teammate and no file says who owns its customers or income ([GitHub findings][GF]; [GitHub inventory][GI]). Get that in writing before you claim it.

The targets in the last rows are my planning numbers, built from other firms' results. In one agency's panel of 33 founder profiles, 15% of messages to target buyers were accepted and 21.4% of follow-ups got a reply ([RevBoss](https://www.revboss.com/founder-led-marketing-benchmarks)). Ten messages a day for 60 working days is 600 messages, about 90 new contacts and about 19 replies. On general benchmarks for professional services (seen in a summary only), 30 to 50% of good leads become real openings and 25 to 40% of those are won, which closes two or three ([Unbiased](https://www.unbiased.co.uk/pro/discover/business-growth/b2b-conversion-rates-benchmarks-strategies-and-industry-insights)). Past clients should do better than cold contacts, but that is my assumption. None of this is your own data yet, so count your real numbers every Friday: messages sent, calls held, check-ups sold.

You said you are good at sales but unpolished. The fix with the most support, though the support comes mostly from sellers of sales tools, is to record your own calls and review them. Among professional speakers and consultants, 65% of lost deals were lost right after the first conversation, and those who tracked their deals in a system earned far more than those who kept them in their heads ([SpeakerFlow](https://pages.speakerflow.com/2026-ssi-report/)). One seller's study of 326,000 sales calls found that won deals had shorter stretches of the seller talking ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/)). So ask more questions, talk in shorter turns, and always agree the next step before the call ends.

## What this research could not check

Every research file behind this report hit a web-search limit, and several pages were blocked, so some numbers were seen only in search summaries. Those are marked above where it matters.

## Conclusion

The useful change in view is this: the adviser seat is not the business. It is the door to the business. Advice alone caps at two or three clients and your own hours. Advice that brings a team behind it turns each "yes" into a monthly fee plus builders, and that is the only form in this research where a person with five years of experience can grow past the veterans' ceiling. The title follows the results, not the other way round: three written results let you raise the price by your own rate-card rule and call yourself what you now only want to be called.

Three dates line up, and they set the order. The lawyer's answer in October decides where you may sell at all. The rate card's expiry on 8 December is the natural day to reissue it with the two prices. Your degree ending in August 2027 reopens the Saudi residency routes just as the first results arrive. The test of this plan is not this file. It is whether a lawyer's appointment, one client story and the first ten messages exist by the end of next week.
