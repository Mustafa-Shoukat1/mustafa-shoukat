# UK small law firms (1-20 fee earners, outside London): practice areas, workflows, operational problems, regulatory burdens, and a website sweep

Research date: 10 October 2026. Scope: England and Wales primarily; Scotland (Dundee) and Northern Ireland (Derry/Londonderry) noted only where a swept firm is from there. Problem and workflow research only; no solutions or tools proposed.

Method note: web search budget ran out part-way through, so later evidence came from direct page fetches of firm sites, SRA/HMCTS/gov.uk pages and trade press. Several primary pages (lawsociety.org.uk, lawgazette.co.uk, some gov.uk pages) returned 403/404 to the fetcher; where a fact comes only from a search-result snippet rather than the full page it is marked "(snippet)". Where a firm's own site could not be reached, the failure mode is recorded because it is itself evidence about small-firm web infrastructure.

## Key question 1: Which practice areas do small UK firms offer (and which show up in regulatory price-transparency rules)?

### Takeaway
The 24 swept firms are overwhelmingly "high-street general practice": residential conveyancing, wills/probate/LPA and family appear on almost every site, followed by personal injury, employment, criminal defence, commercial property and dispute resolution. Immigration was absent from every reachable small-firm site in the target cities, and the SRA's price-transparency list (conveyancing, probate, immigration, motoring, employment, debt recovery, licensing) maps closely onto the consumer work these firms sell.

## Key question 2: Conveyancing — steps, manual steps, where delays and errors come from

### Takeaway
A standard residential purchase runs 16-22 weeks and is dominated by waiting on third parties (searches, lenders, the other side's enquiries, HM Land Registry), while the firm's own manual load concentrates in ID/AML/source-of-funds evidence, chasing unanswered enquiries, the 14-day SDLT deadline and avoiding Land Registry requisitions, which HMLR says are mostly caused by avoidable conveyancer errors such as name discrepancies and poor scans.

### Cited Findings
Workflow and timescales
- Stage estimates for a purchase: pre-contract work, ID checks and searches 2-4 weeks; mortgage offer 3-6 weeks; enquiries and legal checks 4-8 weeks; exchange to completion 1-4 weeks; total 16-18 weeks. Residential conveyancing "currently takes an average of 120 days (about 17 weeks)" on 2024 data, down from 123 days in 2023. Local authority searches usually ~10 working days but 3-5 weeks in busier areas; lenders often take 2-4 weeks to issue a formal offer. — [Carbon Law Partners, 11 Sep 2025](https://carbonlawpartners.com/2025/09/11/wondering-how-long-uk-property-conveyancing-takes-in-2025-we-break-it-all-down/)
- Causes of delay at the enquiries stage: slow seller responses, specialist reports, and "incomplete forms such as TA6 and TA10"; mortgage delays from missing/late financial documents and offers expiring after 3-6 months; any slow link in a chain pauses everything. — [Carbon Law Partners](https://carbonlawpartners.com/2025/09/11/wondering-how-long-uk-property-conveyancing-takes-in-2025-we-break-it-all-down/)
- A property legal expert quoted on 2 Sep 2025 puts the average transaction at 22 weeks against an expected 12-16 weeks, blames "delays in ID and proof-of-funds checks, slow local authority searches, miscommunication between solicitors, buyers, and sellers, and queries left unanswered for long periods", and says "paperwork wasn't ready early enough" is a common cause of wasted weeks; nearly one in three sales fall through (Zoopla data). — [Property Reporter, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/how-buyers-and-sellers-can-speed-up-conveyancing-delays.html)
- Lockings (Hull) claims "3 weeks quicker completion than the national average" and offers "no-sale, no-fee"; no absolute timescales are published; intake is "Submit an enquiry... the office team contacts you for an informal, obligation-free chat... matches you to a solicitor". — [Lockings conveyancing](https://www.lockings.co.uk/conveyancing/)

SDLT and registration deadlines
- "You must send the SDLT return and pay any tax within 14 days of the effective date of the transaction"; late filing penalty and interest apply; representatives using HMRC's online service "must file electronically" and "We reject returns that are printed and posted to us"; the SDLT5 certificate must accompany the Land Registry application (online via Business Gateway, or by post for paper). — [GOV.UK SDLT returns](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/stamp-duty-land-tax-online-and-paper-returns)
- HM Land Registry processing (July 2026 data, page updated 22 Sep 2026): register updates (~440,000/month) 41.6% completed within 1 day but 32.2% take over 3 months; complex changes and new entries (~37,000/month) 61.3% take up to 12 months and 12.5% longer; first registrations: half done in ~8 months, almost all in ~12; transfers of part/new leases: half in ~9 months, almost all in 11-13 months; the free expedite service clears the "vast majority" within 10 working days. — [HMLR processing times](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/hm-land-registry-processing-times)
- Avoidable requisitions (HMLR, 8 Dec 2025): 29% of professional customers reduced their avoidable requisition rate in six months; 57% now below 5%; 20% below 1%; some firms still "under 30%"; common causes are "name discrepancies, incomplete identity evidence, poorly scanned or missing documents, missing or incomplete information in forms"; 4,000+ staff trained by HMLR's academy since 2021. — [GOV.UK, HMLR requisitions](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/hm-land-registry-gives-more-information-to-help-cut-delays-and-costs)
- Earlier HMLR figures (Jan 2023, snippet): roughly one million requisitions a year; over half of 2022 requisitions involved avoidable issues; each requisition delays an application by an average of two weeks in simple cases and up to six weeks in complex ones. — [Mortgage Solutions, 10 Jan 2023](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/news/2023/01/10/one-in-five-transactions-held-up-by-basic-conveyancing-errors-land-registry/)
- Trade-press pushback (27 Aug 2024): two-thirds of complex applications take over 12 months (citing The Times); the Society of Licensed Conveyancers says delays in first registrations, transfers of part and complex leasehold "go back to 2016"; an anonymous conveyancer: "The Land Registry has thousands of transactions awaiting their attention for registration". — [Today's Conveyancer](https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/?p=72184)

AML, ID and source of funds (the firm-side manual burden)
- SRA AML report 2024-25 (activity to 5 April 2025): 935 proactive engagements; "almost a third of firms examined were assessed as non-compliant" and a further 54% only partially compliant; 16% of reviewed files had no or incomplete client/matter risk assessment and a further 39% "did not effectively evaluate money laundering risk" (largest single cause of SRA referrals); source-of-funds checks "often collected but not analysed" and explanations sometimes did not match funds received; some firms treat a positive electronic verification result as completing CDD; the SRA itself submitted 19 SARs from inspections, "many involving conveyancing". — [Legal Futures, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/blog/the-sras-2025-aml-report-what-law-firms-need-to-know)
- Same report via a vendor-written article (SearchFlow, flagged promotional): 833 firms inspected or desk-reviewed, 5,873 files reviewed, 47 sanctions inspections; 35+ fines totalling £565,000+ by late 2025 (individual fines £300,000 down to £658); 70% of early-2025 fines involved inadequate firm-wide risk assessments; conveyancing named the highest-risk practice area. — [Today's Conveyancer, 11 May 2026](https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/sra-tightens-grip-aml-enforcement-intensifies-2026/)
- The November 2023 CTS cyber-attack "delayed many conveyancing completions and affected around 80 law firms"; small firms and sole practitioners are "focusing on cashflow". — [SRA Risk Outlook, 19 Dec 2024](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/clients-changing-legal-market/)
- Conveyancing "accounts for the highest proportion of complaints referred to the Legal Ombudsman". — [Legal Ombudsman horizon scan, Oct 2023](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/media/ne1jmwfa/129-11-23-horizon-scan.pdf)

### Inferences
- The firm's controllable work in a conveyance is front-loaded (ID/AML/source of funds, obtaining TA6/TA10, raising enquiries) and back-loaded (completion statement, SDLT within 14 days, AP1 with correct names and scans); the middle 8-12 weeks is mostly waiting and chasing.
- Requisition statistics imply that for firms still at 20-30% avoidable requisitions, one in four or five post-completion registrations bounces back, adding 2-6 weeks of unbilled work per bounce.
- The SRA findings (risk assessments on file but not reasoned; source-of-funds gathered but not analysed) describe a documentation problem rather than a knowledge problem: the evidence exists but the written reasoning does not.

### Gaps
- The Law Society Conveyancing Protocol page (step list, forms TA6/TA7/TA10/TA13, completion information and undertakings) returned 403, so the authoritative step list could not be quoted.
- No small-firm-specific data on how long ID/AML onboarding takes per client, or what proportion of conveyancers use electronic ID verification.
- No current (2026) total requisition count from HMLR.

## Key question 3: Wills, probate and estate administration — forms, asset gathering, estate accounts, where time goes

### Takeaway
Grant applications are now fast when digital (4.9 weeks average, some under 24 hours) but still slow on paper (12.3 weeks), and the real time sink is third parties: HMRC's IHT400/unique-code step (~20 working days), banks and share registrars, and wet-ink IHT400 signatures; a typical small-firm estate still takes 6-9 months and distribution waits on 2-month and 6-month statutory periods.

### Cited Findings
Forms and sequencing
- If the estate is not "excepted", form IHT400 must be sent to HMRC "before applying", tax started, and the applicant must wait for HMRC's unique code (paper applicants wait for a letter with a code); PA1P (will) or PA1A (no will) are the paper forms; probate is "usually" issued within 12 weeks and "It takes longer to process paper applications than online applications". — [GOV.UK apply for probate](https://www.gov.uk/applying-for-probate/apply-for-probate)
- HMRC asks executors to "allow 20 working days from the date they sent their IHT400" before applying so the IHT421 can be matched; applying early means HMCTS cannot match it and the grant is delayed; HMRC targets 15 working days to process IHT400 and issue IHT421. — [The Gazette, 18 Dec 2020](https://thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/103873)
- HMCTS estimated 9,000 "stops" in 2022 were due to IHT400 forms submitted incorrectly; a stopped application goes into a separate queue and "can take more than double the amount of administrative time to issue a grant" (snippet). — [Myerson](https://myerson.co.uk/news-insights-and-events/changes-probate-application-process)

HMCTS performance
- April 2025 data: average time to grant 4.9 weeks (digital) and 12.3 weeks (paper); ~35,000 cases in the system at 31 May 2025; "some digital grants are now issued in less than 24 hours"; stops caused by scanning/paper errors (e.g. staple marks misread); automated stop reminders at 8 and 12 weeks; dormant-case alerts planned at 5 months; a second-tier phone line was trialled then dropped; phone SLA 15 minutes; revised PA1P/PA1A in development; "Paper application times remain inconsistent, and scanning delays are causing frustration". — [ICAEW, HMCTS update June 2025 (pub. 2 Jul 2025)](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2025-06/hmcts-updates-for-probate-practitioners-june-2025)
- December 2024 average submission-to-grant 9.6 weeks, down from 15 weeks in October 2023 (snippet). — [ICAEW, Jan 2025](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2025-01/hm-courts-and-tribunals-service-update)

Third-party delays and manual chasing
- HMRC is "the most common cause of a delay" on taxable estates; the electronic tax code "usually takes around 20 days"; online grant "can still take up to four weeks"; paper grants (e.g. intestacy without a spouse beneficiary) 9-12 weeks; banks vary (one releases up to £50,000 with a copy will and indemnity form, another requires probate above £5,000); tracing old shares via registrars is time-consuming; IHT400 "still need original ('wet') signatures" and clients being away or waiting for post holds up signing; practitioners must "chase third parties at appropriate points", track requests and responses, send estimates and regular updates "even when nothing has changed"; one firm uses case-management flags to email a bank's bereavement team when a date-of-death balance is overdue. — [ICAEW, Sep 2025](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2025-09/third-party-delays-in-probate-what-you-need-to-know)

What a small firm actually charges and does (worked example)
- Burton & Co (Lincoln): Stage 1 grant only — IHT205 estate £500 + VAT, IHT400 estate £950 + VAT; court fee £273, sealed copies £1.50; Stage 2 administration "in the region of £2500 - £4500 plus VAT" charged by grade and time (Partner £275/h, Assistant Solicitor £200/h, Legal Assistant £125/h); disbursements: bankruptcy searches £2.40 per beneficiary, statutory adverts (London Gazette and local press) ~£220-250, bank transfer £30; "the Probate Registry is estimating it takes 16 weeks" to issue a grant; estates "dealt with within 6-9 months"; distribution waits 2 months from adverts and 6 months from grant; the firm "liaises with the Coroner's office, Registrars, funeral directors, insurers, locksmiths, house clearance firms, and estate agents". — [Burton & Co probate pricing](https://www.burtonlaw.co.uk/probate-pricing/)
- TBI's pricing index offers downloadable PDFs for "Estate Administration (non-fixed fee)" and "Fixed Fee Probate... obtaining the Grant of Probate only service", and says that for some matters "the firm needs to learn more about the client's situation before giving a more accurate estimate". — [TBI pricing](https://tbilaw.co.uk/pricing/)
- Gard & Co (Plymouth) advertises "Fast Fixed Fee Probate" with no price shown. — [Gard & Co](https://www.gardandco.com/)
- Northern Ireland: probate can be applied for online or on paper; each online executor needs "a separate email address and mobile phone number" to sign the statement of truth; fee payable where assets exceed £10,000; a grant is "almost always needed" where ~£20,000 sits in one account or there is property; probate offices in Belfast and Londonderry; paper applications are posted or taken to the Probate Office. — [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/applying-probate)

### Inferences
- The grant itself is no longer the bottleneck for digital applicants; the 6-9 month estate timeline is driven by asset gathering (date-of-death balances, share tracing, pensions), the IHT400 wet-signature loop, statutory advert periods and beneficiary distribution checks.
- Fixed "grant-only" fees (Burton, TBI, Gard) push the unpredictable administration phase onto hourly billing, which is where time recording and client updates matter most.

### Gaps
- No source quantifying hours per estate or the proportion of small-firm probate applications still submitted on paper.
- Scottish executry/confirmation process (C1/C5, bond of caution, small-estate threshold) could not be sourced (scotcourts page 404; mygov page returned death-registration content).
- Estate-accounts preparation practice (format, who prepares) not sourced.

## Key question 4: Family law — Form E, court bundles, Legal Aid certificates, correspondence volume

### Takeaway
From 2 March 2026 a rewritten PD27A makes e-bundles the default, moves deadlines earlier (contents agreed 7 working days and bundle lodged 5 working days before every hearing), caps position statements at 6-15 pages, requires a fresh bundle and position statement for each hearing, and backs this with costs sanctions; family legal aid providers meanwhile operate at median break-even on fees frozen since 1996.

### Cited Findings
Bundles (PD27A as rewritten, in force 2 March 2026)
- Default: "the applicant should prepare, file and serve the bundle"; where the applicant is a litigant in person and the respondent is represented, the respondent prepares; bundle contents agreed "within 7 working days of the hearing", agreed bundle filed and served "within 5 working days of the hearing", preliminary documents "by 11a.m. on the working day before the hearing"; "a new bundle is needed for each subsequent hearing"; e-bundles are the norm, paper only in "exceptional circumstances" or for a judge/witness/LiP who cannot access an e-bundle; correspondence, emails, texts, voice notes, financial records and photographs are excluded unless directed; position statement limits: first appointment 6 pages, other interim 8, FDR 12, final hearing 15 (including schedules); chronology max 10 pages, witness statement 25, expert report 40, care plan 10; sanctions include removal from the list, adverse or wasted costs orders. — [Resolution guidance note, updated 26 Mar 2026](https://resolution.org.uk/guidance-note-court-bundles/)
- The new PD is "about twice the length of the previous version"; deadlines moved from 4 to 7 working days (contents) and 2 to 5 working days (lodging); FM5 and a trial template (for hearings of two hours or more) are new preliminary documents; "A position statement must be prepared for each hearing. Adding an 'update' to an earlier statement is not appropriate"; e-bundle page numbers must match PDF numbering, with OCR and orientation rules. — [Financial Remedies Journal, Alexander Chandler KC](https://financialremediesjournal.com/bundle-of-joy-new-practice-direction-on-bundles-position-statements-etc-effective-2-march-2026/)
- MyHMCTS (family public law, updated 3 Sep 2026): documents must be named by type, person and role (e.g. "Bundle_LA_Applicant"); the organisation must be registered and each user have an account; "Do not send any documents by email unless you have been asked to by a judge, or if the order is confidential". — [GOV.UK MyHMCTS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/myhmcts-how-to-apply-for-a-family-public-law-order/upload-other-documents-orders-or-bundles)

Legal aid economics in family work
- Legal aid fees "have not increased since 1996, and in 2011 were cut by 10%" while typical costs rose 90% since 1996; "The median family legal aid provider in the sample only reached their break-even point"; family providers fell from 2,401 (2010-11) to 1,254 (2019-20) (snippet). — [Law Society civil legal aid sustainability research](https://lawsociety.org.uk/topics/research/civil-legal-aid-sustainability)
- Housing and immigration fees were raised but "providers in nine other contracted categories of law, such as family, mental health and community care have yet to receive similar uplifts" (snippet). — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/laa-tries-to-avert-legal-aid-crisis-in-45-regions/5126988.article)

Small-firm family offerings
- Thompson & Jackson sells "Financial Agreements - Fixed Fee" and "Divorce & Separation - Fixed Fee". — [Thompson & Jackson](https://www.thompsonandjackson.co.uk/)
- Taylor Bracewell's family section is titled "Our Costs and Fixed Fees"; the firm states "This Firm does not accept service of documents by Email or Fax". — [Taylor Bracewell](https://www.taylorbracewell.co.uk/)
- Wragg Mark-Bell links a "Family Legal Aid" page; Allington Hughes offers "Legal Aid Solicitors" with a "How To Know If You Qualify For Legal Aid" page; Richard Reed is "contracted with the Legal Aid Agency"; Lockings handles care proceedings and PLO meetings. — [Wragg Mark-Bell](https://www.wraggmarkbell.co.uk/); [Allington Hughes](https://www.allingtonhughes.co.uk/); [Richard Reed](https://www.richardreed.co.uk/); [Lockings](https://www.lockings.co.uk/)

### Inferences
- Each financial-remedy hearing now generates a fixed cycle of work (agree index 7 days out, lodge 5 days out, draft a fresh position statement, file by 11am the day before), so a case with first appointment, FDR and final hearing means at least three full bundle builds under threat of costs sanctions.
- The exclusion of correspondence and messages from bundles means solicitors must triage large volumes of client communication rather than dump it, which is a sorting task not a drafting one.

### Gaps
- Form E content and attachment list (12 months' bank statements, pension CETV, valuations, P60/payslips) could not be sourced; the GOV.UK Form E page returned 403.
- No quantified data on correspondence volume per family matter or on time to obtain a Legal Aid certificate through CCMS.

## Key question 5: Immigration — Home Office forms, evidence bundles, deadline sensitivity

### Takeaway
Immigration work is deadline-driven by the Home Office and the Tribunal rather than by the firm: requests for further information jumped from 15% to 49% of one charity's applications between 2020 and 2024 (each adding about a month), tribunal e-bundles must meet strict single-PDF/OCR/bookmark rules or be rejected, and practitioners describe an inability to plan their week; none of the swept small firms in the target cities listed immigration.

### Cited Findings
- Change-of-conditions applications: the Unity Project received requests for further information in 15% of applications in 2020 and 49% in 2024; each request adds "around another month"; processing rose from 29 days to ten weeks as at November 2024 (confirmed by the Home Office in litigation). — [Free Movement, 19 Feb 2025](https://freemovement.org.uk/the-home-office-is-creating-unnecessary-additional-work-and-delays-in-change-of-conditions-applications/)
- An anonymous immigration solicitor: Home Office interview invitations or document deadlines "can arrive at any time"; "unless you're only doing straightforward visa applications, it's incredibly hard to plan your week"; tribunal bundle deadlines depend on when the Secretary of State files their bundle (snippet; page returned 403). — [Law Society](https://lawsociety.org.uk/topics/immigration/youre-under-pressure-to-stop-clients-being-tortured-or-killed-anonymous-immigration-solicitor-speaks)

## Key question 6: Personal injury and clinical negligence — medical records, OIC portal, MOJ portal, fixed recoverable costs

### Takeaway
Low-value PI has been pushed into portals and fixed costs: the OIC portal had 349,000 unresolved claims and a 251-day average settlement time in 2023 with 90% of claimants still represented despite its lawyer-free design, FRC now covers most £25k-£100k claims, and fixed costs for clinical negligence up to £25k are expected to deter small firms from that work; small firms in the sweep sell PI on "No Win No Fee" with phone-first intake.

### Cited Findings
- OIC ("whiplash") portal, live from May 2021: the Justice Committee reported 349,000 unresolved cases with an average 251 days to settle, up from 212,000 in May 2022; "only 10% of claims were brought by unrepresented claimants"; unrepresented claimants made 24,478 support calls against 38,438 claims (more than six calls per ten claimants); delays and low recoverable costs are "a significant risk to service providers choosing to not take on this work". — [Legal Ombudsman horizon scan, Oct 2023](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/media/ne1jmwfa/129-11-23-horizon-scan.pdf)
- FRC went live 1 October 2023 across the fast track and new intermediate track, so "the vast number of cases valued between £25,000 and £100,000" are captured; clinical negligence claims up to £25,000 were announced for fixed costs from April 2024 and fixed costs apply even if an overvalued claim settles below £25,000; "smaller businesses will not risk taking on these claims", a risk the Government acknowledged in its impact assessment. — [Legal Ombudsman horizon scan](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/media/ne1jmwfa/129-11-23-horizon-scan.pdf)
- GOV.UK: the clin-neg FRC scheme applies to claims "valued up to and including £25,000 in England and Wales"; after consultation (outcome 15 Sep 2023) fixed costs were raised at all stages, bolt-ons raised for protected parties and children, defendants made responsible for neutral evaluation costs, some complex claims excluded; the page gives no start date and a further consultation on disbursements is under way. — [GOV.UK consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/fixed-recoverable-costs-in-lower-value-clinical-negligence-claims)
- Small firms warned FRC for sub-£25k clinical negligence "could push them out of the clinical negligence market if set too low"; a minister declined to commit to inflation-linked FRC updates (snippet). — [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/government-lays-out-cautious-approach-to-extending-fixed-fee-system-across-personal-injury/print/)
- Official data exists but is report-based: OIC publishes quarterly (latest 1 Apr-30 Jun 2026) and monthly (Sep 2026) reports covering claims volumes, representation types, exceptional injuries, service exits and contact-centre enquiries. — [OIC data](https://www.officialinjuryclaim.org.uk/resources-for-professionals/data/); the MOJ Claims Portal executive dashboard (updated 21 Sep 2026) publishes RTA/EL/PL dashboards and warns that claims sent to insurers "include duplicates" and Court Pack numbers may not reflect court volumes. — [Claims Portal](https://www.claimsportal.org.uk/about/executive-dashboard/)
- Sweep evidence: Macks advertises "Call us for a free No Win - No Fee consultation now", phone hours "8am - 9pm" and a "Free Call Back" form; PSR offers "No Win No Fee" and online compensation calculators with appointments "by phone, Zoom or Skype video call or in person"; Salmons' PI solicitor is an "Assessor of the Law Society's Personal Injury Accreditation Scheme"; TBI lists Infected Bloods, industrial and medical negligence work. — [Macks](https://www.mackssolicitors.co.uk/); [PSR](https://www.psrsolicitors.co.uk/); [Salmons](https://www.salmonssolicitors.co.uk/); [TBI](https://tbilaw.co.uk/contact/sunderland-solicitors)
- Costs disputes: firms exist "whose business model is largely based on pursuing claims against service providers on behalf of their former clients" following Belsner v CAM Legal Services (Oct 2022), and the Ombudsman expected more costs complaints under FRC. — [Legal Ombudsman horizon scan](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/media/ne1jmwfa/129-11-23-horizon-scan.pdf)

### Inferences
- A small PI practice's unit economics are set by portal stage fees and FRC bands, so unbilled time (portal re-keying, chasing medical agencies, support calls) directly erodes margin.
- The "90% represented" figure shows claimants still route through solicitors, so the portal has added a data-entry layer for firms rather than removed them.

## Key question 7: Employment, criminal (Legal Aid, disclosure), landlord and tenant, debt recovery, small commercial

### Cited Findings
Employment
- Early conciliation doubled from six to 12 weeks from 1 December 2025 (snippet). — [Farrer & Co](https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/acas-early-conciliation-time-limits-extended-to-12-weeks--what-you-need-to-know/)
- ACAS Jul-Sep 2025 (published 23 Mar 2026): 34,333 worker-led individual notifications in the quarter; monthly receipts up 40% from July 2023 to September 2025; 66% of notifications did not progress to an ET1; 79% of tribunal claims concluded without a hearing, ~70% of those settled by ACAS. — [ACAS statistics](https://www.acas.org.uk/about-us/service-statistics/early-conciliation/early-conciliation-and-employment-tribunal-cases-data-2025-to-2026/july-september-2025)

## Key question 8: Administrative and regulatory burdens that apply to every matter

### Takeaway
Every file carries a fixed regulatory overhead (written client-care information at engagement, conflict check, AML risk assessment with recorded reasoning, bill before any client-account transfer, five-weekly three-way reconciliation signed by the COFA, 8-week complaints clock, price and complaints information on the website, annual competence declaration, PII renewal, accountant's report within six months); surveys consistently rank "administrative tasks" (52%) and compliance as small firms' biggest workflow and business challenges.

### Cited Findings
Client care, costs and complaints
- Code of Conduct 8.3: "clients are informed in writing at the time of engagement about" their right to complain, how and to whom, and any right to go to the Legal Ombudsman; 8.4: if not resolved "within 8 weeks following the making of a complaint" the client must be told in writing of the Ombudsman route and time frame; 8.5 complaints "dealt with promptly, fairly, and free of charge"; 8.6 information "in a way they can understand"; 8.7 "the best possible information about how their matter will be priced" at engagement and as the matter progresses. — [SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/)
- Consumers "must start your complaint by telling them first" and give the provider "eight weeks to respond" before the Ombudsman. — [Legal Ombudsman](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/for-consumers/complaints-process/complaining-to-a-legal-service-provider/)
- Website duties: price, basis of charging, experience of fee earners and supervisors, disbursements, VAT, key stages and likely timescales, CFA/DBA conditions (Rule 1.5) "clear and accessible" in a prominent place (1.6); complaints procedure including how and when to complain to the Ombudsman and SRA (2.1); SRA number and digital badge on the website (4.1); authorisation wording on letterhead and emails (4.2). — [SRA Transparency Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/transparency-rules/)
- TBI's pricing page: "The prices quoted on our website are not binding"; a separate quote follows formal engagement "which involves a Client Care Letter and Terms and Conditions of Business". — [TBI pricing](https://tbilaw.co.uk/pricing/)

Conflicts and supervision
- 6.1: "You do not act if there is an own interest conflict or a significant risk of such a conflict"; 6.2: no acting with a client conflict unless substantially common interest or competing for the same objective, with informed written consent, safeguards and reasonableness; 3.5: supervisors "remain accountable for the work carried out through them"; 3.6: ensure managed individuals are competent and up to date. — [SRA Code of Conduct](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/)

Client money and accounts
- Rule 8.2: obtain bank statements "at least every five weeks"; 8.3: "at least every five weeks... a reconciliation of the bank or building society statement balance with the cash book balance and the client ledger total, a record of which must be signed off by the COFA or a manager of the firm"; 4.3: give a bill or written notification of costs "before you transfer any client money from a client account to make the payment"; 8.1: accurate, contemporaneous, chronological ledgers, balance list and cash book; 12.1: accountant's report within six months of period end, delivered to the SRA if qualified; 12.2 exemptions where all client money is from the LAA or balances do not exceed an average £10,000 and maximum £250,000; 13.1: retain accounting records "for at least six years". — [SRA Accounts Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/accounts-rules/)
- Common reconciliation failures (ILFM, 12 May 2026): aged reconciling items carried forward for months, unpresented cheques never sent, receipts posted to the wrong ledger, duplicate entries, one-sided transfers, debit balances on client ledgers, "Reconciling to zero and moving on"; residual balances on dormant matters where the client cannot be traced or the matter was never closed; SRA inspections check timeliness, seniority of reviewer, aged items, debit balances, residual balances and whether the COFA is "genuinely engaged". — [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/blog/the-hidden-risks-in-client-account-reconciliations)

AML
- See KQ2: one-third of inspected firms non-compliant, 54% partial; 16% of files had no risk assessment and 39% an ineffective one; template forms that only tick high/medium/low are criticised because "the rationale for the risk level and level of due diligence should be clear on file" (snippet). — [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/blog/the-sras-2025-aml-report-what-law-firms-need-to-know)

Continuing competence
- Solicitors "make a declaration to us when renewing their practising certificate" that they have an up-to-date understanding of obligations and have "reflected and addressed any identified learning and development needs"; no minimum hours; recording is "not a regulatory requirement" but firms should consider how to evidence competence; "We do not currently specify any minimum or maximum period for retaining training records"; the SRA checks firms' training and supervision arrangements and reviews training records during inspections. — [SRA continuing competence FAQs](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/resources/continuing-competence/continuing-competence-faqs/)

Accreditations and PII

- The per-file overhead is a document set (client care letter, terms, risk assessment, conflict record, bill) plus a calendar (8 weeks, 5 weeks, 14 days, 6 months) that does not scale down for small matters, which is why fixed-fee commodity work is the most compliance-dense per pound billed.

## Key question 9: What Legal Aid firms specifically struggle with (LAA billing, CCMS, fixed fees, rates)

### Takeaway
Legal aid providers run on fees unchanged since 1996 (cut 10% in 2011), with median family providers at break-even and housing providers losing £33,000 per fee earner, through a billing system (CCMS) that costs lawyers warned in 2015 would make bills "more likely to be rejected" and slow payment, and which in 2025 was taken offline by a cyber-attack that forced months of manual contingency processes, dummy reference numbers and a replacement bulk-claim service.

### Cited Findings
- Fees "have not increased since 1996, and in 2011 were cut by 10%"; typical costs up 90% since 1996 and 40% since 2011; "Median providers in the sample are losing £33,000 for each full-time equivalent (FTE) fee earner providing housing legal aid"; median family provider at break-even; ~40% of the population lack a local housing legal aid provider; family providers 2,401 (2010-11) to 1,254 (2019-20); "Persistent high barriers to entry are disincentivising new providers" (snippets). — [Law Society sustainability research](https://lawsociety.org.uk/topics/research/civil-legal-aid-sustainability)
- The LAA invited housing and debt providers in 45 areas "to strengthen access and availability"; housing and immigration fees were raised, nine other categories were not (snippet). — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/laa-tries-to-avert-legal-aid-crisis-in-45-regions/5126988.article)
- Justice Committee (2021): "a rigid system of fixed fees and low pay is leaving legal aid firms struggling"; fixed fees under the 2007 Carter reforms do not reflect complexity so providers prefer hourly-rate cases. — [EIN](https://www.ein.org.uk/news/house-commons-justice-committee-finds-rigid-system-fixed-fees-and-low-pay-leaving-legal-aid)
- CCMS (ACL report, 13 May 2015): the system "deteriorates existing business processes", bills expected to take longer because the LAA requests information that cannot be included in the initial bill, bills "more likely to be rejected", "significant delays to payment" affecting cash flow "since they operate on margins with little contingency", bug fixes "can take months, even when they affect the wrong remuneration rate"; mandatory from 1 October 2015; administers £670m of civil legal aid; projected £69m lifetime cost. — [Family Law](https://familylaw.co.uk/news_and_comment/acl-issues-stark-warning-over-legal-aid-billing-system)
- CCMS cost £35m to develop; a senior partner using it for 80% of applications called it "yet another government IT horror show"; "Most of the profession predicts CCMS will drive small firms out of business" (June 2015). — [LAG](https://lag.org.uk/article/202742/legal-aid-agency-35m-billing-scheme-is-car-crash-waiting-to-happen-)
- Later: only one minor issue of the 23 the ACL detailed had been resolved (snippet, undated). — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/law/legal-aid-billing-issues-unresolved/5052937.article); the LAA apologised for "latest CCMS disruption" (snippet, undated). — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/legal-aid-agency-apologises-for-latest-ccms-disruption/5104877.article)

- Accounts Rules exempt a firm from the accountant's report only if "all of the client money held or received during an accounting period is money received from the Legal Aid Agency". — [SRA Accounts Rules](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/accounts-rules/)
- Sweep: of 24 reachable firms, four mention legal aid (Wragg Mark-Bell family legal aid; Allington Hughes "Legal Aid Solicitors"; Richard Reed "contracted with the Legal Aid Agency"; Lawson Coull & Duncan "Legal Aid"); a regional Bradford branch explicitly excludes legal aid for divorce/finance and immigration. — firm links above; [Ison Harrison Bradford](https://www.isonharrison.co.uk/branches/bradford/)

### Inferences
- A small legal aid department carries three overlapping clocks: LAA submission deadlines, CCMS rejection/resubmission loops, and (in 2025-26) reconciliation of contingency-period claims filed with dummy identifiers.
- Low mention of legal aid on small-firm websites in these cities is consistent with provider exit and "advice desert" data.

### Gaps

- Law Society legal aid pages (sustainability research, criminal justice campaign) returned 403; figures above rely on snippets.

## Key question 10: What court and HMCTS processes impose (bundles, CE-File, MyHMCTS, delays)

### Takeaway
Digital filing has added hard format rules (50 MB single-PDF bundles with OCR and bookmarks; named uploads; no email) and irreversible failure modes (a CE-File application rejected for a wrong auto-calculated fee "cannot be undone"), while queues have grown in the Employment Tribunal (hearings into 2028) and possession courts (26-32 weeks), even as probate grants sped up for digital applicants.

### Cited Findings
- Administrative Court User Group, 10 Feb 2025: callers to the Admin Court were "cut off" or told "to put this in an email, which often was the purpose of the call"; CE-File sets the fee automatically and "applications were being rejected because an incorrect fee had been used. Once the application is rejected there is no way of resurrecting it"; the former "insistence" procedure no longer exists; the out-of-hours form has no box for the PBA number; fee receipts go to the firm's accounts department not the case solicitor; bundle limits are 50 MB in CMS and 20 MB by email; PBA fees "not being taken... on time"; a user "was expecting specific guidance on CE-Filing before it became mandatory". — [Judiciary.uk minutes (PDF)](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACO-CUG-Meeting-10.02.2025-Minutes.pdf)
- Upper Tribunal IAC bundles: single PDF ≤50 MB, OCR, 200-300 DPI, continuous computer pagination, hyperlinked index and bookmarks, naming with the UI/JR reference; non-compliant bundles "likely to be rejected". — [EIN](https://www.ein.org.uk/news/updated-upper-tribunal-immigration-and-asylum-chamber-guidance-electronic-bundles-and-use-ce)
- Family PD27A from 2 March 2026: 7/5 working-day deadlines, e-bundle default, page caps, new bundle per hearing, costs sanctions (see KQ4). — [Resolution](https://resolution.org.uk/guidance-note-court-bundles/)
- MyHMCTS family uploads: organisation registration, user accounts, naming convention, no email except confidential orders. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/myhmcts-how-to-apply-for-a-family-public-law-order/upload-other-documents-orders-or-bundles)
- Probate: 4.9 weeks digital vs 12.3 weeks paper; 15-minute phone SLA; scanning errors cause stops. — [ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/regulatory-news/regulatory-news-2025-06/hmcts-updates-for-probate-practitioners-june-2025)
- Employment Tribunal: 515,000 open claims; receipts 26,000 vs disposals 10,000 in a quarter; long hearings listed into 2028. — [Higgs](https://www.higgsllp.co.uk/articles/employment-tribunal-backlog-continues-to-increase)
- Possession: median 26.4 weeks claim to repossession (Jan-Mar 2026); PCOL for rent-arrears claims only, paper N5/N119 posted or emailed for other grounds. — [OTS](https://www.otssolicitors.co.uk/news/landlord-possession-court-delays-2026/); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/evicting-tenants/standard-possession-orders)
- HM Land Registry: 32.2% of register updates take over 3 months; complex applications mostly 12 months. — [HMLR](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/hm-land-registry-processing-times)
- Firm-side signals: Taylor Bracewell "does not accept service of documents by Email or Fax"; Allington Hughes warns "we cannot guarantee that your e-mail will be received by us" and asks that urgent emails be "follow[ed] up by telephone"; Lockings, Blackhurst Budd, Salmons, Bowcock & Pursaill and Burton & Co still publish fax numbers. — [Taylor Bracewell](https://www.taylorbracewell.co.uk/); [Allington Hughes](https://www.allingtonhughes.co.uk/); [Lockings](https://www.lockings.co.uk/); [Blackhurst Budd](https://www.blackhurstbudd.co.uk/); [Salmons](https://www.salmonssolicitors.co.uk/); [Bowcock & Pursaill](https://www.bowcockpursaill.co.uk/); [Burton & Co](https://www.burtonlaw.co.uk/)

### Inferences
- Court digitisation has shifted formatting and fee-coding risk onto firms: the court no longer corrects a wrong fee or an unbookmarked bundle, it rejects it, and in CE-File the rejection is final.
- Long queues turn each matter into a maintenance task (periodic client updates, limitation and listing checks) rather than a drafting task.

### Gaps
- HMCTS civil (county court money claims) processing times not sourced.
- No data on how many small firms have MyHMCTS/CE-File accounts or rely on paper routes.

### Inferences
- The client-side journey at every swept firm is: find the phone number (or fill a 4-field form), wait for a call-back, be "matched" to a fee earner, then receive a client care letter and terms by email or post; ID, proof of funds and documents are handled after that first call by whatever means the fee earner chooses, since no site describes it.
- Conveyancing is the only product treated as a commodity (instant quotes, no-move-no-fee, CQS badges); everything else is sold as a relationship with an individual, which matches the Bellwether finding that a "single client point of contact" is a top-cited strength.
- The failure rate of small-firm domains in lower-tech cities suggests that a meaningful minority of these practices have no functioning web presence, so directory listings (Law Society NI, reviewsolicitors, reallymoving) do the discovery work instead.
- Published fax numbers, "no service by email" notices and warnings that email may not arrive indicate that post and telephone remain the trusted channels for formal documents in several firms.
