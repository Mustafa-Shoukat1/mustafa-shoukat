# Quantified operational and financial metrics: small law firms, UK and US (problem research only)

Scope note. UK = England and Wales unless stated. "Small firm" definitions differ by source: Clio = Clio-platform firms (mostly under 20 lawyers, US); Thomson Reuters = 29 or fewer lawyers (10 or fewer in the 2022 edition); Law Society/Hazlewoods benchmarking = SME firms, mostly under £35m turnover; SRA tech study = up to 4 partners and under £400k turnover; Armstrong Watson "small" = 1 to 7 full equity partners. Where a figure covers all firm sizes it is labelled "(all sizes)". Figures marked "(search summary; page blocked)" were seen only in search-engine summaries because the host returned HTTP 403 to fetches; treat them as second-hand until checked against the original. Research date: 10 October 2026.

## 1. Clio Legal Trends Report (US): utilization, realization, collection, lockup

### Takeaway
A US lawyer bills about 3 hours of an 8-hour day (38% utilization in 2025, up from 29% in 2017), invoices 88% of that and collects 93% of what is invoiced, so roughly 2.4 hours of every 8 worked turn into cash; the median firm carries 97 days of unbilled plus uncollected work. Solos are worse on every KPI (25% utilization, about 2 hours a day, in 2022).

### Cited Findings
- 2025 (Clio users, mostly small firms): average utilization 38% = 3.0 billable hours per 8-hour day; realization 88% = 2.6 hours invoiced per day; collection 93% = 2.4 hours collected per day — [Clio Legal Trends benchmarks page](https://clio.com/resources/legal-trends/benchmarks/) (search summary; page blocked); same 2025 figures repeated by [Vanta Insights](https://vantainsights.com/insights/law-firm-realization-rate)
- 2024: lawyers spend 2.9 hours per workday on billable work = 37% of an 8-hour day, "up from 29% in previous years"; about 1,693 billable hours a year — [LeanLaw summary of the 2024 report](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/how-many-billable-hours-in-a-year/) (secondary); the 2.9 of 8 hours figure is also cited by [Snelling, May 2026](https://www.snelling.com/insights/paralegal-turnover-cost-law-firm-billable-hours/)
- 2023 (data: tens of thousands of US Clio users; survey of 1,446 legal professionals 31 May to 20 July 2023; 1,012 US adults): utilization up 32% since 2016 but "nearly two-thirds of the workday is still not spent on billable client work"; realization up 12% since 2016; collection rate projected to fall to 87% by end 2023; vs 2016 lawyers work 40% more cases, bill 70% more, collect 72% more — [Legal Futures, 20 Dec 2023](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/highlights-from-the-2023-legal-trends-report)
- 2023 lockup (median, US): realization (WIP) lockup 38 days; collection (debtor) lockup 45 days; total lockup 97 days, i.e. more than three months of work at any time either unbilled or unpaid; Clio customers averaged 43 days shorter — [Legal Futures, 20 Dec 2023](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/highlights-from-the-2023-legal-trends-report); [DC Bar, 16 Feb 2024](https://www.dcbar.org/news-events/publications/d-c-bar-blog/2023-clio-report-firms-enjoying-steady-growth,-but)
- Lockup formulas: realization lockup = WIP / prior-year collections x 365; collection lockup = AR / prior-year collections x 365; collection rates rose from 86% (2016) to 90% (2021), 89% (2022) — [Attorney at Work, Oct 2023](https://www.attorneyatwork.com/lockup-metric-monitors-law-firm-cash-flow/)
- 2022 (survey Apr to May 2022: 1,134 legal professionals, 458 other-industry professionals, 1,168 US consumers): utilization 33% (about one-third of time billable), up 18% over six years; the average firm collects $748 for every $1,000 of billable work, so it could collect up to 34% more; caseload up 10% vs 2019; CPI rose faster than lawyer rates since June 2021 — [2Civility, 14 Oct 2022](https://www.2civility.org/clio-legal-trends-report-anticipates-stress-in-a-strong-legal-marketplace/)
- 2021: lawyers spend 2.5 hours per workday on billable work (cited in Law Society partner content) — [Law Society partner content, 2025](https://www.lawsociety.org.uk/topics/business-management/partner-content/key-trends-shaping-the-legal-industry-in-2025) (search summary; page blocked); realization averaged 84% in 2021, implying about 16% of billable time never invoiced — [FindLaw summary](https://www.findlaw.com/?p=50153) (search summary, secondary)
- 2017: lawyers spend 2.3 hours, or 29%, of a workday on billable tasks — Clio 2017 report as cited in [LawNext, 25 Mar 2019](https://www.lawnext.com/2019/03/exclusive-survey-report-small-firms-see-many-challenges-but-do-little-to-address-them.html)
- Solo vs non-solo, 2022 (Clio "2023 Legal Trends for Solo Law Firms", survey of 1,134 legal professionals and 1,168 consumers): solo utilization 25% = about 2 hours of billable work a day vs 36% = 2.9 hours for non-solos; over 240 working days non-solos bill 211 more hours; solo realization 1 to 3 points lower and collection 2 points lower than non-solos; each 1-point utilization gain = 19 billable hours a year — [Clio, 2023 Legal Trends for Solo Law Firms (PDF, modified 14 Apr 2023)](https://www.clio.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2023-Legal-Trends-for-Solo-Law-Firms.pdf)
- Solo hourly rates: non-solos charge about 20% more per hour; solo rates rose 11% from 2019 to end 2022 and non-solo 12%, while CPI rose 16%; both would need +$12 per hour to keep pace — same Clio solo PDF
- Solo working hours: 54% of solos work Saturdays and 40% work Sundays; solos are "much more likely" than larger-firm lawyers to work beyond 6 pm; 62% prefer working from home; 31% of lawyers who quit their firm left to start a solo practice — same Clio solo PDF
- 2024 lawyer AI and flat fees: lawyers using AI daily rose from 19% (2023) to 79% (2024); Clio says 74% of billable tasks and 66% of hourly billable work could be automated and estimates AI could cut hourly billing by $27,000 per lawyer a year; firms using flat fees up 6% in the year and 34% since 2016; 71% of clients prefer a flat fee for the whole case — [2Civility, 25 Oct 2024](https://www.2civility.org/?p=25479)
- 2022 fee pressure: 67% of consumers want flat-fee options but only 36% of firms offer them; 97% of firms offer hourly rates; 70% of clients want payment plans — [2Civility, 14 Oct 2022](https://www.2civility.org/clio-legal-trends-report-anticipates-stress-in-a-strong-legal-marketplace/)
- Online payments (2023): firms using online payments are paid more than twice as fast, median 7 days vs 15 days; firms collecting 75%+ electronically collect half their bills within 3 days — [Legal Futures, 20 Dec 2023](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/highlights-from-the-2023-legal-trends-report)

### Inferences
- Using the 2025 chain (3.0 billed, 2.6 invoiced, 2.4 collected of 8 hours), about 0.6 hours a day of work done is never turned into cash and 5 hours a day are non-billable; for a solo at 2022 levels the non-billable share is 6 of 8 hours.
- The 97-day total lockup means a firm collecting $500k a year has roughly $133k outstanding at any moment (97/365 x $500k).
- Clio's data skews to Clio-platform users, who the company says outperform the median on lockup; non-user small firms are probably worse.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch any clio.com page (HTTP 403), so 2025 and 2024 KPIs rest on search summaries and secondary sites; 2024 and 2025 lockup figures were not found anywhere.
- No per-year realization and collection figures for 2022 and 2024 beyond those above.
- Clio UK (England and Wales) utilization or hours-billed figures: not found; Clio's UK research is survey-based (see section 6).

## 2. Lead response and client communication (US, Clio secret-shopper studies)

### Takeaway
In Clio's 2024 mystery-shop of 500 US firms only 33% replied to an email and 40% answered the phone; 48% were effectively unreachable by phone, and only 12% of shoppers would recommend the firm. Both figures are worse than 2019 (40% and 56%).

### Cited Findings
- 2019 study (1,000 firms emailed, 500 called; surveys of 2,507 legal professionals and 2,000 consumers): 40% of firms replied to the email; 56% of calls answered by a person, 39% went to voicemail; more than half of firms did not return voicemails within 72 hours; 7 in 10 email responses and 6 in 10 phone responses rated unsatisfactory; 64% of consumers said they had contacted a firm that never responded; 82% rated timeliness of response the most important factor — [2Civility, 23 Oct 2019](https://www.2civility.org/clio-legal-trends-report-finds-law-firms-arent-responsive-to-potential-clients/); Clio press release at [clio.com](https://www.clio.com/about/press/clios-legal-trends-report-reveals-law-firms-struggle-to-respond-to-client-inquiries/) (search summary: of firms that did respond, 82% did so within 24 hours; 79% of consumers expect a reply within 24 hours)
- 2024 study (500 firms): email response 33% (down from 40% in 2019); phone answered 40% (down from 56%); 48% neither answered nor called back; 12% of shoppers likely to recommend; 73% would not recommend; 39% would recommend firms reached directly by phone; 84% could find contact details on the website; only 36% found the process of finding a lawyer seamless — [2Civility, 1 Nov 2024](https://www.2civility.org/2024-clio-legal-trends-report-fixing-the-first-impression-problem-for-law-firms/); [2Civility, 25 Oct 2024](https://www.2civility.org/?p=25479)
- Chatbots (2024): 7% of firms use website chatbots; 51% of prospective clients agree a chatbot is a helpful starting point; 3 in 5 would use one only if they could switch to a human — [2Civility, 1 Nov 2024](https://www.2civility.org/2024-clio-legal-trends-report-fixing-the-first-impression-problem-for-law-firms/)
- Billing communication (2023): 41% of lawyers say clients pay late and 24% say many never pay; 15% of clients say they never received a bill and 28% of those who hired a lawyer waited a noticeably long time for one; almost 1 in 3 legal professionals say their firm takes too long to send bills; fewer than half say they bill promptly — [Legal Futures, 20 Dec 2023](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/highlights-from-the-2023-legal-trends-report)
- Client channel preferences (2021 report, survey May to June 2021): 79% of consumers look for remote options (23% in 2018); 66% prefer paying online, 61% automated payments, 61% mobile-app payments, 47% mailed payment; 53% say texting is a preferred way to contact a law office; 58% prefer video for the first consult; about 60% want faster status updates via secure client portal or video; for document sharing 71% in person, 63% email, 60% secure portal, 56% online file sharing — [NextChapter, 1 Dec 2021](https://blog.nextchapterbk.com/posts/2021-clio-legal-trends-report); [2Civility (2021 report)](https://www.2civility.org/clio-legal-trends-report-reveals-how-clients-want-to-communicate-with-attorneys)
- Payment method (2023): credit cards the most preferred method, chosen by almost half of respondents in every age group; about 1 in 5 prefer cheque — [Legal Futures, 20 Dec 2023](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/highlights-from-the-2023-legal-trends-report)
- Meeting preference (2022): 35% of clients prefer virtual meetings vs 28% in person; 45% of lawyers prefer virtual — [2Civility, 14 Oct 2022](https://www.2civility.org/clio-legal-trends-report-anticipates-stress-in-a-strong-legal-marketplace/)
- Tracking: in 2020, 83% of small firms said client satisfaction ratings are important but 36% track them; 86% said repeat business is important, 52% track it — [LawNext, Sep 2020](https://lawnext.com/2020/09/exclusive-small-firms-face-significant-challenges-but-fail-to-act-on-them-thomson-reuters-survey-finds.html)

### Inferences
- Between 2019 and 2024 first-contact responsiveness declined on both channels, so the intake leak grew even as technology adoption rose.
- A firm answering 40% of calls and 33% of emails loses the majority of inbound enquiries before any fee discussion.

### Gaps
- No 2024 or 2025 Clio figure on "share responding within 24 hours"; that framing exists only in the 2019 study.
- A secondary source (Lawyers Mutual NC) gives conflicting 2024 numbers (60% no email response, 27% no phone answer); the Clio figures above are the better-corroborated set.
- Clio 2023 and 2024 client preference percentages for texting and portals were not found (clio.com blocked).

## 3. Thomson Reuters State of US Small Law Firms (admin time, challenges)

### Takeaway
Across 2017 to 2023 small-firm lawyers (29 or fewer) report spending only 56% to 61% of their time practising law; about three-quarters call admin time a significant or moderate challenge, yet only about one in five has acted on it.

### Cited Findings
- 2019 report (3rd edition, released 25 Mar 2019): about 60% of the day on legal work (solos slightly lower); "at best only six hours" of a 10-hour day potentially billable; acquiring new business a moderate or serious challenge for 73% (69% two years earlier); 31% have plans to address it; 45% adopted new technology; 80%+ say client satisfaction matters, barely 40% track it; 12% cite DIY sites as competition — [LawNext, 25 Mar 2019](https://www.lawnext.com/2019/03/exclusive-survey-report-small-firms-see-many-challenges-but-do-little-to-address-them.html)
- 2019 report (300 respondents): 72% said too much time on administrative tasks was at least a moderate challenge, up 2 points on the prior year; 76% define success by overall profits — [Above the Law, 8 Oct 2019](https://abovethelaw.com/2019/10/the-status-quo-could-be-hindering-your-efficiency-there-is-a-solution/)
- 2020 report (400+ solo and small-firm lawyers, fielded Mar to May 2020): 76% call acquiring new business a significant or moderate challenge; 74% say admin work cutting into practice time is a significant or moderate challenge; 19% say getting paid is a significant challenge (14% prior survey); lawyers spend on average 60% of time on client work; 29% have plans to address client acquisition and 20% have plans to reduce admin workload; 30% plan more technology investment — [Thomson Reuters press release, Sep 2020](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2020/september/small-law-firms-still-struggling-to-manage-administrative-tasks-says-thomson-reuters-survey); [LawNext, Sep 2020](https://lawnext.com/2020/09/exclusive-small-firms-face-significant-challenges-but-fail-to-act-on-them-thomson-reuters-survey-finds.html)
- 2020 technology in use: time and billing 74%; accounting 52%; document management 47%; conflict checking 46%; e-signature 40%; case/matter management 39%; e-billing 36%; document drafting 31%; client intake 25%; document automation 25%; practice management 25%; client portals 12%; CRM 12%; 72% plan no new technology — [LawNext, Sep 2020](https://lawnext.com/2020/09/exclusive-small-firms-face-significant-challenges-but-fail-to-act-on-them-thomson-reuters-survey-finds.html)
- 2020 solos: 28% saw their client base decline over three years (24% by more than 5% a year); 73% cite similar-size firms as the main competition — same LawNext 2020 article
- 2022 report (firms of 10 or fewer; TR estimates more than 175,000 such US firms): lawyers spend 56% of time practising law, down slightly on 2020; 54% rate getting paid a significant or moderate challenge (64% in 2020); administrative tasks remain the top challenge for the majority — [Legal IT Insider, 11 Nov 2022](https://legaltechnology.com/small-us-law-firms-still-rate-time-spent-on-admin-as-number-one-challenge/)
- 2023 report (8th edition, fielded Aug 2023, firms of 29 or fewer): time practising law 61%, up from 56% (first rise since 2016; TR equates 5 points to roughly 150 extra billable hours a year); admin time still the top challenge; controlling costs and expenses a significant or moderate challenge for 72% (56% two years earlier); 64% raised billing rates (59% in 2022, under half in 2021); 42% adopted new technology; 72% had heard of generative AI but not used it; 93% run marketing campaigns (84% in 2021) — [LawNext, Nov 2023](https://www.lawnext.com/2023/11/small-law-firms-made-progress-this-year-on-their-biggest-challenge-thomson-reuters-survey-finds.html); [Legal.io, 7 Dec 2023](https://www.legal.io/articles/5455197/Thomson-Reuters-Survey-Reveals-Progress-in-Small-Law-Firms)

### Inferences
- At 56% to 61% legal work, a lawyer working a 50-hour week spends 20 to 22 hours a week on non-legal tasks (management, marketing, admin, client contact).
- The gap between the share citing admin as a challenge (72% to 74%) and the share acting (20%) has been stable for years.

### Gaps
- No 2024 or 2025 edition of the State of US Small Law Firms report was found (the TR Institute index lists only all-market reports); the 2023 edition is the latest with figures.
- No TR figure for hours per week lost to admin; only the percentage-of-time split.

## 4. ABA Profile of the Legal Profession and ABA TechReport (US size and technology)

### Takeaway
ABA pages were blocked, so only partial figures are available: 1.37m US lawyers in 2025, solos about 65% cloud adoption vs 75% for all lawyers (2024 TechReport), and the widely quoted "about 48 to 49% of private-practice lawyers are solos" could not be traced to a current ABA page.

### Cited Findings
- US lawyer population rose from 1.35m (2024) to 1.37m (2025), first significant rise since 2020 — [ABA press release, Dec 2025](https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2025/12/aba-2025-profile-of-the-legal-profession-report/) (search summary; page blocked)
- 2024 ABA Cloud Computing TechReport: about 75% of attorneys use cloud computing (69% in 2023); solo practitioners the slowest adopters at about 65% vs more than 94% at firms of 50 to 99 lawyers; Clio used by about 15% and MyCase about 5%, concentrated in small firms; confidentiality and security the top concern for about 55% — [ABA 2024 Cloud Computing TechReport](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2024/2024-cloud-computing-techreport/) (search summary; page blocked)
- Older estimate: about 435,000 US solo practitioners, roughly 48% of private-practice lawyers, and about 200,000 lawyers in firms of 2 to 20, roughly 22% (source not named, dated 20 Jan 2012) — [Legal News, 20 Jan 2012](https://legalnews.com/macomb/1183887)
- Thomson Reuters estimate: more than 175,000 US firms with 10 or fewer lawyers (2022) — [Legal IT Insider, 11 Nov 2022](https://legaltechnology.com/small-us-law-firms-still-rate-time-spent-on-admin-as-number-one-challenge/)
- Solo well-being (ALPS 2026 report, 200+ solo attorneys): 43.56% have experienced occupational burnout; 7.61% have fully standardised workflows; 71.78% satisfied with professional life — [ALPS Solo Well-Being Report](https://www.alpsinsurance.com/2025-solo-well-being-report)
- Lawyer turnover (Clio 2022): nearly 1 in 5 lawyers left their firm in the 12 months to April 2022; 9% planned to leave within six months — [2Civility, 14 Oct 2022](https://www.2civility.org/clio-legal-trends-report-anticipates-stress-in-a-strong-legal-marketplace/)

### Inferences
- Solo cloud adoption lagging the profession by about 10 points implies roughly one in three solos still runs on local software or paper.

### Gaps
- ABA Profile firm-size percentages (solo and 2 to 9 lawyer shares) for 2024 or 2025: not retrievable (americanbar.org returned 403; no secondary source quoted them with a date).
- ABA 2024 Solo and Small Firm TechReport figures on document management, e-signature, practice management and security incidents by firm size: not retrievable.

## 5. UK firm structure (Law Society, SRA)

### Takeaway
England and Wales has just under 9,000 SRA-regulated firms; the Law Society counted 43% of registered firms as sole practices in 2022 and 86% of all firms had four or fewer partners in 2016, while the SRA's August 2026 structural count shows 1,321 "recognised sole practices" (14.8%) because most one-owner firms are now incorporated.

### Cited Findings
- Law Society Annual Statistics Report 2022: sole practices 43% of registered firms, down 3.6% in the year to July 2022; number of SRA-registered firms fell below 9,000 — [Law Society, Annual Statistics Report 2022](https://lawsociety.org.uk/topics/research/annual-statistics-report-2022) (search summary; page blocked)
- 2016 market report: 9,430 private-practice firms; some 86% of all firms have no more than four partners; 475 ABSs — [Legal Futures (2016)](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/abss-making-mark-22bn-legal-market-report-finds/print/) (search summary)
- SRA regulated-community statistics at end August 2026: 8,923 firms (head offices); sole practitioners 1,321 (14.8%); partnerships 897 (10.1%); incorporated companies 5,261 (59.0%); LLPs 1,416 (15.9%) — [SRA, Breakdown of solicitor firms](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/regulated-community-statistics/data/solicitor_firms/)
- 2012 (Law Society data at 31 July 2012): 10,102 registered firms, down 1% from a peak over 10,400 in 2010; sole practices down 2.5%; 2 to 4 partner firms down 1.1%; 5 to 10 partner firms up 5.3% — [Solicitors Journal, 18 Apr 2013](https://www.solicitorsjournal.com/sjarticle/number-of-law-firms-declines-to-2007-level)
- Consumer use of small firms: 59% of consumers used a small local firm (up 8 points year on year), 13% a national brand with a local office, 9% a large corporate firm (LSCP tracker 2026) — [Today's Conveyancer on LSCP 2026 tracker](https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/?p=92154)

### Inferences
- The Law Society "sole practice" share (43%) and SRA "recognised sole practice" share (14.8%) measure different things (ownership vs legal structure); for market sizing the Law Society/partner-count series is the better proxy for "one to four partners".
- Firm count fell about 12% from 10,102 (2012) to 8,923 (2026).

### Gaps
- Law Society Annual Statistics Reports for 2023, 2024 and 2025 (firms by partner count, solicitors by firm size) were not retrievable (lawsociety.org.uk returned 403 and no news coverage with figures surfaced).
- Law Society sole-practitioner research (challenges survey): not found.

## 6. UK small-firm operations and stress: LexisNexis Bellwether and Clio UK

### Takeaway
Bellwether 2026 (568 respondents) puts routine administration as the biggest back-office problem for more than half of small and mid-sized firms (52%), with slow case management (40%) and drafting/review (about 31%) next; earlier editions show 61% of small-firm solicitors with high stress (2019) and 75% at stress 6 to 10 out of 10 (2022).

### Cited Findings
- Bellwether 2026 "Lean, focused, profitable" (568 legal professionals, England and Wales, surveyed Feb 2026): more than half name routine administration their biggest back-office issue; 52% say billing, document management, client communications and research support are blockers; 40% say slow case management holds them back; just under a third cite document drafting and review; almost two-thirds reported higher revenue since 2023; 1 in 4 expect >10% growth in 2026; 84% rate their client experience good or excellent; 30% investing in standardising documents and workflows; 1 in 5 investing more in client experience; >40% strong interest in AI — [Legal Futures, 18 May 2026](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/small-law-firms-held-back-by-operational-drag-in-back-office); [Solicitor News, 19 May 2026](https://solicitornews.co.uk/?p=20533)
- Bellwether 2026 overheads: only 9% rated low overheads as excellent and 42% said the cost of overheads is a problem; admin tasks the biggest workflow issue at 52%, case management 41%, document drafting and review 28% — [Legal Futures associate news on Bellwether 2026](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/small-law-firms-have-raised-the-bar-for-client-service) (search summary)
- Bellwether 2022 "Transformation troubles" (May 2022): 75% operating at stress 6 to 10 on a 10-point scale, 44% at 8 to 10; 49% name attracting and retaining talent their biggest challenge; 77% say attracting good lawyers is a very or quite significant threat; 58% say clients expect the same or more service for less than a year earlier; 45% say client expectations noticeably higher; 51% say the firm is growing (66% in 2021) — [BCL Legal summary](https://www.bcllegal.com/the-brief/insights/lexisnexis-report-annual-bellwether-survey-uk-sme-law-firms)
- Bellwether 2021 (305 solicitors, fieldwork May to June 2021): average stress 7/10, job satisfaction 8/10; PII premiums the second biggest cost pressure after salaries, 66% call PII a very significant or significant threat; recruiting and retaining talent the biggest reported challenge; 93% confident in the firm's future; 70% predict tough challenges — [Today's Conveyancer, 14 Sep 2021](https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/?p=45907); [Insurance Edge, 14 Sep 2021](https://insurance-edge.net/2021/09/14/lexisnexis-report-looks-at-legal-sector/)
- Bellwether "Stress in the Legal Profession" (176 legal professionals, mainly firms under 20 fee-earners; reported Apr 2019): 61% report high stress, 11% "extremely high"; 82% high job satisfaction; 23% say their firm does not do enough on stress — [Legal Futures, 24 Apr 2019](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/small-firm-solicitors-highly-stressed-but-enjoying-their-jobs)
- Bellwether editions 2013 to 2026 are listed (2025 "Marginal gains", 2024 "Lessons on law firm growth", 2023 "Bold ambitions?") — [LexisNexis Bellwether reports index](https://lexisnexis.co.uk/bellwether/reports.html)
- Clio UK research (Censuswide, 500 solicitors, 82% at firms of 20 lawyers or fewer; Nov 2024): 96% say their firm has adopted AI in some way; 56% say use is widespread; 62% expect more use next year; only 29% say AI saved costs; 54% expect fixed-fee use to rise because of client demand and competition; a third plan to invest over £100,000 in technology in the next year; client communication preferences: telephone 45%, firm website 43%, chatbots 42%, mobile apps about 40%, email 38% — [Legal Futures, 7 Nov 2024](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/small-law-firms-embracing-ai-as-they-move-away-from-hourly-billing)
- Conveyancer workload (Landmark 2026 report): 42% of conveyancers cite transaction timescales as their biggest frustration, 35% volume of workload; 78% of law firms use AI to support fee earners (double the 2024 figure); 31% say less admin would help attract and retain talent — [Legal Futures, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/property-transactions-spike-at-123-days-driving-industry-to-seek-greater-certainty/print/)

### Inferences
- The 2026 Bellwether "52% admin" figure is the closest UK equivalent to Thomson Reuters' 72 to 74% "admin is a challenge" US figure; both put admin at the top of the problem list.
- Bellwether stress readings have stayed high across 2019, 2021 and 2022 (61% high stress; 7/10; 75% at 6+), so stress is chronic rather than pandemic-specific.

### Gaps
- Bellwether 2023, 2024 and 2025 headline figures (hours worked, time on admin, cash-flow worry, pricing pressure) were not retrievable; the LexisNexis reports page lists titles only.
- No UK survey found that quantifies hours per week of admin for small-firm solicitors.

## 7. UK technology adoption among small firms (SRA, LSB, academic, LawtechUK)

### Takeaway
Almost all small firms use some technology (95%), but it is basic: communication tools 78%, accounting 67%; AI automation 14% and generative AI 12% (SRA 2025, firms of up to 4 partners and under £400k turnover). Across all firm types, advanced tools are used by only 2% to 5% and the share launching new digital services fell from 28% (2018) to 21% (2022).

### Cited Findings
- SRA sole practitioner and small firm study (published 20 Jun 2025; Thinks Insight; 22 interviews, survey of 138 sole practitioners and small firms, firms of up to four partners with turnover below £400,000): 95% use at least one technology or innovation; communication 78%; accounting software 67%; AI automation 14%; generative AI 12%; legal chatbots 12%; 25% considering generative AI; barriers: cost 25%, data breach or compliance risk 15%, quality and reliability 11%; drivers: efficiency 61%, consumer expectations 59%; 90% recognise the importance of keeping pace; only 21% had been approached directly by a tech provider — [SRA research findings](https://publications.sra.org.uk/sole-practitioners-and-small-firms-use-of-technology/research-findings); [Legal Futures, 20 Jun 2025](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/call-for-action-to-help-small-law-firms-adopt-technology)
- Same study: 72% concerned about investing in technology that becomes outdated — [SRA press release](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/news/press/small-firms-tech-adoption-research/) (search summary)

## 8. UK legal aid: rates, provider exodus

### Takeaway
Civil legal aid fees had no increase from 1996 to 2025, a 49.4% real-terms cut to 2022; civil providers starting work fell from 3,896 (2011) to 2,101 (2022) and offices from 3,452 to 1,860; criminal duty solicitors fell 25% (1,317 fewer) between October 2017 and October 2024 with an average age of 50+ in many areas. The 2025 uplifts are 24% to 30% of spend for housing and immigration (new minimum £65.35 an hour) and about 12% of criminal solicitor spend.

### Cited Findings
- Civil fees last raised in 1996, a further 10% cut in 2011, a real-terms cut of 49.4% to 2022 (Law Society); public legal aid spending down about a quarter between 2009 and March 2022 (JUSTICE); Law Society asked for £11.3m emergency investment and a 15% interim uplift for early legal advice — [Today's Family Lawyer, 14 Sep 2023](https://todaysfamilylawyer.co.uk/legal-aid-cut-in-half-since-1996/)
- Civil providers starting legal aid work fell from 3,896 (2011) to 2,101 (2022); offices starting work in Jan to Mar fell from 3,452 (2012) to 1,860 (2022); Law Society projected a further one-third fall by 2025 — [Law Society press release, 2022](https://lawsociety.org.uk/contact-or-visit-us/press-office/press-releases/Civil%20legal%20aid%20increasingly%20out%20of%20reach%20for%20those%20in%20need) (search summary; page blocked)

- Demand: police station advice instances rose from 133,982 (Apr to Jun 2023) to 152,985 (Jul to Sep 2023); 71 duty solicitors left between April and October 2023 — [Solicitors Journal, 29 Dec 2023](https://solicitorsjournal.com/sjarticle/rising-demand-for-legal-aid-amid-declining-duty-solicitor-numbers)

### Inferences
- At the actual 773 median chargeable hours (section 13), a £67,476 median fee-earner costs about £87 per chargeable hour before overheads, so a £65.35 civil legal aid rate loses money for the median SME firm unless hours are far above median.

### Gaps

### Inferences
- At 773 chargeable hours and £146,000 fee income per fee-earner, the realised rate is about £189 per chargeable hour; the same fee-earner costs £67,476 plus about £22k support and about £36k overheads (older ratios), leaving the thin margin that the 27% net-profit figure confirms.
- UK sole practices spend roughly 6% of turnover on PII and typically nothing on cyber cover (91% uninsured), so a single cyber loss is uninsured for most of them.
- A three-user practice-management subscription at the category average ($81) costs about $2,900 a year, small next to a single $30,500+ paralegal replacement.
