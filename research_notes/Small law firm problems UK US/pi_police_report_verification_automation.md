# US PI firms: obtaining, verifying and automating police reports and other official-record checks at case opening (UK note at end)

Research date: 11 Oct 2026. All pages were fetched on that date unless a publication date is shown.

Method note for the report writer: the shared WebSearch budget for this turn was already exhausted when this researcher started, so every finding below comes from direct fetches of URLs known in advance. Most state crash-report portals (Texas CRIS, Florida FLHSMV, CHP, Georgia DPS, Kentucky, Tennessee, Oklahoma, Alabama, Nebraska, Idaho, NYPD/NY DMV), LexisNexis BuyCrash, CrashDocs/Carfax, Justia/LawServer/legislature statute sites, G2/Capterra, Nolo/FindLaw, Reddit and every police.uk force site refused the fetcher (HTTP 403, connection refused or DNS failure). Those gaps are listed explicitly under each question. Nothing in the Cited Findings is from memory.

## Key question 1: What does a PI paralegal do in the first 72 hours after a car-accident client signs, and where does the police report fit?

### Takeaway
Vendor and practitioner checklists agree on the same first-72-hour sequence: sign retainer and HIPAA/records authorizations, calendar the statute of limitations, send letters of representation to every known insurer within 24-48 hours, open the claims with the carriers and get adjuster assignments, then request the police report, send preservation letters and start pulling policy limits. The police report sits in the second stage because it is the document that identifies the defendant, the defendant's carrier and the officer's narrative, which the firm needs before it can open the adverse claim properly.

### Cited Findings
- CASEpeer's "Personal Injury Case Management Checklist & Guide" (last updated 2 Sep 2026) puts the following in Stage 1: "Good intake starts with gathering essential information from the client"; screen against acceptance criteria; sign the retainer; execute HIPAA and records-release authorizations; "Identify the statute of limitations and calendar it immediately"; "Open the claim and enter client, incident, and insurance details"; and "send the letter of representation to all known insurers within 24 to 48 hours". — [CASEpeer checklist](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-case-management-guide-and-checklist/)
- Same CASEpeer checklist, Stage 2: "Request the police or incident report"; "photograph the scene, vehicle, or hazard while preserving any physical evidence"; "Send spoliation letters to preserve video, black-box, or maintenance records before they're overwritten"; "Identify and interview witnesses, and pull the at-fault party's insurance policy limits". No day-count is given for the police report, preservation letters or policy-limit requests. — [CASEpeer checklist](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-case-management-guide-and-checklist/)
- Same checklist, Stage 3: "Request records and bills from every provider, log them as they come in"; set a recurring client check-in of about every 14 days; "Check for Medicare or Medicaid involvement and open MSA reporting if needed". Stage 4: "Identify all potential lienholders, including health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, ERISA plans, and hospital liens" and "Request itemized lien statements early, since they often take weeks to arrive". — [CASEpeer checklist](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-case-management-guide-and-checklist/)
- CASEpeer's letter-of-representation guide (last updated 19 Dec 2025) says the letter is "Typically sent shortly after a lawyer is retained", goes to "insurance companies, medical providers, and opposing counsel" (also at-fault individuals and employers), should carry "Relevant claim or policy numbers to assist in accurately identifying a case in their system", and may include an optional "Request to preserve evidence ... such as video footage or records". It does not mention policy limits or police reports. — [CASEpeer LOR guide](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-letter-of-representation/)
- Finch (a pre-litigation service for PI firms) tells firms the police report "has the defendant, the carrier, and the officer's narrative" and to "Get it first". — [Finch homepage](https://www.finchlegal.com/)
- Finch's guides hub lists "Opening the claim and the letter of representation" with the line "The first 24 hours after you open a claim decide whether the file moves or stalls", "The welcome call" with "Why the first two weeks decide whether a case is buildable", and "Running a great intake call" with "The most perishable minutes in the whole case ... sign the client while they're still on the phone". — [Finch guides hub](https://www.finchlegal.com/guides)
- EvenUp's Intake page describes the carrier step as happening right after signing: "Once you've signed the client, AI Agents call carriers to open 5x more claims and get adjuster assignments" and "Agents contact carriers, confirm liability and coverage, and deliver written confirmation to your team", with the headline "Save 16+ Minutes Per Case On Coverage Checks". — [EvenUp Intake](https://www.evenuplaw.com/intake)
- Morgan & Morgan's consumer guide (dated 13 Mar 2025, reviewed 6 Jan 2025) says the officer "typically gives you a report or incident number" at the scene, advises clients to "Confirm with the officer how and when the report will be available", and says an insurer or attorney "can often obtain the report for you". — [Morgan & Morgan police-report guide](https://www.forthepeople.com/blog/how-get-copy-police-report/)
- Injury Claim Coach says an adjuster "will typically request an official copy when a claim opens", and lists four items needed to request a report: "The investigating officer's service number", "The accident location", "The accident date and approximate time", "Your identification". — [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/police-report.html)
- Lawmatics' generic intake-steps article (8 Jun 2026) lists: capture the lead; qualify and screen (case type, timeline, "all involved parties"); run conflict checks ("an ethical requirement for all law firms"); schedule the consultation; convert with an e-signed retainer and first payment. It has no PI-specific steps and does not mention police reports. — [Lawmatics intake steps](https://www.lawmatics.com/blog/what-are-the-steps-in-the-intake-process)
- A YoCierge guest post on Supio's blog (15 Jan 2026) sizes the records workload: a firm opening 400 PI matters a month makes "roughly 1,600 record requests per month (about four per matter)". — [YoCierge on Supio blog](https://www.supio.com/blog/how-flat-fee-record-retrieval-saves-firms-money-and-improves-predictability)

### Inferences
- The letter of representation and claim opening (24-48 hours) come before the police report because the carrier's claim number is needed on every later document, while the report itself often is not yet filed in the first 72 hours (see Q2 and Q4 on 5-10 business day and up-to-6-week availability).
- The report is the firm's primary source for the adverse carrier when the client only has a driver's name or plate, which is why practitioners say "get it first" even though it arrives second.

### Gaps
- No paralegal-forum (r/paralegal, r/Lawyertalk) or bar practice-management (ABA GPSolo, state bar) source could be fetched; the sequence above comes from vendor checklists and firm blogs only.
- No source gave a measured count of minutes or hours a paralegal spends on the police-report step specifically; EvenUp's "16+ minutes per case" is for carrier coverage checks, not report retrieval.

## Key question 2: How long does it take to get a crash report, what does it cost, and which states require the requester to be an involved party or attorney of record? (All channels)

### Takeaway
Primary statutory text was retrievable for Texas, Florida and California: Texas charges $6 (+$2 certified) and restricts the full report to a list of "directly concerned" persons with a redacted copy open to anyone; Florida locks reports to parties, their legal representatives, insurers and agencies for 60 days with a per-report sworn statement under felony penalty; California releases confidential reports to persons with a "proper interest" including "any attorney who declares under penalty of perjury" that they represent one. Firm-side explainers put cost at $5-$25 and availability at 5-10 business days to "a week or two", while Ohio's state portal warns reports "may take up to 6 weeks" and are "statistical use only". The commercial aggregators (BuyCrash/LexisNexis, CrashDocs/Carfax, ReportBeam) could not be fetched for fees.

### Cited Findings
Texas
- Texas Transportation Code 550.065(d): "The fee for a copy of the collision report is $6." and "The copy may be certified by the department or the governmental entity for an additional fee of $2." A certification that no report is on file also costs $6. — [Tex. Transp. Code 550.065 via public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_550.065)
- 550.065(c): release is "On written request and payment of any required fee" to "any person directly concerned in the collision or having a proper interest therein", listed as: any person involved or "their authorized representative"; a driver involved; "an employer, parent, or legal guardian of a driver involved in the collision"; "the owner of a vehicle or property damaged in the collision"; a person who established financial responsibility for an involved vehicle including a policyholder; an insurer that issued a policy covering an involved vehicle or person; a contractor providing claims or underwriting information; an FCC-licensed radio or TV station; a qualifying newspaper; "any person who may sue because of death resulting from the collision"; and a vehicle-storage-facility representative. Attorneys are not named; they fall under "authorized representative" or "proper interest" only by implication. — [Tex. Transp. Code 550.065 via public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_550.065)
- 550.065(c-1): the department "shall create a redacted collision report that may be requested by any person", which "may not include the items of information described by Subsection (f)(2)". — [Tex. Transp. Code 550.065 via public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_550.065)

Florida
- Fla. Stat. 316.066(2)(a): crash reports "are confidential and exempt from s. 119.07(1) and s. 24(a), Art. I of the State Constitution for a period of 60 days". — [Fla. Stat. 316.066 via public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._316.066)
- 316.066(2)(b): reports are "made immediately available to the parties involved in the crash, their legal representatives," insurers and their agents, law enforcement and government agencies, victim services programs, and "radio and television stations licensed by the Federal Communications Commission" (the media copy excludes home addresses, licence numbers, birth dates and phone numbers). — [Fla. Stat. 316.066 via public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._316.066)
- 316.066(2)(d): the requester must "file a written sworn statement with the state or local agency in possession of the information" that it "will not be used for any commercial solicitation of accident victims", and the statement "must be completed and sworn to by the requesting party for each individual crash report". 316.066(3)(d): knowing misuse "commits a felony of the third degree". — [Fla. Stat. 316.066 via public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._316.066)
- 316.066(1)(a): the officer's long-form report "must be completed and submitted to the department within 10 days after an investigation is completed". 316.066(2)(f): reports "may be made available 60 days after the date the report is filed to any person or entity eligible". The statute text fetched contains no fee figure. — [Fla. Stat. 316.066 via public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._316.066)

California
- Cal. Veh. Code 20012: reports "shall be for the confidential use of the Department of Motor Vehicles and the Department of the California Highway Patrol" but must be disclosed "to any person who may have a proper interest therein, including, but not limited to, the driver or drivers involved", "the guardian or conservator thereof, the parent of a minor driver, the authorized representative of a driver", "any named person injured therein, the owners of vehicles or property damaged thereby", "persons who may incur civil liability", and "any attorney who declares under penalty of perjury that he or she represents any of the above persons". Insurers are not named. Copy fees may not exceed the actual cost of the copy. — [Cal. Veh. Code 20012 via public.law](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_20012)

Ohio
- Ohio DPS Crash Retrieval portal: "Crash reports may take up to 6 weeks to be available on this website"; "These reports are to be used for statistical use only and are not sufficient to be used for any court proceeding"; "The investigating agency is the official record keeper and the report with the diagram can be obtained from them"; search is by Crash Number or "up to 10 Document Numbers separated by semicolon", or by crash date range, county, law-enforcement agency and last name; a case-sensitive CAPTCHA is required; "Crash reports will be shown for past five years plus current year"; the agency list includes "OHIO STATE HIGHWAY PATROL - OHP39" plus city, county, township and university agencies. No fee is stated. — [Ohio Crash Retrieval](https://ohtrafficdata.dps.ohio.gov/CrashRetrieval)

Illinois
- Illinois State Police crash-reports page: drivers must file a report when the crash involved "a death, bodily injury, or more than $1,500 of property damage when all drivers are insured" ($500 if any driver is uninsured), "as soon as possible, within 10 days" if no officer attended; the page links to "Obtain a Copy of a Crash Report Online" and "by Mail" pages but states no fee, availability time or eligibility rules on the page itself. — [ISP Crash Reports](https://isp.illinois.gov/CrashReports)

### Inferences
- Attorney-of-record rules differ in form: California names attorneys with a perjury declaration; Texas and Florida fold attorneys into "authorized representative" / "legal representatives"; Florida additionally demands a sworn non-solicitation statement per report, which is a signed, per-case act a bot cannot lawfully perform on the firm's behalf.
- Ohio's free statewide portal is not the evidentiary copy; firms still have to go to the investigating agency for the report with the diagram, which doubles the retrieval work in that state.

## Key question 3: What data on the report does the firm use and verify, and against what?

### Takeaway
The report supplies the defendant's identity and carrier, the officer's narrative and opinion of fault, citations, contributing-factor codes, the scene diagram, witness and passenger details, and injury descriptions; firms check these against the client's intake account and the carrier's claim set-up, and must watch for amended versions (fatal crashes, pending toxicology). Newer AI tools claim to extract "incident facts" from the file and flag what is still missing from the client.

### Cited Findings
- Report contents per Injury Claim Coach: date, time and location; weather, visibility and road conditions; make, model, year and VIN of each vehicle; "driver addresses, phone numbers, and insurance information"; passenger details including seating position; a short description of injuries; "a drawn diagram showing where the vehicles ended up"; description and location of vehicle damage; witness names and information; statements from drivers, passengers and witnesses; traffic citations; driver alcohol levels; and "the officer's opinion of fault and cause". The Contributing Factors section "Contains the officer's written opinion of how the crash happened" (examples: bad weather, blown tire, speeding, non-working brake lights); "the most often cited factors are violations of state traffic laws, documented through citations". Roadway and weather conditions "may appear as code numbers" and the page advises calling the department to decode them. — [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/police-report.html)
- Amended reports: "In fatal crashes, or when drug or alcohol testing is pending, the report is amended as information becomes available" and the reader should "get a final copy". — [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/police-report.html)
- How adjusters treat it: adjusters "generally accept police accident reports as credible, unbiased evidence of fault" and rely on it "for determining fault"; a moving-violation ticket against the other driver puts the claimant "in a stronger negotiating position"; the report is "very persuasive in personal injury claims". — [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/police-report.html)
- Report contents per Morgan & Morgan: date/time/location; vehicle details (make, model, plate); names and contact details of drivers, passengers and witnesses; injury and property-damage descriptions; weather, road and traffic conditions; "Narrative of how the accident occurred, as reported by those involved"; "Scene diagram showing vehicle positions and points of impact"; citations; and the officer's assessment of fault, which is "not definitive" but "can carry significant weight in legal and insurance proceedings". — [Morgan & Morgan police-report guide](https://www.forthepeople.com/blog/how-get-copy-police-report/)
- Finch: the police report "has the defendant, the carrier, and the officer's narrative". — [Finch homepage](https://www.finchlegal.com/)
- Texas: the public redacted copy "may not include the items of information described by Subsection (f)(2)", so only entitled requesters receive the full-identifier version. — [Tex. Transp. Code 550.065 via public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_550.065)
- Supio's March 2026 release (16 Mar 2026) adds a Case Review Agent that "automatically extracts incident facts from case files", "shows which information is present and which still needs to be collected from the client", so "Paralegals can use this to send targeted follow-up requests for missing details". It does not name police reports as an input. — [Supio March 2026 release](https://www.supio.com/blog/march-2026-release)
- EvenUp's intake agents "contact carriers, confirm liability and coverage, and deliver written confirmation to your team". — [EvenUp Intake](https://www.evenuplaw.com/intake)
- Corrections: "Mistakes in a traffic collision report can be fixed, especially if it's a factual error" (wrong address, name spelling); "Changing the officer's opinion of fault is 'much more difficult'". — [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/police-report.html); Morgan & Morgan: "you can request an amendment or submit a supplemental statement", contacting the issuing agency "and provide supporting evidence". — [Morgan & Morgan police-report guide](https://www.forthepeople.com/blog/how-get-copy-police-report/)

### Inferences
- The verification targets are: party names and insurance fields (against the client's intake sheet and the carrier's claim set-up), the citation and contributing-factor codes (against the liability theory), the narrative and diagram (against the client's account), and the report version (against any later amendment).
- Because contributing factors and conditions are coded, extraction tools need the state's code tables; no fetched vendor page claims a state-specific crash-report parser.

### Gaps
- No source was fetched on 911/CAD logs, body-worn camera requests, tow or impound records, or EMS run reports as intake verifications; the fetcher could not reach any agency records page describing them.
- No source gave an error rate for police reports or a frequency for amended reports.

## Key question 4: Other verifications at intake (coverage and policy limits, prior claims, Medicare/Medicaid, defendant/asset, employment, social media)

### Takeaway
Policy-limits disclosure is statutory in Florida (sworn statement within 30 days of a written request, including umbrella/excess policies and a copy of the policy); Medicare's BCRC expects the beneficiary or attorney to report the case "as soon as they become aware other insurance is available"; checklists add Medicare/Medicaid screening and lienholder identification at intake. Vendor automation now targets carrier calls for coverage confirmation and lien classification; nothing fetched covers ISO ClaimSearch access for plaintiffs, asset searches, employment or social-media checks.

### Cited Findings
- Fla. Stat. 627.4137(1): an insurer that "does or may provide liability coverage" must respond to a claimant's written request within 30 days with a sworn statement covering each known policy including excess or umbrella coverage, giving "The name of the insurer", "The name of each insured", "The limits of the liability coverage", "A statement of any policy or coverage defense which such insurer reasonably believes is available to such insurer at the time of filing such statement", and "A copy of the policy". 627.4137(2): the statement "shall be amended immediately upon discovery of facts calling for an amendment to such statement". — [Fla. Stat. 627.4137 via public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._627.4137)
- CMS Benefits Coordination & Recovery Center guidance: "The Medicare beneficiary or his/her attorney should inform Medicare as soon as they become aware other insurance is available"; if the responsible insurer does not pay promptly (usually after 120 days) a provider may bill Medicare and Medicare may make a conditional payment; Medicare "is entitled to be repaid for the items and services it paid" out of any settlement, judgment or award; BCRC phone 1-855-798-2627, Mon-Fri 8am-8pm ET. — [CMS BCRC beneficiary services](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/beneficiary-services)

## Key question 5: What goes wrong and what delays occur, in practitioners' words?

### Takeaway
The sourced failure modes are: the report is not yet filed or is still being amended (fatal/toxicology cases), the state portal copy is statistical-only and arrives up to six weeks later, non-parties are locked out for 60 days, factual errors can be corrected but the officer's fault opinion effectively cannot, and the surrounding records and lien statements take weeks. Direct paralegal-forum quotes could not be collected.

### Cited Findings
- Ohio DPS portal: "Crash reports may take up to 6 weeks to be available on this website"; "These reports are to be used for statistical use only and are not sufficient to be used for any court proceeding"; "Changes to a report may have been made to clarify the data prior to being entered"; "Due to the 2019 changes, the crash data may be significantly impacted". — [Ohio Crash Retrieval](https://ohtrafficdata.dps.ohio.gov/CrashRetrieval)

## Key question 7: What part cannot be automated because of portal terms, ID checks, CAPTCHAs or state law?

### Takeaway
The sourced blockers are legal and procedural rather than technical: per-report sworn statements under felony penalty (Florida), attorney declarations under penalty of perjury (California), written requests restricted to enumerated "directly concerned" persons (Texas), a CAPTCHA on Ohio's statewide portal whose output is explicitly non-evidentiary, and the need to return to the investigating agency for the court-usable copy.

### Cited Findings
- Florida requires a written sworn statement "completed and sworn to by the requesting party for each individual crash report" and makes knowing misuse "a felony of the third degree"; non-parties are locked out for 60 days. — [Fla. Stat. 316.066 via public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._316.066)
- California releases reports to "any attorney who declares under penalty of perjury that he or she represents any of the above persons". — [Cal. Veh. Code 20012 via public.law](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_20012)
- Texas releases the full report only "On written request and payment of any required fee" to enumerated persons; anyone else gets the redacted version. — [Tex. Transp. Code 550.065 via public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_550.065)
- Ohio's portal requires a case-sensitive CAPTCHA, delivers reports "for statistical use only" that are "not sufficient to be used for any court proceeding", and directs users to the investigating agency for "the report with the diagram". — [Ohio Crash Retrieval](https://ohtrafficdata.dps.ohio.gov/CrashRetrieval)
- Injury Claim Coach lists "Your identification" among the four items needed to request a report and notes that "Some states limit access to involved drivers, their representatives, and their insurers". — [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/police-report.html)
- CloudLex's own description of its records service, "no portals, no uploads", is framed as a managed human service rather than a software integration. — [CloudLex](https://www.cloudlex.com/)

### Inferences
- Any automation that submits Florida's sworn statement or California's attorney declaration on the attorney's behalf shifts criminal or perjury exposure onto the firm, which explains why vendors stop at "we extract from what you upload".
- The reliable automation boundary is: human obtains the PDF (or the client/adjuster forwards it), software ingests it (Supio Inbox, SmartAdvocate barcode scan, CloudLex mail indexing) and extracts facts.

### Gaps
- Terms of use for BuyCrash, CrashDocs, Texas CRIS and the Florida Crash Portal (which would show any anti-bot or account-sharing clauses) could not be fetched.
- No source on whether aggregators offer API or bulk law-firm accounts.

## Key question 8: UK equivalent (police collision report, askMID/MIB, Official Injury Claim, STATS19)

### Takeaway
In England and Wales the low-value route is the MIB-run Official Injury Claim portal, which needs the other driver's name and registration and police details only "if the police attended"; the other vehicle's insurer is found through askMID (one-off search £10.00, subscriptions £774 a year). STATS19 is the police statistical return, not the disclosable collision report. Police-force collision-report fee pages could not be fetched, so the typical £100-£200 figure in the brief is unverified here.

### Cited Findings
- askMID: "Check your own vehicle for free"; "Accident? Need to check the other vehicle is insured?" is a "One-off search £10.00"; existing subscribers pay "£774 per annum"; at the next renewal after migration the subscription covers 18 months for "£1,161"; new subscriptions are paused "from 23 October until after go-live" with new customers registering "in Q1 2027"; the replacement "Check Their Vehicle" service goes live "Monday 7 December". — [askMID](https://www.askmid.com/)
- MIB: "About 335,000 uninsured vehicles are on UK roads on any given day"; MIB "has helped 30,000 victims of uninsured and untraced drivers"; uninsured driving "costs the UK £1 billion a year". — [MIB homepage](https://www.mib.org.uk/)
- MIB claim types: "An uninsured driver is an identified person who does not have a valid insurance policy at the time of the accident"; "An untraced driver is someone who leaves the scene of the incident without making their identity known" (including false details); foreign-registered vehicles; claimants "must register on MIB's online claim portal" and MIB applies the Uninsured Drivers' Agreement and Untraced Drivers' Agreement. — [MIB making a claim](https://www.mib.org.uk/making-a-claim/)
- Official Injury Claim: "only suitable to claim for accidents that have happened in England or Wales"; excluded if "your personal injury claim is worth more than £5,000" or the total including protocol damages exceeds £10,000; "intended for an unrepresented claimant"; required information includes "The full name and UK vehicle registration number of the driver you feel is responsible", your own registration, witness details, National Insurance number, and police details "if the police attended the scene" (station, reporting officer, "Police Reference Number 'if available'"); "The compensator is the insurer/representative of the person you feel is at fault" and "If the person you feel is at fault is uninsured, the compensator will be the Motor Insurers' Bureau (MIB)"; the compensator has "up to 30 business days (6 weeks)" on liability and "20 business days (4 weeks)" to make a first offer, payment "within 10 business days (2 weeks)" of settlement; court proceedings within a "3-year time period". The OIC homepage states "We work on behalf of the Ministry of Justice". — [OIC start a new claim](https://www.officialinjuryclaim.org.uk/help-hub/start-a-new-claim/); [OIC homepage](https://www.officialinjuryclaim.org.uk/)
- STATS19: road casualty statistics "are based on data reported by police using the STATS19 data collection system"; the current form is the "2024 specification" (9-page PDF) with police guidance "STATS20" (138 pages); sensitive fields are released only to approved researchers; the 2024 figures were published 25 September 2025; oversight by the Standing Committee on Road Injury Collision Statistics (SCRICS). — [gov.uk STATS19 guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/road-accident-and-safety-statistics-guidance); the statistics are "mostly based on personal-injury collisions reported to the police in the STATS19 data collection". — [gov.uk road safety statistics collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/road-accidents-and-safety-statistics)

### Inferences
- For a UK PI solicitor the manual steps mirror the US ones: identify the insurer (askMID), obtain the police reference from the client, then request the collision report/abstract from the force under its charging scheme; the OIC portal removes the police report as a precondition for low-value whiplash claims.
- "SCRIM" in the brief does not appear in any fetched source as a collision-record system; the police statistical return is STATS19 (with STATS20 guidance). No source was found describing SCRIM in this context, so the writer should not treat it as a report channel without checking.
