# Clio audits and the PI case-opening handoff gap (small and mid-size US and UK firms)

*Method note (11 Oct 2026): the shared web-search budget for this run was already exhausted when this researcher started, so every finding below comes from direct fetches of primary pages whose URLs were known in advance. Several key hosts refused automated fetches (HTTP 403): clio.com and help.clio.com (all Clio feature, help-centre, Legal Trends and Certified Consultant pages), G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Software Advice, GetApp, Upwork, Fiverr, reddit.com, americanbar.org. ## Key question 1: What can Clio (and comparable systems) do today for triggers, required fields, task assignment, overdue alerts and exception reports, and where are the documented limits?

### Takeaway
Clio Manage's native automation is template-based (matter templates, task-template lists applied to a matter, matter stages) rather than event-driven; event triggers, cross-object conditions and exception reports require Zapier/Make, the Clio API (50 requests per minute at peak) or a third party, and the API docs expose no webhook page. Among comparables, MyCase and PracticePanther document trigger-based task chains; CASEpeer documents PI-specific treatment tracking and "Automated Statute Alerts"; Osprey (UK) documents key-date reports; none of the vendor pages fetched documents a required-field rule, an overdue-task exception report or missing-provider detection.

### Inferences
- Clio's documented native model (task-template lists applied per matter, matter templates, stages) can standardise the *set* of tasks a new PI matter gets, but cannot by itself enforce that a provider list, HIPAA form or SOL field is populated, nor flag a matter that has no tasks: every one of those is a cross-object condition that needs the API or a Zapier/Make flow, and Zapier only fires on object creation or status change, not on custom-field values.
- The 50 req/min peak rate limit is enough for a nightly or daily sweep across a 1–30 lawyer firm's open matters, but any "daily exception report" would be a polling job, since no webhook documentation was found.
- Clio Operate (200+ users) and the "Clio Advanced" conference track suggest Clio itself sees workflow configuration as a gap for sophisticated users, while small firms are left with templates plus third-party automation.
- Across every US competitor page fetched, "overdue alert", "required field" and "exception report" are absent, which is consistent with the problem being a configuration/operations gap rather than something any vendor sells out of the box.

### Gaps
- Clio help-centre articles on task templates (due-date offsets, assignee by role vs named user), custom fields (whether a field can be marked required), matter templates and reports all returned 403; those specifics must be verified in-app or via the help centre by the user.
- Clio API field-level detail on TaskTemplate, TaskTemplateList, CustomField ("required"/"default" flags) and whether webhooks exist could not be confirmed (API reference page too large to fetch; no webhooks page in docs).
- Make.com's Clio module list and Clio's own App Directory were not retrievable (marketplace.clio.com does not resolve; clio.com blocked).

## Key question 2: How many Clio users use workflows or custom fields, and what do consultants say firms get wrong?

### Takeaway
No public statistic on the share of Clio users using task templates, workflows or custom fields was found; Clio's Legal Trends Reports only correlate "key workflow" adoption with growth (growing firms 18% more likely to adopt them). Under-use and misconfiguration evidence is indirect but consistent: insurer ALPS says routine calendaring errors persist "despite widespread adoption of legal technology", reviewers describe "sink or swim" setup, duplicate records and Grow/Manage not working together, and a LEAP consultancy sells a "health check" explicitly for "underused features" and "data quality".

### Cited Findings
- Clio 2025 Legal Trends Report (16 Oct 2025): "Growing firms are 18 percent more likely to adopt key workflows, including electronic payments, online scheduling, and automated communications"; growing firms "use 12 percent more Clio services than stagnant or shrinking firms"; growing firms are 2x more likely to use automation than stable firms; 77% of firms that grew revenue with AI credited "improved operations" such as document generation and workflow automation. The release contains no feature-usage rates. — [PR Newswire, 16 Oct 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-science-behind-smarter-law-clios-2025-legal-trends-report-reveals-how-technology-is-rewiring-the-way-lawyers-work-302585725.html)
- Clio 2024 Legal Trends Report (7 Oct 2024): law-firm AI use rose from 19% to 79% in a year; software spending has grown 20% a year since 2013 vs 9% revenue growth; the industry average is 33% of the workday billed; 2024 secret-shopper study of 500 firms found 33% email response rate, 40% phone answer rate, 48% "essentially unreachable by phone"; firms with online intake tools have 50% more incoming potential clients and 50% more revenue. — [PR Newswire, 7 Oct 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-powered-legal-practices-surge-clios-latest-legal-trends-report-reveals-major-shift-302268966.html)
- Clio mid-sized report (30 Jan 2024, firms with 20+ employees): median realisation lockup 36 days, collection lockup 52 days; Clio Payments use associated with 13 fewer days of total lockup; "over 1,000 mid-sized firms use Clio"; CEO Jack Newton: "tech adoption, particularly in collections and payments, is still lagging." — [PR Newswire, 30 Jan 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/clio-unveils-new-2024-report-legal-trends-for-mid-sized-law-firms-302048145.html)
- Clio reported annual recurring revenue above US$500 million (12 May 2026) and a US$5 billion valuation after the US$1 billion vLex acquisition (10 Nov 2025). — [PR Newswire Clio listing](https://www.prnewswire.com/news/clio/)
- Trustpilot (297 reviews, 3.5 stars): "the reporting is not remotely close to be as great as they say" (19 Sep 2026); "Clio creates duplicate invoices for the already existing invoices" and "Some trust activity is Sent to QBO as a triple entry" (21 Aug 2026); "Its basically sink or swim, based on your own level of accounting knowledge" (11 Jul 2026); "Grow and manage do not work together at all" and "We got a 34% increase in per month per user increase two months ago" (17 Aug 2026); Xero payments "not synchronising with Clio properly" (16 Jan 2026). — [Trustpilot Clio](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/clio.com)
- Lawyerist community reviews: "it seems like we should get a lot more reporting capabilities than we do at our pricing level"; a 2022 user advised against Clio "for anything beyond timekeeping" after a failed intake-form project; "a firm will need to spend time getting set-up"; "not all of your needed functionality will be included in Clio's price." — [Lawyerist Clio review](https://lawyerist.com/reviews/law-practice-management-software/clio/)
- ALPS (risk manager Mark Bassingthwaighte, 21 Jul 2026): "Routine errors, especially calendaring and the filing and service of documents, remain a primary cause of claims despite widespread adoption of legal technology"; "Technology is not a substitute for process"; "Software can't fix inconsistent routines, ineffective internal processes, or poor documentation habits"; firms should rely on "institutional systems" not "institutional memory". — [ALPS blog, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.alpsinsurance.com/blog/safeguarding-your-practice-what-current-malpractice-trends-are-telling-us-about-managing-risk)
- Jayva Global (LEAP consultancy, offices Boston, Manchester, Cumbria, Dublin, Auckland): "Are you wasting your software investment? Don't let your LEAP system drift!"; the health check covers "Underused features and tools", "Data quality, reporting and operational visibility", "Consistency across teams and departments", "Legal accounting processes and compliance controls"; drift triggers listed are new functionality, team changes, process changes, regulatory changes and reporting needs. — [Jayva, "Are you wasting your software investment"](https://www.jayvaglobal.com/news/are-you-wasting-your-software-investment)
- Law Right (UK LEAP consultant) markets its services as a way to "unlock the full capabilities of Leap". — [Law Right](https://www.lawright.co.uk/)
- Baskerville Drummond (UK) asks "whether the systems you already have could deliver more". — [Baskerville Drummond](https://baskervilledrummond.com/selecting-the-right-technology-for-your-law-firm/)
- 3Kites (UK) publishes "Why I'd ban 'Phase 2' from practice management projects". — [3Kites](https://www.3kites.com/single-post/why-i-d-ban-phase-2-from-practice-management-projects)
- Clio's customer community (Gainsight-powered "Law Community") has an ideas board and template exchange, but the ideas page returned 403. — [community.clio.com](https://community.clio.com/)

### Inferences
- The absence of any published usage rate for task templates or custom fields, combined with Clio's own "growing firms adopt 18% more key workflows" framing, suggests Clio measures workflow adoption internally but does not publish it; a consultant pitch can only cite the correlation, not a base rate.
- The reviewer complaints cluster on data integrity (duplicates, sync triple entries), reporting and Grow-to-Manage handoff, which is the same class of problem a "Clio audit" would target; the 34% price-rise complaint is a demand signal for "get value from what you pay for".
- Jayva's health-check scope (under-used features, data quality, reporting, consistency across teams) is the closest public specification of what a practice-management audit covers and transfers directly to Clio.

### Gaps
- No Clio Cloud Conference talk transcripts or consultant blog posts stating "firms only use Clio for billing" or quantifying matters without tasks, duplicate contacts or unreconciled trust were retrievable without search; Reddit (r/Lawyertalk, r/LawFirm) and G2/Capterra were blocked.
- No statistic on Clio Grow pipeline abandonment was found; the only evidence is the Aug 2026 Trustpilot line that Grow and Manage "do not work together at all".

## Key question 6: What are the PI case-opening steps and the documented failure points, in practitioners' words?

### Takeaway
Practitioner-facing checklists (CASEpeer Sep 2026, Oregon PLF 2025 practice aids, Lawyers Mutual NC Sep 2026) converge on the same opening sequence: retainer and fee explanation, HIPAA/employment authorisations, letters of representation to all insurers "within 24 to 48 hours", SOL and tort-claim notices calendared immediately with advance reminders, police/incident records, policy limits, spoliation letters, Medicare/Medicaid/lien notices, records and bills requested from every provider including pre-incident treatment, and 14-day client check-ins. The documented failure points are liens not tracked, treatment gaps unexplained, SOL "slipped through the cracks during a handoff", requests never chased ("providers may overlook requests if not reminded") and files with no reminder date. None of the fetched checklists assigns an owner per step, and none includes an explicit "build and maintain the provider list" step.

### Cited Findings
**Case-opening sequence**
- CASEpeer PI checklist (updated 2 Sep 2026): gather client information and screen against acceptance criteria; sign the retainer; get HIPAA and records-release authorisations executed; open the claim and enter client, incident and insurance details; send a letter of representation to all known insurers "within 24 to 48 hours"; identify the statute of limitations and calendar it immediately; request the police report; photograph and preserve evidence; send spoliation letters; identify witnesses; pull the at-fault party's policy limits; track treatment and flag gaps; "request records and bills from every provider and log them as they arrive"; check for Medicare/Medicaid and open MSA reporting; check in with the client about every 14 days; identify all lienholders (health insurer, Medicare/Medicaid, ERISA, hospital liens) and request itemised lien statements early; run a 30/60/90-day audit of SOL calendared, records current, lien statements outstanding. "The article does not include a separate medical provider list step" and "does not assign owners to these steps." — [CASEpeer checklist](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-case-management-guide-and-checklist/)
- CASEpeer letter-of-representation guide (updated 19 Dec 2025): "Typically sent shortly after a lawyer is retained" to insurers, at-fault individuals, medical providers, employers and opposing counsel; delayed, vague or inconsistent letters cause "Misdirected communications", "Delayed claim processing", "Unauthorized contact with the client", "Compromised legal position". — [CASEpeer LOR](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-letter-of-representation/)
- CASEpeer medical-records guide (updated 4 Sep 2026): obtain signed HIPAA authorisation before requesting; specify timeframe and document types; request records "as soon as work on the case begins"; "Follow up regularly" because providers "may overlook requests if not reminded"; errors such as "transcription mistakes, missing pages, or misfiled documents" can compromise evidence; the guide "does not address finding providers that are not yet known from received records or bills." — [CASEpeer medical records](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/medical-records-management/)
- Oregon PLF "Issues for Consideration in Commencing or Settling a Personal Injury Case" (rev Oct 2025): "Have you obtained the pertinent HIPAA-compliant medical authorizations?"; "Have you obtained an employment and tax records authorization?"; "Have you determined whether the client is Medicare or Medicaid eligible and have you put the agencies on notice"; workers' compensation and Crime Victims Compensation Fund liens; "phantom vehicle" cases "require that immediate notice be provided to their Uninsured Motorist insurer"; "Have you obtained medical, imaging, and billing records from your client's medical providers including treatment prior to the litigated incident?"; "Do you have a system to calendar important dates and provide reminders for upcoming events?"; "Have you correctly recorded and calendared the time within which a tort claim notice must be sent?"; "Have you calendared not only the applicable timelines, but also a reminder well in advance of the timeline?"; after filing, notice to "Medicare, Medicaid, Health Insurers, Auto Insurers"; UM/UIM notice; spoliation letters. — [OSB PLF PI checklist (.docx)](https://assets.osbplf.org/forms/practice_forms/issues-for-consideration-in-commencing-and-settling-a-personal-injury-case.docx)
- Oregon PLF "Roadmap of a Civil Litigation Matter from the Plaintiff's Perspective" (rev Nov 2025): investigate before taking the case ("Obtain medical records via releases", police reports, all insurance policies including "Liability, UM/UIM, PIP"); "Determine the statute of limitations. Are you close to the deadline?"; "Is a tort claim notice required?"; on engagement "Send a preservation of evidence letter"; "As records come in, prepare a medical bill summary"; notify lienholders before mediation and "Immediately confirm any negotiated deduction in writing." — [OSB PLF roadmap (PDF)](https://assets.osbplf.org/forms/practice_forms/roadmap-of-a-civil-litigation-matter-from-the-plaintiff-s-perspective.pdf)
- Oregon PLF "Plaintiff's Key Information Sheet" (rev Nov 2025) is a one-page sheet with venue, adjuster, claim number, date of incident, injuries, damages, policy limits, "PIP Ledger", demand/offer history and to-dos. — [OSB PLF key info sheet (.docx)](https://assets.osbplf.org/forms/practice_forms/plaintiff-s-key-information-sheet.docx)
- Lawyers Mutual NC (Will Graebe, 29 Sep 2026): an intake checklist should capture "important dates and deadlines", and "makes it less likely that an important question gets missed"; set a response-time standard and "someone should be responsible for making sure it happens"; an "imminent statute of limitations" is a warning sign; send non-engagement letters that remind the person of known deadlines. — [Lawyers Mutual NC, "Don't Leave Client Intake to Chance"](https://lawyersmutualnc.com/article/dont-leave-client-intake-to-chance/)

**Failure points in practitioners' words**
- CASEpeer: a PI case "most often fails because a lien wasn't tracked, a treatment gap went unexplained, or a statute of limitations deadline slipped through the cracks during a handoff"; unexplained treatment gaps are "one of the fastest ways to lose value". — [CASEpeer checklist](https://www.casepeer.com/blog/personal-injury-case-management-guide-and-checklist/)
- Oregon PLF Docketing and Calendaring Checklist (rev May 2025): a docket system must provide "Immediate and automatic entry of dates", "Double-checking of all entries", "Follow-up checking" and backup; "Use a new matter intake form, which asks the question, 'What deadline dates need to be calendared?'"; "Have someone assigned to screen incoming mail for calendaring needs BEFORE the mail is distributed"; daily and at least weekly lawyer–staff conferences to "double-check that all dates and events have been properly entered"; a "final reminder" strategy covering illness, procrastination and "Preventing the file or memo from getting buried on a desk"; "DO NOT assume anything. Verify it." — [OSB PLF docketing checklist (PDF)](https://assets.osbplf.org/forms/practice_forms/docketing-and-calendaring-checklist.pdf)
- Oregon PLF Reminder and Tickler Systems (rev May 2025): "No file should ever be set aside without a reminder date on it or be tickled for more than 60 days"; "review all files at least once in any 30-day period, ideally during a weekly file review meeting"; "Each morning you or your assistant should pull all files tickled for that day"; a reminder system "prevents files from falling through the cracks because nothing has happened to create any activity on the file." — [OSB PLF tickler systems (PDF)](https://assets.osbplf.org/forms/practice_forms/reminder-and-tickler-systems.pdf)
- Finch (vendor, PI operations outsourcer): records are requested "in parallel with treatment", status tracked, gaps flagged; "14-day average time to records and 80% received within 30 days"; "Case managers check in with clients every two weeks so matters don't stall"; the page "says nothing about detecting new referrals or additional providers inside received records." — [Finch](https://www.finchlegal.com/)
- EvenUp Medical Management (vendor): claims "about 20% of missed appointments going unnoticed without the product" and "about 37% of issues needing follow-up"; flags "treatment gaps, missing records, and upcoming appointments" and offers a "missing docs check"; missing providers or "providers referenced in records but never requested" are "not found on the page." — [EvenUp Medical Management](https://www.evenuplaw.com/products/medical-management)

### Inferences
- The step most relevant to the exception-report idea, "maintain a provider list and reconcile it against providers named inside received records and bills", does not appear in any of the fetched checklists; the closest items are PLF's "records... including treatment prior to the litigated incident" and CASEpeer's "request records and bills from every provider", which presuppose the list is already complete.
- A daily missing-task report for a PI practice, built from these sources, would need to check at least: retainer signed; HIPAA and employment authorisations on file; LOR sent to every known insurer within 48 hours; SOL and tort-claim-notice dates entered with advance reminder; police report requested; policy limits requested; spoliation letters sent; Medicare/Medicaid/UM notices sent; each provider on the list has a request date, a follow-up date within the firm's interval, and a received date; lien statements requested; last client contact within 14 days; and no matter without a next reminder date (PLF's 60-day rule).
- The handoff failure named by CASEpeer (SOL slipping "during a handoff") and PLF's "double-checking of all entries" both point to the intake-to-case-manager transfer as the control point.

### Gaps
- The August 2026 r/paralegal thread on identifying missing providers and incomplete records with no attorney guidance could not be retrieved (reddit.com and old.reddit.com are blocked for this tool); no mirror was used, per the research constraints.
