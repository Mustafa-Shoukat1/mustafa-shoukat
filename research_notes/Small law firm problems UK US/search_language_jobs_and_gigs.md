# Search language, job ads and freelance gigs: how small UK and US law firms ask for help (vocabulary and demand map)

Research date: 10 October 2026. Scope: UK (England and Wales) and US small firms and sole practitioners. This note records exact words, counts, prices and demand signals only. It does not propose solutions or recommend tools.

Method notes the report writer needs:
- UK job-ad counts come from a scrape I ran of 40 live Reed.co.uk ads (the first 25 "Conveyancing Paralegal" ads and the first 15 "Legal Secretary" ads returned on 10 Oct 2026). Counts are "number of ads containing the phrase", matched case-insensitively on whole words.
- US job-ad counts are my manual count over 22 ads I read in full: the 14 sample postings reproduced on Indeed's US "Paralegal" and "Legal Assistant" job-description pages, plus 8 Craigslist Manhattan postings fetched on 10 Oct 2026. Treat them as indicative, not statistical.
- Autocomplete phrases were pulled from Google's public suggest endpoint (suggestqueries.google.com, client=firefox) on 10 Oct 2026 with gl=us and gl=gb. The two country settings returned almost identical lists, so treat them as global English suggestions with light UK/US flavour.
- Fiverr blocks automated fetching (HTTP 403 on every page), and Reddit blocks this crawler entirely. Fiverr figures below come from search-result snippets; review counts and review texts could not be obtained (see Gaps).
- Upwork job text was recovered from the JSON embedded in Upwork's category pages (60 unique posts dated 14 Sep to 9 Oct 2026); Upwork's own hire pages were readable via curl.

## Key question 1: Which duties appear most often in small-firm paralegal, legal secretary and legal assistant ads (UK and US)?

### Takeaway
UK ads are dominated by conveyancing process words (completion, Land Registry, searches, SDLT, exchange), "supporting fee earners", "liaising with clients, estate agents and lenders", audio typing/digital dictation, diary management and "opening and closing files"; US ads are dominated by litigation-process words (pleadings, motions, discovery, e-filing, calendaring deadlines), answering phones, client intake, medical records, billing/time entry, and bilingual (Spanish) client contact. Both sides describe the small-firm job as "wearing many hats".

### Inferences
- UK support-role ads are written around the conveyancing and private-client process (instruction, searches, exchange, completion, post-completion, Land Registry, SDLT; wills, LPAs, grants, IHT forms), so the vocabulary a UK small firm uses to describe "help" is process-stage vocabulary rather than task-type vocabulary.
- US support-role ads are written around litigation mechanics plus front-of-house (phones, intake, bilingual client contact) and billing; "case manager" and "intake specialist" are US job titles with no UK equivalent in the ads read.
- "Fee earner" (21 of 40 UK ads) is the single most useful marker of UK-authored text; US ads say "attorney(s)" and never "fee earner".
- Audio typing/digital dictation remains a named UK secretary skill (7 of 40 ads, both employer-written ads); in the US set only two ads mention transcription or dictation.
- Neither market's ads name case-management brands often (0 of 40 UK ads; 1 ad each for Clio/MyCase/Timeslips in the US set); brand familiarity is asked for mainly in freelance posts (see KQ2).

## Key question 5: How does UK vocabulary differ from US vocabulary?

### Takeaway
The job ads and regulator texts confirm a consistent split: solicitor/fee earner/matter/client account/completion/exchange/searches/Land Registry/precedent/bundle/disclosure/audio typing/legal cashier/private client (UK) against attorney/lawyer/timekeeper-style billing words/case/trust account (IOLTA)/closing/escrow/title company/template/binder/discovery/transcription/biller/estate planning (US). Some words cross over: "matter" and "pleadings" appear in both; "intake" has started to appear in UK private-client ads.
