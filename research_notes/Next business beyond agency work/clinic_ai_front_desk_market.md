## 1. Who are the main vendors, what do they charge, how much have they raised, and is the category consolidating or commoditising?

### Takeaway
A single clinic location can buy a PMS-integrated AI receptionist from a specialist for roughly USD 250 to 700 a month, often with unlimited minutes and month-to-month terms, and generic small-business AI receptionists start at USD 29 to 99 a month. The category is both consolidating (Weave bought TrueLark; incumbents Adit, Dental Intelligence, Peerlogic and NexHealth now ship their own AI receptionist) and commoditising, while medical-side players have raised hundreds of millions.

### Inferences
- Four layers now compete for the same clinic: (a) incumbents that already own the phone line or patient messaging (Weave, Adit, Dental Intelligence, NexHealth, Peerlogic) and add AI as a feature; (b) dental-only startups at about USD 250 to 500 per location with unlimited minutes (Arini, Dentina, PatientDesk, Viva); (c) venture giants in medical groups and health systems (Assort, Hello Patient, EliseAI), which are not chasing single clinics; (d) horizontal USD 29 to 99 tools. A small agency selling to single clinics is squeezed between (a) and (b) on integration depth and (d) on price.
- Weave's revenue per location works out to about USD 500 a month (USD 239.0M / 39,625 locations / 12) for phones, texting, reminders, payments and now AI. That is the budget anchor a clinic owner already has in mind; a stand-alone AI front desk at USD 300 to 500 is asking for a second bill of the same size.
- Beside's numbers imply about USD 17 a month per paying customer (USD 4M / 20,000 / 12), which shows how low the generic price floor has gone.
- "Unlimited minutes, month-to-month, no setup fee" (PatientDesk, Dentina trial, Adit) is becoming the standard offer, so a per-minute or setup-fee-heavy agency offer will look expensive beside it.
- Consolidation signals: one acquisition with a disclosed price (TrueLark, USD 35M) and at least three incumbents launching native AI receptionists between February and June 2026. The stand-alone "AI answers your phone" feature is turning into a checkbox inside practice software.

- Arini's true funding: aggregators disagree and look incomplete.

## 2. What do small agencies and resellers charge for AI voice receptionist and WhatsApp/SMS booking bots built on Retell, Vapi, Synthflow, GoHighLevel and similar, and what gross margins do they report?

### Takeaway
The going reseller price is USD 250 to 600 a month per client or location, with setup fees from zero to a few thousand dollars; claimed gross margins are 60 to 80% once past about 20 clients, but nearly every number comes from white-label platform vendors, not audited agency accounts. Underlying usage cost is roughly USD 0.09 to 0.15 a minute all-in.

### Cited Findings
**Underlying platform costs**
- [HARD] Retell AI: voice infrastructure USD 0.055/min, Retell TTS USD 0.015/min, US telephony USD 0.015/min, LLM extra by model; calculator default about USD 0.11/min; stated range USD 0.07 to 0.31/min; 20 concurrent calls free; enterprise is custom — [Retell pricing](https://www.retellai.com/pricing)
- [HARD] Vapi: USD 0.05/min hosting plus model, voice and telephony passed through at cost; HIPAA add-on USD 2,000/month; extra concurrency USD 10/line/month — [Vapi pricing](https://vapi.ai/pricing)
- [3P] Real production cost on Retell is put at USD 0.13 to 0.31/min once STT, TTS, LLM and telephony are added — [CloudTalk on Retell pricing](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/retell-ai-pricing/)
- [HARD] GoHighLevel: Starter USD 97, Unlimited USD 297, Agency Pro (SaaS mode, rebilling with markup) USD 497/month; AI Employee USD 50 or USD 97 per sub-account per month; HIPAA add-on USD 297/month — [HighLevel pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)
- [3P] GoHighLevel Voice AI usage: about USD 0.045/min engine plus USD 0.015 to 0.17/min voice plus model and call costs; agencies commonly resell AI Employee at USD 197 to 297 per client — [Apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/gohighlevel-pricing); [Ecosire](https://ecosire.com/blog/gohighlevel-pricing-worth-it-2026)

**What resellers charge clients**
- [VENDOR] My AI Front Desk white label: wholesale USD 54.99 per receptionist, overage USD 0.12/min; "many resellers package at $250–$500+ per month per client" — [My AI Front Desk white label](https://llms.myaifrontdesk.com/white-label-reseller-agencies)
- [VENDOR] Vida: resellers price at "$295 and $595 per month per location", up to USD 750+ for multi-location; wholesale USD 75 to 175 per agent; worked examples give 62.5% to about 80% gross margin; 50 agents at USD 400 and 60% margin = USD 12,000/month gross profit — [Vida](https://vida.io/blog/white-label-ai-receptionist)
- [VENDOR] Trillet example tiers: USD 300/month (200 min), USD 450 (500 min), from USD 600 (750 min), overage USD 0.25 to 0.30/min; "a $300/month retainer per client, plus setup fee and per-minute markup" called a common benchmark; a USD 400 retainer leaves "roughly $264 before plan fee, telephony" — [Trillet](https://trillet.ai/blogs/ai-receptionist-white-label-pricing)
- [VENDOR] Vendasta: Starter USD 199 to 299 (chat, SMS), Professional USD 399 to 599 (voice + chat + SMS, booking, CRM sync), Enterprise USD 799+; platform cost USD 50 to 100 per client; "70–80% gross margins" — [Vendasta, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/white-label-ai-receptionist/)
- [VENDOR] Leadlock: USD 297 to 497/month per client; modelled gross margin 46% at 5 clients, 71% at 20, 76% at 50, 78% at 100 — [Leadlock, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.leadlock.ai/blog/white-label-ai-voice-agents-how-agencies-scale-to-100-clients/)
- [VENDOR] CallSphere (the high end): setup USD 1,500 to 5,000, managed USD 499 to 1,499/month, overage USD 0.20 to 0.35/min over USD 0.09 wholesale; "70-90% gross margin once they pass 10-20 clients" — [CallSphere](https://callsphere.ai/blog/vw7a-white-label-ai-voice-agency-model-2026)
- [VENDOR] Autocalls playbook: clients pay USD 400 to 600/month, platform cost about USD 80 per client, "80%+" margin — [Autocalls](https://autocalls.ai/voice-ai-playbook)
- [3P] Broader "AI automation agency" retainers for small businesses run USD 500 to 2,000/month; productised packages USD 1,800 to 4,500/month — [Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/)
- [ANECDOTE via VENDOR] A solo consultant went from 3 to 17 clients in under a year at 68% gross margin and USD 13,500 MRR (about USD 794 per client); a marketing consultancy cross-sold voice AI to 45% of its existing clients at USD 750 MRR each — [Leadlock citing VoiceAIWrapper](https://www.leadlock.ai/blog/white-label-ai-voice-agents-how-agencies-scale-to-100-clients/)
- [3P] UK agencies: £150 to £650/month plus £500 to £3,000 setup — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-much-does-a-dental-ai-receptionist-cost/)

### Inferences
- Unit economics for one clinic (my assumption: 500 to 1,500 AI minutes a month, not sourced): at about USD 0.125/min on Retell that is USD 60 to 190 of usage. At a USD 350 retail price the usage-only gross margin is roughly 45 to 83%, before PMS API fees, a HIPAA add-on, support labour and refunds. Margins quoted by vendors ignore support labour, which is the main real cost for an agency with a 15 to 20 person team.
- Fixed compliance costs hit small books hard: Vapi's USD 2,000/month HIPAA add-on needs about six USD 350 clients just to cover it; GoHighLevel's USD 297 needs one.
- The agency price band (USD 250 to 600) is the same as the direct price of specialist vendors that already have PMS write-back (Dentina USD 299 to 499, PatientDesk A$250 to 500, Peerlogic USD 699). An agency therefore cannot charge a premium for the bot itself; any premium has to come from bundled services (ads, recall campaigns, reporting).
- Setup fees above about USD 1,000 are common in agency marketing but the specialist vendors mostly charge none, so setup fees are hard to defend to a clinic that compares offers.

### Gaps
- No audited or independently reported agency margin data was found; all margin figures are vendor examples.
- Reddit (r/AI_Agents, r/agency) and the Indie Hackers post "Building a Profitable AI Voice SaaS/Agency: $300-800 MRR per client" could not be opened, so first-hand operator pricing is missing.
- No reliable price data specifically for WhatsApp booking bots sold to clinics in the US, UK, Canada or Australia was found; pricing found is for voice plus SMS.

## 3. What is known about churn and retention for AI receptionist products and for AI automation agency retainers with small businesses?

### Takeaway
No vendor or agency publishes a real churn number for AI receptionists. The best hard anchor is Weave (89% dollar gross retention, 93% net retention at end of 2025) for a deeply embedded clinic product; generic SMB software benchmarks and unsourced agency claims point to much worse retention (3 to 7% a month) for cheap, month-to-month, unintegrated tools.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD] Weave dollar-based gross revenue retention 89% and net revenue retention 93% as of 31 December 2025, with a majority of customers on monthly billing — [Weave Q4/FY2025 results](https://www.01net.it/weave-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results/); [Weave 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609151/000160915126000016/weav-20251231.htm)
- [HARD, older: 2023 report on 2022 data] ChartMogul's study of 2,100+ SaaS companies: retention rises with price. For ARPA above USD 500/month, top-quartile customer retention is 85.7% and 35.7% of companies have gross revenue retention above 85%; for ARPA under USD 10/month top-quartile customer retention is 63.1% — [ChartMogul SaaS Retention Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-report/)
- [3P] Aggregated benchmarks: SMB SaaS sees 3 to 7% average monthly churn (30 to 58% a year); monthly contracts churn at 3 to 5 times the rate of annual contracts — [Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/saas-churn-rate); [StealthAgents compilation](https://stealthagents.com/research/saas-churn-rate-statistics-2026)
- [VENDOR, unsourced] "The average client retention on a GHL-powered SaaS platform is 18 to 24 months, compared to 4 to 6 months for a typical agency retainer"; no source is given and the publisher sells GoHighLevel implementation — [ghlcrms](https://ghlcrms.com/scale-ghl-saas-mode/)
- [VENDOR] Trillet, a white-label platform, writes that "Neither architecture guarantees margin or client retention; those depend on delivery quality and pricing" and that "the agency still needs to justify its markup through implementation, optimization, reporting, and support" — [Trillet](https://trillet.ai/blogs/my-ai-front-desk-white-label-alternative)
- [VENDOR] Vendasta says retention is "significantly higher" than project work because "clients become operationally dependent on the tool", but gives no number — [Vendasta](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/white-label-ai-receptionist/)
- [VENDOR, summarising Reddit threads without quoting them] "The plateau is always a sales plateau"; typical path is first retainers in the low four figures a month, reaching a mid-four to low-five figure monthly run rate in year one — [Ciela, 2 Jul 2026](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-realistic-income-reddit)
- [3P] One reviewer's caution on brand-new incumbent AI receptionists: treat as "a pilot rather than something to standardize twenty locations on this year" — [Medix Dental](https://medixdental.com/ai-tools-for-dental-practices/)
- [3P] UK buyers face ongoing hidden costs that feed dissatisfaction: about 20 to 30 minutes of staff review time weekly and £50 to £150 per script change — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-much-does-a-dental-ai-receptionist-cost/)

### Inferences
- Weave's 89% annual gross dollar retention equals roughly 1% monthly revenue churn. That is the ceiling for a product that owns the phone system, is integrated with the PMS and has a field sales and support organisation. A stand-alone bot from a small agency should expect worse.
- A reasonable planning range for a USD 300 to 500, month-to-month, single-location clinic product sold by a small agency is 3 to 6% logo churn a month (average customer life about 17 to 33 months), improving toward 1 to 2% only with real PMS write-back, annual contracts and bundled services. This is my estimate from the benchmarks above, not a measured figure.
- Likely cancellation reasons, inferred from how vendors position against each other: (1) the clinic's existing platform (Weave, Adit, Dental Intelligence, NexHealth) adds an AI receptionist at little or no extra cost; (2) booking failures or misheard patient details erode staff trust; (3) the owner cannot see booked-appointment proof of return; (4) the bot was never properly configured after sale.
- Churn risk is highest exactly where the agency model is weakest: no integration, no on-site relationship, and a clinic manager who did not choose the tool.

### Gaps
- No published monthly churn, customer lifetime or cancellation-reason data for any AI receptionist vendor (Arini, Dentina, PatientDesk, My AI Front Desk, Goodcall, Smith.ai) was found.
- A My AI Front Desk reseller blog was quoted in search results as saying "nearly 30% of users abandon AI solutions within the first six months"; the page now returns HTTP 410 and no source for the figure could be checked.
- Search summaries mentioned (a) small agencies with 1 to 10 staff losing 32% of clients a year, (b) onboarding calls in the first 7 days cutting 90-day churn by 3x, and (c) patients getting frustrated repeating name and date of birth to an AI; none could be traced to a verifiable source.
- Reddit operator threads on churn were not accessible.

## 4. What white-label and reseller programmes exist and what do they cost? How do dental marketing agencies and consultants resell these?

### Takeaway
White-label voice platforms cost an agency about USD 99 to 500 a month plus USD 0.09 to 0.12 a minute, or about USD 55 to 175 per deployed receptionist; most cheap ones are not HIPAA-ready, and one major platform (Synthflow) has moved to enterprise contracts. Evidence on how dental marketing agencies resell is thin; the visible pattern is bundling the receptionist with ads and websites.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD] GoHighLevel SaaS mode: Agency Pro USD 497/month includes "SaaS Mode", "Automated Sub-Account Creation" and "Rebill Phone & Email with Markup"; AI Employee USD 50 or 97 per sub-account; HIPAA add-on USD 297/month — [HighLevel pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)
- [VENDOR] Trillet: Studio USD 99/month (1,000 shared minutes, max 3 workspaces); Agency USD 299/month (3,000 shared minutes pooled across clients, unlimited workspaces); USD 0.12/min after that; telephony USD 0.014/min; SMS USD 0.02; claims HIPAA, SOC 2, GDPR and TCPA compliance included — [Trillet, updated 30 Sep 2026](https://trillet.ai/blogs/ai-receptionist-white-label-pricing)

### Inferences
- Platform risk is real: the same platforms agencies build on (Retell, My AI Front Desk, GoHighLevel) also sell directly to clinics, and Synthflow's move to USD 30,000+ contracts shows a platform can withdraw its agency tier.
- For clinics in the US, the cheapest white-label options are unusable because they will not sign a BAA; the HIPAA-capable routes are Retell (BAA available), Trillet (claims included), GoHighLevel (+USD 297) and Vapi (+USD 2,000).
- The durable reseller model in dental looks like "marketing agency plus front desk": the receptionist is one line in a larger monthly retainer (ads, website, recall, reviews), which raises ticket size and makes the bot harder to cancel on its own.

## 5. Which integrations decide the sale, how hard are they for a small team, and what compliance is required?

### Takeaway
Direct write-back into the clinic's practice management system is the feature specialists lead with, and access is gated: Open Dental and Cliniko are open and cheap, Dentrix costs about USD 10,000 up front, Jane and Dentally require partner approval, and the practical shortcut (NexHealth Synchronizer) is metered per API call. In the US, WhatsApp cannot carry patient data because Meta will not sign a BAA.

**Practice management system access**

**Compliance**
- [3P] Meta "does not offer a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) for any version of WhatsApp — personal accounts, WhatsApp Business, or WhatsApp Business API", so WhatsApp cannot be used for PHI under HIPAA; a compliant chain needs a BAA with every party that touches PHI — [Bloomtext](https://bloomtext.com/is-whatsapp-hipaa-compliant/); [LuxSci](https://www.luxsci.com/blog/is-whatsapp-hipaa-compliant/)
- [HARD] Retell's dental page says HIPAA with a "self-service BAA portal available from the dashboard", SOC 2 Type II, PII redaction — [Retell dental page](https://www.retellai.com/solutions/ai-dental-receptionist); Retell's pricing page mentions custom BAA terms under Enterprise, and third parties cite a USD 1,000/month HIPAA add-on for pay-as-you-go (conflicting; confirm with Retell) — [Retell pricing](https://www.retellai.com/pricing); [CloudTalk on Retell](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/retell-ai-pricing/)
- [HARD] Vapi HIPAA add-on USD 2,000/month; GoHighLevel HIPAA USD 297/month — [Vapi pricing](https://vapi.ai/pricing); [HighLevel pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)
- [HARD, quoted via search result; direct fetch returned 403] US offshore handling: HHS says the HIPAA Rules "do not include requirements specific to protection of electronic protected health information (ePHI) processed or stored by a business associate outside of the United States", but offshore location must be weighed in the Security Rule risk analysis — [HHS FAQ 2083](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2083/do-the-hipaa-rules-allow-a-covered-entity-or-business-associate-to-use-a-csp-that-stores-ephi-on-servers-outside-of-the-united-states/index.html)
- [3P, older; publication date not checked] Some state Medicaid programmes ban or restrict offshore processing of Medicaid data (Arizona, Alaska, Ohio and Wisconsin named as outright bans) — [Healthcare IT News](https://healthcareitnews.com/node/530316)
- [3P] UK GDPR Chapter V prohibits transfers of personal data outside the UK unless there is an adequacy regulation, appropriate safeguards or a derogation; the ICO issued updated international-transfer guidance — [White & Case, March 2026](https://www.whitecase.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/from-adequacy-to-TRAs-decoding-the-ICO-latest-international-transfers-guidance.pdf)
- [HARD] Australia: APP 8.1 requires an entity to "take such steps as are reasonable in the circumstances to ensure that the recipient does not breach the APPs" before disclosing personal information overseas, and under section 16C the Australian entity is accountable for the overseas recipient's breaches — [OAIC APP 8 guidelines](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-8-app-8-cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information)

### Inferences
- For a small team the realistic integration order is: Open Dental (direct, USD 30 per location) and Cliniko (open key) first; Dentrix, Eaglesoft and Denticon only through NexHealth Synchronizer; Jane and Dentally only after partner approval that may never come. Calendar-only bots (Google Calendar, Setmore, Calendly) are easy but are exactly what specialists beat in a demo.
- Synchronizer's USD 0.10 per call can become a large cost: a booking conversation that makes many availability and patient-lookup calls could cost several dollars, so call caching and design matter and the cost must be modelled before pricing a flat monthly plan.
- A delivery team in Pakistan that can open call recordings, transcripts or the PMS is an offshore business associate (US), a restricted international transfer (UK) and an overseas disclosure (Australia). None is banned outright, but each needs paperwork (BAA and risk analysis; UK transfer agreement and risk assessment; APP 8 contract terms) and some clinics, DSOs and Medicaid-heavy practices will refuse. A US or UK contracting entity with data kept in-region and offshore staff restricted to de-identified logs lowers the objection. This is my reading, not legal advice.
- The "WhatsApp front desk" pitch that works in the Gulf does not transfer to the US: Meta will not sign a BAA, and US clinics use phone and SMS. WhatsApp is more plausible for UK and Australian clinics, but no demand data for those markets was found.

### Gaps
- No source was found on the actual build time for a Dentrix, Eaglesoft or Synchronizer integration by a small team.
- Whether Pakistan-based processing satisfies UK and Australian requirements in practice was not researched to legal standard; neither was TCPA (US outbound call and text consent) nor call-recording consent law, which matter for recall and reminder campaigns.
- Setmore, Eaglesoft direct terms, Denticon and veterinary/aesthetic PMS (ezyVet, Zenoti, Mindbody) were not checked.

## 6. How do successful sellers acquire clinic customers, and what are the sales cycle and acquisition cost?

### Takeaway
Independent practices are reached by cold call, cold email and dental trade events, with a 30 to 90 day cycle; DSOs take 60 to 180+ days and need brand credibility first. No reliable customer-acquisition-cost figure exists in public sources, and agencies that grow fastest sell into an existing client book rather than cold.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Arithmetic: at USD 300 to 500 a month, USD 30,000 MRR needs 60 to 100 clinics and USD 100,000 MRR needs 200 to 330. At 4% monthly churn, a 100-clinic book loses 4 clinics a month, so the seller must close 4 a month just to stand still and about 8 to 10 a month to reach 100 within 18 months.
- DentaAI's stated figures imply roughly £140,000 a year (about £12,000 a month) from 20+ clinics at a high annual price: a believable picture of what a focused small operator reaches, and well below the target range.
- The paths that plausibly reach USD 30,000+ for a small offshore team are: (1) a higher ticket by bundling (ads, recall and reactivation campaigns paid per outcome, website) as PatientDesk does; (2) selling through dental marketing agencies or consultants who already hold clinic relationships; (3) a few multi-location groups at USD 300 to 600 per location; (4) markets where specialists are thin (UK, Australia, Canada allied health on Cliniko) rather than US dental, where Arini, Dentina, Weave, Adit and Dental Intelligence already compete.
- The product alone is not the scarce asset; distribution, PMS integration and trust are. The seller's low-cost delivery team helps margins and support, not acquisition.

### Gaps
- Reddit, Indie Hackers and YouTube operator accounts could not be checked, so genuine first-hand cases at USD 30,000+ MRR may exist and were not seen. Absence here is "not found", not "does not exist".
- No offshore (South Asia-based) agency case for English-speaking clinic markets was found at any revenue level.
- Revenue of the small specialists (Arini, PatientDesk, Dentina, Viva) is not public.
