## 1. State of Saudi and Gulf enterprise AI spending in 2025-2026, and how much reaches private mid-market buyers

### Takeaway
The money is real but top-heavy: published Saudi "AI market" estimates for 2025-2026 range from under USD 1 billion to about USD 6 billion depending on the publisher's definition, and the headline HUMAIN, PIF and hyperscaler numbers are data-centre and compute capital, not application services. No source splits AI spend by buyer size; the indirect evidence (most Gulf firms still in pilots, PIF cutting advisory mandates, the largest integrator living on very large contracts) says mid-market private demand exists mainly as pilots and small first projects.

## 2. Regulations that push organisations to private or in-Kingdom AI, and whether enforcement is active in 2026

### Takeaway
Enforcement of the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law is live: 48 decisions by January 2026 with fines possible up to SAR 5 million, though no fine amounts have been published. The rules do not ban foreign or cloud AI outright; they make transfers conditional (safeguards, risk assessment) and make in-Kingdom hosting the default for banks, government and critical infrastructure, which is now being met by in-Kingdom hyperscaler regions as much as by on-premise builds. UAE federal privacy enforcement is still not operational.

### Cited Findings
Saudi PDPL
- PDPL enforcement began 14 September 2024. By mid-January 2026 SDAIA announced 48 enforcement decisions across multiple sectors. Committees can issue warnings, fine up to SAR 5 million (doubled for repeat violations) and order publication. No actual fine amounts were disclosed. Respondents get 5 days to answer, decisions are notified within 15 days, appeal within 60 days. — [Clyde & Co, Mar 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/03/enforcement-of-the-saudi-pdp-law) (fetched)
- Violations named in the decisions include failing to take organisational and technical measures to keep personal data safe, failing to appoint a data protection officer, processing without a legal basis, unauthorised disclosure, and marketing without consent. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/active-enforcement-of-saudi-arabia-3202525/) (snippet)
- Cross-border transfers: SDAIA's amended Regulation on Personal Data Transfer Outside the Kingdom (1 Sep 2024) narrowed the safeguards to three: standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, and certificates of accreditation. — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/saudi-arabia-updates-data-transfer-regulations-and-introduces-the-first-set-of-standard-contractual-clauses) (snippet); [Mayer Brown, Oct 2024](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/10/updates-to-saudi-arabias-personal-data-protection-regulations-sccs-guidelines-and-more) (snippet)
- The PDPL uses an adequacy system but the adequacy list had not been released (as of March 2025), so transfers rely on safeguards. A transfer risk assessment is mandatory when a safeguard is used or when sensitive data (health, biometric, genetic, religious, ethnic and similar) is transferred continuously or widely. SDAIA's February 2025 guideline sets a four-phase assessment that includes a "national interest" review; firms were told to audit overseas cloud and AI service arrangements. — [Clyde & Co, Mar 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/update-on-saudi-arabia-risk-assessment-guidelines) (fetched)

NDMO, NCA, SAMA
- SDAIA/NDMO data classification has four levels (reported as Public, Confidential, Secret, Top Secret); NCA Cloud Cybersecurity Controls and NDMO rules restrict sensitive national and personal data from being processed outside the Kingdom without authorisation. — [Zoho blog](https://www.zoho.com/blog/general/saudi-data-localisation-compliance-ready-clouds.html) (snippet; vendor blog); [MomentumX blog](https://momentumx.cloud/blog/saudi-nca-ccc2-cloud-compliance-checklist/) (snippet; vendor blog)
- NCA Cloud Cybersecurity Controls (issued 2021, older; later updates) include data-localisation requirements and bind cloud providers and cloud tenants that are government entities or critical national infrastructure operators. — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/10497-updated-cloud-cybersecurity-controls-including-data-localisation-requirements) (fetched); primary text: [NCA CCC](https://nca.gov.sa/ar/cloud_cybersecurity_controls_draft_en.pdf)
- SAMA Cloud Computing Framework: sensitive customer data, transaction records and operationally critical information of SAMA-regulated entities (banks, insurers, finance companies, payment providers, fintechs) must sit on infrastructure physically in Saudi Arabia; public cloud is permitted if the provider runs certified in-Kingdom regions and SAMA has inspection rights; examiners expect immutable audit logs and verifiable evidence. — [Kiteworks, updated 23 Feb 2026](https://www.kiteworks.com/regulatory-compliance/sama-data-sovereignty-hosting-compliance/) (fetched; vendor blog, not SAMA's text)
- Claim that SAMA supervisors are auditing cloud more often and checking whether AI workloads meet the same residency and vendor-management rules. Vendor claim, not confirmed from SAMA. — [MomentumX blog](https://momentumx.cloud/blog/sama-cloud-compliance-checklist-2026/) (snippet)

In-Kingdom cloud options that satisfy residency without on-premise hardware
- Google Cloud Dammam region (live Nov 2023) is CST Class C-certified; Vertex AI went live there May 2024; "Sovereign Controls by CNTXT" adds external key management, key-access justification and Saudi-based support. — [Google Cloud blog, 20 Aug 2024](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/google-cloud-expands-services-in-saudi-arabia-delivering-enhanced-data-sovereignty-and-ai-capabilities?hl=en) (fetched; older)
- Microsoft's Saudi Arabia East region (three availability zones, Eastern Province) becomes available November 2026; Microsoft, PIF and SITE signed an MoU in November 2025 to explore sovereign-cloud services. — [W.Media](https://w.media/microsofts-saudi-arabia-east-data-center-region-to-be-available-in-november-2026/) (snippet)
- AWS's Saudi region is on track for December 2026 (more than USD 5.3 billion), with Amazon Bedrock. — [Channel Insider, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.channelinsider.com/infrastructure/news-aws-cloud-region-launch-emea-saudi-arabia/) (fetched)

UAE
- UAE created the Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data on 14 June 2026, absorbing the AI Office, TDRA's digital-government functions and the never-operational Emirates Data Office. The federal PDPL (Decree-Law 45 of 2021) still has no implementing regulations and no designated private-sector supervisor. — [Morgan Lewis, Jun 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/uae-establishes-federal-authority-for-artificial-intelligence-and-data) (fetched)
- A vendor blog states the opposite (regulations issued, full compliance due 1 January 2027). — [PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/uae-pdpl-data-sovereignty-local-ai-2026) (snippet); contradicted by [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/uae-establishes-federal-authority-for-artificial-intelligence-and-data). The law-firm source is the more reliable.
- DIFC and ADGM run their own data-protection regimes; the federal PDPL does not apply inside them. — [ConsentStack](https://consentstack.io/regulations/uae-pdpl) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The regulatory driver is strongest for SAMA-regulated firms, government entities and critical infrastructure. For an ordinary private mid-market company the PDPL makes foreign AI services a paperwork problem (clauses plus risk assessment), not a prohibition, so "the law forces you on-premise" overstates it for that segment.
- By December 2026 all three hyperscalers will have Saudi regions. "Data stays in the Kingdom" will then be available from Azure, Google and AWS as a checkbox, which weakens residency alone as a selling point and shifts the differentiator to air-gapped or classified-data cases, access control, audit evidence and Arabic quality.
- The SDAIA violation most often cited, weak organisational and technical safeguards, is directly addressed by access control and audit logs, so the compliance story for his product is real for PDPL-exposed buyers.
- In the UAE the push to private AI comes from sector and free-zone rules and sovereign-cloud policy, not from federal privacy enforcement.

### Gaps
- No published PDPL fine amounts or named companies found; SDAIA's own announcement page was not retrieved.
- Whether SDAIA has since published an adequacy list (after March 2025) was not confirmed.
- Official NDMO level names should be checked against the NDMO policy text (English versions may use "Restricted" rather than "Confidential"); only vendor summaries were reached.
- No SAMA rulebook text or SAMA AI-specific guidance was found; a search for SAMA generative-AI rules returned only Singapore material. NCA ECC/CCC primary text was not read.
- UAE Central Bank and health-data localisation rules were not researched.

## 4. Typical deal size, contract structure and sales cycle for small and mid-sized AI vendors in Saudi Arabia and UAE; what local integrators charge

### Takeaway
No Gulf-specific price list for this work was found. Global vendor benchmarks put a production multi-source system with access control at USD 30,000-60,000 and a multi-tenant governed platform at USD 70,000-120,000 or more, so his USD 18,000-26,000 build price sits below those. The better-documented risk is cash: payment delays of 60-90 days and up to 180 days are reported in both countries.

### Cited Findings
- Global benchmark (software vendor's blog, Aug 2025): simple document Q&A USD 12,000-30,000 (4-6 weeks); production multi-source system with hybrid search, per-user access control and observability USD 30,000-60,000 (8-14 weeks); enterprise platform with multi-tenant isolation, governance and audit trails USD 70,000-120,000+ (14-22 weeks); running cost USD 300-2,500 a month; data preparation and ingestion are 40-50% of the engineering work. Not Gulf-specific. — [RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/rag-development-cost) (fetched)
- Another vendor range: USD 20,000 for a proof of concept to USD 300,000+ for enterprise deployments. Not Gulf-specific. — [Biz4Group](https://www.biz4group.com/blog/rag-development-services) (snippet)
- Sales-cycle claims from a lead-generation agency's city pages: software-as-a-service in Riyadh 30-90 days; supply-chain technology in Riyadh 60-180 days. Weak source (marketing pages). — [DevCommX](https://www.devcommx.com/abm-agency/riyadh/saas) (snippet)
- McKinsey: well-designed AI transformations can show measurable results within 6-12 months. — [Enterprise AM](https://enterpriseam.com/uae/2025/11/19/gcc-firms-ramp-up-ai-adoption-but-only-11-are-realizing-meaningful-value-mckinsey/) (fetched)
- US Commercial Service (updated 12 May 2026): companies "may experience payment delays for contracts performed for Saudi public- and private-sector entities"; firms are advised not to start work without an executed contract and to use advance payment, letters of credit or export credit insurance. — [trade.gov Saudi Arabia Market Challenges](https://www.trade.gov/knowledge-product/saudi-arabia-market-challenges) (fetched)
- AGBI (Sep 2026): contractors in Saudi Arabia and UAE typically wait 60-90 days for payment, sometimes 180 days on large projects. — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/banking-finance/2026/09/saudi-uae-payment-delays-demand-costly-workarounds/) (snippet; page blocked on fetch)

- Scale of the largest local integrator: solutions by stc, SAR 12,730 million revenue and 220+ contracts signed in 2025, one of them SAR 1.4 billion. — [solutions by stc](https://solutions.com.sa/news/solutions-by-stc-reports-over-5-52-revenue-growth-for-fy-2025/) (snippet)

### Inferences

## 6. Routes open to a small foreign-owned firm, and the real barriers

### Takeaway
Direct government tendering needs a Saudi legal entity plus a stack of certificates, a local-content score and Saudization, which a small foreign firm will score poorly on. The open routes are selling to private companies, and supplying or white-labelling to licensed local firms and integrators that hold the registrations. A MISA licence with 100% foreign ownership is quick to obtain but is the start of the compliance load, not the end.

### Cited Findings
- Regional-headquarters rule (in force since early 2024): state entities may not contract with foreign companies whose regional headquarters is outside Saudi Arabia. Exceptions: contracts of SAR 1 million (USD 266,000) or less; only one technically compliant bid; or a bid that is technically among the best and at least 25% cheaper than the next. Agencies apply for exemptions through an Etimad service launched November 2025. More than 700 multinationals had moved regional headquarters by early 2026 against a target of 500 by 2030. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2633989) (fetched); [Enterprise AM, 24 Feb 2026](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2026/02/24/hq-rule-gets-a-pressure-valve-as-saudi-opens-door-to-strategic-exemptions/) (snippet)
- MISA investment licence allows 100% foreign ownership; typically 3-10 business days; licence fees suspended in 2026 (previously SAR 12,000); structures are LLC, branch or joint venture. Etimad registration is free and takes 2-5 business days but requires a commercial registration, articles of association, Chamber of Commerce, ZATCA, GOSI and Saudization certificates, a bank IBAN letter and an Absher login. The guide describes an eight-week path from licence to first bid. — [NobleCore procurement guide](https://noblecoreksa.com/procurement-law-foreign-firms-saudi/) (fetched; company-formation vendor, so optimistic on timing)
- "One expired certificate is the most common reason a technically strong bid is disqualified"; most public buyers require a compliant Nitaqat (Saudization) band; SME status registered with Monsha'at earns evaluation credit on mid-size tenders. — [NobleCore procurement guide](https://noblecoreksa.com/procurement-law-foreign-firms-saudi/) (fetched)
- Local content: a Local Content Certificate from LCGPA is required for qualifying tenders, is valid 19 months, and local content can carry up to 40% of the commercial score; the score is built from Saudi employment, local sourcing and local R&D and capacity building. — [SetupInSaudi, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.setupinsaudi.com/en/news/success-stories/it-companies-are-queuing-to-enter-saudi-what-the-shortlist-looks-like) (fetched; company-formation vendor)
- Saudization: quotas are "progressively stricter"; a three-tier work-permit system runs through Qiwa; "public procurement regulations now require companies to 'Saudize' project teams as a condition of contract award". — [trade.gov, updated 12 May 2026](https://www.trade.gov/knowledge-product/saudi-arabia-market-challenges) (fetched)
- Subcontracting to Saudi-registered suppliers is allowed and counts toward the prime contractor's local-content score when documented. — [NobleCore procurement guide](https://noblecoreksa.com/procurement-law-foreign-firms-saudi/) (fetched)
- Partner-led channels exist: Google Cloud is bought in the Kingdom through CNTXT's portal; Core42 and Solutions+ use referral and reseller arrangements; HUMAIN fronts the customer while a partner supplies implementation. — [Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/google-cloud-expands-services-in-saudi-arabia-delivering-enhanced-data-sovereignty-and-ai-capabilities?hl=en) (fetched); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c025vvk-core42-solutions+-partner-build-sovereign) (snippet); [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21572125/humain-collabs-with-brain-to-develop-enterprise-ai-solutions-for-saudi-customers) (fetched)

### Inferences
- A prime contractor earns local-content credit for subcontracting to Saudi-registered suppliers, not to a Pakistani entity. So an offshore team is attractive to a Saudi prime on cost and skill, not on scoring; a Saudi-registered vehicle (own MISA entity or a Saudi partner's company) makes him more useful to primes.
- His deal size (about SAR 70,000-100,000) is far under the SAR 1 million threshold, so the regional-headquarters rule itself is not the obstacle on small government work; entity registration, certificates, Saudization and local-content scoring are.
- Private-sector buyers are not bound by Etimad or the headquarters rule, but banks and government-linked companies run their own vendor registration and security review, which in practice also expects a local entity, a Saudi bank account and ZATCA e-invoicing.
- The lowest-friction sequence is: white-label or subcontract delivery to licensed Saudi AI firms and resellers first, private mid-market direct sales second, own MISA entity once there is recurring revenue to justify Saudization and compliance cost.

### Gaps
- No source found on an SDAIA vendor-registration scheme or AI-provider accreditation as a route.
- No primary LCGPA or Etimad text was read; the procurement detail comes from two company-formation vendors and one US government page.
- Agent or distributor arrangements and their legal standing for services were not covered by any source reached.
- Whether private Saudi enterprises will contract a foreign (Pakistan- or UAE-registered) entity for on-premise AI, and withholding-tax treatment, was not researched (licensing detail belongs to another researcher).
- His personal constraint (employed by a Saudi AI company; code inside the employer's organisation) raises non-compete and intellectual-property questions no source here addresses.

## 7. How mature and commoditised the offer is, and what remains scarce

### Takeaway
The basic layer is being commoditised fast in the second half of 2026: ALLaM is on IBM watsonx and now Microsoft Foundry and Copilot, HUMAIN ONE is bundled with Microsoft 365, Cohere North is deployed privately in Riyadh, and all three hyperscalers will have Saudi regions by December 2026.
