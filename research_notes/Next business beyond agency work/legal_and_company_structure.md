Reader notes before the sections:

- Two primary texts were read directly (not through summaries): the MISA English translation of the Anti-Concealment Law, and ZATCA's January 2025 circular on withholding tax under tax treaties.

## 1. Saudi Labour Law and residency rules: may an employee on a work iqama do side work, own a business, or sell to Saudi customers? Penalties? Is employer consent enough?

### Takeaway
No. A work-iqama holder may work only for the employer on his work permit; working "for his own account" inside the Kingdom is a residency violation that carries deportation on the first offence, and the employer's permission does not make it legal. Using a Saudi person or company as a front to run a business is a separate crime (tasattur) with up to 5 years in prison, a fine up to SAR 5 million, and deportation with a ban on return.

### Cited Findings
- Article 39 of the Labour Law contains three prohibitions: an employer may not let his worker work for someone else, a worker may not work for another employer, and an employer may not use another employer's worker, unless the official procedures are followed (article dated 10 September 2026) — [Saudi Life Guide](https://saudilifeguide.com/?p=1751)
- On 25 August 2026 the Ministry of Interior warned expatriates against working for their own account. Stated penalties: first offence SAR 10,000 fine and deportation; second offence SAR 25,000 fine, one month in prison and deportation; third or later offence up to SAR 50,000 fine, up to six months in prison and deportation — [Gulf News, 25 Aug 2026](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-warns-expatriates-of-jail-sr50000-fine-for-working-for-themselves-1.500651242)
- The same warning says people or businesses that "enable expatriates to work for themselves or for employers other than those legally authorised" face fines that can reach SAR 100,000 and prison terms of up to six months — [Gulf News, 25 Aug 2026](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-warns-expatriates-of-jail-sr50000-fine-for-working-for-themselves-1.500651242)
- A second source gives the ceiling penalties as: worker up to SAR 50,000, up to 6 months in prison and deportation; individual employer up to SAR 100,000, up to 6 months and a recruitment ban of up to 5 years; company up to SAR 100,000, up to 1 year in prison for the responsible manager, recruitment ban up to 5 years, and deportation of the manager if he is an expatriate — [Saudi Life Guide](https://saudilifeguide.com/?p=1751)
- Employer consent is not enough. "Permission from your sponsor protects nobody. A WhatsApp message saying it is fine does not make the arrangement lawful." The only documented legal routes to work for a second party are an Ajeer permit (temporary work authorisation through the MHRSD platform) or a formal transfer of services — [Saudi Life Guide](https://saudilifeguide.com/?p=1751)
- The Ministry of Human Resources (MHRSD) violations table published for consultation in June 2025 lists: employee working for another employer, SAR 5,000; employer allowing a worker to work for others, SAR 10,000 to 20,000; employing a foreign worker without a permit, SAR 10,000 per worker — [Middle East Briefing, 10 Jun 2025](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/saudi-arabia-revises-labor-law-penalties/)
- DLA Piper (22 December 2025) confirms the updated table is in force for employers: "Employers who permit their workers to leave and work for other parties now face penalties between SAR10,000 and SAR20,000." — [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/Saudi-Arabia-updates-Labour-Law)
- Labour Law amendments under Royal Decree M/44 of 1446 AH took effect on 19 February 2025; they made no explicit change to Article 83 (non-compete) or Article 65 (duties during employment) — [Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/learn/non-compete-confidentiality-clause-saudi-labor-law/)
- One source reports that the sponsorship (kafala) system was abolished in June 2025, which made moving between employers easier, but "the permit requirement did not disappear and work permits still must match the employer actually worked for" (date not verified against an official source) — [Saudi Life Guide](https://saudilifeguide.com/?p=1751)
- Anti-Concealment Law (Royal Decree M/4, 20 August 2020), Article 1: "Economic Activity" means "any for-profit activity that requires approvals or licenses from the competent authorities, be it an investment or service provision activity, or commercial, professional, industrial, agricultural, or otherwise" — [MISA translation of the Anti-Concealment Law](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Anti-Concealment-Law.pdf)
- Article 2: concealment is "an agreement or arrangement whereby a person enables a non-Saudi to practice an economic activity in the Kingdom that he is not licensed to practice by using the license or approval issued in his favor." Article 3(b) makes it an offence for "a non-Saudi who practices in the Kingdom an economic activity he is not licensed to practice, for his benefit, through a person who enables him to do so" — [MISA translation](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Anti-Concealment-Law.pdf)
- Article 9: imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to SAR 5 million, or either; doubled for a repeat offence within three years. Article 10: proceeds are confiscated. Article 11(2): a convicted non-Saudi is deported after serving the sentence and paying the fines "and shall not be permitted to return." Article 12: the entity is dissolved, the licence and commercial registration revoked, and the Saudi convict is banned from commercial activity for five years. Article 15: any contract whose purpose is concealment is void — [MISA translation](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Anti-Concealment-Law.pdf)
- Article 4 creates lesser violations: a non-Saudi "illegally possessing or using any means which allow him to have unrestricted authority over an entity", and an entity "using a bank account other than its own for its economic activity transactions". Article 14: fine up to SAR 500,000 and/or closure for up to 90 days — [MISA translation](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Anti-Concealment-Law.pdf)
- Informants can receive up to 30% of the fine collected after final judgment — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1400287)
- A Saudi incubator's guide states that "foreigners practicing business under the guise of 'freelancing' without a formal investment license is a serious violation of the Anti-Concealment Law" — [Motaded, April 2026](https://motaded.com.sa/blog/saudi-freelance-regulations-guide)

### Inferences
- Employer consent helps in only two ways: (a) the employer itself signs the customer contract and he does the work as an employee (possibly with a commission), or (b) the employer lends him to another Saudi-licensed establishment through Ajeer. It cannot authorise him to sell in his own name or for his own company inside Saudi Arabia, and an employer who knowingly allows own-account work is itself exposed to the SAR 10,000 to 20,000 fine.
- The sources talk about activity carried out in the Kingdom. None of them says that simply holding shares in a company registered in Pakistan, the UAE or the US is a violation. The risk comes from what he personally does from Riyadh: pitching, signing, invoicing and delivering for his own business looks like "working for his own account". Quietly owning an offshore company whose team and clients are outside Saudi Arabia is a grey area; selling face to face to Saudi clinics while on an employer iqama is the clearest breach.
- The tasattur crime needs a Saudi "enabler". Any plan where a Saudi friend or a Saudi company invoices customers on his behalf, while he really runs and benefits from the business, fits Article 3 exactly. A broad power of attorney over a Saudi's establishment, or running sales through someone else's bank account, fits the Article 4 violations.
- Being "the public face" on LinkedIn and at Saudi events for his own agency creates evidence of own-account work even if contracts are signed abroad.

### Gaps
- The Arabic text of the residency regulation behind the Ministry of Interior warning, and the final MHRSD violations table, were not retrieved. The SAR 5,000 (MHRSD) and SAR 10,000 (Interior) first-offence figures come from different instruments and may both apply.
- No source found that states whether a work-iqama holder may be a passive shareholder or director of a foreign company, or do remote work for foreign clients in his free time. This needs a written opinion from a Saudi labour lawyer.
- His own employment contract (exclusivity, outside-activities, IP and non-compete clauses) was not available. It may be stricter than the law.
- How often individual professionals (as opposed to shops and labourers) are actually prosecuted for own-account work could not be established.

## 2. Routes that let a foreigner legally own a business in Saudi Arabia in 2026 (MISA, Premium Residency, Saudi partner) and what the 2025 Investment Law changed

### Takeaway
There are three lawful routes: register as a foreign investor with MISA (the ordinary route expects an existing foreign company with a track record; the entrepreneur licence replaces that with a support letter from a recognised Saudi incubator, accelerator or VC), obtain Premium Residency (which carries the right to do business), or be a real, registered shareholder alongside a Saudi partner. For someone without a finished degree and without a foreign parent company, the realistic options are the entrepreneur licence (needs an innovative product, not a plain services agency) or paying SAR 100,000 a year for limited-duration Premium Residency.

### Cited Findings
**Investment Law (new regime)**
- The new Investment Law was issued by Royal Decree M/19 (11 August 2024); its Implementing Regulations were issued by Ministerial Decision 1086 on 7 February 2025 — [Clyde & Co, Sept 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/09/ksa-investment-law-implementing-regulations)
- Foreign investors "are no longer required to obtain a MISA License and pay license fees"; they register with MISA in a national investor register, and one registration can cover several sectors — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/09/ksa-investment-law-implementing-regulations)
- Natural persons now qualify as foreign investors, alongside companies — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/09/ksa-investment-law-implementing-regulations)
- "Foreign investors will receive the same treatment as local investors under comparable circumstances"; a list of excluded or restricted activities is kept by a screening committee and was to be published in an Investor Guide that had not been released when the article was written; capital requirements remain for specific activities — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/09/ksa-investment-law-implementing-regulations)

**Ordinary MISA route (services company)**
- Service licence requirements as listed in April 2025: "one-year operational history outside Saudi Arabia; minimum capital of SAR 25,000; submission of notarized commercial registration and certified financials"; 100% foreign ownership allowed; MISA annual fee SAR 2,000; processing typically 2 to 4 days once documents are complete — [Middle East Briefing, 11 Apr 2025](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/types-of-business-licenses-in-saudi-arabia-2025/)
- A December 2025 guide still lists a "certificate of incorporation from the foreign parent company" and "audited financial statements for the most recent fiscal year" as required documents, gives a minimum capital of "SAR 500,000 for 100% foreign-owned LLCs", and a timeline of 1 to 3 months — [Emerhub, Dec 2025](https://emerhub.com/saudi-arabia/setting-up-a-business-saudi-arabia-for-foreigners/); this conflicts with the SAR 25,000 figure in [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/types-of-business-licenses-in-saudi-arabia-2025/)
- Saudization: companies with five or fewer employees need at least one Saudi employee; a registered national address is mandatory — [Emerhub](https://emerhub.com/saudi-arabia/setting-up-a-business-saudi-arabia-for-foreigners/)
- Trading (commercial) activity with 100% foreign ownership needs SAR 30 million capital and presence in at least three countries; a joint venture route needs at least 25% Saudi shareholding and SAR 26.6 million — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/types-of-business-licenses-in-saudi-arabia-2025/)

**Entrepreneur licence**
- The entrepreneur licence needs no established parent company, no audit reports and no positive cash flow; founders can set up in their own names. It requires a support letter from a VC or angel group (when investing), an incubator or an accelerator. Approval takes about one week — [Wamda, 2 Sep 2024](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2024/09/tawaref-series-saudi-entrepreneurship-licence-glance)
- Eligibility is limited to innovative tech startups; "traditional IT companies and microbusinesses are excluded" — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2024/09/tawaref-series-saudi-entrepreneurship-licence-glance)
- Fees reported in 2024: SAR 2,000 a year for years 1 to 3 (SAR 60,000 of the standard fee waived), SAR 12,000 for years 4 and 5, SAR 62,000 from year 6 — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2024/09/tawaref-series-saudi-entrepreneurship-licence-glance)
- 2026 cost estimate: MISA licence fee "suspended" (previously SAR 12,000 issuance and SAR 62,000 renewal); annual activity fee about SAR 2,000 or more; commercial registration SAR 1,200 to 2,000; chamber membership SAR 2,000 to 3,000 a year; total first-year government charges roughly SAR 5,200 to 8,000 before professional fees; one provider's bundled service starts at SAR 36,999. MISA approval 3 to 7 business days; realistic end-to-end time 4 to 8 weeks — [Noble Core, 2026](https://noblecoreksa.com/entrepreneur-license-saudi-arabia/)
- Endorsing bodies named: The Garage, university hubs, Monsha'at, Misk, Flat6Labs and MISA-recognised VC funds; the approved list changes and should be checked before committing — [Noble Core](https://noblecoreksa.com/entrepreneur-license-saudi-arabia/)
- KAUST's TAQADAM accelerator issues MISA support letters only to startups actively in its programme or graduated from it — [KAUST TAQADAM](https://hubspot.kaust.edu.sa/taqadam/misa-support-letter)
- After the licence and commercial registration are issued the founder "can sponsor a General Manager / investor visa and Iqama for yourself through Muqeem and Absher"; the company must then register with the Chamber, ZATCA, GOSI and Qiwa before invoicing and hiring — [Noble Core](https://noblecoreksa.com/entrepreneur-license-saudi-arabia/)

**Premium Residency**
- Special Talent, researchers: employment contract, minimum salary SAR 14,000 a month, bachelor's degree or higher, more than three years' experience, at least three publications, minimum points score, employer recommendation letter — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Special Talent, healthcare and scientific professionals: minimum salary SAR 35,000 a month, bachelor's degree or higher, more than three years' experience, minimum points score, employer recommendation letter — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Special Talent, executives: first or second level executive contract with total monthly wage of at least SAR 80,000 and an employer recommendation letter; no degree requirement is listed for this sub-track — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Entrepreneur residency, category 1: MISA entrepreneur licence, at least SAR 400,000 of investment from an accredited entity, a recommendation letter from that entity, and at least 20% ownership; five-year renewable residency. Category 2: at least SAR 15 million raised, 10% ownership, 10 jobs in year one and 10 more in year two; direct permanent residency — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Investor residency: MISA registration and at least SAR 7 million invested, with 10 jobs created in the first two years — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Fee for the category products (Special Talent, Gifted, Investor, Entrepreneur, Real Estate Owner): SAR 4,000 one-time. Special Talent and Entrepreneur category 1 are issued for five years; permanent residency is possible after 30 months of residence within five years — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Limited-duration Premium Residency: SAR 100,000 per year, requires only proof of financial capability and a valid passport; unlimited-duration: SAR 800,000 one-time. No degree requirement is listed for either — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)
- Rights attached: live in Saudi Arabia with family, work in the private sector and move between establishments, "own/conduct business", own real estate, exit and re-enter without a visa, and exemption from expatriate and dependant fees — [Centuro Global](https://www.centuroglobal.com/article/saudi-premium-residency/)

**Saudi partner**
- A Saudi national lending his name, licence or commercial register so that a non-Saudi can run an unlicensed activity is the core tasattur offence (Article 3(a)); the non-Saudi commits the matching offence under Article 3(b) — [MISA translation of the Anti-Concealment Law](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Anti-Concealment-Law.pdf)

### Inferences
- A partnership with a Saudi national is lawful only when the foreigner appears as a registered shareholder (which itself needs MISA registration). A handshake or side agreement under which the Saudi partner holds 100% on paper is tasattur.
- For this person in October 2026: the Special Talent professional and researcher tracks are closed until the degree is finished (August 2027) because both list a bachelor's degree; the executive track needs a SAR 80,000 monthly wage; the entrepreneur track needs an entrepreneur licence plus SAR 400,000 from an accredited investor; the only route with no degree, salary or investor condition is the paid one at SAR 100,000 per year.
- An AI "services agency" may be refused the entrepreneur licence because traditional IT service firms are excluded. A product company (for example a clinic AI product with its own IP) fits the criteria better.
- Any Saudi entity brings fixed running costs that are heavy at under USD 10,000 a month revenue: at least one Saudi employee, an office or national address, GOSI, ZATCA filings, and corporate income tax on the foreign-owned share (20% is the rate quoted for a permanent establishment in section 4).
- An investor or general-manager iqama under his own company would replace his employer-sponsored iqama. Keeping the job and holding an investor iqama at the same time does not look possible on these sources, whereas Premium Residency explicitly allows both employment and business.

### Gaps
- It is unclear whether, under the registration regime, MISA still asks an individual applicant (not a company) for a foreign commercial registration and audited accounts. Clyde & Co says natural persons can register; provider guides from 2025 still list parent-company documents. Ask MISA or a setup firm in writing.
- The minimum capital for a 100% foreign-owned services LLC is disputed (SAR 25,000 vs SAR 500,000).
- The exact date the new Investment Law came into force was not cleanly confirmed (the decree date and the regulations date are sourced above).
- Whether a person already inside Saudi Arabia on an employee iqama can apply for the entrepreneur licence without first leaving the employer, and whether an employer no-objection letter is needed, was not answered by any source.
- The official Premium Residency Center site (pr.gov.sa) could not be read by the tool; the figures above come from an immigration adviser's summary. A January 2026 news headline suggests new entry tiers were being considered ([Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2026/01/27/the-kingdom-considers-usd-30-mn-entry-tier-for-premium-residency/)); the article was not read, so check for 2026 changes.
- The Special Talent points system and the list of "priority specialisations" (whether AI engineering counts) were not found.

## 3. Is the Saudi freelance permit (freelance.sa) open to non-Saudis? Any 2025 to 2026 changes?

### Takeaway
No. The freelance document is for Saudi citizens only, and no source found shows it was opened to expatriates in 2025 or 2026.

### Cited Findings
- "Saudi citizens aged between 18 and 60 years are entitled to apply for the freelance document." It was created by MHRSD Ministerial Decree No. 99439 dated 15/05/1439 AH, is valid for one year and renewable — [Motaded, April 2026](https://motaded.com.sa/blog/saudi-freelance-regulations-guide)
- The freelance document is reserved for Saudi citizens, while entrepreneur licences are the designated route for non-Saudis — [Motaded](https://motaded.com.sa/blog/saudi-freelance-regulations-guide)
- A 2026 contractor guide likewise states the official freelance licence is available only to Saudi nationals, and that expatriates must instead set up a company or work under an entity with a valid commercial registration — [Mellow, 2026](https://mellow.io/news-and-articles/hire-independent-contractor-saudi-arabia-2026-7394)
- The routes offered to non-Saudis are the MISA entrepreneur licence (support letter from a certified incubator; project must be innovative and scalable) and the Premium Residency entrepreneur path — [Motaded](https://motaded.com.sa/blog/saudi-freelance-regulations-guide)

### Inferences
- A Saudi clinic or company that pays him personally as a "freelancer" would be paying an unlicensed person; both sides carry risk (see section 1).

### Gaps
- freelance.sa itself was not loaded; the finding rests on two secondary sources dated 2026 that agree with each other.
- Whether Premium Residency holders can obtain a freelance document was not found.

## 4. Can a foreign company with no Saudi entity legally sell software and AI services to Saudi private companies from abroad? Withholding tax, VAT, permanent establishment, e-invoicing, regional headquarters rule

### Takeaway
Yes, a foreign company can sell cross-border to Saudi private companies without a Saudi entity; the Saudi customer withholds tax (5% for technical and consulting services even if all work is done abroad, 15% for royalties and software licences) and self-accounts for 15% VAT. The real danger is him: a person living in Saudi Arabia who negotiates and signs for the foreign company can give that company a taxable presence (permanent establishment) in Saudi Arabia, on top of the residency violation in section 1.

### Cited Findings
**Withholding tax (WHT)**
- ZATCA's January 2025 circular lists as subject to WHT: "Technical or consulting services, even when they are entirely performed abroad" and "services performed in whole or in part in the KSA". Its footnote adds: "Payments towards technical and consulting services in nature would be subject to WHT regardless of the place of performance of such services." — [ZATCA Tax Circular, WHT under Double Taxation Agreements, Jan 2025](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Tax-Circular-WHT-Implementation-Under-Double-Taxation-Agreement.PDF)
- Rates in the same circular: management fees 20%; royalties 15%; technical and consulting services 5%; rent, dividends, loan returns, insurance, airline tickets and international telecom 5%; any other services from Saudi sources 15% — [ZATCA Tax Circular](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Tax-Circular-WHT-Implementation-Under-Double-Taxation-Agreement.PDF)
- Ministerial Resolution No. 25 (26 July 2023) unified technical and consulting services at 5% whether the provider is a related party or not (it was 15% for related parties before) — [Mondaq, 1 Apr 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/withholding-tax/1581596/withholding-tax)
- Conflict: the same Mondaq article says services performed entirely outside the Kingdom are outside WHT — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/withholding-tax/1581596/withholding-tax); contradicted for technical and consulting services by the [ZATCA Tax Circular](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Tax-Circular-WHT-Implementation-Under-Double-Taxation-Agreement.PDF). The ZATCA text should be treated as the authority's position.
- "Software licence fees for on-premise software are royalties at 15%... SaaS subscriptions are genuinely ambiguous, and the conservative approach is 15% absent a well-reasoned service analysis." — [Dariba WHT FAQ, 28 May 2026](https://www.dariba.co/?p=299)
- The Saudi payer must remit WHT within the first ten days of the month after payment; a 1% penalty applies per 30 days of delay; if the payer fails to withhold, ZATCA assesses the payer for the full amount — [PwC Tax Summaries, reviewed 29 Jul 2026](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/saudi-arabia/corporate/withholding-taxes); [Dariba WHT FAQ](https://www.dariba.co/?p=299)
- Treaty relief: under the business-profits article of a tax treaty, the right to tax belongs to the provider's home state unless the provider has a permanent establishment in Saudi Arabia. ZATCA's own example is a treaty-country company providing online consultancy to a Saudi resident with no PE: the residence state has the taxing right — [ZATCA Tax Circular](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Tax-Circular-WHT-Implementation-Under-Double-Taxation-Agreement.PDF)
- To use a treaty at source, the Saudi payer applies on the ZATCA portal with the supplier's tax residency certificate (legalised by the Saudi embassy or apostilled), the treaty application form, and an undertaking (Form Q/7C). Otherwise the payer withholds and a refund is claimed. The time limit is five years — [ZATCA Tax Circular](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Tax-Circular-WHT-Implementation-Under-Double-Taxation-Agreement.PDF)
- Saudi Arabia has tax treaties with both Pakistan (royalties 10%) and the UAE (royalties 10%) — [PwC Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/saudi-arabia/corporate/withholding-taxes)

**VAT and e-invoicing**
- Saudi VAT is 15%. When a non-resident supplies services to a VAT-registered Saudi business, the Saudi customer accounts for the VAT under the reverse charge and the supplier invoices without Saudi VAT — [Dariba, non-resident suppliers](https://www.dariba.co/non-resident-suppliers-and-vat-obligations/)
- If the Saudi customer is not VAT-registered (consumers, very small businesses), the reverse charge cannot work and the non-resident supplier must register for Saudi VAT, through a ZATCA-approved Saudi-resident tax representative who is jointly liable — [Dariba](https://www.dariba.co/non-resident-suppliers-and-vat-obligations/)
- There is no registration threshold for a foreign supplier selling to non-registered customers; registration is required from the first such supply (seen through a search summary) — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/saudi-arabia/vat-registration-for-foreign-companies); [PayPro Global](https://payproglobal.com/saas-sales-tax/saudi-arabia/)
- Non-resident suppliers are currently outside the mandatory Fatoora e-invoicing phases 1 and 2 (seen through a search summary; confirm on ZATCA's e-invoicing pages) — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/saudi-arabia/vat-registration-for-foreign-companies); [PayPro Global](https://payproglobal.com/saas-sales-tax/saudi-arabia/)

**Permanent establishment (PE)**
- Under Article 4 of the Income Tax Law a PE arises from a fixed place of business or from a dependent agent who can negotiate or conclude contracts for the non-resident. A Saudi-based individual negotiating and signing contracts for a foreign company is "a classic dependent agent PE situation — particularly where that employee is the primary point of commercial contact in the Kingdom." — [Dariba, PE in Saudi Arabia](https://www.dariba.co/?p=264)
- Consequences of a PE: 20% corporate income tax on profits attributable to it, a registration duty, annual filing within 120 days of year end, and penalties — [Dariba](https://www.dariba.co/?p=264)
- Service PE under most Saudi treaties: services furnished through employees or other personnel physically present in Saudi Arabia for more than 183 days in any 12 months for the same or a connected project. ZATCA's May 2023 circular dropped the old "virtual service PE" idea and requires physical presence — [EY, 31 May 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/saudi-arabia-clarifies-interpretation-of-service-pe-provisions-i); [KPMG, May 2023](https://kpmg.com/sa/en/home/insights/2023/05/tax-alert-updates-on-the-concept-of-service-pe.html)

**Regional headquarters (RHQ) rule**
- From 1 January 2024 foreign companies without a regional headquarters in Saudi Arabia were barred from government contracts; contracts under SAR 1 million were exempt — [Vistra](https://www.vistra.com/insights/what-you-need-know-about-saudi-arabias-local-headquarters-rule)
- On 21 February 2026 Saudi Arabia introduced an exception-request process on the Etimad platform: a company without an RHQ can win only if it submits the only technically compliant bid or is at least 25% cheaper than the next bidder after evaluation; projects of SAR 1 million or less are exempt. The rule covers "all government entities, institutions, funds, and affiliated authorities" — [Gulf News, 21 Feb 2026](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/new-rule-saudi-arabia-reverses-restriction-allows-foreign-companies-in-government-projects-1.500451195)
- An RHQ licence needs at least 15 full-time staff by the end of year one, including three or more senior executives — [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/types-of-business-licenses-in-saudi-arabia-2025/)

### Inferences
- For private-sector Saudi customers the RHQ rule does not matter; it only limits government work, and small government jobs under SAR 1 million are outside it.
- Pricing to Saudi customers should assume 5% WHT on services (or 15% if the deal is written as a software licence or SaaS), unless the selling company can give a tax residency certificate from Pakistan or the UAE and the customer is willing to file the treaty claim. Contracts should say who bears the WHT and that invoices are exclusive of Saudi VAT.
- Many small private clinics may not be VAT-registered. Selling to them directly from abroad would push the VAT registration duty onto his foreign company. Selling through a Saudi reseller (for example his employer) avoids this.
- Because he lives in Saudi Arabia all year, he is physically present well beyond 183 days. If he sells, negotiates or delivers for his foreign company from Riyadh, ZATCA could treat the company as having a Saudi PE (20% tax plus registration), and a treaty will not protect a company that has a PE. The safe pattern is that someone outside Saudi Arabia (or a licensed Saudi reseller) does the selling and signing for Saudi deals.

### Gaps

## 6. Pakistan structure: PSEB registration, IT export tax regime, foreign currency accounts, receiving payments, staff, and tax position of a non-resident owner

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
**Tax regime**

## 8. Employer conflict: non-compete and confidentiality (Article 83), ownership of work made during employment, and what he needs to sell or reuse a system built for his employer

### Takeaway
Work he built as an employee, relating to the employer's business, belongs to the employer by default under both the patent rules and the new Copyright Law (in force 12 August 2026). He cannot reuse or sell that code without a written licence, assignment or reseller agreement from the employer. A non-compete binds him after leaving only if it is written and limited in time (maximum two years), place and type of work.

### Cited Findings
**Non-compete and confidentiality**
- Article 83: if the work lets the worker know the employer's customers, the employer may require him in the contract not to compete after the contract ends. To be valid the clause must be in writing and specific in time, place and type of work, and may not exceed two years from the end of the relationship — [Etqan Law Firm](https://etqanlawfirm-sa.com/en/article-83-saudi-labor-law/); [Argaam](https://argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1427605)
- Article 83 also allows a clause against revealing the employer's business secrets after the contract ends, on the same conditions (written; specific in time, place and type of work) — [Argaam](https://argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1427605)
- The employer must file a claim within one year from the date he discovers the breach — [Etqan Law Firm](https://etqanlawfirm-sa.com/en/article-83-saudi-labor-law/)
- Article 65 separately requires workers to keep the employer's secrets during employment; Article 83 deals with the period after employment. The 2025 Labour Law amendments (Royal Decree M/44, effective 19 February 2025) made no explicit change to either article. The law sets no fixed compensation amount for breach — [Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/learn/non-compete-confidentiality-clause-saudi-labor-law/)
- A non-compete cannot be imposed on roles that have no access to clients or confidential business information, and it must protect a genuine commercial interest — [Etqan Law Firm](https://etqanlawfirm-sa.com/en/article-83-saudi-labor-law/)
- In October 2020 MHRSD said it was reviewing Article 83 to fit the labour mobility reforms while protecting employers' proprietary information — [Argaam, 12 Oct 2020](https://argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1427605) (older item)

**Ownership of inventions and code**
- Patents: the employer owns the protection document, unless the employment contract says otherwise, where the invention results from carrying out the contract, or where the employer proves the employee would not have developed it without the facilities, means or data made available through his employment. The employee keeps a right to a special award, agreed or set by a committee. A patent application filed by the employee within two years after leaving is treated as filed during employment — [Saudi Law of Patents, JPO copy](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/system/laws/gaikoku/document/index/saudi_arabia-e_tokkyo.pdf); [AGIP country guide](https://www.agip.com/Agip-Country-Service/en/120/p) (content seen through a search summary; the law text is older, check for amendments)
- New Copyright Law: Royal Decree M/169 of 2 February 2026 (Cabinet Decision 560/1447 of 27 January 2026), in force 12 August 2026 — [Baker McKenzie, July 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- It introduces a statutory work-for-hire rule: "Works created by employees in the course of their employment, and which relate to the employer's business, will belong to the employer by default." Works outside the employer's business stay with the employee. Both defaults can be changed by contract — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- The rule sits in Article 18: where the employee's work relates to the employer's activities the author's rights vest in the employer unless agreed otherwise; where it does not, the employee keeps the financial rights unless agreed otherwise (article number seen through a search summary) — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/saudi-arabias-new-copyright-law-key-changes-and-implications); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/copyright/1751404/saudi-arabias-new-copyright-law-key-changes-and-practical-steps-for-businesses)
- Commissioned works (work done by an outside contractor): ownership stays with the author unless the parties expressly agree otherwise in writing — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- Software licences are now expressly enforceable by statute, including click-wrap licences; registration with the Saudi Authority for Intellectual Property (SAIP) creates a rebuttable presumption of ownership; maximum penalties rose and are doubled for repeat offences — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)

**Working through the employer**
- An employer who lets his worker work for others or for his own account faces a SAR 10,000 to 20,000 fine, so informal "do your own deals on the side" permission exposes the employer as well — [DLA Piper, 22 Dec 2025](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/Saudi-Arabia-updates-Labour-Law)

### Inferences
- The systems in his employer's repositories are the employer's property on three grounds: the contract (very likely), the copyright work-for-hire default, and confidentiality duties. Copying that code, its prompts, data or architecture documents into his own company's product would expose him to an IP claim and a breach-of-secrets claim, and a criminal complaint could put his residency at risk.
- What he may freely take is general skill and know-how, and anything he rebuilds from scratch without the employer's code, data or confidential designs. A clean-room rebuild should be documented (new repository, dated commits, no copied files).
- To sell the employer's system lawfully he needs one of these in writing, signed by an authorised signatory:
  1. A licence or reseller agreement from the employer to a licensed company (for example his Pakistani or UAE company) allowing it to resell or white-label the system outside Saudi Arabia, with price or revenue share, territory, and who owns improvements. Payments from the Saudi employer to his foreign company would be subject to Saudi withholding tax (section 4).
  2. A sales commission or bonus written into his employment contract, so that he sells the employer's product to Saudi and Gulf customers as an employee and is paid through payroll. This is the only arrangement that is clearly lawful for selling inside Saudi Arabia on his current iqama.
  3. An assignment or carve-out letter confirming that specified projects, or work done outside working hours and unrelated to the employer's business, belong to him.
- A revenue-share deal between the employer and him personally (not through payroll and not through a licensed company) would look like own-account work. It should be structured as salary and commission, or as a company-to-company contract.
- If his contract has a non-compete, it bites after he leaves, for up to two years, and only within the stated place and type of work. Running a competing business while still employed is a separate problem under the duty of loyalty and confidentiality during employment and can justify dismissal.

### Gaps
- No Saudi-specific source was found describing how commission, revenue-share or reseller arrangements between an employee and employer are usually written. ## 9. Health data: compliance when the company processes patient data for US, UK or Gulf clinics from Pakistan

### Takeaway
Processing patient data from Pakistan is lawful for US and UK clinics if the right contracts are in place (a HIPAA business associate agreement; UK transfer clauses plus a risk assessment), but many US buyers forbid offshore data by contract and several US states restrict it. For Gulf clinics it is much harder: UAE law keeps health data inside the UAE by default, and Saudi law restricts transfers of sensitive health data abroad.

### Cited Findings
**United States (HIPAA)**
- HIPAA itself does not prohibit offshore processing or storage of protected health information (PHI). It requires a business associate agreement (BAA) between the clinic (covered entity) and any vendor handling PHI, and the vendor's own subcontractors must sign BAAs with the same restrictions — [McDermott Will & Emery, 1 Jul 2025](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=322505)
- HIPAA obligations attach to a foreign vendor through the BAA whenever it creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI for a US covered entity or business associate; geography does not remove them — [Accountable HQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-and-international-baas-a-practical-guide-to-cross-border-compliance)
- Medicare Advantage: CMS requires plans to collect details of offshore vendors' safeguards and a signed attestation (42 C.F.R. § 422.503) — [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=322505)
- State limits: Wisconsin and Texas require certain services and data storage inside the US; Florida requires electronic health records to be kept in the continental US, its territories or Canada; Arizona, Ohio, Missouri and New Jersey have regulatory or executive-order restrictions; Texas Senate Bill 1188 (pending when the article was written) would require health records to be stored in the US — [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=322505)
- "Many payers and provider networks impose stricter terms than law requires", prohibiting PHI from leaving US territory. Recommended safeguards: minimum-necessary access, encryption, breach reporting timelines, cyber liability insurance, arbitration clauses and annual audits — [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=322505)
- Example of a hospital BAA clause (seen through a search summary): neither the business associate nor any subcontractor "shall store, process or otherwise handle PHI outside of the United States" — [Covenant Health BAA](https://www.covenanthealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Business-Associate-Agreement-vers.-9.7.2021-002.pdf)
- US regulators may lack jurisdiction to act directly against an offshore subcontractor after a breach (seen through a search summary; attribution between these two sources not confirmed) — [HHS Office of Inspector General](https://oig.hhs.gov/oei/reports/oei-09-12-00530.asp); [Medtrade](https://medtrade.com/news/subcontracting-with-offshore-company/144741/)

**United Kingdom (UK GDPR)**
- Sending personal data to a country without UK adequacy regulations is a restricted transfer. It needs an appropriate safeguard, namely the International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA), the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses, or binding corporate rules, or else a narrow exception — [ICO international transfers guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/international-transfers-a-guide/)
- The exporter must also complete a transfer risk assessment showing "that the standard of protection for people's information is not materially lower" after the transfer — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/international-transfers-a-guide/)
- The risk assessment looks at the destination country's rules on third-party and government access to data — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/update-international-data-transfer-agreement-and-addendum-replace-sccs)

**Saudi Arabia (PDPL)**
- The Personal Data Protection Law became enforceable on 14 September 2024 and applies to personal data of Saudi residents even when processed by entities outside the Kingdom — [King & Spalding, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/international-personal-data-transfers-under-saudi-arabias-data-protection-law)
- A transfer abroad needs a permitted purpose and safeguards. SDAIA has not yet published its list of adequate countries, so transfers rely on Saudi standard contractual clauses (with controller-to-processor and processor-to-processor modules), binding corporate rules (within a group only) or accreditation certificates — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/international-personal-data-transfers-under-saudi-arabias-data-protection-law); [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/saudi-arabia-updates-data-transfer-regulations-and-introduces-the-first-set-of-standard-contractual-clauses)
- Health information is sensitive data. Transfers of sensitive data are not allowed under the "direct benefit or service to the data subject" and "scientific research" transfer purposes — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/international-personal-data-transfers-under-saudi-arabias-data-protection-law)
- A risk assessment is required before any transfer that relies on safeguards such as SCCs, or that involves sensitive data on a continuous or large-scale basis; SDAIA issued its risk assessment guideline in February 2025 — [Clyde & Co, Mar 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/update-on-saudi-arabia-risk-assessment-guidelines)

**United Arab Emirates**
- Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 prohibits by default storing, processing, generating or transferring outside the UAE any health data relating to health services provided in the UAE. Ministerial Resolution 51 of 2021 lists ten permitted exceptions (for example pharmacovigilance, scientific research, insurance claims, wearables). Penalties for breaching the localisation rule range from AED 500,000 to AED 700,000 — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/united-arab-emirates-health-data-law-permitted-transfers-of-health-data); [Taylor Wessing, 3 Aug 2021](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2021/08/me-health-data-in-the-uae-how-the-health-data-law-works-what-it-protects-and-what-exemptions-exist)

### Inferences
- US clinics: he needs a BAA with each clinic (or with the software vendor he works under), written security policies, access controls and training for the Pakistan team, and BAAs with his own subcontractors. The practical barrier is the buyer's contract and state law, not HIPAA: expect "no PHI outside the US" clauses. The workable design is to keep PHI in US-hosted systems and give the Pakistan team de-identified data or tightly controlled, logged remote access, where the clinic's contract allows it.
- UK clinics: the clinic stays the controller and his company is a processor. He should expect to sign a processor agreement plus the IDTA or Addendum, and to help the clinic complete a transfer risk assessment for Pakistan. No source found lists Pakistan as covered by UK adequacy regulations, so the safeguards route should be assumed.
- UAE clinics: patient data should stay on UAE-hosted systems; a Pakistan-based team touching identifiable patient data is likely to breach the localisation rule unless a listed exception or health-authority approval applies. This favours products deployed inside the clinic's own UAE environment.
- Saudi clinics: the PDPL reaches his company even with no Saudi entity. Sending identifiable patient data to Pakistan would need Saudi SCCs and a documented risk assessment at minimum, and the restriction on sensitive data makes in-Kingdom hosting with no offshore access to identifiable data the safer design.
- Across all four markets, a product that runs inside the customer's own cloud region and exposes only de-identified data to the Pakistan team removes most of the transfer problem and is a selling point.

### Gaps
- No source was retrieved on Pakistan's own data protection law or its status; UK and Saudi risk assessments would need this.
- Whether the UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changed the transfer test, and the UK special-category (health data) conditions and processor contract rules, were not covered by the page read.
- Saudi health-sector specific rules (Ministry of Health, Saudi Health Council, National Cybersecurity Authority cloud controls, any in-Kingdom hosting duty for health records) were not researched.
- UAE free zone regimes (DIFC and ADGM data protection laws) and emirate health authority rules (Dubai Health Authority, Abu Dhabi Department of Health) were not researched.
- PDPL penalty amounts and SDAIA enforcement practice against foreign processors were not captured.
- HIPAA Security Rule technical requirements, and the cost of a compliance programme or audit (for example SOC 2 or HITRUST), were not researched.
