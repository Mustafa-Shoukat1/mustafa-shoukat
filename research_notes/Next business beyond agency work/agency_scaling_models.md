# Agency scaling models: moving an offshore project agency to recurring revenue (evidence as of 3 October 2026)

Source labels used below: **[P]** primary or official (company release, original report, press release). **[B]** broker, analyst or benchmark publisher. **[S]** self-reported by a founder, not audited. **[V]** vendor, affiliate, coaching or course-seller content, treat as marketing. **[A]** aggregator or secondary blog summarising someone else's data. **[F]** practitioner framework or opinion, used for ideas and not for numbers. **[Old]** published before 2025. "Not opened" means the figure came from a search-result summary and the page itself was blocked or not fetched, so the attribution to that exact page is less certain.

Research limits: the session's web-search allowance ran out part-way through, and several pages returned HTTP 403 (Fiverr newsroom, Staffing Industry Analysts, Indie Hackers, Upwork help centre fee page, one beehiiv newsletter). Where that affected a finding it is flagged.

## 1. What are the main models and their economics (productised vertical service, managed AI retainers, white-label, micro-SaaS, licensing or revenue share, staff augmentation)?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced routes are a narrow productised or managed service sold on a monthly fee (normal outcome: 13 to 22% net margin, 20 to 30% of clients lost per year) and, over several years, a product funded by that service cash flow. White-label reselling and revenue-share deals rest almost entirely on vendor marketing or old data, and staff augmentation has the clearest price points for a low-cost team but the least differentiation.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Vertical productised service with a monthly fee**
- DesignJoy (one person, flat monthly design subscription) charges about USD 4,995 to 7,995 per client per month and is reported at anywhere from USD 1.3M to "over USD 3M" a year depending on the source and year; tools cost under USD 200 a month. All figures are founder-reported, and the sources conflict. [S] — [Indie Hackers: "hit 50k MRR"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/my-solo-design-service-just-hit-50k-mrr-cbf399cd78); [Indie Hackers: "0 to 120k/month"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-designjoy-got-from-0-to-120k-month-with-no-paid-advertising-a31375fce0); [Memberstack showcase](https://memberstack.io/showcase/how-i-built-designjoy); [USD 1.3M/yr summary](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/youtube.com/yt-dXKzST0FE-A) (pages not opened, 403)
- Design Pickle (flat-fee "unlimited" graphic design) launched January 2015 and reached USD 160K monthly recurring revenue in two years with 45 full-time staff. [S][Old] — [SaaS Club interview with Russ Perry](https://saasclub.io/podcast/russ-perry-designpickle/)
- A later figure of USD 86.3M revenue for Design Pickle in 2024 appears on GetLatka; Latka figures are self-reported or estimated and this one was not corroborated. [S] — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/designpickle)
- WP Curve (unlimited small WordPress fixes from USD 79 a month) was founded in 2013, claimed over 1,000 paying members, and was acquired by GoDaddy in December 2016. [P][Old] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/05/godaddy-wp-curve/)
- ManyPixels (design subscription, founded 2018, Singapore) sells a starter plan at USD 399 a month and says it has served over 3,000 customers. [V] — [Genesys Growth comparison](https://genesysgrowth.com/blog/design-pickle-alternatives) (not opened)
- Retainer agencies: annual client retention of 70% is a "red flag" and 80% or more is "good"; for project agencies the equivalents are 40% and 50 to 70%. [A, citing a coaching firm] — [Agiled client retention statistics](https://agiled.app/statistics/client-retention-statistics)
- In a 300-agency benchmark, 90% of digital agencies use retainers and the most common retainer is under USD 5,000 a month; 7-figure agencies keep 78% of clients a year (client worth about USD 125,000 over 18 months), 8-figure agencies keep 92% (about USD 450,000 over 36 months). Methodology is not disclosed and the publisher sells agency coaching. [V] — [Predictable Profits 2025 Agency Growth Benchmark](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Agencies using value-based pricing report about 18% net margin against 13% for hourly billing. [A, citing Promethean Research] — [Agiled profitability statistics](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics)

**(b) Managed AI operations retainers**
- Gross margins: pure subscription SaaS 75 to 85%, AI-inference businesses 50 to 65%, services-heavy or managed AI 35 to 55% because implementation labour, model spend and support sit in cost of goods. [A] — [Causo seed-stage gross margin guide 2026](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/saas-gross-margin-benchmarks-at-seed-2026)
- Typical small AI-agency prices quoted without a data source: builds USD 1,500 to 15,000, retainers USD 500 to 5,000 a month, "reported" gross margins 70 to 90%. The publisher sells a demo tool to AI agencies. [V] — [Ciela AI](https://ciela.ai/blogs/why-ai-automation-agencies-fail-data)
- The headline pitch of "five clients at USD 5,000 a month" is described as rarely reached; the hard part is getting to five clients. Opinion piece. [A] — [Medium, Automation Labs](https://medium.com/@automation.labs/ai-automation-agencies-charge-5k-a-client-few-get-five-f0e9ad0b5dde)
- MIT NANDA (August 2025; 150 interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 public deployments): about 5% of generative AI pilots produce rapid revenue gains; buying from specialised vendors and partnering succeeds about 67% of the time, internal builds about one third as often; over half of budgets go to sales and marketing tools but the best returns come from back-office automation, eliminating outsourcing and cutting external agency costs. [P via press] — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)
- Large IT services firms are moving to subscription and outcome pricing: in June 2025 Globant launched "AI Pods" on a tokenised subscription and Grid Dynamics launched credit-based pricing "based on value instead of effort". [B] — [William Blair, "Entering 2026"](https://www.williamblair.com/-/media/downloads/eqr/2026/williamblair_entering-2026-ai-pricing-takes-hold-stabilizing-demand-strategic-ma.pdf) (not opened; attribution to this page not verified)

**(c) White-label delivery for other agencies and resellers**
- Claimed reseller economics for white-label voice AI: agencies charge USD 297 to 997 per client per month while paying USD 99 to 299 a month in platform fees plus about USD 0.12 a minute, for a claimed 50 to 75% margin. The source is a platform selling to agencies. [V] — [Trillet](https://www.trillet.ai/blogs/white-label-ai-profit-margins)
- Agencies licensing white-label chatbots and voice agents are said to charge USD 300 to 500 per tool per month at 50 to 75% margin. [V] — [RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/white-label-ai-development) (not opened)
- GoHighLevel pricing: USD 97, USD 297 and USD 497 (SaaS Pro) a month; SaaS Pro lets an agency resell the platform under its own brand with its own price tiers and automated billing; usage charges add an estimated USD 50 to 200 a month. No ARR, agency-count or churn figures were given. [A, reads like promotional coverage] — [The Next Web, 16 March 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/gohighlevel-agency-software-platform)
- Claim that agencies reselling GoHighLevel average USD 15,000 to 45,000 MRR at 70%+ margins and that the best run 200+ clients with three staff: no data source given, publisher is a GoHighLevel services firm. [V] — [Ecosire](https://ecosire.com/blog/gohighlevel-white-label-saas-complete-guide)

**(d) Vertical software (micro-SaaS) built out of service work**
- The typical small SaaS for sale on Acquire.com in 2026 has about USD 203K revenue and USD 106K profit and asks USD 484K (2.6x revenue, 10.7x profit), based on 600+ live listings. These are asking prices, not closed prices. [A, citing Acquire.com] — [Beancount.io, 11 July 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)
- SMB-focused SaaS typically loses 3 to 7% of customers a month (30 to 58% a year); under 2% a month is the target. [A] — [UserJot](https://userjot.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks); [Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/saas-churn-rate)
- Mailchimp launched in 2001 as a side product of a web design agency, went freemium in 2009 (users rose from 85,000 to 450,000 within a year), had USD 700M revenue in 2019, never took outside investment and was bought by Intuit for about USD 12B in 2021. [A][Old] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailchimp)
- An Indian cloud services firm grew slowly for five years, built a product for one client, shifted focus to the product about two years later (2019) and was acquired by Yotta in 2024. [S] — [Practical Founders, Sunando Bhattacharya](https://practicalfounders.com/?p=22656)
- A more typical small outcome: a digital agency that pivoted to SaaS and reported about USD 3,000 a month. [S][Old] — [Indie Hackers, Widgetic](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/pivoting-from-a-digital-agency-to-a-3k-mo-saas-company-ba5ad6a41c)

**(e) Licensing and revenue-share deals**
- In a 4A's survey, only 39% of agencies had any performance-based deal, for two thirds of them those deals produced under 2% of annual gross income, and 53% of deals required the agency to put base compensation at risk. [B][Old, 2014] — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/pay-for-performance-deals-still-risky-for-agencies/261080/)
- Further detail is in section 9.

**(f) Staff augmentation and dedicated teams**
- A Lahore staff-augmentation firm (CodeNinja) lists USD 25 to 49 an hour on Clutch. [B] — [Clutch Pakistan staff augmentation](https://clutch.co/pk/it-services/staff-augmentation)
- Indian dedicated-developer rates for 2026: junior USD 15 to 30 an hour (USD 2,500 to 5,000 a month), mid-level USD 25 to 45 (USD 4,000 to 7,500), senior USD 45 to 75 (USD 7,500 to 12,000). Vendor price guides. [V] — [Acquaint Softtech](https://acquaintsoft.com/blog/dedicated-development-team-pricing); [nCube](https://ncube.com/?p=158502) (not opened)
- Common pricing is salary plus a fixed fee or percentage shown as a separate line; buyers are told to budget an extra 20 to 30% for onboarding, tooling, margin and coordination. [V] — [Full Scale](https://fullscale.io/blog/staff-augmentation-cost/) (not opened)
- IT services and development firms sell for 4 to 8x EBITDA, above digital marketing agencies at 2 to 5x. [B] — [Productive.io, updated 30 July 2026](https://productive.io/blog/which-factors-can-positively-influence-your-agency-valuation/)

### Inferences
- Side-by-side reading of the evidence (derived from the findings above; "no data" means no reliable source was found):

| Model | Typical price point | Gross margin | Churn | Sales effort | Capital | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Productised vertical service | USD 79 to 8,000 per client per month | not reported directly; net 13 to 22% for agencies | 20 to 30% of clients a year is normal | medium; needs one channel that produces steady leads | low | mixed: some verified exits, revenue self-reported |
| (b) Managed AI retainer | USD 500 to 5,000 a month (unsourced vendor figure) | 35 to 55% | no data; Gartner's cancellation forecast suggests high | high; trust and proof needed | low to medium (model and tooling costs) | weak on small-agency numbers, strong on demand direction |
| (c) White-label reseller or supplier | USD 300 to 1,000 per end client a month | 50 to 75% claimed | no data | low per deal if a partner sells | low | vendor marketing only |
| (d) Micro-SaaS | no data retrieved | 75 to 85% (50 to 65% if AI-heavy) | 3 to 7% of customers a month | high and continuous | highest; years of build before payback | strong on multiples, survivor bias on examples |
| (e) Revenue share or licensing | variable | variable | no data | high; long negotiation | agency carries the risk | old data, mostly negative |
| (f) Staff augmentation | USD 2,500 to 7,500 per developer a month | salary plus 20 to 30% or more | no data | medium; few large accounts | low | price data usable, margin data thin |

- For a 15 to 20 person low-cost team, staff augmentation and dedicated teams give the fastest route to a large, recurring invoice: one mid-level developer placed at USD 25 an hour for 160 hours bills USD 4,000 a month, about eight times the agency's current revenue per head. It is also the least protected model, since the buyer can replace the supplier with another low-cost shop.
- The productised and managed-service routes fit a founder who sells well and no longer codes, because the offer is fixed and the delivery can be written down; micro-SaaS fits least, because it needs sustained product and engineering leadership and the typical outcome is small.
- The MIT finding that buying from specialist vendors works about twice as often as building in-house supports the managed-service pitch to mid-sized firms. Its finding that the best returns come from back-office automation suggests the chatbot and receptionist end of the market is not where buyers see the largest savings.
- White-label has two sides. Reselling someone else's platform is easy to start but the margin belongs to whoever owns the client. Being the delivery arm for other agencies gives recurring volume without sales cost but at wholesale prices; no reliable numbers were found for that side.

### Gaps
- No independent churn or margin data for small AI-automation retainers; every figure found came from a vendor or opinion piece.
- No reliable data on wholesale pricing or margins for an offshore team acting as white-label supplier to Western agencies.
- No typical price points for micro-SaaS and no MicroConf or ChartMogul figures on median time to USD 1M ARR for bootstrapped SaaS were retrieved (search allowance ran out), so the "years of build" point rests on case histories.
- HighLevel's own ARR, agency count and churn were not found in a verifiable source.

## 2. Benchmarks: revenue per employee, net margins, what "healthy" looks like for 15 to 20 people, and what about USD 500 per employee per month indicates

### Takeaway
USD 500 per employee per month (USD 6,000 a year) is roughly 3 to 4% of the Western digital-agency benchmark and about a quarter of the level that most Pakistani software exporters were at or below in 2018; it indicates a team several times larger than its revenue can support, not a pricing problem alone. A healthy 15 to 20 person offshore shop would be billing several hundred thousand dollars a year at a net margin in the mid teens or better.

### Cited Findings
- Digital agencies averaged a 13% after-tax net margin in 2025 against a long-run average of about 15% since 2015. By size: under 10 staff 19%, 10 to 24 staff about 15%, 25 to 49 staff about 13%, 50 or more staff 8%. Source survey: Promethean Research, 119 agency leaders. [A, citing Promethean] — [Agiled profitability statistics](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics)
- Revenue per employee USD 172,000 (2023 figure); average agency revenue USD 4.43M; development agencies average USD 9.11M revenue at 11% margin, design USD 1.77M at 18%, marketing USD 1.96M at 13%; average project margin 35%; production utilisation target 72%. [A, citing Promethean] — [Agiled profitability statistics](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics)
- 7-figure agencies: USD 150,000 revenue per employee, 18 to 22% net margin, 28 billable hours a week, staff stay 2.1 years. 8-figure agencies: USD 225,000 per employee, 25 to 32% net margin, 32 billable hours, staff stay 3.4 years. Methodology not disclosed; publisher sells coaching. [V] — [Predictable Profits 2025 benchmark](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Pakistan Technology Industry Survey 2018 (94 software and IT-enabled services exporters): 63% of companies earned USD 23,000 or less per employee per year; 58% earned under USD 800,000 a year in total; 86% had fewer than 180 employees. [B][Old, 2018] — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2018/12/05/pakistans-it-sector-will-produce-18000-new-jobs-in-2019-report/)
- Pakistan's IT exports reached a record USD 4.6B in FY2025-26, up 20% from USD 3.814B, against a government target of USD 5B (State Bank data, reported with P@SHA comment). [P via press] — [ProPakistani, 17 July 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/07/17/pakistans-it-exports-reach-all-time-high-of-4-6-billion/)
- The Pakistani IT sector is described as supporting over 600,000 professionals and freelancers across more than 10,000 companies. The exact page was not verified; it surfaced in search results alongside P@SHA material. [A] — [P@SHA 2025-26 budget recommendations](https://pasha.org.pk/wp-content/uploads/2025-26-Budget-Recommendations-by-P@SHA.pdf) (not opened)
- Systems Limited, Pakistan's largest listed IT services firm, is listed with USD 241M revenue and 5,083 employees, about USD 47,400 per employee. Directory data, low reliability. [A] — [Bitscale directory](https://bitscale.ai/directory/systems-limited)
- A mid-sized Pakistani services firm (Salsoft) is estimated at about USD 27,000 revenue per employee. Estimate from a data vendor. [A] — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/salsoft-technologies-pvt-revenue)
- Indian IT majors' revenue per employee rose from USD 25,000 to 35,000 in early offshoring years to about USD 40,000 to 55,000. [A] — [Equities India glossary](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/revenue-per-employee)
- India's technology sector revenue was USD 282.6B in FY25, up 5.1%, according to NASSCOM. [P via press] — [YourStory](https://nextjs-prod.yourstory.com/enterprise-story/2025/02/indian-tech-sector-fy25-revenues-grow-51-to-282-billion) (not opened)
- Buyers treat an EBITDA margin above 20% as a sign of control and below 15% as margin risk. [B] — [FE International, 20 May 2026](https://feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation)

### Inferences
- USD 6,000 per employee per year compares with USD 150,000 to 172,000 for US-centred agencies (about 3.5 to 4%), USD 40,000 to 55,000 for Indian majors and Systems Limited (11 to 15%), and the USD 23,000 line that 63% of Pakistani exporters sat at or under in 2018 (about 26%).
- Dividing USD 4.6B of exports by roughly 600,000 professionals and freelancers gives about USD 7,700 per person per year (about USD 640 a month). That is a crude national average that includes part-time freelancers and excludes domestic revenue, but it means the agency is at or slightly below the average for the whole Pakistani IT workforce, not for organised software houses.
- At under USD 10,000 a month, revenue is what three to five productive people normally bill offshore. Fifteen to twenty people on that revenue implies most are part-time, commission-based, on the bench or paid far below market; whichever it is, headcount is not the asset it appears to be.
- A rough "healthy" target for 15 to 20 people in Pakistan: at the 2018 survey's USD 23,000 line, USD 345,000 to 460,000 a year (USD 29,000 to 38,000 a month); at the Salsoft or Systems Limited level (USD 27,000 to 47,000), USD 400,000 to 940,000 a year. Net margin in line with Promethean's 10-to-24-staff band would be about 15%, and offshore cost levels should allow more.
- The implication for strategy is that the first gain is raising revenue per head on the existing team (fewer, larger, repeat accounts), before any change of business model.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 P@SHA figure for revenue per employee in Pakistani software houses was found; the 2018 survey is the latest sourced.
- Pakistani salary benchmarks were not retrieved, so the claim that USD 500 a month per head is near payroll cost could not be sourced.
- NASSCOM headcount for FY25 was not verified in this session, so no India-wide revenue per employee was calculated.
- The Promethean numbers come through an aggregator; the original report was not opened. One aggregator line ("specialised agencies: 13% average growth, 30% net margins") looks inconsistent with the 13% industry average and should not be quoted without checking the original.
- SoDA, Parakeeto, Agency Analytics, Clutch and Productive.io benchmark reports on margins and utilisation were not retrieved.

## 3. What happened to the "AI automation agency" model in 2024 to 2026, and how are no-code and agent tools changing prices and headcount?

### Takeaway
Simple automation builds have become a commodity and marketplace demand for low-complexity work is shrinking, while spending on AI-related services overall is growing and shifting toward outcome or subscription pricing. Hard evidence on small-agency survival rates is absent; what exists points to survivors being those who sell an operated result in a defined niche, not a build.

### Cited Findings
- Fiverr's annual active buyers fell to 2.7M at 30 June 2026 from 3.4M a year earlier (down 21.9%); Q2 2026 revenue was USD 97.8M against USD 108.6M (down 10.0%). The decline was driven by lower-complexity categories such as basic copywriting, simple graphic design and entry-level programming, where customers are using generative AI tools instead. [P, not opened; 403] — [Fiverr Q2 2026 results](https://www.fiverr.com/news/fiverr-q2-2026-earnings); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/fiverr-q2-revenue-slips-10-amid-ai-led-market-shift)
- Fiverr cut 250 jobs, about 30% of staff, in September 2025 to become an "AI-first company". [P via press] — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/fiverr-is-laying-off-250-employees-to-become-an-ai-first-company-215730063.html); [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001521884)
- Upwork's Q2 2026 gross services volume fell 3.6% year on year to USD 966.4M, while volume from AI-related work rose more than 22% to about USD 330M and the "AI strategy and consulting" sub-category grew more than 50%. [P via press, not opened; 403] — [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/upwork-q2-revenue-falls-17-though-ai-related-work-provides-bright-spot)
- Gartner (25 June 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 because of rising costs, unclear business value or weak risk controls; only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming agentic AI are genuine ("agent washing"). [P] — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
- MIT NANDA (August 2025): about 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots show no measurable profit-and-loss impact; the main barrier is that systems do not retain feedback or adapt to context, not model quality. [P via press] — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/); [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/Articles/2025/08/19/MIT-Report-Finds-Most-AI-Business-Investments-Fail-Reveals-GenAI-Divide.aspx)
- Sequoia (Julien Bek, 5 March 2026): "For every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services"; "A copilot sells the tool. An autopilot sells the work."; the best entry point is "the outsourced, intelligence-heavy task" because a budget line, acceptance of an outside supplier and a clear measure of return already exist. Named examples include Crosby (NDAs), WithCoverage and Harper (insurance), Rillet (accounting), Anterior (medical coding), Edra (IT services). [B, investor thesis] — [Sequoia Capital](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- A counter-argument: when machines do the work it is repriced at machine rates, so labour budgets shrink instead of moving to AI suppliers; one newsletter puts it at USD 0.03 spent on AI for every USD 1 no longer spent on people. Opinion, figure not verified. [A] — [Linas's Newsletter](https://linas.substack.com/p/sequoiathesis)
- HFS Research sizes "services-as-software" as a USD 1.5 trillion opportunity combining automation with outcome-based pricing. [B] — [FutureCIO](https://futurecio.tech/the-future-of-enterprise-is-outcome-based/)
- Buyer preference (Futurum, first half 2026): 43% prefer consumption-based pricing and 27% outcome-based. A 2026 AWS market study says 80% of customers are shifting to outcome-based commercial models. [B] — [Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=89753); [Channel Dive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/aws-pushes-partners-to-rethink-pricing-for-the-ai-era/829107/) (not opened)
- TSIA's State of Technology Services 2026: AI, data and analytics services are now the most common service category, ahead of business applications for the first time. [B] — [TSIA](https://www.tsia.com/blog/state-of-technology-services-2026-ai-services) (not opened)
- Practitioner view of commoditisation: "Two years ago, wiring a lead form to a CRM with an AI step in the middle was a skill. In 2026 it's a Tuesday." Opinion. [A] — [Medium, Automation Labs](https://medium.com/@automation.labs/ai-automation-agencies-charge-5k-a-client-few-get-five-f0e9ad0b5dde)
- Thousands of new AI automation agencies are said to launch monthly with most failing within a year; no data source is given. [A] — [IdeaProof guide](https://ideaproof.io/guides/ai-automation-agency) (not opened; attribution from search summary)
- Marketing agencies are squeezed from both sides: automation lowers cost and clients use the same tools to question fees, retainers and mark-ups. Opinion. [A] — [Mean CEO blog](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=8699)
- Software development companies surveyed for 2026 rates: 77.2% say new AI tools are pushing their rates up (28.8% in 2023); to add capacity 85.8% are adopting AI tools and 66.9% improving processes, while only 16.5% rely on hiring more developers. [B, secondary summary of a Techreviewer survey] — [Techreviewer Insights podcast summary](https://podscan.fm/podcasts/techreviewer-insights-the-data-behind-tech-1/episodes/global-software-development-rates-in-2026) (not opened)
- 89% of agencies in one benchmark say they use AI, with reported productivity gains of up to 49%. [V] — [Predictable Profits 2025 benchmark](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)

### Inferences
- The two marketplaces show a split, not a collapse: buyers of simple, low-priced work are leaving (Fiverr buyers down about a fifth in a year) while AI-related and advisory work is growing at 20 to 50%. An agency selling USD 200 to 6,000 bots on Fiverr is on the shrinking side of that split.
- If only one in six development firms plans to grow by hiring, a 15 to 20 person low-cost team is no longer an advantage by itself; the advantage moves to whoever owns the client relationship, the niche knowledge and the operating responsibility.
- The Gartner and MIT failure figures cut both ways: they mean high churn for agencies selling pilots, and an opening for agencies that take responsibility for a working result, since vendor-partnered deployments succeed about twice as often as in-house ones.
- The survival traits the brief asked about (vertical focus, compliance, integrations, results-based pricing) are consistent with the Sequoia and HFS theses, but no dataset was found that measures them for small agencies.

### Gaps
- No reliable count of AI automation agencies, their failure rate, or price trends for standard builds (for example a WhatsApp bot in 2024 against 2026) was found. All "most fail within a year" claims are unsourced.
- No verified data on n8n, Make, GoHighLevel, OpenAI or Anthropic agent builders, or Claude Code and similar tools (user growth, or measured effect on agency pricing and headcount) was retrieved before the search allowance ran out.
- a16z, Bessemer and Everest Group material on services-as-software was not retrieved.
- The Fiverr and Upwork 2026 figures come from search summaries of the companies' own releases; the pages returned 403 and were not read in full. Upwork's revenue change was not confirmed (the headline slug is ambiguous between 1.7% and 17%).

## 4. Real examples of services businesses that became productised or SaaS businesses and reached USD 1M or more a year, and what they changed first

### Takeaway
The verifiable cases changed packaging and scope first (a fixed offer at a fixed monthly price, or a product built for one repeated client problem) and changed channel second; the well-known product spin-outs ran the agency alongside the product for years before the product carried the business. South Asian examples exist but are few and mostly not small agencies.

### Cited Findings
- 37signals was founded in 1999 as a web design firm, launched Basecamp commercially in 2004 after starting work in 2003, and "by 2005 … had moved away from consulting work to focus exclusively on its own web applications". [A][Old] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37signals)
- Mailchimp was launched in 2001 by the founders of the Rocket Science Group web design agency, stayed bootstrapped, adopted freemium in 2009, reached USD 700M revenue by 2019 and sold to Intuit for about USD 12B (USD 5.7B cash, USD 6.3B stock) in 2021. [A][Old] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailchimp)
- The Mailchimp founders did not take the email tool seriously at first; they switched focus when their numbers showed the product growing while the agency was flat or declining, and the product took about two years to get going and four to reach first traction. [A] — [Unheard Roots newsletter](https://unheard-roots.beehiiv.com/p/mailchimp-2); [Typefully thread](https://typefully.com/JohnIsBuilding/buy-an-agency-build-a-saas-empire-8mB9EQ9) (not opened)
- Campaign Monitor and Basecamp are cited in the same pattern: founders built a tool for their own agency's pain, as a side project funded by agency profit. [A] — [Typefully thread](https://typefully.com/JohnIsBuilding/buy-an-agency-build-a-saas-empire-8mB9EQ9) (not opened)
- Wildbit ran for over 21 years, built Beanstalk about 2008 and later Postmark, and sold Postmark and DMARC Digests to ActiveCampaign on 3 May 2022, sharing 10% of proceeds with the team. The page read does not state its consultancy origins. [P][Old] — [Wildbit blog](https://wildbit.com/blog/postmark-has-been-acquired-by-activecampaign)
- Jellyvision operated for years as an agency before pivoting to SaaS and growing to hundreds of employees. [S] — [Jellyvision](https://jellyvision.com/news-press/pivoting-from-agency-to-saas-with-jellyvision-ceo-amanda-lannert) (not opened)
- WP Curve: productised WordPress support from USD 79 a month, 1,000+ paying members and 44,000 jobs completed in about three years, acquired by GoDaddy in December 2016. [P][Old] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/05/godaddy-wp-curve/)
- Design Pickle: USD 160K monthly recurring revenue within two years of a January 2015 launch, with 45 full-time staff. [S][Old] — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/russ-perry-designpickle/)
- DesignJoy: one person, fixed monthly price, requests handled through a Trello board with no meetings, clients acquired mainly through Twitter and building in public; revenue reported between USD 1.3M and over USD 3M a year. [S] — [Memberstack showcase](https://memberstack.io/showcase/how-i-built-designjoy); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-designjoy-got-from-0-to-120k-month-with-no-paid-advertising-a31375fce0) (not opened)
- Pakistan: i2c was founded in 2001 by Amir Wain, who concluded in 1996 that outsourced software development was a poor model for Pakistan because every client assignment starts from scratch, relationships are transactional and the firm never builds its own intellectual property; i2c became one of the country's largest software exporters as a payments SaaS. [B][Old, 2019] — [Profit, Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2019/01/28/pakistan-does-not-have-a-billion-dollar-software-company-meet-the-man-who-wants-to-change-that)
- India: Sunando Bhattacharya's cloud services firm grew slowly for five years, built a product for one client, had a few more product customers two years later, shifted focus to the product in 2019 and was acquired by Yotta in 2024. [S] — [Practical Founders](https://practicalfounders.com/?p=22656)

### Inferences
- What changed first, by case: DesignJoy, Design Pickle and WP Curve changed the offer and price (one narrow service, flat monthly fee, defined limits), then found one channel that fed it. Mailchimp, 37signals, Campaign Monitor and the Indian cloud firm built a product from a problem they had already solved for clients and moved over only when product revenue was visibly outgrowing services. i2c chose a niche (payments) and ownership of intellectual property from the start.
- None of the verifiable cases began by changing channel alone, and none became a product company quickly: 37signals took about six years from founding to leaving client work; Mailchimp ran inside the agency for years, with first traction after about four and the freemium step that unlocked growth eight years after launch; the Indian firm took about seven years from founding to product focus.
- The productised-service cases reached USD 1M to 2M a year with delivery staff (Design Pickle, 45 people) or with one exceptional operator (DesignJoy). The staffed version is the relevant comparison for an agency with an existing team.
- The well-known names are survivors; the Widgetic case in section 1 (agency to a USD 3,000-a-month SaaS) is probably closer to the usual result.
- DesignJoy's figures are the weakest evidence in this section: founder-reported, inconsistent between sources, and not independently checked.

### Gaps
- No verified AI-era (2024 to 2026) example of a small automation agency that turned into a product or productised business above USD 1M a year was found.
- No verified Pakistani small-agency-to-product case beyond i2c was retrieved; Indian examples beyond the one cited were not researched before the search allowance ran out.
- Tighten was not researched. Wildbit's early consulting years are widely described but were not confirmed in the page read.
- Starter Story, MicroConf and Indie Hackers pages could not be opened (403), so self-reported figures were not checked against the original posts.

## 5. Niche focus: do specialised agencies earn higher margins and valuations, and how long does repositioning take?

### Takeaway
Practitioner authorities and the available benchmarks agree that specialists price higher and earn more, but the hard numbers found are thin and partly from interested parties. Repositioning shows up in revenue slowly: the only timing evidence found is an execution anecdote of about eight months before the new positioning is even in market.

### Cited Findings
- Hinge Research Institute's 2025 High Growth Study covers 770 professional services firms with USD 87B combined revenue and about 440,000 employees; high-growth firms grow four times faster and are 30% more profitable than peers; industry-wide growth declined for the first time in four years. [B] — [Hinge Marketing](https://hingemarketing.com/library/article/high-growth-study-2025)
- Niche agencies report gross margins of 40 to 75% and are said to command higher fees, close faster and run simpler operations. Methodology not disclosed. [V] — [Predictable Profits 2025 benchmark](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- Value-based pricing is associated with about 18% net margin against 13% for hourly billing. [A, citing Promethean] — [Agiled profitability statistics](https://agiled.app/statistics/agency-profitability-statistics)
- David C. Baker's rule of thumb: a well-positioned firm has between 10 and 200 competitors; expertise is "selling your thinking to people who are losing sleep, trying to find those answers"; what drags firm performance down is "almost exclusively relationships with clients who keep fighting your budgets"; owners resist narrowing because they fear losing opportunities, which he says is the opposite of what happens. [F] — [Parakeeto podcast, episode 48](https://parakeeto.com/blog/how-to-become-an-expert-agency-with-david-c-baker-episode-48)
- Baker on execution lag: a firm decides to narrow its positioning, "then it takes them three months to get website up, five more months to get materials ready"; good ideas are not enough without consistent action. [F] — [SharpSpring interview](https://en.sharpspring.com/blog/agency-acceleration-advice-from-david-c-baker/) (not opened)
- Baker's long-standing position is that marketing firms should be as narrowly positioned as possible to maximise effectiveness and profit. [F][Old, 2010] — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/opinions/2010/22920/5-insights-into-narrow-positioning) (not opened)
- Valuation does not reward niche directly in the broker material found; it rewards recurring revenue, low client concentration, low owner dependence and EBITDA margin above 20%. [B] — [FE International](https://feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports "specialists earn more" as a direction, not as a measured premium. The clearest mechanism is pricing power: a specialist can refuse clients who fight the budget, and value-priced firms show about five points more net margin.
- Niche affects valuation indirectly, through the things buyers do pay for: repeatable delivery, retainers and margin.
- On timing, the Baker anecdote implies roughly eight months of preparation before the market sees the new positioning, and the case histories in section 4 suggest one to three years before a new focus dominates revenue. This is an estimate, not a measured figure.
- The Sequoia point in section 3, that the best entry is an already-outsourced, clearly measured task, is a practical test for choosing a niche.

### Gaps
- Hinge's specific findings on specialisation among high-growth firms were not retrieved; the summary found gives only overall growth and profit differences.
- SoDA, Blair Enns, Agency Analytics and Clutch data on specialist against generalist margins were not retrieved.
- No study measuring how long repositioning takes to show in revenue was found.
- The aggregator's "30% net margin for specialised agencies" attributed to Promethean could not be verified and conflicts with the 13% industry average.

## 6. Channel shift: moving from marketplaces to direct, referral and partner channels; how much marketplace revenue is risky; fee and policy changes in 2025 to 2026

### Takeaway
No source gives a formal "safe" share of revenue from marketplaces, but buyers of agencies treat any single source above 10 to 20% of revenue as a risk, and a marketplace is in effect one counterparty that controls access, fees and the client relationship. Upwork's fees became variable and less predictable in 2025, and low-complexity demand on Fiverr is falling.

### Cited Findings
- Upwork replaced its previous fee structure with a variable 0 to 15% freelancer fee set per contract from May 2025, fixed for the life of the contract; community reports put the effective rate for mid-level freelancers at about 12 to 13%, above the 10% flat rate established agencies had before. Source is a competing platform's blog and describes the earlier structure inconsistently. [A][V] — [Jobbers fee encyclopedia](https://www.jobbers.io/the-freelance-platform-fee-encyclopedia-every-fee-on-every-platform-documented/) (not opened)
- Fiverr charges sellers 20% and buyers a 5.5% service fee. [A] — [AIProposer comparison](https://aiproposer.com/comparisons/fiverr-vs-upwork) (not opened)
- Upwork treats taking a client found on the platform off the platform without paying a conversion fee as circumvention, a serious breach of its terms; relationships that began outside Upwork and were brought on can be taken off again. [P] — [Upwork help centre](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048105134-Why-Can-t-I-Get-Paid-Directly) (not opened)
- Reported exit routes: wait two years, pay a conversion fee of 13.5% of hourly rate times 2,080 hours (minimum USD 1,000), or use Direct Contracts at 5% for clients with no existing Upwork account. For agencies the lock-in applies to every member and one member's breach can suspend the whole agency. Source sells Upwork tooling. [V] — [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-exclusive-contracts) (not opened)
- Platform-dependence risks listed by commentators: suspension without warning, fee changes, ranking changes, and the platform owning the client relationship; rebuilding reputation elsewhere is said to take 6 to 12 months. [A] — [Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/should-you-use-upwork-or-build-your-own-network-in-2026-the-strategic-decision-that-defines-your-freelance-career/) (not opened)
- Concentration thresholds used by acquirers: no single client above 10% of revenue and the top three below 25%; a single client at 20% or more cuts the multiple by 1.5 to 2x. [B] — [FE International](https://feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation)
- A client above 10% of annual revenue is a red flag in valuation. [B] — [Productive.io](https://productive.io/blog/which-factors-can-positively-influence-your-agency-valuation/)
- Demand trend: Fiverr's active buyers fell 21.9% in the year to June 2026; Upwork's volume fell 3.6% while AI-related work grew over 22%. [P, not opened] — [Fiverr Q2 2026](https://www.fiverr.com/news/fiverr-q2-2026-earnings); [Staffing Industry Analysts on Upwork](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/upwork-q2-revenue-falls-17-though-ai-related-work-provides-bright-spot)

### Inferences
- An agency earning most of its revenue through Fiverr and Upwork fails the concentration test that acquirers apply to single clients, and by a wide margin. By analogy, anything above 20 to 25% from one platform would be discounted, and "mostly marketplace" would make the business close to unsellable regardless of client count.
- The fee difference is material at this size: 20% on Fiverr against roughly 10 to 13% on Upwork against a few per cent for direct payment. On USD 10,000 a month, moving half of revenue from Fiverr to direct would add roughly USD 800 to 1,000 a month.
- The compliant path off Upwork is to win new clients outside it (they are not covered by the non-circumvention rule) and to let existing platform clients stay until the two-year window passes, not to move them early.
- The marketplaces remain useful as a paid lead source for AI-related and advisory work, which is the part that is growing; they are a weak base for small fixed-price builds.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found for the share of agency revenue that comes from referrals, outbound, partners or marketplaces, or for how long the shift takes.
- No data on partner or reseller channel economics for small agencies (referral fees, typical share of pipeline) was retrieved.
- Upwork's official fee page returned 403, so the May 2025 change and the conversion-fee numbers are sourced only from third-party blogs and should be checked against Upwork's terms before being relied on.
- Fiverr policy changes in 2026 beyond the results and layoffs were not researched.

## 7. Valuation: multiples for project agencies, retainer agencies and small SaaS in 2025 to 2026, and what that implies for recurring against project revenue

### Takeaway
Small agencies sell for about 2 to 5 times earnings, with retainer-heavy, owner-independent ones one to two turns higher than project shops; small SaaS sells for about 2.5 to 4 times revenue. A dollar of recurring software revenue is therefore worth several times a dollar of project revenue, and retainer service revenue sits in between.

### Cited Findings
- FE International (20 May 2026), digital agencies: under USD 500K EBITDA, 2.5 to 4x seller's discretionary earnings; USD 500K to 1M, 3 to 5x; USD 1M to 2.5M EBITDA, 4 to 6.5x; USD 2.5M to 5M, 5.5 to 8.5x; over USD 5M, 7 to 12x. [B] — [FE International](https://feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation)
- Agencies with more than 60% of revenue on retainer get multiples 1.0 to 2.0x higher than project-based peers; a strong managing director, head of growth and head of delivery add 0.5 to 1.5x; a single client at 20% or more removes 1.5 to 2x. [B] — [FE International](https://feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation)
- By type (EBITDA multiples): digital marketing 2 to 5x, IT services and development 4 to 8x, business consulting 2 to 5x, advertising 3 to 4.5x, PR 5 to 7.5x. By size: under USD 1M revenue, 0.8 to 1.4x revenue or 2 to 4x earnings; USD 1M to 2M, 1 to 2x revenue or 4 to 6x earnings; USD 2M to 5M, 1.5 to 3x revenue or 6 to 12x earnings. Yearly contracts are valued above monthly ones. [B] — [Productive.io, updated 30 July 2026](https://productive.io/blog/which-factors-can-positively-influence-your-agency-valuation/)
- Worked example from an M&A adviser: USD 800,000 adjusted EBITDA, mostly project revenue, one client at 40% of billings, might price at 3.5x (USD 2.8M); the same earnings with 75% of revenue on rolling retainers, no client above 15% and a general manager running delivery could command 6x (USD 4.8M). Most 2026 deals land between 3x and 7x, median 4.2 to 5.8x. [B] — [Lightning Path Partners](https://lightningpathpartners.com/agency-blog/digital-agency-ebitda-multiples) (not opened)
- Long-term auto-renewing retainers of 12 months or more are said to earn 70 to 80% of the premium that recurring SaaS revenue earns; project-only revenue caps the multiple at the bottom of the sector range. Exact source page not verified. [A] — [Upswitch sector guide](https://www.upswitch.app/en/sectoren/marketing-agency) (not opened)
- Small SaaS, 2026: under USD 1M ARR, 2.5 to 4x annual revenue or 4 to 6x seller's earnings if owner-dependent; USD 1M to 5M ARR, 4 to 6x ARR. Acquire.com's 600+ listings average 2.6x revenue and 10.7x profit at the small end (asking prices). Customer concentration above 30% in one client reduces value or triggers an earn-out; gross margin of 70 to 85% and gross revenue retention above 90% are expected. [A, citing Acquire.com] — [Beancount.io, 11 July 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)
- Private SaaS broadly trades at about 2 to 7x ARR depending on retention and growth; the median private SaaS company is put at about 4.5x ARR in 2026; SaaS Capital's 2025 survey gives 4.8x for bootstrapped and 5.3x for venture-backed companies with USD 3M to 20M ARR. [A] — [FE International SaaS multiples](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/saas-valuation-multiples) (not opened); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/saas-valuation-statistics-2026/) (not opened); [Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/saas-valuation-multiples) (not opened); contradicted on which group gets 5.3x by [Beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)
- The SaaS Capital Index of public B2B SaaS is quoted as 5.5x in Q1 2026 by one secondary source and about 3.8x in March 2026 (down from 7.0x at the start of 2025) by another. SaaS Capital's own page was current to 30 September 2026 but the value is shown only in a chart. [A, conflicting] — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/saas-valuation-statistics-2026/); [Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/saas-valuation-multiples); [SaaS Capital Index](https://www.saas-capital.com/the-saas-capital-index/)

### Inferences
- Value of USD 1 of annual revenue, using the ranges above: project agency at a 15% margin and 2 to 4x earnings, about USD 0.30 to 0.60; retainer agency at a 20% margin and 4 to 6x, about USD 0.80 to 1.20; small SaaS at 2.5 to 4x revenue, USD 2.50 to 4.00. Recurring software revenue is worth roughly five to ten times project revenue, and retainer service revenue roughly two to three times.
- At the agency's present size (about USD 120,000 a year, mostly marketplace projects, founder-dependent), the business has little or no sale value: assuming a 12 to 20% margin, 2 to 4x of USD 15,000 to 25,000 of earnings is USD 30,000 to 100,000 at best, and marketplace dependence would push it lower. The value today is the owner's income, not equity.
- Development and IT services firms get higher multiples than marketing agencies (4 to 8x against 2 to 5x), so a retainer-based technical operations business is the better-valued kind of service firm to build.
- The same four things raise the multiple in every source: recurring contracts, no dominant client, a management layer under the owner, and margin above 20%. These can be worked on before any product exists.
- Public SaaS multiples compressed during 2025 to 2026 on at least one reading, so the software premium is real but smaller than in 2021.

### Gaps
- Empire Flippers, Software Equity Group and closed-deal data from Acquire.com were not retrieved; the SaaS figures at the small end are asking prices.
- The current SaaS Capital Index value could not be read and the two secondary figures conflict.
- No valuation data specific to offshore or South Asian agencies, or to AI automation agencies, was found.
- No data on how buyers value productised-service subscriptions (monthly, cancel any time) against 12-month retainers beyond the general "annual beats monthly" statement.

## 8. Owner role: moving the founder from delivery to sales, and failure modes when the founder stops technical work in a low-cost team

### Takeaway
Frameworks point the same way (hand work off in a set order, put delivery under a named lead with written processes) and acquirers pay a measurable premium for an agency that runs without its owner, but there is almost no quantitative evidence on how often the transition fails. The failure modes reported are anecdotal: decisions routing back to the founder, delivery-team incentives diverging from the owner's, and quality slipping unnoticed.

### Cited Findings
- Dan Martell's "Replacement Ladder" from Buy Back Your Time sets an order for hiring so the founder's time is bought back step by step. [F] — [Kevin's Newsletter](https://box.beehiiv.com/p/the-replacement-ladder) (page returned 403; summary only, the rungs themselves were not confirmed)
- Freeing the founder's time is "necessary but not sufficient"; growth also needs a different leadership structure. In one account, after a chief operating officer was hired "the first three months were rough" and two senior delivery leads tested the structure by routing decisions around the new hire back to the founder. Anecdote. [S] — [Ankush Vij, "The Founder Who Becomes Replaceable"](https://ankushvij.substack.com/p/the-founder-who-becomes-replaceable) (not opened)
- "The Principal-Agent Problem is the root cause of most delivery failures in agencies": when team incentives differ from the founder's, client experience suffers and growth stalls. Opinion from a small agency's blog. [A] — [CoderCops](https://blog.codercops.com/blog/agency-operations-systems-delegation-2026) (not opened)
- Acquirers add 0.5 to 1.5x to the multiple for agencies with a strong managing director, head of growth and head of delivery, and discount for founder dependence. [B] — [FE International](https://feinternational.com/blog/digital-marketing-agency-valuation)
- In the adviser example in section 7, "a general manager running delivery" is one of three conditions separating a 6x from a 3.5x outcome. [B] — [Lightning Path Partners](https://lightningpathpartners.com/agency-blog/digital-agency-ebitda-multiples) (not opened)
- 85% of 8-figure agencies document their processes; their staff stay 3.4 years against 2.1 years at 7-figure agencies, and they spend USD 7,500 a year per employee on training against USD 2,500. [V] — [Predictable Profits 2025 benchmark](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)
- David C. Baker frames the principal's value as thinking and expertise ("this small island, which is essentially our brain"), not execution. [F] — [Parakeeto podcast, episode 48](https://parakeeto.com/blog/how-to-become-an-expert-agency-with-david-c-baker-episode-48)
- Agency advisers list specific hires that free the leader to focus on growth. [F] — [Rise with Drew](https://risewithdrew.com/five-key-hires-that-free-leaders-to-focus-on-what-matters-most/) (not opened)

### Inferences
- A founder who is strongest at sales moving fully into sales matches what acquirers reward. The risk is specific to this agency: with 15 to 20 low-cost staff and no senior technical owner, quality control leaves with the founder unless one accountable delivery lead and written standards replace him.
- Likely failure modes, reasoned from the sources and not measured: no one technically senior enough to catch bad work before the client does; the delivery lead becoming the new single point of failure, or leaving with clients; low pay driving turnover (the benchmark links longer tenure and more training with larger, more profitable agencies); and the founder being pulled back into delivery on every escalation.
- Low revenue per head (section 2) makes this harder: there is little margin to pay a strong delivery lead. Reducing headcount to fund one senior person is consistent with the benchmarks.
- A productised offer lowers the technical bar for delivery because the same thing is built repeatedly, which is one reason the productised route suits a founder leaving technical work.

### Gaps
- No survey or study was found on success rates of the seller-doer transition, the timing of the first delivery lead or sales hire, or quality outcomes after a technical founder steps back in an offshore team.
- David C. Baker's and Blair Enns's specific guidance on the principal's role, and the full Replacement Ladder text, were not retrieved.
- No evidence specific to Pakistani or Indian teams on key-person risk or staff poaching was found.

## 9. Results-based, performance, equity and revenue-share deals for small agencies: what they look like and how often they pay off

### Takeaway
The available evidence is old and mostly negative: few agencies earn meaningful income from performance deals, and they fail when measurement, attribution and control are not all in the agency's hands. Buyer interest in outcome pricing for AI services is rising in 2026, but no data shows how often such deals pay off for small suppliers.

### Cited Findings
- 4A's survey: 39% of agencies had entered any performance-based deal; for two thirds of those, such deals produced under 2% of annual gross income; 53% of deals required the agency to risk base compensation. [B][Old, 2014] — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/pay-for-performance-deals-still-risky-for-agencies/261080/)
- TrinityP3 (first published 2010, updated January 2024) lists five reasons performance-based remuneration fails: the penalty outweighs the bonus; targets are unrealistic (one client offered a 20% bonus for a sales goal it had missed for three years); calculations are too complex (12 or more measures); measures are unrelated to business results; and risk-sharing is one-sided. It states that "in most cases, both the agency and the client will tell you that it does not work." No statistics. [F] — [TrinityP3](https://www.trinityp3.com/fee-models-performance/reasons-performance-based-often-fails/)
- For outcome pricing to work, three things must hold together: measurement, attribution and control; when attribution is not agreed in advance it becomes a negotiation at the worst moment. [V] — [ProjectCor](https://live.projectcor.com/blog/performance-pricing-for-agencies-measurement-attribution-and-control) (not opened)
- Buyer side, 2026: 27% of buyers prefer outcome-based structures and 43% consumption-based; an AWS study says 80% of customers are shifting to outcome-based commercial models. [B] — [Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=89753); [Channel Dive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/aws-pushes-partners-to-rethink-pricing-for-the-ai-era/829107/) (not opened)
- Sequoia's thesis is that AI-native firms should sell the completed work, taking the labour budget, with outsourced and clearly measured tasks as the place to start. [B, investor thesis] — [Sequoia Capital](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- Large services firms have introduced token, credit or subscription pricing tied to output instead of hours (Globant, Grid Dynamics, June 2025). [B] — [William Blair](https://www.williamblair.com/-/media/downloads/eqr/2026/williamblair_entering-2026-ai-pricing-takes-hold-stabilizing-demand-strategic-ma.pdf) (not opened; attribution to this page not verified)

### Inferences
- Results-based pricing works where the agency controls the whole outcome and it can be counted without argument (calls answered, bookings made, documents processed). It works poorly where the client's own sales team, pricing or product decides the result, which is the usual case in revenue-share deals.
- For a small agency the practical form is a base fee that covers cost plus a per-unit or per-outcome charge, not a pure share of client revenue. This matches the consumption-based structure that most buyers say they prefer.
- Equity or revenue share in a client's business concentrates risk in a few deals with small firms that mostly do not grow; with under USD 10,000 a month of revenue the agency cannot carry many unpaid builds. No evidence was found that these deals pay off often enough to plan around.
- Licensing the agency's own repeatable build (a vertical bot or workflow) to resellers is closer to the white-label model in section 1 than to a revenue share, and is the lower-risk way to earn on usage.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 data on how many small agencies use performance, revenue-share or equity deals, or on their payoff rate, was found. The only statistics are from 2014.
- No verified examples of small AI agencies earning substantial income from revenue-share or equity deals were found.
- Blair Enns's and David C. Baker's views on performance and equity compensation were not retrieved.
