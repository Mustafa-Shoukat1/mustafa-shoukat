Scan date: 3 October 2026. Emphasis: sovereign / on-premise / Arabic-first enterprise AI.

Method notes that apply to every section:
- Posting details were read from the public (logged-out) LinkedIn job pages, employer career sites and Workable feeds on 3 October 2026, through an automated page reader. "Not stated" means the reader did not find it in the posting text, not that the employer has no such requirement.
- LinkedIn shows relative dates ("2 weeks ago"). Absolute dates below are approximations counted back from 3 October 2026 and are marked with "~".
- Bayt.com, GulfTalent, Robert Walters, Indeed, Glassdoor and the HUMAIN careers site all refused automated reads (HTTP 403). Sabbar and several aggregator pages returned 410/404 (expired). Postings known only from search-engine snippets are kept in a separate "unverified" table and are excluded from all counts.

## Key question 1: What are 20 to 25 real Gulf AI architect / senior AI engineer postings from the last month?

### Takeaway
32 postings dated roughly 5 September to 2 October 2026 were opened and read individually (22 Saudi Arabia, 6 UAE, 4 Qatar); none of the 32 states a salary. The live demand is dominated by integrators, global vendors' Riyadh field teams (SAP, Salesforce, Cohere, Scale AI, IBM) and bank-facing specialists, not by the sovereign champions' own career pages.

## Key question 4: Is a completed bachelor's degree required for a Saudi Iqama / work visa in engineering roles, and for the UAE? What about Saudi Council of Engineers registration?

### Takeaway
Saudi Arabia's 2025 skill-based work-permit system ties the "high-skilled" tier to a bachelor's degree plus five years' experience, but it is points-based and advisers say strong experience and wage can offset missing education; separately, any qualification claimed must be verifiable through the Professional Verification programme, and an "Engineer" Iqama title requires Saudi Council of Engineers registration, which needs an engineering degree. In the UAE, professional (level 1-2) job titles need an attested degree, and "skilled" status needs an attested post-secondary certificate plus AED 4,000 salary.

### Cited Findings

Saudi Arabia: work-permit classification
- On 6 July 2025 the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development announced a decision reclassifying expatriate work permits into three categories: high-skill, skilled and basic, based on "educational qualifications, professional experience, professional skills, wages and age" — [Erickson Immigration Group, 9 Jul 2025](https://eiglaw.com/saudi-arabia-introduces-skill-based-classification-for-work-permits/)
- High-skilled corresponds to Saudi Standard Classification of Occupations groups 1 to 3: managers, specialists, and technicians and associate professionals — [Erickson Immigration Group](https://eiglaw.com/saudi-arabia-introduces-skill-based-classification-for-work-permits/)
- The system is points-based and lets applicants "compensate for not meeting some criteria with stronger performance in others"; the example given is that extensive experience can qualify someone as high-skilled without meeting the education requirement — [Erickson Immigration Group](https://eiglaw.com/saudi-arabia-introduces-skill-based-classification-for-work-permits/)
- KPMG (11 Jul 2025) confirms the three tiers, the basis in Ministerial Resolution No. 4602 and guidelines dated 28 May 2025, and gives the start dates as 18 June 2025 for reclassifying existing permits and 1 July 2025 for new arrivals; EIG gives 5 July 2025 and 3 August 2025. The two firms' dates conflict — [KPMG Flash Alert 2025-130](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/flash-alert-2025-130.html); [Erickson Immigration Group](https://eiglaw.com/saudi-arabia-introduces-skill-based-classification-for-work-permits/)
- A relocation firm's guide (updated 1 Sep 2026) states the tiers as: high-skilled "hold at least a Bachelor's degree and demonstrate a minimum of 5 years of relevant professional experience", salary SAR 15,000+; skilled, secondary education and 2 years, SAR 7,000-14,999; basic, SAR 3,000-6,999 — [Jobbatical](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/saudi-arabia-visa-residence-permit-hr-guide)

Saudi Arabia: qualification verification
- The Professional Verification programme was expanded to 160 countries and covers 1,007 professions in occupation groups 1-3, "such as engineering and health roles", to ensure workers meet "academic, scientific, and practical skill requirements ... before arriving in the Kingdom"; run by MHRSD with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs — [Gulf News, 21 Jan 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-expands-professional-verification-programme-to-160-countries-1.500019969)
- "The Qualification Verification Program (QVP) confirms that an expat's degrees and professional credentials are genuine and match the job they were hired for." "After an expansion announced in December 2024, QVP now applies to all industries. Most professionals applying for a Saudi work visa today must clear it, whatever their field." Technical trades go to the Skill Verification Program instead — [Wego, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://blog.wego.com/saudi-qualification-verification-program/)
- For Pakistani degree holders the attestation chain is fixed: Higher Education Commission, then Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then the Saudi embassy; HEC launched an online Degree Attestation System in May 2026 (search snippet) — [Wego](https://blog.wego.com/mofa-attestation-pakistan/)

Saudi Arabia: Saudi Council of Engineers (SCE)
- If the Iqama carries an engineering job title, SCE registration is required; without it "Your Iqama cannot be renewed under an engineering title, you may be reclassified under a different profession". The titles listed are traditional disciplines (civil, electrical, mechanical, telecommunications, electronics and so on); software and IT titles are not mentioned. Requires an "Attested engineering degree" — [Arab Manpower, 14 Jun 2025](https://arabmanpower.net/do-you-need-sce-registration-to-work-in-saudi-arabia-as-an-engineer-heres-who-it-applies-to-and-why-its-required/)
- SCE professional accreditation requires a "Bachelor of Engineering or equivalent", and "Registration for those with less than five years of experience shall not be accepted, unless they are Saudi" (from a search snippet of the SCE page; the page itself could not be loaded) — [Saudi Council of Engineers](https://saudieng.sa/English/Eservices/Accreditation/Pages/GoalsAndConditions.aspx)
- Expat-forum reports (anecdotal): a computer science bachelor's holder is placed by SCE in the "Allied Engineering Specialist" category, and a "programmer" profession on the Iqama does not need registration with any authority (search snippets) — [Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/en/forum/middle-east/saudi-arabia/909511-what-is-allied-engineering-specialist.html); [Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/en/forum/middle-east/saudi-arabia/999514-profession-change-for-technician-computer-programmer-sce.html)

UAE
- MOHRE's four conditions for a "skilled" worker: professional level 1 to 5; a certificate higher than secondary; the certificate attested by the competent authorities; monthly salary not less than AED 4,000. Level 1 is legislators, managers and business executives; level 2 is professionals in scientific, technical and human fields; level 3 is technicians (search snippet) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/do-you-qualify-as-a-skilled-worker-in-uae-heres-why-it-matters-1.1671544641684)

What the postings themselves ask (n = 32, from key question 1)
- 14 state a bachelor's (or bachelor's/master's) degree as a requirement; 2 prefer an advanced degree (Deloitte, IBM); 2 accept "equivalent practical experience" (Landmark Group in Dubai, Scale AI in Doha); 14 state no degree requirement in the text read.

### Inferences
- For a candidate whose bachelor's completes in 2027, the Saudi risk is not one single "degree required" rule but three interacting ones: (a) the high-skilled permit tier is built around a bachelor's, though points can offset it; (b) whatever qualification the employer declares must pass verification, and an unfinished degree cannot be verified as a degree; (c) an "Engineer" title on the Iqama triggers SCE, which needs a completed engineering-type degree. A non-engineer occupation title (for example programmer, developer, specialist or consultant-type titles) avoids (c) but not (a) or (b).
- Because the candidate is already resident in Riyadh, the binding question is what profession and tier the current Iqama carries and whether a new employer would need to change the profession; transfers that keep a non-degree-gated profession are simpler than new visas under a professional title. This is an inference; it was not confirmed against Qiwa or MHRSD text.
- In the UAE, a mainland MOHRE permit under a level 1-2 title (Software Engineer, AI Architect) needs an attested degree. Without one, the realistic routes are a level 3 technician-type title with an attested diploma-level certificate, or a free-zone or freelance arrangement; these alternatives were not verified in this scan.
- Half of the verified postings state a degree requirement and only two explicitly accept equivalent experience (neither in Saudi Arabia), so the degree is also an employer-screening issue, independent of immigration rules.

### Gaps
