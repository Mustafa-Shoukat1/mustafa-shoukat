Research date: 12 October 2026. Scope: independents with 1-3 branches outside London. Method: 12 web searches (shared search budget then ran out), ~95 direct page fetches. ## 1. Services inventory and the residential sales workflow

### Takeaway
Small independents outside London typically bundle residential sales + lettings (let-only and fully managed) from one or two offices, and bolt on auctions (via iamsold/Pattinson/SDL white-label), mortgage referrals and occasionally commercial; block management, land and new homes are rare. The sales pipeline in 2025 ran at roughly 1.26m sales agreed, 0.99m exchanges and 0.30m fall-throughs, with 123 days from sale agreed to exchange, so chasing a stalled chain is a core daily activity.

Sales workflow steps and documents evidenced:
- Instruction/agency agreement and the Estate Agents Act 1979: the National Trading Standards Estate and Letting Agency Team says Powys County Council (lead authority for estate agency across the UK) "issues prohibition and formal warning orders to those found unfit to engage in estate agency work in the UK"; NTS published Part A material information guidance on 12 July 2022; published an opinion on 5 May 2022 that "price on application" in listings is unlawful; and issued an "NTSEAT statement - conditional selling" on 15 July 2025 — [National Trading Standards, Estate and Letting Agency Team](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-agency-team/)
- AML customer due diligence on both sides of a sale: HMRC's updated guidance (published 8 July 2026 after regulation amendments in force 30 June 2026) says an estate agency business "must apply for registration with HMRC before trading", "CDD must be undertaken for all customers", businesses must "identify and verify the identity of all parties to a property transaction", "Extra care should always be taken with cash buyers", firms must "document both the steps taken and the rationale for their conclusions" on source of funds, keep "an up-to-date written risk assessment" covering money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing, and when using third-party ID tools "responsibility for compliance with the MLRs remains with the estate agency business"; providers not on the DVS register "cannot be relied upon as a robust means of identity verification" — [Devonshires, 7 Oct 2026](https://insights.devonshires.com/post/102nbd1/hmrc-updates-anti-money-laundering-guidance-for-estate-agency-businesses-key-tak)

- Time from sale agreed to exchange: UK average 123 days in 2025 (4.1 months) vs 121 days in 2024 and 91 days in 2019; by region (months): Outer London 4.6, East of England 4.6, South East 4.5, South West 4.3, Inner London 4.2, Northern Ireland 4.1, North West 4.0, East Midlands 4.0, West Midlands 3.9, Yorkshire and the Humber 3.8, Wales 3.8, North East 3.6, Scotland 2.9; TwentyEA's "Project 28" aims to cut sale-agreed-to-exchange from 100+ days to 28; fall-through risk rises "especially when interest rates or economic conditions shift partway through the process" — [Property Reporter, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/time-to-exchange-stretches-to-123-days-twentyea.html)
- Causes TwentyEA cites for 2025 softness: stamp duty nil-rate threshold reverted to £125,000 on 31 March 2025 (first-time buyer threshold from £425,000 to £300,000); pre-Budget caution in H2 2025; the announced "mansion tax" on £2m+ homes from April 2028; higher second-home council tax — [Mortgage Solutions, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/01/19/exchanges-in-2025-nearly-hit-one-million/)
- Fall-through reasons: Quick Move Now data cited by HomeOwners Alliance: "Four in 10 (41%) residential property sales fell through before completion between April-June 2025"; of those, mortgage difficulties 45%, chain breakdowns 18%, buyer changing mind 14%; average seller in a failed transaction £2,727 out of pocket, one in ten more than £5,000; listed causes: buyer changes mind, gazumping, mortgage refusal or expired offer, conveyancing delays "where both parties are not as responsive as they should be", bad survey, broken chain, gazundering "just before contracts are exchanged"; "Most collapsed house sales happen before exchange" — [HomeOwners Alliance](https://hoa.org.uk/advice/guides-for-homeowners/i-am-buying/why-do-house-sales-fall-through/)
- Government reform context: the NTS material-information page notes MHCLG announced plans for homebuying "sales packs" as part of home buying and selling reforms in June 2026 — [National Trading Standards, material information page](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-and-letting-agency-team/material-information/)

### Inferences
- Using the 2025 TwentyEA counts, fall-throughs were about 24% of sales agreed (303,538 / 1,257,053), i.e. roughly one in four agreed sales never exchanges; the Quick Move Now 41% figure for Q2 2025 uses a different base/sample, so the two should not be averaged.
- With 123 days between sale agreed and exchange and about a quarter of deals failing, a two-office agency's sales staff spend a large share of each week on progression calls to solicitors, lenders, surveyors and the other agents in the chain; only one swept site (Bartlams) advertises any client-facing progression portal.
- The 1.13m price changes and 0.80m withdrawals in 2025 against 1.71m instructions imply repeated re-listing, re-pricing and re-marketing admin per instruction.

- No sourced split of time spent per sale on progression; TwentyEA quotes totals only.

## 2. Lettings workflow and the Renters' Rights Act rules from 1 May 2026

### Takeaway
Every tenancy now runs through a fixed compliance ladder (Right to Rent, referencing, written terms, deposit in 30 days, gas/EICR/EPC/alarms, information sheet) and, since 1 May 2026, all tenancies are periodic, section 21 is gone, rent rises are once a year on Form 4A with two months' notice, pets must be answered within 28 days, and councils fine up to £7,000 or £40,000; the PRS database and landlord ombudsman follow in "late 2026".

### Cited Findings

Tenancy structure and dates:
- "All assured shorthold tenancies automatically became assured periodic tenancies on 1 May 2026"; "You cannot create an assured shorthold tenancy (AST)"; "You cannot create an assured tenancy agreement with an end date" and if you do "Your tenant can complain to the council and you could be fined"; tenancies "cannot be for longer than a month at a time"; oral agreements allowed but "you must also give them the key terms in writing"; joint tenants all responsible for rent — [gov.uk, Renting out your property: guidance for landlords and letting agents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renting-out-your-property-guidance-for-landlords-and-letting-agents)
- "From 1 May 2026 you can no longer be evicted using the 'no fault' section 21 process"; court processes started before 1 May 2026 can in some cases complete after — [gov.uk, Renters' Rights Act overview for tenants](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants)
- Information sheet: landlords or agents must give tenants the government Information Sheet by 31 May 2026 or "could receive a fine" — [gov.uk tenants overview](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants); fine "up to £7,000"; applies to written or partly written tenancies that started before 1 May 2026; "Every tenant named on the agreement must receive a copy"; "Agents must also provide the sheet, even if the landlord already has"; must be "the exact PDF from GOV.UK", not a link, by post, hand, or attachment — [Woking Borough Council](https://www.woking.gov.uk/housing/private-rented-housing/renters-rights-act-information-landlords-letting-agents)
- Phases: Phase 1 from 1 May 2026; Phase 2 "from late 2026": mandatory PRS database and new PRS landlord ombudsman; Phase 3 "date to be confirmed": minimum EPC C "with a target date of 2030" and the Decent Homes Standard "including extending Awaab's Law to the private rented sector"; council investigation powers from 27 December 2025 include "entering a letting business without notice" — [Woking Borough Council](https://www.woking.gov.uk/housing/private-rented-housing/renters-rights-act-information-landlords-letting-agents)

Rent, bidding, advance rent, discrimination, pets:
- Rent can rise "only once a year, not in the first 12 months", using "Form 4A" with "at least 2 months' notice"; tenants can challenge an increase above market rent — [gov.uk tenants overview](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants)
- "Any written advertisement or offer must include a specific price"; landlords "cannot accept or encourage offers above the advertised rent"; after signing "the maximum that can be requested is 1 month's rent in advance"; no refusal for benefits or children — [gov.uk tenants overview](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants)
- Pet requests "must be answered within 28 days" with reasons for refusal — [Woking Borough Council](https://www.woking.gov.uk/housing/private-rented-housing/renters-rights-act-information-landlords-letting-agents)
- Goodlord survey: "22% of tenants expect to challenge rent increases under the new powers"; "19% of landlords plan to raise advertised prices"; "53% of landlords will set prices using comparable listings; 19% will seek advice from their letting agent" — [Goodlord, State of the Lettings Industry 2025](https://www.goodlord.com/state-of-the-lettings-industry-report-2025)

Possession and notices:
- Landlords "must give a ground for possession, use the correct forms, and usually give 4 months' notice"; sale/move-in ground "cannot be used within the first 12 months"; a student HMO ground exists; arrears and antisocial behaviour grounds; tenants give 2 months' written notice "on the rent due day or the day before"; rent repayment orders "for up to 2 years" of rent — [gov.uk tenants overview](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants)

Penalties (local authority civil penalties):
- "fines of up to £7000 for minor non-compliance or up to £40,000 for serious repeat non-compliance"; indicative amounts: not giving the information sheet £4,000; no written statement within 28 days for new tenancies £4,000; discriminating against benefit claimants or families £6,000; adverts omitting asking rent £3,000; accepting offers above advertised rent £4,000; attempting a fixed term £4,000; "Serving a section 21 notice after the 1st of May 2026 - £6000"; relying on a possession ground without reasonable belief £6,000; re-letting within the 12-month no-let period after the sale/move-in ground £25,000; rent repayment orders "up to two years' rent"; prosecution in magistrates' courts with unlimited fines for serious repeat cases — [Anthony Collins, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.anthonycollins.com/blog/102lxyr/renters-rights-act-guidance-and-fines-for-private-landlords/)
- MHCLG published "Civil penalties under the Renters' Rights Act 2025 and other housing legislation" on 13 November 2025 for councils, covering the Protection from Eviction Act 1977, Housing Act 1988, Housing Act 2004, Housing and Planning Act 2016 and Renters' Rights Act 2025 — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-penalties-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation)

Pre-tenancy compliance ladder:
- Right to Rent: "you must check all tenants aged 18 and over" even if not named on the agreement; "Check all new tenants"; checks "Before the start of a new tenancy"; for time-limited permission "in the 28 days before the start of the tenancy"; the page notes rules change on 1 May 2026 — [gov.uk, Check tenant right to rent](https://www.gov.uk/check-tenant-right-to-rent-documents); landlords can ask managing/letting agents to do the check: "You should have this agreement in writing" — [gov.uk, Agents and subletting](https://www.gov.uk/check-tenant-right-to-rent-documents/agents-and-subletting); civil penalties: lodgers £5,000 first / £10,000 further; tenants £10,000 first / £20,000 further; knowingly renting to someone without the right: up to 5 years' prison — [gov.uk, Penalties for illegal renting](https://www.gov.uk/penalties-illegal-renting)
- Deposit: "You (or your letting agent) must put your tenants' deposit in the scheme within 30 days of getting it"; holding deposit need not be protected until the applicant becomes a tenant, then "the holding deposit becomes a deposit, and you must protect it"; in an insured scheme the agent must pass the disputed deposit to the scheme — [gov.uk, Deposit protection schemes and landlords](https://www.gov.uk/deposit-protection-schemes-and-landlords); three schemes (Deposit Protection Service, MyDeposits, Tenancy Deposit Scheme); "Your landlord must return your deposit within 10 days of you both agreeing how much you'll get back" — [gov.uk, Tenancy deposit protection](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection)
- Gas: landlord must have "a registered engineer do an annual gas safety check on each appliance and flue" and "give you a copy of the gas safety check record before you move in, or within 28 days of the check"; "provide a smoke alarm on each storey"; CO alarm "in any room with a solid fuel burning appliance"; furniture "fire safe" — [gov.uk, Your landlord's safety responsibilities](https://www.gov.uk/private-renting/your-landlords-safety-responsibilities)
- Electrical: "have the electrics in their rented properties checked at least every 5 years by a properly qualified person" and "give their tenants proof" — [gov.uk, Electrical safety standards in the PRS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector-guidance-for-landlords-tenants-and-local-authorities)
- Landlord duty list: Right to Rent (England), deposit in approved scheme, gas and electrical safety, "provide an Energy Performance Certificate", "fit and test smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms", fire safety rules, HHSRS inspections scoring 29 hazard areas as category 1 or 2 — [gov.uk, Landlord responsibilities](https://www.gov.uk/renting-out-a-property/landlord-responsibilities)
- EPC/MEES: "landlords can no longer let or continue to let properties covered by the MEES Regulations if they have an EPC rating below E"; spend cap "£3,500 (including VAT)"; public Exemptions Register; "The maximum amount you can be fined per property is £5,000 in total"; government aims for as many PRS homes as possible to reach EPC C by 2030 and is "exploring policy design options following the 2026 consultation" — [gov.uk, Domestic PRS MEES landlord guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-private-rented-property-minimum-energy-efficiency-standard-landlord-guidance)
- How to Rent guide: "This publication was withdrawn on 1 May 2026" "due to changes from the Renters' Rights Act"; retained only for tenancies where a section 21 notice was served before 1 May 2026; last substantive version October 2023 — [gov.uk, How to rent](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-rent)
- Tenant Fees Act guidance: withdrawn 8 May 2026 as "no longer up to date" and replaced by RRA-updated guidance; the Act became retrospective on 1 June 2020 — [gov.uk, Tenant Fees Act 2019 guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tenant-fees-act-2019-guidance)
- HMO: mandatory licence when "rented to 5 or more people who form more than 1 household", sharing facilities, with rent paid; "A licence is valid for a maximum of 5 years"; conditions include sending the council "an updated gas safety certificate every year", smoke alarms, electrical certificates on request, "fit and proper" manager; "You could get an unlimited fine for renting out an unlicensed HMO"; smaller properties may need a licence "depending on the area" — [gov.uk, HMO licence](https://www.gov.uk/house-in-multiple-occupation-licence)

Rent collection, statements and non-resident landlords:
- Non-resident Landlord Scheme: agent registers on form NRL4; must deduct basic-rate tax unless HMRC has approved gross payment in writing; pay "within 30 days of the end of each tax quarter" (quarters to 30 June, 30 September, 31 December, 31 March); annual return NRLY and certificate NRL6 "by 5 July each year"; keep records 4 years — [gov.uk, Paying tax on rent to landlords abroad](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/paying-tax-on-rent-to-landlords-abroad)
- PayProp markets "Reduce and manage tenant arrears through text and e-mail reminders", real-time bank reconciliation and client accounts "with 100% accurate totals"; claims 1,300+ UK letting agencies and £2.6bn processed annually — [PayProp UK](https://www.payprop.com/uk)
- Goodlord describes each tenancy as "a queue of tasks: qualify, reference, sign, pay, chase, document"; claims "80 %" admin-time reduction and "40 +" compliance steps automated; says agents need not spend hours on the phone to utility suppliers at tenancy end — [Goodlord](https://www.goodlord.com/)

Market and agency shape (Goodlord 2025 survey of 2,750+ agents, landlords, tenants: 350+ agents, 1,350+ landlords, ~1,000 tenants):
- "The average firm manages 340 properties and employs 14 people"; "54% of agencies have five or fewer staff"; agencies earn "27% of annual revenue from tenancy renewals" and the report says abolishing fixed terms removes this income; "34% of landlords cite keeping compliant with rental regulations as a main benefit of using an agent"; 38% cite referencing; 44% cite sourcing tenants; "47% of agencies are experimenting with AI"; 67% of agents report rising demand; 30% report fewer available properties; "43% of landlords self-manage; 26% have used a letting agent in the past but no longer do"; almost 1 in 5 landlords sold at least one property in the past 12 months — [Goodlord, State of the Lettings Industry 2025](https://www.goodlord.com/state-of-the-lettings-industry-report-2025)
- TwentyEA 2025 lettings: average let agreed £1,495 pcm; new letting instructions +9%; lets agreed +6%; Northern Ireland the only region where lets agreed fell (-6.3%) — [Mortgage Solutions, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/01/19/exchanges-in-2025-nearly-hit-one-million/)

Devolved differences (only where a swept agency is there):
- Scotland (Westport, Dundee): letting agents must "follow a Letting Agent Code of Practice" and "be registered on the Scottish Letting Agent Register"; unregistered letting agency work is a criminal offence with "a fine of up to £50,000, up to 6 months imprisonment or both" — [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/letting-agent-registration); Westport displays "LARN 1902016" — [Westport Property](https://www.westportproperty.co.uk/)
- Wales (Bay, Swansea; Reid and Roberts, Wrexham): Rent Smart Wales requires landlord registration and a licence for agents and self-managing landlords ("must obtain a licence to do so"), with training, under the Housing (Wales) Act 2014; the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016 regime applies — [Rent Smart Wales](https://www.rentsmart.gov.wales/en/)

### Inferences
- For a 14-person agency managing 340 homes, the one-off 31 May 2026 information-sheet exercise meant 340 PDF-by-post-or-attachment deliveries to every named tenant, each a potential £4,000 penalty if missed.
- Renewals previously generated around a quarter of revenue and a large admin cycle (new fixed-term agreements); periodic tenancies remove that cycle but replace it with an annual Form 4A rent-review cycle and more tribunal challenges (22% of tenants say they expect to challenge).
- "Fully managed" from a small agency means the agent, not the landlord, now carries the 28-day pet-response clock, the 30-day deposit clock, the annual gas and 5-yearly EICR clocks and the quarterly NRL tax calendar for each property.

## 3. Property and block management

### Takeaway
Small block/service-charge work turns on the section 20 consultation thresholds (£250 per leaseholder for works, £100 a year for long-term agreements), a statutory right to summaries and receipts, and tribunal challenge; Awaab's Law applies to social landlords from 27 October 2025 and the Decent Homes Standard for private renting is being reported for 2035, with MPs pressing for Awaab's Law in the PRS sooner.

### Cited Findings
- Leaseholders must be consulted if they would pay "over £250 for planned work, or over £100 a year for work or services lasting more than 12 months"; "There are steps your landlord must follow when they consult you, known as a 'Section 20' consultation"; "There's a limit on how much you have to pay if you have not been consulted properly"; leaseholders can request a summary of how a service charge is calculated and "see any paperwork supporting the summary, such as receipts" and "it's a criminal offence if they do not"; charges can be challenged at a tribunal; unpaid ground rent recoverable "going back 6 years" — [gov.uk, Leasehold property: service charges](https://www.gov.uk/leasehold-property/service-charges-and-other-expenses)
- Property Redress 2025: "Property management and leasehold" complaints centre on service quality, maintenance and access to documentation; leasehold "described as among the most entrenched areas of complaint" — [Property Reporter, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/lettings-complaints-drive-sharp-rise-in-property-redress-enquiries.html)
- Awaab's Law regulations for social landlords "came into force on 27 October 2025"; further guidance supports "Phase 2 of Awaab's Law" — [gov.uk, Awaab's Law guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-draft-guidance-for-social-landlords)
- Housing Committee (reported 3 Jul 2026): MPs criticised the "lack of a time frame" for Awaab's Law in the PRS, urged roll-out "this year" and protections "fully in place across the private rented sector by the end of 2028/29"; warned the Decent Homes Standard "2035 deadline" would see landlords wait; MEES "take effect in 2030"; councils should enforce the whole standard, "not only Criterion A" — [Mortgage Solutions, 3 Jul 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/07/03/landlord-fees-should-fund-prs-regulatory-enforcement-mps-say/)
- Simply-Docs: Decent Homes Standard "will be likely implemented in 2035 due to secondary legislation and consultation requirements" and is "the final phase of the Renters' Rights Act"; it also states "By 2028, new tenancies will require an EPC rating of C" — [Simply-Docs](https://simply-docs.co.uk/Renters-Rights-Bill/The-Renters-Rights-Bill-Awaabs-Law-and-the-Decent-Homes-Standard); this 2028 claim conflicts with gov.uk, which gives a 2030 aim and says policy design is still being explored — [gov.uk MEES guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-private-rented-property-minimum-energy-efficiency-standard-landlord-guidance)

## 4. Regulatory and administrative burdens stacked on top

### Takeaway
A two-branch independent doing sales and lettings must simultaneously satisfy HMRC AML supervision (CDD on every party, written risk assessment, registration before trading), the DMCC/CPR material-information regime policed by NTSELAT, redress-scheme membership (TPO or PRS, £5,000 fine), client money protection, Propertymark/RICS codes where joined, the Renters' Rights Act penalty schedule, Right to Rent, deposit, gas, electrical, EPC, HMO/selective licensing and the NRL tax calendar; HMRC's latest half-year list fined estate agency businesses 170 times (£835,842), mostly for not being registered at all.

### Cited Findings

## 8. Consolidated inventory of manual, repetitive, document-heavy or deadline-driven steps (cross-reference to sources above)

### Takeaway
The recurring work is calendar-driven (30-day deposit, 28-day gas record and pet response, 31 May information sheet, annual gas, 5-yearly EICR, quarterly NRL tax, annual Form 4A, 5-year HMO licence) and document-driven (ID/CDD files on every party, written risk assessment, material information before listing, written terms, prescribed information, certificates to tenants and councils), while the daily load is chasing (solicitors in a 123-day exchange window, arrears, contractors, out-of-hours repairs).

### Cited Findings (each already cited above; grouped by trigger)
- Before listing a sale: HMRC registration and CDD on all parties with documented rationale and written risk assessment — [Devonshires](https://insights.devonshires.com/post/102nbd1/hmrc-updates-anti-money-laundering-guidance-for-estate-agency-businesses-key-tak); material information Parts A (2022) and B/C (2023) gathered for the listing — [NTS](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-agency-team/), [Conveyancing Association](https://www.conveyancingassociation.org.uk/category/coverage/page/3); no "price on application" — [NTS](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-agency-team/)
- During a sale: 123 days sale-agreed to exchange, 24% of agreed sales fall through, 1.13m price changes and 0.80m withdrawals a year — [Property Reporter](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/time-to-exchange-stretches-to-123-days-twentyea.html), [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/01/19/exchanges-in-2025-nearly-hit-one-million/)
- Before a let: Right to Rent on every adult, 28-day window for time-limited status, written agent agreement — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/check-tenant-right-to-rent-documents); advertised price mandatory, no bidding, max 1 month advance — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants); key terms in writing; no end date — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renting-out-your-property-guidance-for-landlords-and-letting-agents); gas record before move-in; smoke alarm each storey — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/private-renting/your-landlords-safety-responsibilities); EICR proof — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector-guidance-for-landlords-tenants-and-local-authorities); EPC at E or better, exemptions register — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-private-rented-property-minimum-energy-efficiency-standard-landlord-guidance); HMO licence and annual gas certificate to council — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/house-in-multiple-occupation-licence)
- Within 30 days of money received: deposit protected (holding deposit converts) — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/deposit-protection-schemes-and-landlords)
- One-off by 31 May 2026: exact PDF information sheet to every named tenant, by agents as well as landlords, £7,000 maximum / £4,000 indicative penalty — [Woking](https://www.woking.gov.uk/housing/private-rented-housing/renters-rights-act-information-landlords-letting-agents), [Anthony Collins](https://www.anthonycollins.com/blog/102lxyr/renters-rights-act-guidance-and-fines-for-private-landlords/)
- During a tenancy: pet requests answered within 28 days — [Woking](https://www.woking.gov.uk/housing/private-rented-housing/renters-rights-act-information-landlords-letting-agents); annual Form 4A rent notice with 2 months' notice, challengeable — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants); arrears reminders — [PayProp](https://www.payprop.com/uk); repairs incl. out-of-hours — [Fixflo](https://www.fixflo.com/); quarterly NRL tax within 30 days of quarter end, NRLY/NRL6 by 5 July, 4-year records — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/paying-tax-on-rent-to-landlords-abroad)
- Ending a tenancy: grounds-based notices, usually 4 months, correct forms, 12-month no-let after sale/move-in ground with £25,000 indicative penalty; tenant 2 months' notice on rent day — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants), [Anthony Collins](https://www.anthonycollins.com/blog/102lxyr/renters-rights-act-guidance-and-fines-for-private-landlords/); deposit returned within 10 days of agreement — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection); utilities calls at tenancy end — [Goodlord](https://www.goodlord.com/)
- Block work: section 20 thresholds £250/£100, summaries and receipts on request (criminal offence to withhold), tribunal — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/leasehold-property/service-charges-and-other-expenses)
- Always: redress membership (TPO/PRS) or £5,000 fine — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/redress-scheme-estate-agencies); CMP and prohibited-fee enforcement by Bristol — [NTS](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-agency-team/); complaints procedure provided on request and written records kept — [Propertymark/TPO](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/resource/ombudsman-s-casework-highlights-practical-lessons-for-property-professionals.html)

### Inferences
- The same small team (median agency 5 or fewer staff per Goodlord) handles every one of these clocks across both sales and lettings, which is why the top complaint categories at both schemes are communication, record-keeping and management rather than fraud or valuation error.

### Gaps
- No time-and-motion data on hours per tenancy or per sale for a small agency was found; the only quantified claims are vendor ones (Goodlord "80%" admin reduction) and should be treated as marketing.
