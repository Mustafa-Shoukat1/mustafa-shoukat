# Search language, job ads and freelance gigs: how small UK and US real estate businesses describe the help they need

Scope note: UK and US only; small brokerages, teams, estate and letting agencies, property managers and landlords. Data pulled 11-12 Oct 2026. Problem and language research only; no solutions proposed. The shared web-search budget ran out after the first round, so most evidence comes from direct fetches of job boards (Reed, Indeed UK, OnlineJobs.ph, Upwork job and services search, PeoplePerHour) and from the Google autocomplete endpoint (gl=us and gl=uk). Fiverr, Reddit, ZipRecruiter, Totaljobs, Craigslist (JS-rendered), Zillow and HUD glossaries, and the Indeed US job-description pages for transaction coordinator, maintenance coordinator and real estate assistant could not be fetched (403/404); these are recorded as gaps.

## Key question 1: Which duties appear most often in small-business ads in the UK and the US, and what do they pay?

### Takeaway
UK ads (lettings negotiator, sales negotiator, sales progressor, lettings/property administrator, property manager) are dominated by "viewings", "referencing", "deposit" administration, "gas safety / EICR / EPC" certificates, "maintenance / contractors", "arrears", "inspections / check-in / check-out" and "progressing sales to exchange and completion", at £19,000-£35,000 basic (£25,000 is the modal negotiator basic on Reed in Sept-Oct 2026). US ads (transaction coordinator, ISA, listing coordinator, leasing agent, property manager, real estate VA) are dominated by "contract to close", "follow up with leads", "update the CRM", "schedule showings", "MLS" entry, "drip campaigns / action plans", "maintenance requests / work orders / vendors" and "cold calling / dials per shift"; onshore US pay is $15-$21/hour for admin and leasing and $33,000-$48,000 national averages for coordinator roles, while the same duty lists posted to OnlineJobs.ph and Upwork pay $2-$12/hour or $450-$1,600/month.

- inventory / check-in / check-out report (UK) versus move-in / move-out inspection (US)
- gas safety certificate (CP12), EICR, EPC (UK) versus no direct equivalent; US ads cite HUD, REAC, Fair Housing instead
- HMO licensing / selective licensing (UK) versus HOA (US, a different concept: homeowner association)
- section 8 notice / section 13 notice / section 21 (UK) versus eviction notice, "3-day, 5-day" notices, notice to quit (US; "notice to quit" did not appear in fetched ads)

- let-only / tenant find only versus fully managed (UK) versus tenant placement versus full-service management (US; the US pair did not surface in fetched ads, only the UK pair did)

- block management / resident management companies (UK) versus HOA or community association management (US)

### Inferences
- A single vocabulary cannot serve both markets: eight of the ten most frequent UK duty phrases (viewings, referencing, deposit, gas safety/EICR/EPC, right to rent, check-in/out, arrears, exchange and completion) have no exact US counterpart in US ads, and vice versa (contract to close, MLS, showings, escrow, Fair Housing).
- Even the shared word "property manager" means different work: UK ads attach certificates, deposits and section notices; US ads attach work orders, vendors, HAP/HUD and evictions.

### Gaps
- No US glossary page could be fetched (Zillow 403, HUD glossary 403, NAR glossary 404, HomeLight 404, Rocket Mortgage 500), so US definitions are taken from the ads themselves rather than from a reference source.
- "Owner statement", "full-service management", "notice to quit" and "listing agreement" in a property-management context were not found verbatim in the US ads sampled; they appear only in the question brief and in the one PeoplePerHour gig ("Owner-Ready Financials").
