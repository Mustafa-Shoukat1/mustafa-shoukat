## Key Question 1: United States — agent economics, leads, transactions, property management, software, pay

### Takeaway
The typical US Realtor is a solo independent contractor (86%) earning a median $59,200 gross and $37,000 net on 9 transaction sides a year at 35 hours a week, with 15% of members in their first two years earning a median $8,000; one in twenty contracts is terminated and one in seven is delayed, closings take 30 days (median) to 43 days (lender data), and purchased internet leads convert at 1–3% while 48% of inquiries in the only independent audit were never answered. Property-management, transaction-coordinator and eviction figures exist only from vendors and aggregators; no public NARPM/AppFolio benchmark for units per manager or turn days was reachable.

### Inferences
- Net median income of $37,000 on 35 hours/week implies roughly $20/hour before the agent's own unpaid time; at 9 sides a year, the median agent closes less than one transaction a month, so each lost lead or failed contract is a large share of annual output.
- At $223 per Zillow lead and a 1–3% close rate, a solo agent needs $7,400–22,300 of lead spend per closing, which exceeds the median net commission in the Clever example ($7,000); the economics only work at the 4%+ conversion Zillow itself sets as its Preferred standard.
- 48% non-response (2013) plus 68% of consumers hiring the first agent who answers means slow response is a direct revenue leak, but the only independent audit is 13 years old; everything newer is vendor marketing.
- The 2025-to-2026 shift in NAR tech-survey AI figures (32% never used to 21% non-users; "no impact" 46% to 35%) shows adoption is rising but a third of agents still see no business effect, and cost (59%) and learning curve (63%) are the stated barriers for small operators.
- No reachable benchmark gives units per property manager, maintenance requests per unit, or days to turn a unit from a primary source; the 56% "hired for maintenance" and 35–45% staff-time figures are the only quantification of the maintenance burden and are vendor-sourced.

## Key Question 2: United Kingdom — agencies, sales and lettings timings, fees, compliance, complaints, software, pay

### Takeaway
UK sales agencies wait a record 216 days from listing to completion (62 to find a buyer, 154 from agreed sale to completion, 36 days longer than 2019), lose about one in four agreed sales (23.7–24% fall-through rate; 303,538 fall-throughs in 2025 costing roughly £3,550 each, £1.065bn in total), and earn an average £3,909 per completed sale at about 1.3–1.42% inc VAT. Lettings agencies average 340 managed properties and 14 staff (54% have five or fewer), face 6–9 applicants per property, lose 27% of revenue tied to tenancy renewals under the Renters' Rights Act, and operate under rising enforcement (170 AML penalties totalling £835,842 in six months; 73,035 ombudsman contacts in 2024, up 27%).

### Inferences
- With £3,909 average fee and a 23.7–24% fall-through rate, roughly one in four agreed sales produces no fee after an average 154-day wait; TwentyCi's 303,538 fall-throughs against 986,665 exchanges means about 31 fall-throughs for every 100 completions in 2025.
- A 340-property, 14-person agency (Goodlord average) is about 24 managed properties per employee; the 54% of firms with five or fewer staff manage proportionally smaller books but face the same compliance load.
- Losing 27% of revenue tied to renewals, plus 50% of agents expecting more admin from the Renters' Rights Act, is the clearest quantified margin threat for small lettings firms.
- AML penalties average under £5,000 but 90% are for not registering at all, which points to a knowledge/admin failure concentrated in small firms rather than sophisticated breaches.
- TPO's record 73,035 contacts against 19,495 member offices is roughly 3.7 contacts per office per year; lettings generates the most enquiries and tenants and landlords complain in near-equal numbers.

## Key Question 3: Both markets — AI and CRM adoption, spreadsheets, admin hours

### Takeaway
Roughly half of small operators in both countries are now touching AI (US agents 48% weekly in 2026, up from 42% daily-or-weekly in 2025; UK lettings agencies 47% experimenting; US property managers 58% adopting), but a third of US agents report no business impact, cost and learning curve are the top barriers, and no primary source quantifies the share of small firms without a CRM or the hours per week spent on admin.

### Cited Findings
- US agents (NAR 2025): 32% had never used AI; 20% daily, 22% weekly; 46% saw no impact; 67% say their brokerage supplies all needed tools; 34% spend $50–250/month on technology, 20% $251–500, 24% over $500 — [HGAR, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.hgar.com/nar-survey-finds-realtors-embrace-ai-and-digital-tools-to-enhance-client-service)
- US agents (NAR 2026): 48% use AI at least weekly; non-users down to 21%; interest in AI CRM 51%; barriers: learning curve 63%, cost 59%, too many options 44%, privacy 36% — [Real Estate News, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/09/22/agents-branching-out-from-generative-ai-as-adoption-grows)
- US brokerages pay for transaction management (39%), eSignature (37%) and CRM (36%) for their agents; agents pay for their own social tools — [Florida Realtors, Sep 2025](https://floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2025/09/nar-realtors-embrace-ai-digital-tools) (search summary only)
- US agents name social media, CRM and the local MLS as their top lead-generating tools — [NAR Quick Real Estate Statistics](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/quick-real-estate-statistics)
- US property managers: AI adoption 58% in 2025 (20% in 2024); 8% have fully automated any process — [Buildium 2026 report page](https://www.buildium.com/resource/2026-property-management-industry-report/) (vendor)
- UK lettings agencies: 47% experimenting with AI to ease workload — [Goodlord SOLI 2025](https://www.goodlord.com/state-of-the-lettings-industry-report-2025) (vendor)
- US agents: 73% maintain a business website; 96% use a smartphone daily for business — [HousingWire, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2026-member-profile-experience/)
- Admin burden (vendor claims only): agents with support staff reclaim 13–15 hours/week; the hire trigger cited is more than 15 hours/week on admin — [Stealth Agents, 15 May 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/real-estate-virtual-assistant-hiring-cost-what-agents-actually-pay-in-2025) (vendor); ~45 hours of work per residential transaction with ~30 on paperwork, attributed to NAR without a citation — [ListedKit, 2026](https://www.listedkit.com/resources/real-estate-transaction-management-cost) (vendor; attribution unverified)

### Inferences
- The "no impact" share (35% US 2026) and the "experimenting" wording (UK 47%) suggest adoption is shallow; combined with 59% citing cost as a barrier, small firms are trialling free generative tools rather than paying for integrated systems.
- Because 86% of US agents are independent contractors and only 36% have CRM paid for by their brokerage, a majority of solo agents carry their own CRM cost or go without; no source quantifies the "without" share.
