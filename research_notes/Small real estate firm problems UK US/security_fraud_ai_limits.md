# Data-security, fraud, compliance and AI-restriction problems facing small real estate businesses (UK and US)

Research date: 12 October 2026. Scope: estate and letting agents, brokerages, agent teams and property managers; problems only, no solutions or vendors. Method note: the shared web-search budget ran out part-way through; the remainder was done by direct fetches of known URLs and local text extraction of four PDFs (FBI IC3 2023, 2024, 2025 reports; ALTA 2026 seller-impersonation study). Items marked "(search snippet only)" come from a search-result summary where the page itself returned 403/404 on fetch; items marked "(large-company data)" come from institutional or multifamily samples, not small firms.

## Key question 1: Fraud and cyber incidents hitting these businesses

### Takeaway
Real estate fraud reported to the FBI jumped to 12,368 complaints and $275.1m in 2025 (from $173.6m in 2024 and $145.2m in 2023), with over-60 victims alone losing $123.7m; seller-impersonation attempts more than doubled between ALTA's 2024 and 2026 surveys, and half of firms with a paid claim lost over $100,000. In the UK, tenancy-application fraud rose about 40% in 2025 with AI-built false identities, rental-scam losses reached £12.8m in FY2025/26 even as report counts fell, and HMRC fined 170 estate agents £835,842 in a single six-month list in 2025.

### Inferences
- Average loss per FBI real-estate complaint was about $22,200 in 2025 ($275.1m / 12,368) and about $50,000 for over-60 victims ($123.7m / 2,473); the category is not limited to wire fraud, so these blend rental scams, investment fraud and closing-fund theft.
- Reported US real-estate losses fell sharply from 2022 ($397m) to 2023 ($145m) and have since climbed every year; the 2025 IC3 "AI Related" tag is new, so there is no like-for-like AI trend before 2025.
- UK rental-fraud report counts are falling while the loss per victim rises, which suggests fewer but more targeted, higher-value scams rather than a shrinking problem.
- The largest single enforcement exposure for a small UK agency is HMRC registration failure (332 of 369 fines in one 2025 list were for unregistered trading), not sophisticated laundering.

- No 2024 or 2025 cyber incident or outage at a UK agency software vendor (Reapit, Alto, etc.) was found; the search found only the 2019 Alto incident and the 2023 CTS attack.

## Key question 2: AI rules and fears, US

### Takeaway
There is no single US AI rule for small brokerages; instead agents face overlapping duties: NAR says AI content still must be accurate and fair-housing compliant and "should not go straight from prompt to publication", MLSs and California AB 723 (effective 1 Jan 2026) require disclosure of AI-altered photos with access to originals, HUD's May 2024 guidance applies the Fair Housing Act to AI screening and ad targeting, the SafeRent ($2.3m) and RealPage (DOJ settlement, state and city bans) cases show algorithmic screening and pricing liability, and TCPA treats AI voices as "artificial or prerecorded" even though the one-to-one consent rule was vacated on 24 January 2025.

### Cited Findings

**NAR and brokerage policy**
- NAR (24 Mar 2026) lists the risks it wants every brokerage AI policy to cover: hallucination ("wrong square footage or invented property features"), fair-housing drift ("who a home is 'perfect for'" or neighbourhood descriptions that become steering), privacy ("Many consumer AI tools store prompts or use them to improve their models. Entering client, financial, or transaction information into an unapproved tool could expose that data"), and that AI cannot make agency decisions, give legal advice or value property without human oversight; AI content "should not go straight from prompt to publication"; "most agents already use AI" — [NAR, Why Every Brokerage Needs an AI Use Policy](https://www.nar.realtor/news/broker-news/why-every-brokerage-needs-an-ai-use-policy)
- NAR's AI Policy Template for Brokers is member-login only; a third-party summary says it requires human review before listing content, client communications, marketing, transaction documents and valuations leave the office — [The Paperless Agent](https://thepaperlessagent.com/blog/nar-released-an-ai-policy-template-for-brokerages-here-is-what-it-means-for-you-as-an-agent) (search snippet only; secondary)
- North Carolina REALTORS: brokers "remain fully responsible for the accuracy and compliance of any content they publish, and using AI does not reduce or transfer those duties" — [NC REALTORS](https://www.ncrealtors.org/?p=56282) (search snippet only)
- Fair-housing checklist for AI-written remarks flags "perfect for young professionals", "ideal for families", "quiet neighborhood", "walking distance to church", "safe", "exclusive" as implying protected-class preferences; the article cites no complaint or enforcement action tied to AI-written ads — [HousingWire, 15 May 2026](https://housingwire.com/articles/ai-fair-housing-remarks-checklist)

**MLS and state rules on AI photos and virtual staging**
- NorthstarMLS proposed guidelines (10 Jul 2026): virtually staged, AI-generated or AI-enhanced photos must be labelled "in the photo caption, on the photo or in agent or public remarks"; each altered room needs at least one unaltered "Before" image; edits may not change or hide walls, floors, doors, windows, roofing, siding, ceilings, fireplaces, driveways or grading, or show features that do not exist; no fines stated — [NorthstarMLS](https://northstarmls.com/insights/guidelines-for-virtual-staging-and-ai-enhanced-listing-photos/)
- Other MLS rules (secondary summary): MLS Now allows staging of movable personal property only, with disclosure; CRMLS requires the unaltered original immediately before or after the enhanced image; SmartMLS requires a stamp or watermark; Canopy MLS says captions or remarks alone are not acceptable disclosure; MRED bans removing power lines or highways — [The Close](https://theclose.com/news-ai-listing-photo-rules/) and [MLS Now rules](https://support.mlsnow.com/hc/en-us/articles/47635315367067-MLS-Now-Rules-Section-3-Images-Media-Virtual-Staging) (search snippets only; MLS Now page rate-limited on fetch)
- California AB 723 (approved October 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026): brokers and salespersons must disclose digitally altered images in advertising with a "reasonably conspicuous" statement on or next to the image and give access to the unaltered original via a public website, URL or QR code; covered changes include furniture, fixtures, appliances, flooring, walls, paint colour, landscaping, facades, floor plans, window views, utility poles, streetlights and neighbouring properties; lighting, white balance, cropping and exposure are excluded; New York officials issued a November 2025 alert on misleading AI-generated listing images but no NY law has passed; no penalties are described — [The Close, 25 Jun 2026](https://theclose.com/news-ai-listing-photo-disclosure/)
- Wisconsin is also reported to have a disclosure law in effect; NAR reported in February 2026 that California and Wisconsin were adopting disclosure laws — [HousingWire, 15 May 2026](https://housingwire.com/articles/ai-fair-housing-remarks-checklist) and [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/ais-impact-real-estate-photos-023000552.html) (search snippet only)
- A Zillow spokesperson is reported as saying its content standards require listing photos to accurately represent the home — [We Get Around Network](https://www.wegetaroundnetwork.com/topic/21669/whats-new-in-proptech-july-31-2026/) (search snippet only)

**HUD fair-housing guidance on AI (2 May 2024)**
- Two documents: "FHEO Guidance on Screening of Applicants for Rental Housing" and "FHEO Guidance on Advertising through Digital Platforms"; third-party screening companies using AI "must comply with the Fair Housing Act" and give every applicant an equal chance to be evaluated on their own merits; risky ad practices named: targeting vulnerable consumers for predatory products, displaying content that could discourage or deter, charging different amounts for delivered ads; the Act prohibits both intentional discrimination and unjustified discriminatory effect — [HousingWire, 2 May 2024](https://housingwire.com/articles/hud-issues-fair-housing-act-guidance-on-ai-use); also [Housing Finance](https://www.housingfinance.com/management-operations/hud-issues-fair-housing-act-guidance-on-ai-use_s)

**Algorithmic tenant screening: SafeRent**
- Louis v. SafeRent Solutions: final approval 20 Nov 2024 by US District Judge Angel Kelley of a roughly $2.3m settlement; the 2022 Massachusetts class action alleged the SafeRent Score (credit history and non-rental debts) disproportionately harmed Black and Hispanic applicants and voucher holders and was not transparent to landlords; for five years SafeRent will not display a score or accept/deny recommendation for voucher holders nationwide; claims under the Fair Housing Act and Massachusetts law — [Cohen Milstein](https://cohenmilstein.com/ai-landlord-screening-tool-will-stop-scoring-low-income-tenants-after-discrimination-suit)

**Algorithmic rent setting: RealPage (large-company data)**
- DOJ sued RealPage and six large landlords including Greystar in August 2024; the November 2025 DOJ settlement bars RealPage from using real-time confidential competitor data, requires nonpublic training data to be at least 12 months old, imposes a court-appointed monitor for three years, no fine and no admission; ten states (California, Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Massachusetts, Minnesota, North Carolina, Oregon, Tennessee, Washington) did not sign; Greystar settled with DOJ in August 2025, paid $50m in a private class action (October 2025) and $7m to states (November 2025); Cortland settled January 2025; a Tennessee landlord settlement totalled $141.8m; New York enacted a statute making multi-landlord rent-setting software a state antitrust offence and RealPage has sued to block it; California passed similar legislation; Philadelphia and Seattle have ordinances; "Sharing competitor data, even aged and anonymized, may still face legal challenges" — [Propmodo, 22 Feb 2026](https://www.propmodo.com/the-realpage-settlement-wont-end-the-fight-over-revenue-management-software/)
- Minneapolis, Philadelphia and Berkeley passed ordinances banning RealPage-type software; San Francisco and San Diego also passed bans; Colorado's HB25-1004 passed both chambers but was vetoed by Gov. Polis; more than 22 states introduced over 40 bills on algorithmic rent-setting in 2025 (Fiscal Note) — [Private Equity Stakeholder Project](https://pestakeholder.org/news/states-pursue-legislation-against-thoma-bravo-owned-realpage-and-similar-software/) and [Common Dreams](https://www.commondreams.org/news/realpage-sues-new-york) (search snippets only)

**TCPA: AI calling and texting**
- "The One-to-One Consent Rule was scheduled to take effect on January 27, 2025. However, on January 24, 2025, the FCC issued an Order postponing the rule up to one year, and on the same day, the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals vacated the One-to-One Consent Rule"; agents "can telemarket without prior express written consent so long as they are not using a random or sequential number generating ATDS, do not use artificial voice messages, and adhere to the Do-Not-Call registry restrictions"; NAR advises knowing state "mini-TCPA" laws and asking vendors to warrant compliance or indemnify — [NAR TCPA Best Practices one-sheet, 17 Mar 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-03/TCPA-Best-Practices-NAR-One-Sheet-2025-03-17.pdf)

**E&O insurance**
- Pearl Insurance (5 Jun 2026): AI tools are "a grey area within most E&O policies"; it has not seen new AI-specific exclusions yet but warns AI language could be added; an AI chatbot that hallucinates could be the basis of a misrepresentation claim, and if a technical issue causes the error "E&O policies might not cover technology failures or unregulated AI tools"; "AI-enhanced images that lack a clear disclaimer" could lead to misrepresentation or negligence claims; agents remain responsible for the final output — [Pearl Insurance](https://pearlinsurance.com/how-does-your-eo-coverage-handle-ai-navigating-risk-alongside-evolving-technology/)

### Inferences
- For a small brokerage the practical AI exposure is less about new AI statutes and more about existing law applied to AI output: Fair Housing Act wording, misrepresentation in photos and square footage, TCPA for any AI dialer or voice agent, and FTC rules for AI-written testimonials.
- Photo-disclosure duties are fragmenting by state and MLS (label placement, before-image, QR to original), which is hard for a multi-MLS small team to track.
- The RealPage and SafeRent cases involve large vendors and large landlords, but the restrictions (no competitor-data pricing, no scoring of voucher holders) flow down to any small manager who buys those tools.

## Key question 3: AI rules and fears, UK

### Takeaway
UK agents face AI risk mainly through existing consumer-protection and data law rather than AI-specific rules: Trading Standards and trade press treat deceptive AI-edited photos as potential breaches of the CPRs 2008 / DMCC Act 2024 and Rightmove has removed AI-staged images, the Renters' Rights Act 2025 bans rental bidding and discrimination against families and benefit claimants from 1 May 2026, and Right to Rent liability stays with the agent even when a certified digital IDSP is used. No Propertymark or Property Ombudsman AI guidance document could be located.

### Cited Findings
- Trade press reports National Trading Standards saying deceptive AI edits may breach the rules, pointing to the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (images that "contain false information or deceive average consumers") and the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024; "clear labelling is the bare minimum"; in Eaglescliffe an agency whose listing stated some images were virtually staged still had pictures removed by Rightmove at the portal's request; a Daily Mail investigation found multiple agencies using AI to add furniture, brighten lighting and enhance skies — [The Negotiator, Trouble brewing over estate agents' use of AI photography](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/regulation-law-news/trouble-brewing-over-estate-agents-use-of-ai-photography/) and [Estate agent at centre of AI picture storm](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/marketing-news/estate-agent-at-centre-of-ai-picture-storm-refutes-misleading-claims/) (search snippets only; 403 on fetch)
- A photographer's view in the same coverage: using AI to remove power lines, change wall colours or manipulate key features "could lead to misrepresentation" — [The Negotiator, When Material Information meets Photoshop](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/marketing-features/property-photography-when-material-information-meets-photoshop/) (search snippet only)
- The NTS Estate and Letting Agency Team page lists its Part A material-information guidance (12 Jul 2022), its ruling that "price on application" is unlawful (5 May 2022) and a conditional-selling statement (15 Jul 2025), issues Tenant Fees Act financial penalties for prohibited fees, missing redress-scheme membership or missing client money protection, and prohibition orders against people unfit for estate agency work; it says nothing about AI, photos or digital content — [National Trading Standards](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-agency-team/)
- Renters' Rights Act 2025 received Royal Assent on 27 October 2025 — [GOV.UK guide, updated 6 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-bill/guide-to-the-renters-rights-bill); Phase 1 from 1 May 2026: landlords and agents must publish an asking rent and "cannot ask for, encourage, or accept offers above the advertised rent", cannot take more than one month's rent in advance, and cannot make a tenant less likely to rent, or prevent them renting, because they have children or receive benefits, including withholding availability information, stopping a viewing or refusing a tenancy; civil penalties "will be expanded", rent repayment orders extend to superior landlords with the maximum doubled to 24 months and repeat offenders paying the maximum; Phase 2 (late 2026) brings the mandatory PRS database with an annual fee and the landlord ombudsman — [Gloucester City Council](https://www.gloucester.gov.uk/housing/renters-rights-act-2025)
- Council investigatory powers under the Act came into force on 27 December 2025; the discrimination provisions also extend to Wales and Scotland — [Woking Borough Council](https://www.woking.gov.uk/renters-rights-act) and [North Herts summary](https://democracy.north-herts.gov.uk/documents/s30639/Appendix%20B%20-%20Renters%20Rights%20Act%20-%20Summary.pdf) (search snippets only)
- Right to Rent: a statutory excuse requires a manual in-person check, an IDVT check via a certified Identity Service Provider, or the Home Office online check; Propertymark says the Code of Practice "contains some ambiguity over liability for a civil penalty where an agent uses an IDSP" and "Ultimate responsibility for a check remains with the landlord or designated letting agent", so a certified IDSP "does not remove all risk" of a civil penalty; IDSPs are "a chargeable service" and under the Tenant Fees Act 2019 the cost cannot be passed to applicants; Nathan Emerson: "Lettings agencies are facing significant cost increases to remain compliant" — [Propertymark, 14 Jul 2022](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/resource/cost-increases-faced-with-end-of-adjusted-right-to-rent-checks.html)
- Penalty scale from February 2024 (up to £20,000 per occupier for repeat breaches) — [Landlord Today, 29 May 2024](https://landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2024/5/huge-surge-in-right-to-rent-checks-as-non-compliance-fines-soar); see also [Kingsley Napley, 15 Mar 2024](https://www.kingsleynapley.co.uk/our-insights/articles/increased-civil-penalties-under-the-right-to-rent-scheme-implications-for-landlords/) (search snippet only)
- Propertymark's site search for "artificial intelligence" and "ChatGPT" returned only one AI item among the first ten results: a course titled "Managing AI-generated complaints" dated 2 Nov 2026 — [Propertymark course](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/course/managing-ai-generated-complaints.html)
- The Property Ombudsman's press-release listing (first 9 of 221 items) contained no AI item; it reports "unprecedented demand for dispute resolution" driven by the "Renters' Rights effect" (12 Mar 2026) — [TPO news](https://www.tpos.co.uk/news-media-and-press-releases)
- HMRC supervision of estate agents (registration, due diligence, risk assessment): the GOV.UK press release confirms registration is mandatory and trading unregistered can be prosecuted — [GOV.UK, 11 Oct 2022](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/hmrc-cracks-down-on-unlawful-estate-agents)

### Inferences
- Because there is no UK AI-specific rulebook for agents, enforcement risk for AI-written particulars or AI-edited photos sits with Trading Standards under the CPRs/DMCC Act and with the portals' own removal policies, which act faster than regulators.
- The Renters' Rights Act bidding and discrimination bans create a new way for AI tools to breach the law: an AI pricing or enquiry-filtering tool that invites offers above the advertised rent or screens out benefit claimants would put the agent, not the vendor, in breach.
- Right to Rent and AML both leave liability with the agent even where a third-party digital check is used, which is a structural reason agents distrust outsourcing verification to tools.

## Key question 5: Everyday security pains

### Takeaway
The evidence found for day-to-day problems is indirect: UK small businesses report phishing as the dominant breach (42% of small firms), only 17% hold a specific cyber policy and 40% of businesses use two-factor authentication; Right to Rent checks can no longer be done by emailed documents since October 2022; the one detailed UK agency breach on record (passports and bank statements exposed for two years via a file-transfer set-up) and the CTS outage show document-handling and third-party dependence as the failure points. Direct evidence on WhatsApp, shared logins, personal devices, PCI and subject-access requests was not found.

### Cited Findings
- UK Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025 (10 Apr 2025): 43% of businesses identified a breach or attack in the last 12 months; phishing was involved for 85% of those; 35% of micro businesses and 42% of small businesses identified phishing; the average cost of the most disruptive breach was £1,600 (£3,550 excluding £0 cases); 62% of small businesses had some form of cyber insurance but only 17% a specific cyber policy; 59% of small businesses had a formal cyber policy; 40% of businesses use two-factor authentication — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/cyber-security-breaches-survey-2025/cyber-security-breaches-survey-2025) (all-sector data, not real-estate specific)
- Since October 2022 Right to Rent documents "can no longer be accepted by email"; agents must use a certified IDV service or see documents in person; from 14 February 2024 there are three permitted check routes — [Landlord Today, 29 May 2024](https://landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2024/5/huge-surge-in-right-to-rent-checks-as-non-compliance-fines-soar)
- Life at Parliament View: a file-transfer configuration left bank statements, salary details, passport copies, dates of birth and addresses of tenants and landlords open online for about two years; the ICO found a "catalogue of security errors" and inadequate staff training — [Mortgage Solutions, 22 Jul 2019](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2019/07/22/estate-agent-fined-80k-for-exposing-thousands-of-customers-personal-data/) (search snippet only)
- The CTS attack showed the dependence of estate agents and conveyancers on one third-party provider, with no restoration timeline — [Intelligent CIO, 27 Nov 2023](https://www.intelligentcio.com/eu/2023/11/27/cyber-attack-causes-delays-to-house-sales-and-purchases-across-the-uk/)
- The 2019 Alto incident used valid login credentials to reach a data cache, and customers were left to decide whether to notify data-protection authorities — [Estate Agent Today, Dec 2019](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2019/12/zoopla-repels-cyber-attack-on-its-alto-software) (search snippet only)
- US property managers spend most screening time verifying bank statements (72%) and pay stubs (67%) by hand — [NAR Commercial Insights, 26 Sep 2022](https://www.nar.realtor/news/commercial-insights/countering-fraud-in-the-apartment-application-process) (institutional data)
- 58% of wire-fraud victims said the fraudulent message appeared to come from their agent, which makes agent email accounts the main impersonated channel — [Realty News Report, 6 Feb 2025](https://realtynewsreport.com/one-in-four-consumers-targeted-by-real-estate-wire-fraudsters/)

- NTS Tenant Fees Act penalties are issued to agents lacking redress-scheme membership or client money protection — [National Trading Standards](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/work-areas/estate-agency-team/)

### Inferences
- Email remains both the main attack channel (phishing, BEC impersonating the agent) and, for UK agents, a prohibited channel for ID documents, so a small agency's everyday workflow sits inside its biggest risk.
- Low two-factor adoption across UK businesses (40%) combined with credential-based intrusions (Alto 2019) points to shared or weak logins as a plausible but undocumented agency problem.
