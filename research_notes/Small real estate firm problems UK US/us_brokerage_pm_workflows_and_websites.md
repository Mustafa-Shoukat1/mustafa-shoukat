# US small real estate firms outside the coastal hubs: lines of business, workflows, manual steps, regulatory load, and a 30-site website sweep

Research date: 12 October 2026. Scope: US only, small firms (brokerages and teams of 1-30 agents, solo agents, property managers of roughly 50-1,500 units, small investors and wholesalers), cities such as Tulsa, Boise, Omaha, Little Rock, Knoxville, Birmingham AL, Spokane, Wichita, Sioux Falls, Albuquerque, Chattanooga, Greenville SC and Lexington KY. No solutions are proposed; this is an inventory of workflows, documents, manual steps, delays, regulatory burdens and what the websites show.

Method note: the shared web-search budget ran out after the second search batch, so everything after that came from direct fetches of URLs already in hand or from government and association pages with guessable addresses. A list of URLs that could not be reached (403, 404, DNS or connection errors) is in the final Gaps section so the report writer knows what was not verified.

## Key question 1. Sales side: lines of business, the step-by-step workflow and documents, where time goes and where deals fall through

### Takeaway
A small residential brokerage's sales work is a chain of roughly 20 contract-to-close steps spread over a 30-45 day window, each tied to a dated contingency and a document that must be signed, logged and forwarded; the people doing it are mostly independent contractors closing about 10 deals a year, and the measured failure points are appraisal problems and financing delays (14% of contracts delayed, 7% terminated in the latest NAR confidence index).

### Cited Findings

Who does the work and at what volume
- The 2025 NAR Member Profile (4,947 members surveyed March 2025) reports the typical agent closed 10 transactions in 2024, unchanged from 2023; median sales volume per agent was $2.5 million; median gross income $58,100 (up from $55,800); typical member has 12 years of experience; 55% are affiliated with an independent (non-franchise) company; 87% are independent contractors at their firms — [NAR newsroom, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-show-strong-commitment-to-profession-amid-market-headwinds-new-nar-report-finds)
- 25% of members named housing affordability the biggest obstacle for buyers, ahead of inventory — [NAR newsroom, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-show-strong-commitment-to-profession-amid-market-headwinds-new-nar-report-finds)
- NAR now lists a 2026 Member Profile but the public highlights page carries only an infographic image; the firm-size, hours-per-week, team-membership and technology figures are inside the paid full report — [NAR Member Profile highlights](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/highlights-from-the-nar-member-profile)
- Team sizes seen in the target cities: One80 Group (Keller Williams, Omaha) 11 active licensed agents; Southern Home Group (KW, Knoxville) 5 agents; Fox and Fogarty (Knoxville) 8 agents and 167 transaction sides in 2023; The Cooper Group (KW Knoxville-West) 7 agents, 75.5 sides in 2023; The Carter Group (eXp, Knoxville) 89 sides and $39.88M in 2025; Hanninen Home Team (eXp) 81 sides, $37.86M — [RealTrends One80 Group](https://www.realtrends.com/team-profile/one80group-nebraska-kwelitenebraska/); [RealTrends Fox and Fogarty](https://www.realtrends.com/team-profile/foxandfogarty-tennessee-foxandfogarty/); [RealTrends Cooper Group](https://www.realtrends.com/team-profile/thecoopergroup-tennessee-kellerwilliamsrealtyknoxville-west/); [RealTrends Carter Group](https://www.realtrends.com/team-profile/the-carter-group-tennessee-exp-realty/); [RealTrends Southern Home Group](https://www.realtrends.com/team-profile/southern-home-group-tennessee-keller-williams/)

Where deals fall through and how long they take (latest NAR confidence index)

The contract-to-close workflow as transaction-coordinator (TC) companies publish it
- A 20-step contract-to-close template: (1) receive and review the purchase agreement for price, contingencies, dates and signatures; (2) open escrow and deposit earnest money; (3) order title search; (4) coordinate general, pest or specialised inspections; (5) review inspection reports; (6) request repairs and negotiate; (7) order appraisal; (8) review appraisal against price; (9) approval step for documents; (10) confirm every contingency satisfied, waived or pending; (11) check sellers have secured their next home; (12) verify buyer's loan commitment; (13) prepare final closing figures (price, prorated taxes, lender fees); (14) review the Closing Disclosure with buyers and sellers; (15) confirm closing date and time; (16) coordinate the final walk-through; (17) deliver final documents to title; (18) approval step for closing; (19) ensure funds are sent to the closing agent and keep transfer records; (20) confirm the deed is recorded — [Process Street TC checklist](https://www.process.st/templates/transaction-coordinator-contract-to-close-checklist/)
- Serif's five-phase checklist assumes a 35-day close and says residential transactions typically run 30-45 days; Phase 1 (days 1-3, "everything in this phase should be wrapped up within 72 hours of contract execution") includes confirming signed copies, entering the deal into Dotloop or Skyslope, sending an intro email to buyer, seller, both agents, title and lender, verifying the earnest-money amount, due date and delivery instructions, getting the earnest-money receipt from title on day 2-3, ordering title, sending the contract to the lender, adding every contract deadline to a shared calendar, confirming HOA status and requesting HOA documents or resale certificate, and sending seller disclosures to the buyer — [Serif TC checklist](https://www.serif.ai/guides/real-estate-transaction-coordinator-checklist)
- Phase 2 (days 3-12, inspection): schedule inspection, send a reminder 48 hours before the deadline, log the report, track the buyer's election (proceed, request repairs, terminate), draft and get a signed repair addendum, log the resolution, order a home warranty — [Serif](https://www.serif.ai/guides/real-estate-transaction-coordinator-checklist)
- Phase 3 (days 12-25, appraisal and loan): confirm the lender ordered the appraisal, coordinate access, track the appraisal contingency, log the report, if low notify both agents and document the options (renegotiate, gap, waive), confirm conditional approval by day 18-20, track outstanding lender conditions, confirm the homeowner's-insurance binder and title commitment by day 20, request the HOA estoppel certificate — [Serif](https://www.serif.ai/guides/real-estate-transaction-coordinator-checklist)
- Phase 4 (days 25-33, clear to close): written Clear to Close from the lender, confirm closing logistics with title, request the Closing Disclosure three days before closing and confirm buyer acknowledgment, schedule walkthrough 2-3 days before, request utility transfer details, confirm wire instructions "by direct call" three days before closing, confirm cash-to-close, verify commission instructions and splits by day 30 — [Serif](https://www.serif.ai/guides/real-estate-transaction-coordinator-checklist)
- Phase 5 (days 33-45): confirm deed recording, confirm commission disbursement to the broker on closing day, collect and file all closing documents within 48 hours, send the executed package to buyer and seller, update the CRM, request a Google or Zillow review within 72 hours, archive the file per the retention policy — [Serif](https://www.serif.ai/guides/real-estate-transaction-coordinator-checklist)
- Listing-side tasks named by Transactly: enter the listing in the MLS, sign listing agreements and documents, complete all disclosures, maintain the listing until an offer arrives; buyer-side: schedule inspections, order appraisal, order title and survey, utilities and movers, manage document exchange between buyer, agents and title when a lender is involved; local contracts and forms "dictate the deadlines and milestones" — [Transactly TC checklist](https://transactly.com/transaction-coordinator-checklist/)
- Transactly claims its TC network "saves agents, brokerages, and teams an average of 16 hours per transaction" and handles "90% of the contract-to-close process" (vendor claim) — [Transactly](https://transactly.com/transaction-coordinator-checklist/)
- ListedKit's end-of-file sweep: verify contact details for all closing parties, confirm final loan-document approval, schedule the walkthrough, upload the full signed package to the records system — [ListedKit via search summary](https://listedkit.com/?p=2815)

Documents named across the three checklists (de-duplicated)
- Purchase agreement; earnest money receipt; title search and title commitment; seller disclosure documents; HOA documents, resale certificate and estoppel certificate; inspection reports; repair addendum; inspection waiver form; appraisal report; loan commitment or conditional approval; lender condition list; homeowner's insurance binder; home warranty; Clear to Close letter; Closing Disclosure and buyer acknowledgment; wire instructions; cash-to-close figure; commission instructions; deed and settlement statement; final closing package; recorded deed confirmation — [Process Street](https://www.process.st/templates/transaction-coordinator-contract-to-close-checklist/); [Serif](https://www.serif.ai/guides/real-estate-transaction-coordinator-checklist)

Lead handling and showings (what the firm websites show; detail in Key question 6)
- Of the seven brokerage or team sites read, none offered an online showing scheduler or chat; five offered a "What's my home worth" style call to action, and in PowerHaus's case the button simply links to the contact page rather than an automated valuation — [PowerHaus Real Estate](http://www.powerhausrealestate.com); [Templeton Real Estate Group](https://templetonrealestategroup.realgeeks.com/about-team/); [Povich Real Estate Group](https://www.nebraskarealty.com/agents/teams/povich-real-estate-group); [Lifetime Realty Group](https://www.nebraskarealty.com/agents/teams/lifetime-realty-group); [NuKey Realty](https://www.nukeyrealty.com/); [Fogarty Group](http://www.fogarty-group.com/)

### Inferences
- The TC checklists describe at least 45 discrete tasks per closed sale, with eight to ten hard deadlines (earnest money, inspection, repair response, appraisal, loan commitment, Closing Disclosure three-day rule, walkthrough, closing, recording); a solo agent doing 10 deals a year handles this calendar themselves or pays a TC.
- The 72-hour Phase 1 window and the "direct call to confirm wire instructions" step show that the early and late ends of a transaction are the most time-critical and the most manual.
- Appraisal is the only failure cause NAR quantifies (6% of contracts); the remaining 8% of delays and 7% of terminations are undifferentiated in the public data.

### Gaps
- No public per-firm headcount or office-size statistic from NAR; the Member Profile full report is paid.
- No source quantifying hours per transaction for the agent (only Transactly's 16-hours-saved vendor claim).
- NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers release (buyer-agent lead sources, written-agreement adoption) could not be fetched; two guessed URLs returned 404.
- No reliable data on how many showings or how many offers precede a typical accepted contract in these markets.

Trust accounting and owner money (the administrative core)
- Licensed brokers, property management companies and brokerages holding money for owners or tenants generally need a trust account; the licensing board, not the landlord-tenant statute, governs trust rules; the deposit clock starts when funds are received; most states require a monthly three-way reconciliation of bank statement, client ledger and journal; the broker of record remains responsible even when staff make entries; records must be available for inspection on demand; commingling is treated as a serious violation — [Hemlane trust account rules by state](https://hemlane.com/resources/trust-account-rules-by-state/)
- Texas: deposit by close of business on the second working day, reconcile monthly when there is activity, keep records four years; Washington: deposit next banking day, reconcile monthly, keep records three years minimum — [Hemlane](https://hemlane.com/resources/trust-account-rules-by-state/)
- Montana's board requires a trust account labelled as such with its own separate records and a civil fine of $1,000 per cited violation (contrast state, from search summary) — [Montana rpm guide](https://boards.bsd.dli.mt.gov/_docs/rre/rpm-guide.pdf)
- NARPM publishes a "Trust Accounting Chart of Accounts", four designations (CRMC, RMP, MPM, CSS), an antitrust policy and an AI policy dated March 2024; its public site gives no membership or units-per-member statistics — [NARPM](https://www.narpm.org/); [NARPM trust accounting page](https://www.narpm.org/education/trust-accounting/)

### Inferences
- Across the 23 property-management sites read, the digital layer is almost always a vendor portal (AppFolio logos or login links on eight sites, Rentvine on one, Property Meld on one, QuickLeasePro on one, getaptly on one), while the surrounding steps (showing booking, application review, owner approvals, after-hours triage) remain phone and office-hours work.

- Complaint sources cannot be measured from websites, but the firms' own guarantees (24-business-hour response, "Total Transparency Guarantee" for repair invoices, eviction-cost caps, tenant-replacement guarantees) point at response time, repair billing and bad placements as the recurring owner grievances.

## Key question 3. Investors and wholesalers: lead sourcing, skip tracing, comps and offers, contracts and assignments, due diligence

### Takeaway
Wholesaling runs as list-pull, skip-trace, outbound call or text, contract, then assignment or double close; the sources reachable were vendor pages that describe tooling but not formal due diligence, and the one regulatory artefact found is Oklahoma's state-mandated "Notice of Homeowner's Cancellation of Wholesale Real Estate Purchase Contract", which shows that at least one target state has written wholesaler obligations into its real estate commission forms.

### Cited Findings
- Three deal structures: assignment (secure the right to buy under contract, then find an investor; no upfront cash but limited time set by the contract's closing date), double closing (buy and resell minutes apart, needs funds or a short-term loan), and buy-then-sell; "advertising a property you don't own" is described as a legally "shady" area that "should only be undertaken by a licensed realtor"; readers are told to "be very clear on the legalities in your state" — [BatchLeads, 7 Jun 2022](https://batchleads.io/blog/3-wholesale-processes-real-estate-investor)
- Skip tracing is "the process of finding a homeowner's contact information—particularly when it's hard to find"; common mistakes include "ignoring DNC lists, not following up, and relying on outdated skip tracing methods" — [PropStream, 4 Jun 2025, via search summary](https://www.propstream.com/real-estate-investor-blog/why-wholesalers-need-a-reliable-skip-tracing-tool-hint-to-find-off-market-leads-fast)
- A 2026 software comparison describes the tooling chain: aggregated public records (tax liens, foreclosures, code violations) filtered by property type, equity, lien status and area and exported as bulk lists; batch skip tracing (Skip Genie, REISkip, PropStream, BatchLeads, Goliath); an AI assistant that "handles calls, texts, emails, and appointment scheduling"; CRM pipeline from prospect to close; a "DNC Flagging" page in the footer; all five tools list custom or enterprise pricing only; no contact-rate or cost-per-deal statistics — [Goliath Data comparison](https://goliathdata.com/wholesaling-real-estate-software-comparison-2026)
- Investor forum advice: find "an investor friendly title company that is willing to close assignment deals (some title companies won't)" and ask whether it caps assignment fees — [BiggerPockets forum via search summary](https://biggerpockets.com/posts/user/rwhitch?page=5)
- The Oklahoma Real Estate Commission's contract-forms page lists, among its "Other Transaction Documents", a "Notice of Homeowner's Cancellation of Wholesale Real Estate Purchase Contract" alongside a "Notice to Buyer - Signs and Risk of Deed Theft" and a "Wire Fraud Advisory" — [OREC contract forms](https://oklahoma.gov/orec/contract-forms-and-related-addenda.html)
- Small-investor overlap with property management: Progressive Properties of Greenville runs a "We Buy Houses" page and "passive-investor real estate syndications" beside its 650-door management business; Lott Management (Greenville) lists "market analysis, acquisitions, rental management, sales" and repairs through the owner's 25 years in construction; Hoffman Team's principal is in an LLC owning "30+ rental properties" — [Progressive Properties](https://propgreenville.com/); [Lott Management](https://lottmanagement.com/); [Hoffman Team](https://hoffmanteamrealestate.com/)

### Inferences
- The wholesaler's outbound step (skip-traced cold calls and texts to owners who never opted in) is the activity most exposed to the TCPA and Do-Not-Call rules in Key question 5; the vendors acknowledge this only by offering DNC flagging.
- Oklahoma's cancellation-notice form implies a statutory homeowner cancellation right on wholesale contracts, which adds a dated disclosure step to every Oklahoma wholesale deal.

### Gaps
- No source reached for comps/ARV and maximum-allowable-offer practice, earnest-money norms or a due-diligence checklist (the BiggerPockets guide URL returned 404).
- State wholesaler licensing or disclosure laws in South Carolina, Illinois, Pennsylvania and Ohio were not verified (no search budget; no primary page fetched). The Oklahoma Code and Rule Book PDF (1 Nov 2025 edition) that would contain the wholesaler provisions was not read.

## Key question 4. What NAR, NARPM, state commissions and TC guides say about paperwork load per transaction (forms, pages, signatures)

### Takeaway

## Key question 5. Regulatory and administrative burdens on top of the workflows

### Takeaway
Every stage of both businesses carries a dated legal obligation: a signed buyer agreement before a tour, MLS submission within one business day of public marketing, consent and revocation handling for calls and texts (revocations honoured within 10 business days from 11 April 2025), FCRA adverse-action notices, HUD's 2024 AI screening and advertising guidance, state-specific deposit-return windows of 14 to 60 days, eviction notice periods of 3 to 20 days, trust-account deposit and reconciliation rules, 1099 reporting, licensing of property managers in 15 of the 16 target states, and 30 hours of CE per cycle in Oklahoma.

NAR settlement practice changes (effective 17 Aug 2024)
- "As of August 17, 2024, an MLS Participant 'working with' a buyer must enter into a written agreement 'prior to touring a home'", covering "both in-person and live virtual tours"; the agreement must disclose compensation "conspicuously" as an amount, rate or method, "objectively ascertainable" and not "open-ended", must state the participant may not receive compensation from any source exceeding the agreed amount, must say commissions "are not set by law and are fully negotiable", and must include "any provisions required by law"; NAR does not dictate relationship type, term, services or amount — [NAR Written Buyer Agreements 101](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101)
- An agent working only for the seller, or performing ministerial acts without expecting pay, does not need a buyer agreement; a dual agent does — [HousingWire via search summary](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-clarifies-details-on-buyer-broker-agreements/); [Mississippi REC copy of NAR FAQ](https://www.mrec.ms.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/NAR_Settlement_FAQs.pdf) (PDF fetch returned 404 on 12 Oct 2026; search-summary level only)
- A buyer "still has a choice regarding which type of agency relationship they wish to have with a firm" (Wisconsin example) — [WRA Legal Hotline Oct 2024 via search summary](https://wra.org/WREM/Oct24/Hotline)

MLS rules: Clear Cooperation and the 2025 delayed-marketing option
- NAR announced the update on 25 Mar 2025, effective that day, with an implementation deadline of 30 Sep 2025 by which MLSs must set delay periods and create forms; the one-business-day rule is unchanged: a publicly marketed property must be submitted to the MLS within one business day, excluding weekends and holidays; public marketing includes yard signs, flyers, public-website digital marketing, IDX/VOW displays, email blasts and multi-brokerage networks — [HGAR legal corner](https://hgar.com/legal-corner-nars-updated-clear-cooperation-policy-seller-options-listing-requirements-and-brokerage-responses)
- Delayed Marketing Exempt Listings: seller may delay IDX and syndication (Zillow, Realtor.com, Homes.com) for a period set by each local MLS; the listing is filed with the MLS and visible to participants but not on consumer sites; the seller signs a disclosure acknowledging "the MLS benefits they are waiving or delaying"; office exclusives stay within the brokerage, require a seller certification and must still be filed with the MLS; sharing with multiple brokerages counts as public marketing — [HGAR legal corner](https://hgar.com/legal-corner-nars-updated-clear-cooperation-policy-seller-options-listing-requirements-and-brokerage-responses)

TCPA: calling and texting leads
- FCC rules effective 11 April 2025: consumers may revoke consent "in any reasonable way" (key press, replying "stop" or similar, a website or phone number the caller provides); callers must honour revocation "within a reasonable time", outer limit 10 business days; one confirmation text is allowed with no marketing content — [HousingWire](https://housingwire.com/articles/fcc-says-new-rules-for-robocall-and-text-opt-outs-will-take-effect-in-april-2025)
- The one-to-one consent rule (each seller must obtain its own written consent; consent cannot be transferred between companies) was scheduled for 27 Jan 2025 and postponed by the FCC to 26 Jan 2026 "or to a date in a future Public Notice following a decision in the IMC case, whichever comes sooner" — [Lofty TCPA page](https://lofty.com/blog/tcpa); [BAM](https://nowbam.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-new-rules-for-cold-calls-texts/); [Century 21 Redwood helpdesk](https://c21redwood.helpscoutdocs.com/article/401-tcpa-rules-change-delayed-until-january-26-2026)
- The Century 21 Redwood page links to a National Law Review article whose URL refers to the Eleventh Circuit vacating the one-to-one consent rule, but the page itself does not explain it and the article could not be fetched — [Century 21 Redwood helpdesk](https://c21redwood.helpscoutdocs.com/article/401-tcpa-rules-change-delayed-until-january-26-2026)
- Industry guidance for agents: manual dialling is allowed unless the number is on the National Do Not Call list; FSBOs on the DNC list may be called on behalf of a buyer but not to solicit a listing; manual follow-up with past clients within 18 months falls under the existing-business-relationship exception; automated calls, texts, prerecorded messages and AI tools require explicit written consent specific to the business; purchased leads need consent for the buyer's specific business; penalties $500-$1,500 per violation with a four-year statute of limitations — [BAM](https://nowbam.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-new-rules-for-cold-calls-texts/)
- Vendor practice: record date and method of consent in the CRM; web forms should carry an e-signature disclosure, terms, privacy policy and express consent to calls or texts; "regulated technology" includes auto-dialers, prerecorded or AI voice and ringless voicemail; Lofty tags each lead with a consent status ("System Recorded", "User Recorded", "Business Relationship", "Unknown", "Internal DNC") — [Lofty](https://lofty.com/blog/tcpa)
- Several states including Florida, Oklahoma and Washington have stricter "mini-TCPA" laws (vendor statement) — [EZ Texting via search summary](https://www.eztexting.com/real-estate)
- Firm websites now carry SMS consent language on contact forms (Sandblom, Lott, Tyson) and one Albuquerque manager posts a "Text (SMS) Opt out" notice — [Sandblom](https://sandblomproperties.com); [Lott](https://lottmanagement.com/); [Tyson](https://tysonproperties.com/); [RMSI](https://rmsi2000.com/)

Fair Housing Act, HUD advertising and AI guidance
- On 29 April 2024 (reported by most outlets as 2 May) HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity issued two guidance documents: tenant screening and advertising through digital platforms; for screening HUD lists principles including choosing relevant criteria, using only accurate records, following the stated policy, transparency with applicants, designing and testing models for compliance and "allow applicants to challenge negative information"; for advertising, targeted tools can violate the Act "by withholding housing information from consumers or by targeting people based on protected characteristics"; advertisers should use platforms that manage discriminatory delivery risk, label housing ads accurately, analyse audience composition and monitor outcomes; platforms should "avoid providing targeting options for housing-related ads that describe or are related to FHA-protected characteristics" — [Orrick InfoBytes, 10 May 2024](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2024-05-10/hud-guidance-ai-prevent-housing-discrimination/)

Fair Credit Reporting Act: tenant screening adverse action (FTC, July 2023)
- "An adverse action is any action by a landlord that is unfavorable to the interests of a rental applicant or tenant", including denial, requiring a co-signer, or a larger deposit or higher rent than for another applicant; the notice must give the consumer reporting agency's name, address and phone number, state the CRA did not make the decision and "can't give specific reasons for it", and give notice of the right to dispute and to a free report "if the person asks for it within 60 days"; if a credit score is used, the score, its description and key factors must be provided; notice is required even if the report "played a minor role"; oral notice is allowed but written is best practice; reports must be securely disposed of; landlords reporting late rent or evictions become furnishers — [FTC, Using Consumer Reports: What Landlords Need to Know](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know)

### Inferences
- The "digital front door" at these firms is a login button to a vendor portal; the pre-tenancy steps (viewing, applying, document upload, approval) and the pre-listing steps (valuation, showing booking) are where the phone, PDFs and office hours persist.
- The absence of any e-signature or Spanish-language mention on 30 sites does not prove absence in practice (lease e-signing is typically inside AppFolio or Rentvine), but it shows neither is marketed as a service in these markets.
- Signal PM's 7-business-day third-party application processing, Continental's 48-hour minimum and the multi-document upload requirements indicate screening turnaround as a visible bottleneck even where applications are online.
