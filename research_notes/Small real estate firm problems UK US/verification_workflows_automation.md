## Key Question 1: What does a UK letting agent do between application and tenancy signing, step by step, with timings and costs?

### Takeaway
The UK lettings workflow is a fixed sequence: consent and privacy notice, Right to Rent check (manual in-person/video, GOV.UK share code, or a registered digital provider for British/Irish passports only), then a referencing package (credit, ID, affordability via open banking/HMRC/payroll, employer and previous-landlord references, PEP/sanctions), then guarantor or conditions if the reference is "referred", then deposit and (from 1 May 2026) no more than one month's rent in advance before signing. Automated referencing vendors quote 24-72 hours and £5-£40 per applicant; the slow, manual residue is employer and landlord references, document re-requests, and applicants who do not complete.

### Cited Findings

Step 0 - consent and data protection
- Before any referencing the applicant must receive a privacy notice and give consent; "running a credit check without consent is a data protection breach" (page last reviewed 14 May 2026) — [LetSafe UK, Tenant Reference Check UK 2026](https://letsafeuk.co.uk/tenant-reference-check-uk)
- Charging tenants for referencing is prohibited under the Tenant Fees Act 2019; agencies typically cost £20-£40 per applicant, paid by the landlord/agent — [LetSafe UK](https://letsafeuk.co.uk/tenant-reference-check-uk); Goodlord's own blog puts the check at £15-£40 per tenant (citing Axa) and notes the cost is absorbed even when the application fails (3 June 2024) — [Goodlord blog](https://blog.goodlord.co/how-much-does-a-bad-reference-cost-a-letting-agent)

Step 1 - Right to Rent (England), per the Home Office guide updated 1 October 2026
- Three routes: (a) manual document check of List A/B documents, (b) the Home Office online service using a share code plus date of birth, (c) a digital check through a registered "RtR DVSP" (digital verification service provider). Where a person "holds only an eVisa", landlords "must use the Home Office online right to rent checking service" — [Landlord's guide to right to rent checks, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/landlords-guide-to-right-to-rent-checks/landlords-guide-to-right-to-rent-checks-1-october-2026-accessible)
- Manual checks: documents must be checked "in the presence of the holder, either in person or by live video call"; the checker must "be in physical possession of the original documents" and must not "rely only on viewing the document during a live video call" — same source
- Share codes for right to rent "begin with 'R'", are 9 characters, "valid for 90 days and must be used together with the person's date of birth"; codes beginning "W" or "S" are for other services — same source
- DVSP route is limited to holders of valid British or Irish passports (or Irish passport cards), including ones expired up to 6 months; the landlord "remains responsible for ensuring that the check is carried out in accordance with the prescribed requirements"; a clear copy of the output must be retained; the 1 October 2026 update "mandates the use of registered providers when conducting digital right to rent checks" (OfDIA register) — same source
- Timing: unlimited right to rent may be checked any time before the tenancy; time-limited right to rent must be checked "no earlier than 28 calendar days before" the tenancy agreement; follow-up checks are due before the statutory excuse expires (List B: the longest of one year, the permission end date, or document validity) — same source
- Landlord Checking Service: used when a person cannot be verified by the other routes (e.g. outstanding application or eVisa problem); "The LCS will provide a clear 'yes' or 'no' response within two working days"; a Positive Right to Rent Notice gives a 12-month statutory excuse — same source
- Records must be kept "for the duration of the tenancy agreement" and "for at least one year after the tenancy agreement comes to an end", then securely destroyed — same source
- Agent transfer: the agent must accept responsibility in a written agreement stating whether it covers initial checks, follow-up checks, or both; "Liability cannot be transferred beyond the appointed agent" — same source
- Civil penalties: lodger £5,000 first breach / £10,000 repeat within 3 years; occupier £10,000 first / £20,000 repeat; a Fast Payment Option reduces a first-breach penalty by 30% if paid within 21 calendar days — same source (offset read)
- Share-code failure modes seen in practice: tenant used a right-to-work checker instead of right-to-rent; code expired (90 days; unlimited new codes can be generated); name/date-of-birth mismatch with the Home Office record; lost access to the email or phone on the account. Landlords "should not automatically refuse or delay the tenancy" and should use the Landlord Checking Service (page dated 1 April 2026) — [Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/right-to-rent-share-code/)
- Secondary guides disagree on penalty figures (one says £10,000/£20,000 per occupier, another £3,000) — [search-result summary of Augustapp and Davidson Morris guides](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/?p=98090); the primary figures above from GOV.UK should be used
- Vendor claim: Yoti and Post Office describe themselves as "the UK's first certified Identity Services Provider (IDSP) under the Digital Identity and Attributes Trust Framework" and say Home Office guidance allows digital verification "for UK and Irish citizens"; no price or turnaround shown — [Yoti Right to Rent](https://www.yoti.com/business/right-to-rent/)

Step 3 - thresholds, guarantors, outcomes
- Affordability: "Rent should typically not exceed 33 to 40% of the tenant's gross monthly income"; example £1,000/month rent needs at least £30,000/year; last 3 months' payslips and bank statements; self-employed need 2 years' accounts or accountant's letter; employer reference must cover status, start date, contract type, salary; contact the previous landlord, not the current one (current landlords "can give false positives"); outcomes are "recommended", "referred", or "declined"; full agency package "typically 24 to 72 hours" — [LetSafe UK](https://letsafeuk.co.uk/tenant-reference-check-uk)
- Guarantor: UK-based homeowner earning at least 3x the annual rent, referenced as rigorously as the tenant, written agreement signed before the tenancy ("a verbal guarantee is not enforceable"), kept for the term plus 6 years — same source
- HomeLet says its scorecard goes beyond the "Income = 2.5 X annual rent" calculation — [HomeLet](https://homelet.co.uk/letting-agents/tenant-referencing)

Step 4 - money and signing (Renters' Rights Act 2025, England)
- From 1 May 2026 landlords and agents "cannot require, invite, or encourage any person - whether that's the tenant, a guarantor, or anyone acting on the tenant's behalf - to pay more than one month's rent before or at the start of a tenancy" — [Landlord Studio](https://www.landlordstudio.com/uk-blog/rent-in-advance-rules-renters-rights-act); "Landlords and agents cannot collect rent at all until the tenancy agreement has been signed by all parties" — [Lovelle](https://www.lovelle.co.uk/guides/landlord/rent-in-advance-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-what-landlords-must-know/); the restriction covers payments taken "through a third party, such as a letting agent" — [Baker McKenzie, Jan 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/united-kingdom-renters-rights-act-2025)
- One secondary source gives council fines of up to £5,000 per breach rising to £30,000 for repeat offences — [Landlord Advice](https://landlordadvice.co.uk/?p=17549) (single source; not verified against the Act)
- A failed reference should be assessed for mitigation (guarantor, higher deposit within the 5-week cap, extra conditions) before refusal; no blanket "no DSS" policies — [LetSafe UK](https://letsafeuk.co.uk/tenant-reference-check-uk)

Step 5 - financial sanctions (new duty for letting agents)
- "From 14 May 2025, letting agents will be subject to these reporting obligations" to OFSI when they know or suspect a person is designated; for prospective tenants the obligation "generally applies once the landlord accepts the tenant's offer and the parties are concluding an agreement"; for landlords from the point of instruction; no monetary threshold (unlike the €10,000 monthly rent threshold in the Money Laundering Regulations 2017) (page updated 28 January 2026) — [OFSI guidance for letting agents](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/financial-sanctions-guidance-for-letting-agents/financial-sanctions-guidance-for-letting-agents)
- Thirdfort markets "financial sanctions screening for lettings" of tenants, landlords and guarantors and notes letting agents became "relevant firms" on 14 May 2025 (vendor) — [Thirdfort estate agents](https://www.thirdfort.com/estate-agents/)

### Inferences
- The automated part (credit, ID, open-banking affordability, PEP/sanctions) is now minutes; the 24-72 hour range vendors quote is dominated by the human steps (employer and landlord references, document re-requests, applicant non-completion). Vendors themselves label employment and landlord checks as "manual".
- Price points cluster at £5 (Canopy), about £23-£40 (Goodlord via NRLA), £15-£40 (industry range); HomeLet/Let Alliance do not publish prices and sell via agents, so a small agency's real cost is bundled.
- The one-month rent-in-advance cap removes the traditional "pay six months up front" workaround for failed references, pushing agents toward guarantors and rent-guarantee insurance, which is why every referencing vendor bundles a guarantor service or rent protection.

## Key Question 2: What does a US property manager or leasing agent do between application and lease signing, step by step, with timings and costs?

### Takeaway
The US sequence is: written screening criteria (required in some states), application with fee (capped or banned in many states), applicant consent and identity authentication with a consumer reporting agency (TransUnion via SmartMove/RentSpree/Buildium, Experian via Zillow), same-day credit/criminal/eviction report, income verification (pay stubs, bank-link via Plaid, payroll via The Work Number, or document-forensics via Snappt/Findigs), manual employer and landlord calls, then decision with an FCRA adverse-action notice for any denial, co-signer demand, higher deposit or higher rent.

### Cited Findings

Legal frame (FCRA, federal)
- Landlords may obtain consumer reports on people who apply to rent or renew, or with written permission, and must certify to the reporting company that the report is used only for housing purposes; adverse action includes denying the application, requiring a co-signer, requiring a larger deposit, or charging higher rent; the notice must give the CRA's name, address and phone, a statement that the CRA did not make the decision, the right to dispute and to a free report within 60 days, and (if a credit score was used) the score and key factors; "required even if the report played only a small part"; oral notice allowed, written is best practice; reports must be securely disposed of; reports from "tenant screening companies, background check firms, and reference checking services can all be consumer reports" (page dated July 2023) — [FTC, Using Consumer Reports: What Landlords Need to Know](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know)
- Applicant side: free copy within 60 days of the notice; the background check company must investigate a dispute within 30 days and report in writing (page dated August 2025) — [FTC consumer page](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/tenant-background-checks-your-rights)
- HUD's July 2023 letter to housing agencies says any housing provider using tenant screening reports to make adverse decisions must provide FCRA adverse action notices and encourages written notice — [HUD Adverse Action Notice letter (PDF)](https://hud.gov/sites/dfiles/PIH/documents/Adverse%20Action%20Notice.pdf) (from search summary; PDF not fetched)
- Local layers a small landlord must know: California bans most eviction records older than 7 years; Cook County, IL restricts criminal checks until after a conditional offer; Seattle restricts arrest-history inquiries; Washington, D.C. bars denial for certain misdemeanours; some jurisdictions require giving applicants a chance to explain criminal history; HUD advises keeping screening records at least 2 years (page dated 27 July 2026) — [TurboTenant FCRA guide](https://www.turbotenant.com/rental-screening/fcra-background-check/)

Application fees and criteria (state rules, from a 10 July 2026 secondary guide)
- New York: maximum $20 per applicant (RPL § 238-a), waived if the applicant supplies a report completed within the prior 30 days; California: fee limited to actual costs, adjusted annually, collected only after review begins, itemised receipt and refund of unused portion (Civ. Code § 1950.6); Colorado: fee must reflect actual cost, purpose disclosed before payment, and HB23-1099 lets applicants reuse one screening report; Washington: actual cost, written disclosure of criteria before collection (RCW 59.18); Delaware: greater of 10% of a month's rent or $50; Massachusetts: landlords generally cannot charge (brokers excepted); Vermont: no application fees; Minnesota: actual cost with written criteria and refunds if screening not performed; Wisconsin: generally $25; Illinois: no fee when a valid reusable report is supplied — [TenantCloud state guide](https://www.tenantcloud.com/tenant-screening/state-by-state-guide-on-application-fees)
- New Jersey: $50 cap on application and similar fees effective 1 May 2026, CPI-adjusted from 2027 — [NCJAR](https://ncjar.com/industry-360/news-publications/industry-news/new-jersey-rental-application-fee-cap-what-ncjar-r-members-need-to-know) (search summary); California AB 2493 effective 1 Jan 2025 limiting fees to actual costs with refunds — [iPropertyManagement California](https://ipropertymanagement.com/guides/tenant-screening/california) (search summary; statute not fetched)

### Inferences
- For a small operator the credit/criminal/eviction report is commoditised at $17-$49 and same-day; the expensive, slow part is income and employment proof, which is why the fraud vendors (Snappt, Findigs) and data vendors (Plaid, Work Number) sell enterprise contracts without public prices and integrate with large-portfolio PMS (Yardi, RealPage, Entrata) rather than with Buildium/AppFolio-sized tools.
- Fee caps (NY $20, WI $25, MA/VT bans) are below the $35-$50 applicant-paid report price, so in those states the landlord either absorbs the cost or screens less.
- Vendors' own advice (call the employer on an independently found number; request two months of statements) shows document forensics is still paired with manual calls in practice.

## Key Question 3: What does a UK estate agent do for AML and Material Information on a new sales instruction, and again when an offer is accepted?

### Takeaway
At instruction the agent registers the seller as a customer (ID, address, beneficial ownership, title check) and gathers Material Information for the listing; at offer the buyer becomes a customer and must supply ID, proof of funds and source of funds, with enhanced due diligence for PEPs or high-risk cases; the conveyancer and lender then repeat the ID and funds checks because reliance on another firm's check does not shift responsibility. HMRC updated its sector guidance on 8 July 2026; the NTSELAT Material Information guidance was withdrawn in 2025 and replaced in law by the DMCC Act 2024.

### Cited Findings

AML duties and timing
- HMRC published updated estate agency AML guidance on 8 July 2026 reflecting the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (in force 30 June 2026); businesses must register with HMRC before trading; CDD applies to both buyer and seller and "the extent of CDD should reflect the risk assessment"; a written risk assessment covering money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing must be kept and provided to HMRC on request; "Responsibility for MLR compliance stays with the estate agency business, and reliance on a third party does not reduce it"; digital verification providers "should appear on the DVS register"; source-of-funds prompts include whether the source changed mid-transaction, gifted deposits, last-minute mortgage changes, proportionality to income, and "Extra care should always be taken with cash buyers"; breaches can bring financial penalties and "in serious cases, criminal liability" — [Devonshires note on HMRC July 2026 guidance](https://insights.devonshires.com/post/102nbd1/hmrc-updates-anti-money-laundering-guidance-for-estate-agency-businesses-key-tak)
- Since the 2017 Regulations agents must run due diligence on both buyers and sellers, hold ID and proof of address for each customer, identify beneficial owners; senior managers must run ongoing monitoring, apply EDD to PEPs, appoint a nominated officer for SARs, train staff and keep records for five years — [Taylor Wessing 2019 summary](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2019/03/estate-agents-and-aml-compliance) (search summary; page returned 403 on fetch)

## Key Question 4: What does a US brokerage or title company do to verify buyers, sellers and wire instructions, and where does fraud still get through?

### Takeaway
Title companies now layer about five tools (device analytics, biometrics, government-ID database checks, live facial match, direct seller contact with multi-factor authentication) and insure low-risk wires, yet 59% reported a seller-impersonation attempt in 2025 (up from 28%), one in four of those paid a claim, and 87% of catches happen late in curative review; wire fraud still succeeds through spoofed phone numbers, AI voice cloning of loan officers and escrow officers, and buyers wiring early. Buyer pre-approval and earnest-money verification remain phone-and-document manual steps with no primary guidance found.

### Cited Findings

Seller impersonation (ALTA 2026 Critical Issues Study, CertifID-sponsored, 245 respondents vs 783 in 2024)
- 59% of title firms reported at least one seller impersonation attempt in 2025 (28% in 2024); 45% in the last month (19%); 23% three or more in the last month (4%); 58% said deepfake image or voice was at least somewhat common; about 1 in 4 firms with an attempt paid a claim and half of those disclosed average payouts over $100,000; 98% use at least one fraud-detection tool, averaging about five; red flags: sellers avoiding meetings or calls, requests for remote signing or a seller-chosen notary, cash; targets: vacant land, vacation homes, rentals, agricultural land, absentee, free-and-clear and recently deceased owners; fraudsters use spoofed contact info, birth dates and personal financial knowledge (14 Sept 2026) — [NAR on ALTA study](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/6-in-10-title-insurance-firms-experienced-seller-impersonation-fraud-attempts-last-year-study-finds)
- Same study: property targets vacant land 82%, absentee-owned 72%, free-and-clear 68%, recently deceased 55%; spoofed contact information 87%; 94% use multiple tools averaging 5.3; "87% of fraud is caught during clearance or curative review"; safeguards moving from photo-ID checks to device analysis, biometrics, government-ID database checks and live facial matching; "direct seller contact and multifactor authentication" rated most effective; agents can mail a letter to the property address asking whether the owner intended to list; scammers mine obituaries and impersonate heirs; spoofed calls with AI voice cloning pose as lenders or escrow officers and push buyers to wire early, "sometimes before a preliminary closing disclosure is issued"; quote: "One of the biggest red flags is somebody who never wants to get on the phone" (14 Sept 2026) — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/seller-impersonation-fraud-more-than-doubled-since-2024/)

### Inferences
- Verification in US closings is a title-company function; a small brokerage's role is to warn clients, refuse to email wire details and confirm by phone. The tools that automate seller-identity checks (CertifID, Qualia Shield, FNF ionFraud) are sold to title agents, not to small brokerages.
- Insurance ($2M per low-risk wire at Qualia; CertifID's insured wires) is what makes the automated check commercially meaningful; it does not cover wires the model rates as risky, which is where the manual call-back still decides.
- Fraud survives because the human endpoints (buyer taking a spoofed call, a notary chosen by the "seller") sit outside the title company's tools; ALTA's own figure that 87% of catches happen in curative review means the first-contact steps rarely detect impersonation.

- Integration depth tracks customer size: UK referencing and AML tools integrate with Reapit/Alto/MRI-class CRMs that small agencies actually use; US fraud tools integrate with Yardi/RealPage/Entrata and leave Buildium/AppFolio-sized operators with the built-in TransUnion report plus manual income checks.

- Low review counts (SmartMove 3, CertifID 0, Snappt 0 on G2) mean B2B wire/fraud tools lack independent user evidence; vendor claims there cannot be corroborated.

## Key Question 6: What remains manual or legally non-delegable?

### Takeaway
Law pins responsibility to the business, not the tool: Right to Rent liability stays with the landlord or the agent who accepted it in writing and manual checks need the person and original documents present; AML needs a written risk assessment, a nominated officer and SARs, and reliance on third parties does not reduce responsibility; ID1 must be verified by a conveyancer personally; FCRA requires the landlord's own permissible-purpose certification and adverse-action notice even for third-party reports; state law caps fees, restricts criteria and mandates disclosures. Employer and landlord references, exception handling, and call-back wire verification stay human.

### Cited Findings
UK
- Right to Rent: the landlord (or agent, after written transfer) "remains responsible for ensuring that the check is carried out in accordance with the prescribed requirements" even when using a DVSP; DVSP route only for British/Irish passport holders; manual checks require the holder present in person or on live video and the checker in "physical possession of the original documents"; eVisa-only holders must be checked through the Home Office online service; follow-up checks and record retention are the landlord's duty; "It is the landlord's responsibility to demonstrate that they have complied"; from 1 October 2026 only OfDIA-registered providers may be used for digital checks — [Home Office guide, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/landlords-guide-to-right-to-rent-checks/landlords-guide-to-right-to-rent-checks-1-october-2026-accessible)
- Landlord Checking Service must be used when other routes fail and only replies within two working days — same source; landlords should not refuse a tenancy automatically on a failed code — [Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/right-to-rent-share-code/)
- AML: "Responsibility for MLR compliance stays with the estate agency business, and reliance on a third party does not reduce it"; written risk assessment required; only DVS-registered providers can be relied on for digital identity — [Devonshires on HMRC July 2026 guidance](https://insights.devonshires.com/post/102nbd1/hmrc-updates-anti-money-laundering-guidance-for-estate-agency-businesses-key-tak); nominated officer, SARs, 5-year records, PEP EDD — [Taylor Wessing summary](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2019/03/estate-agents-and-aml-compliance) (search summary)
- Sanctions reporting to OFSI by letting agents from 14 May 2025 "as soon as practicable" when they know or suspect — [OFSI guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/financial-sanctions-guidance-for-letting-agents/financial-sanctions-guidance-for-letting-agents)
- Consumer-facing duplication: proof shown to the agent does not satisfy the lender or conveyancer — [Propertymark](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/professional-standards/consumer-guides/buying-selling-houses/aml-proof-of-funds.html)
- ID1: verified by a conveyancer; "A paralegal cannot verify identity"; within three months of lodgement — [HMLR ID1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/698c6c10048fe5f7c6475fe3/ID1__2026-02-16_.pdf); PG81 Safe Harbour is optional and does not cover AML identity checks; no provision for relying on an estate agent's check — [PG81](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/encouraging-the-use-of-digital-technology-in-identity-verification-pg81/practice-guide-81-encouraging-the-use-of-digital-technology-in-identity-verification)

US
- FCRA: the landlord must certify permissible purpose to the CRA; the adverse-action notice is the landlord's duty "even though the report came from a third party" and even where the report was a minor factor; the CRA cannot explain the decision — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-landlords-need-know)
- HUD: housing providers using screening reports must give FCRA adverse-action notices (July 2023 letter) — [HUD letter](https://hud.gov/sites/dfiles/PIH/documents/Adverse%20Action%20Notice.pdf) (search summary)
- State/local: written disclosure of screening criteria before collecting a fee (Washington, Minnesota); itemised receipts and refunds (California, Colorado); portable reports must be accepted (Colorado, Illinois, New York 30-day rule); fees banned (Massachusetts for landlords, Vermont) — [TenantCloud](https://www.tenantcloud.com/tenant-screening/state-by-state-guide-on-application-fees); criminal-history timing and arrest-record limits (Cook County, Seattle, D.C.) — [TurboTenant](https://www.turbotenant.com/rental-screening/fcra-background-check/)

- Income/employment: Buildium tells landlords to "verify employment themselves"; Snappt advises calling employers on an independently sourced number and matching pay dates to deposits — [Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/features/tenant-screening/), [Snappt](https://snappt.com/?p=134); The Work Number is only available to credentialed verifiers with permissible purpose — [Work Number](https://theworknumber.com/)

### Inferences
- In both countries the automatable layer is evidence collection and database matching; the non-delegable layer is (a) a named human accountable for the decision and its records (landlord/agent for Right to Rent, MLRO/nominated officer for AML, conveyancer for ID1, landlord for FCRA notices), (b) consent gates that the applicant controls (credit consent, open-banking consent, share-code generation), and (c) out-of-band confirmation (phone call to employer, seller, or title company on an independently known number).
- Any software for a small firm therefore has to produce the audit trail and the notices rather than the decision: the decision and the signature stay with a person by law.
