## Key question 1: UK practice numbers, NHS versus private split, and what is happening to NHS access

### Takeaway
England has lost roughly one in ten of its NHS general dental contracts since 2017 (about 6,260 to about 5,650 by March 2026) while total practice numbers rose, so the shift is from NHS to private rather than closures; only about 56% of practices now hold a general NHS contract, about 40% of adults were seen by an NHS dentist in the 24 months to March 2026, and only 38% of adults who tried to get a new NHS appointment succeeded.

### Cited Findings
- NHS general dental contracts in England fell from about 6,260 in March 2017 to about 5,650 in March 2026, a loss of at least 600 practices; the share of practices offering general NHS care fell from about 65% to 56%; practices without an NHS contract rose by almost 1,000 (largely private or specialist); the decline appears in 41 of 42 ICB areas, steepest in the South West and East of England — [GDPUK report of Nuffield Trust analysis, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-ten-practices-have-ceased-general-nhs-dentistry-since-2017/); Nuffield Trust report itself at https://www.nuffieldtrust.org.uk/news-item/the-great-extraction-the-changing-state-of-nhs-general-dental-services-in-england (not fetched: 404 on the URL tried)
- Same analysis, as cited by The Probe: share of practices with general NHS services fell from 64.6% to 55.6%; practices without a general NHS contract grew by more than 1,000 — [The Probe, 1 Oct 2026](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/10/the-system-is-moving-but-is-it-changing/)
- BDA puts the figure at 608 practices ceasing NHS general dentistry since 2017, with 135 of those since the July 2024 election — [BADN news citing BDA](https://badn.org.uk/News/Dentists-Ministers-need-to-step-up-to-halt-exodus-from-NHS.aspx)
- Total number of dental practices in England rose by more than 400 over the same period; Mark Dayan (Nuffield Trust): "a shortage of NHS dentistry rather than of dentists" and "the quiet transformation of dentistry into a two-tier service over the last decade" — [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/?p=461154); [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-ten-practices-have-ceased-general-nhs-dentistry-since-2017/)
- Access (GP Patient Survey as cited by Nuffield): half of adults had not sought an NHS dental appointment in the previous two years; one in four of those who had not tried did not believe they would get one; of those who tried to get a new appointment, 38% succeeded — [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/one-in-ten-practices-have-ceased-general-nhs-dentistry-since-2017/)
- Activity vs coverage (NHSBSA figures as cited): 37.6 million NHS courses of treatment in 2025/26 (+6.2%); 18.8 million adults seen in the 24 months to March 2026, about 40% of the adult population, versus 18.0 million the year before; UDAs delivered +5.1%; 25,419 dentists recorded NHS activity; DCPs led 1.83 million Band 1 courses (almost one million more than the previous year) — [The Probe, 1 Oct 2026](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/10/the-system-is-moving-but-is-it-changing/)
- GDC register: nearly 48,000 dentists and 84,000 DCPs; dental therapists up 21% and hygienists up 11% in the last year; internationally qualified dentists outnumbered UK-qualified joiners for the first time; one-fifth of responding dentists provide private care only — [The Probe, 1 Oct 2026](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/10/the-system-is-moving-but-is-it-changing/)
- NHS England workforce survey (December 2024): just 10,727 FTE NHS dentists in England against a headcount of 24,543 GDPs delivering primary care in 2024-25; 70% of recorded chair time used for NHS activity, ranging from 77% in the North East and Yorkshire to 62% in the South East — [BDA written evidence to DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Local ICB reporting: 6% of NHS dental capacity in the Buckinghamshire, Oxfordshire and Berkshire West area lost between 2021 and 2025 through contract handbacks and reductions — [BOB ICB dental HOSC report, Nov 2025](https://decisionmaking.westberks.gov.uk/documents/s139820/6b.%20202511%20BOB%20ICB%20Dental%20HOSC%20report.pdf) (figure also given for 2021-24 in a Reading council paper, so the period is inconsistent between sources)
- Commons debate 15 June 2026: an MP described Scarborough as a dental desert where "practice after practice has gone fully private" and said the £88 million new patient premium produced no measurable access improvement (an MP's characterisation, not an official evaluation); the government announced 50 extra dental school places targeted at poor-access areas; contract changes took effect from April 2026 — [Hansard, 15 Jun 2026](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2026-06-15/debates/00A91E9C-E3C3-4281-9712-6AC4D1751F8E/NHSDentistry) (page returned 403 to fetch; content from search snippet)
- The government has pledged "fundamental reform to the dental contract" by the end of this Parliament — [House of Commons Library briefing CBP-9597](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9597/) (403 to fetch; from search snippet)
- Public Accounts Committee "Fixing NHS Dentistry" (4 April 2025): at best only about half the UK population could see an NHS dentist within two years under current arrangements; the PAC said the previous government's recovery plan "comprehensively failed" and the contract is "not fit for purpose" — [Dentistry.co.uk, 4 Apr 2025](https://dentistry.co.uk/2025/04/04/gap-of-5500-professionals-found-in-nhs-dental-workforce/)
- Hull example of list closure: NHS.uk profile for The White House Dental Practice, 74-76 George Street, Hull HU1 3AB, last confirmed 23 June 2026, states it does not currently accept new NHS patients for routine care (an older entry said it took new patients "when availability allows") — [NHS.uk profile](https://www.nhs.uk/services/dentist/overview/V004916) (fetched page showed name and address only; acceptance status from search snippet)
- Sunderland example: The Southwick and Whitburn Dental Practice Partnership (Avalon House, Sunderland Enterprise Park SR5 3XJ) only accepts new NHS patients for routine care if they are children aged 17 or under, last confirmed 3 June 2026 — [NHS.uk appointments page](https://www.nhs.uk/services/dentist/the-southwick-and-whitburn-dental-practice-partnership/V394019/appointments) (from search snippet)
- Sunderland example: Chester Road Dental Practice, 217 Chester Road SR4 7TU, listed with NHS adult, child and urgent appointments all shown as not accepted and no website — [caredb.co.uk listing](https://caredb.co.uk/location/1-196565108)

### Inferences
- The access problem is concentrated in NHS capacity, not in the number of dental premises: there are more practices than in 2017, but about 1,000 more of them are private-only. A patient in a lower-tech city who phones around is therefore meeting a mix of "NHS list closed", "children only" and "private only" responses, which matches the NHS.uk status lines seen for Hull and Sunderland.
- Because the NHS.uk acceptance status is self-reported and date-stamped ("last confirmed"), practices carry a recurring admin task of re-confirming their profile; stale profiles generate wasted enquiry calls to reception.

- No current official count of practices by NHS/private/mixed status was found; "56% hold a general NHS contract" is the nearest.

## Key question 2: What a UK receptionist or practice manager does each day and where time goes

### Takeaway
The UK sources found describe the front desk as the single point through which booking, phone handling, medical-history and NHS declaration forms, exemption evidence, payment, lab-work logistics and supplier invoices all pass, while the practice-manager standard adds NHS claim management, CQC/GDC compliance monitoring and HR; no fetched source quantified minutes per task.

### Cited Findings
- UK receptionist duties (Indeed UK): answering calls from patients, suppliers and insurance/plan companies; reassuring nervous patients; taking payment on point-of-sale machines and explaining finance or payment plans; handling private dental plan queries and keeping plan prices current; explaining treatment costs that vary by plan, complexity and number of treatments; creating and updating patient records including medical histories, treatment notes, diagnostic records and prescriptions; working with nurses to organise lab moulds by patient and make X-rays available; recording bookings, leaving dentist breaks, rearranging when a dentist is unavailable; maintaining equipment and supplies, liaising with suppliers, checking invoices, managing physical and digital documents, keeping the desk and waiting area clean — [Indeed UK, dental receptionist duties](https://uk.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/dental-receptionist-duties)
- The dental practice manager apprenticeship standard (ST0109) includes duties to manage claims and monitor compliance with claim requirements, and to understand the requirements of service delivery and remuneration under the NHS — [Institute for Apprenticeships / Skills England ST0109](https://skillsengland.education.gov.uk/apprenticeships/st0109-v1-2) (from search snippet; page redirected and was not re-fetched)
- Where a practice operates through a company or partnership it must appoint a CQC registered manager, who is legally responsible for compliance with the 2014 regulations (shared with the provider) — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/uk-who-should-you-appoint-as-your-cqc-registered-manager/)
- A compliance consultancy writing in Dentistry.co.uk says the manager's job involves "daily and weekly compliance tasks" and that the volume of requirements "can feel overwhelming"; the practice should keep comprehensive logs of maintenance and service certificates, audits and risk assessments, plus staff folders with up-to-date CPD attached — [Dentistry.co.uk, 20 Aug 2024, Lamia Murray](https://dentistry.co.uk/2024/08/20/how-to-prepare-for-a-cqc-inspection/)
- NHS exemption workflow at the desk (NHSBSA guide for dental teams, V3 08/2021): before any treatment begins the patient must complete and sign the FP17PR declaration form; if claiming exemption they complete the "Claim for free or reduced cost NHS dental services" section; staff must ask to see evidence, and if a valid certificate is shown must note it on the practice record with its expiry date; if no evidence is shown staff must not refuse treatment, must mark "No" in the "Evidence of exemption or remission seen" box on the FP17PR and on the dental activity claim, tell the patient that NHS checks are routinely undertaken, and have them sign; if the patient is unsure, staff ask them to pay and give them an HC5(D) refund form (refund within three months with receipt) — [NHSBSA, Patient exemption checking: a guide for dental teams in England, V3 08/2021](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-03/Patient_exemption_checking_guide_dental_V3%20_08_2021.pdf)
- The practice collects the NHS patient charge at the time of treatment and NHSBSA then deducts the charge revenue from the contract payment — [UDA Pulse contract guide (secondary, commercial)](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/)
- The 2026 contract changes add "increased admin, monitoring, benchmarking" according to a consultant's analysis (funded appraisals at £213 per associate/therapist/hygienist per year; a £3,400-per-practice quality improvement payment for audit or peer review on nationally set topics) — [Dentistry.co.uk, 2 Feb 2026, Julian Perry](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/02/02/what-does-the-2026-nhs-contract-amendment-mean-for-dentists/)
- Generic (partly US) day-structure descriptions: morning review of the schedule and cancellations; check-in with forms; steady phone and email flow through the day; afternoon payments, records and claims; end-of-day confirmation calls — [Discovery Community College blog](https://discoverycommunitycollege.com/blog/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-dental-receptionist/) (not UK; used only for the shape of the day)

### Inferences
- In a mixed practice the same receptionist handles two different front-end flows per patient: the NHS flow (FP17PR declaration, exemption evidence, band charge, HC5(D) for refunds) and the private flow (plan membership checks, estimates, finance plans, card payment). Each form is paper or in-system and has to be reconciled later with the FP17 claim or the invoice.
- The "last confirmed" date on NHS.uk profiles and the complaints-procedure and "last updated" date required on websites (see Q4) are recurring manual re-confirmation tasks that sit with reception or the manager.

- No UK time-and-motion study of reception or practice-manager time was found; the Dental Practice Managers Association site was not searched before the budget ran out.
- The apprenticeship standard's full duty list was not retrieved (redirect not followed before the budget constraint).

## Key question 3: What the NHS claiming and UDA cycle imposes (FP17 windows, UDA targets, clawback, exemption evidence, PCNs)

### Takeaway
Every NHS course of treatment produces one FP17 that must reach NHSBSA within two months of completion or its UDAs are disallowed; the practice must hit its annual contracted UDA total with a 96% floor below which all under-delivery is recovered, and the year-end reconciliation (SD86c, around July) can claw money back, while exemption declarations are the patient's legal responsibility but are recorded by the practice and audited by NHSBSA with £100 penalties.

### Cited Findings
- "You have 2 months from the date of completion of a course of NHS treatment to submit your FP17" (the "2-month rule"); late claims have their UDAs disallowed and are marked "Late Submitted Claim" on the schedule; the form must still be submitted so the treatment is recorded; FP17O (orthodontic) forms also have a 2-month window, except Failed-To-Return submissions; forms received after the practice's Year End report is published do not count toward that year's activity — [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01820](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01820/en-us)
- A commercial guide states the window as 62 days from completion, one FP17 per course of treatment regardless of visits — [UDA Pulse contract guide (secondary)](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/)
- High Court, Balachandra v NHS Commissioning Board [2026] EWHC 294 (KB): the two-month period in the standard contract could not be read as a condition of payment; the judgment describes the reconciliation carried out by the BSA each July producing a report showing UDAs performed and any clawback, and UDAs between 96% and 100% being carried forward — [ICLR summary](https://www.iclr.co.uk/document/2026001068/2026ewhc294kb_TNA/html)
- 96% threshold (NHS England contractual guidance for the quality and payment reforms): if overall contract delivery is below 96%, payment for all under-delivered UDAs is recovered and a breach notice may follow; if delivery is at or above 96%, the shortfall above that line is carried over to the next year — [NHS England, NHS dentistry: quality and payment reforms contractual guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/nhs-dentistry-quality-payment-reforms-contractual-guidance/) (page returned empty to fetch; from search snippet)
- Mid-year review at six months; year-end reconciliation compares delivered with contracted activity; over-delivery is payable only up to 110% and only if agreed with the commissioner in advance; urgent care is reconciled separately at year-end; if less than 30% of required urgent treatments are delivered by 30 September commissioners may act, and between 30 October and 31 March they may reduce the urgent requirement by up to 15% where local demand is low; NHS England may flag unusual urgent-to-routine claim ratios — [UDA Pulse contract guide (secondary)](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/)
- Compass outputs the practice works from: a monthly superannuation/payment notification per contract (monthly payment, patient charges collected, superannuation deducted, net payment, forms processed, clinician activity by band); DCS129 UDA summary; DCS127 patient-level activity; SD86c year-end statement issued around July with two months to dispute it; NPE/NPEE rollover; period codes such as 262701 = April 2026; claim status codes Processed / Validated / Failed Validation / Deleted — [UDA Pulse contract guide (secondary)](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/)
- UDA values per band (2026/27, as stated by the same guide): Band 1 = 1 UDA; Band 2a = 3; Band 2b = 5; Band 2c = 7; Band 3 = 12; denture repair/reline/modification = 2 UDAs (was 1); bridge repair = 1.2; suture removal and arrest of bleeding = 0; fissure sealants 3 UDAs for 1-2 teeth (2a) and 5 UDAs for 3+ teeth (2b) — [UDA Pulse contract guide (secondary)](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/)
- Patient charges in England: Band 1 £27.90, Band 2 £76.60, Band 3 £332.10, urgent £27.90; one charge per course at the highest band; further treatment within 2 months in the same or lower band is free, a higher band costs the higher fee; removing stitches, stopping bleeding and repairing dentures are free; the dental professional should tell the patient the cost before treatment starts; page last reviewed 13 March 2025 — [NHS.uk, How much NHS dental treatment costs](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/dentists/how-much-nhs-dental-treatment-costs/)
- Urgent (unscheduled) care from the 2026 reforms: flat £75 per urgent course (£60 activity plus £15 fixed), no complexity adjustment; a missed urgent appointment loses the £60; a fixed share of contract activity must be set aside for unscheduled appointments — [Dentistry.co.uk, 2 Feb 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/02/02/what-does-the-2026-nhs-contract-amendment-mean-for-dentists/); the required number of urgent treatments for contracts of 100+ UDAs is stated as (contract value ÷ £10,000) × 11, rounded up, with the £15 element paid monthly in advance — [UDA Pulse (secondary)](https://udapulse.co.uk/nhs-contract-guide/)
- Exemption and PCN rules: the exemption must be valid on the date the course of treatment starts; it is the patient's responsibility to claim correctly "regardless of whether the FP17PR form is completed by the patient, someone on the patient's behalf, or dental practice staff"; NHSBSA checks claims and recovers charges; PCNs are issued under the NHS (Penalty Charge) Regulations 1999; the penalty is five times the original charge up to £100 plus the original charge; practices are asked to display posters, booklets and counter cards — [NHSBSA exemption checking guide V3 08/2021](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-03/Patient_exemption_checking_guide_dental_V3%20_08_2021.pdf)
- Dental exemption categories listed by NHSBSA: under 18, or under 19 in full-time education; pregnant or had a baby in the previous 12 months; treated in an NHS hospital by the hospital dentist; receiving certain benefits (or aged 19 and under and a dependant of someone who is); named on a valid NHS tax credit exemption certificate; named on a valid HC2 certificate; "you could face a £100 penalty charge. Even if it's by mistake" — [NHSBSA, Check before you tick](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/dont-get-caught-out-penalty-charges/check-you-tick)
- Patients normally have 28 days to pay or challenge a PCN, after which a 50% surcharge up to £50 applies — [sorteduk.uk guide (secondary)](https://sorteduk.uk/nhs-penalty-charge)
- NAO investigation (2019): an estimated £212 million lost in 2017-18 to wrongly claimed exemption from prescription and dental charges; around 47% of dental treatments were claimed as exempt in 2017-18 — [NAO, Investigation into penalty charge notices in healthcare, 2019](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Investigation-into-penalty-charge-notices-in-healthcare.pdf) (from search snippet; the report page returned 403)
- A trade blog cites more than 160,000 dental penalty notices issued since 2013 — [Spa Dental blog, 2018](https://www.spadental.co.uk/blog/2018/09/nhs-dental-fines/) (unverified against NHSBSA data)
- BDA: "Clawback against arbitrary targets remains a major source of frustration"; practice owners "face growing business risks, with contract instability and clawback compounding this" — [BDA DDRB 2026-27 evidence](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Older NHSBSA year-end contract-management guidance exists for 2020/21 and 2021/22 but covers COVID-era deemed-activity rules and should not be treated as current — [NHSBSA 2021/22 CM guidance](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2021-03/2122%20CM%20guidance%20FINAL%20v1.1.pdf); [NHS England 2022/23 year-end guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/PRN00350-Guidance-re-dental-contract-management-arrangements-for-2022-23-year-end-reconciliation-June-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- The deadline structure creates three recurring calendar pressures for a small practice: a rolling 2-month FP17 window per patient, a 30 September urgent-care checkpoint (30% of the annual urgent requirement), and the 31 March year-end with the July SD86c and a 2-month dispute window.
- Because patient charges are collected by the practice and then netted off the monthly contract payment, any mismatch between what reception collected and what the FP17 declared (band, exemption box) shows up in the monthly schedule and has to be chased.
- The "Evidence seen: No" box is the practice's protection; the penalty falls on the patient, but the enquiry letters and PCN disputes come back to the desk as complaints and calls.

### Gaps
- The current (2026/27) NHSBSA/NHS England year-end contract-management guidance was not retrieved; the 96% rule and July SD86c timing rest on the NHS England page snippet, the High Court summary and a commercial guide.
- Real Time Exemption Checking for dentistry: no source confirming whether it is live for dental practices was reached.
- No current NHSBSA count of dental PCNs issued per year was found.

## Key question 4: What CQC and GDC inspections and standards require in paperwork

### Takeaway
CQC inspects against the five key questions and checks a sample of staff files against the practice's own recruitment policy (identity, employment history, hepatitis B immunity, DBS), looks at a few clinical records, fire-safety, maintenance and audit evidence; the GDC requires specific items on every practice website (names, addresses, GDC numbers and qualifications of every clinician, complaints route, GDC contact, date last updated) and runs the CPD and annual registration cycle.

### Cited Findings
- Recruitment (CQC Dental mythbuster 19): inspectors check a sample of staff files against the practice's recruitment policy; the checks come from the Regulated Activities Regulations 2014 and cover identity, employment history, hepatitis B immunity evidence and DBS checks for clinical staff — [CQC mythbuster 19](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/dentists/dental-mythbuster-19-staff-recruitment) (page 403 to fetch; from search snippet)
- Dental care records (mythbuster 8): inspectors look at one or two records to corroborate what staff and patients say, more if there are concerns, and may review record-keeping protocols — [CQC mythbuster 8](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/dentists/dental-mythbuster-8-dental-care-records)
- Fire safety (mythbuster 21, updated January 2025): inspectors may review fire policies, certificates and records; CQC now recommends a documented fire risk assessment regardless of staff numbers — [CQC mythbuster 21](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/dentists/dental-mythbuster-21-fire-safety)
- Well-led (mythbuster 27): a practice does not need documented evidence against every prompt; a written strategy and a modern-slavery policy are not required — [CQC mythbuster 27](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/dentists/dental-mythbuster-27-well-led)
- Training (mythbuster 2): providers must show that team members, including the self-employed, are up to date with professional practice and GDC requirements; inspectors would not normally need to see personal development plans — [CQC mythbuster 2](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/dentists/mythbusters-tips-dentists/dental-mythbuster-2-keeping-date-dental-practice-team)
- A premises consultancy states that building certificates (fire, electrical, gas, structural) must be submitted before CQC registration from 9 February 2026 — [Surgery Premises Group blog](https://www.surgerypremisesgroup.co.uk/blogs/3) (secondary; not confirmed against CQC)
- Documents to have ready for inspection: maintenance and service certificates, audits, risk assessments, staff profiles/folders with CPD attached, kept as "comprehensive logs" — [Dentistry.co.uk, 20 Aug 2024](https://dentistry.co.uk/2024/08/20/how-to-prepare-for-a-cqc-inspection/)
- CQC report example: Smallthorne Family Dental Practice, Ford Green Road, Stoke-on-Trent (SDS Dental Ltd), report published 3 July 2025, met regulations in all five areas; registered manager has changed since — [CQC location 1-225063452](https://www.cqc.org.uk/location/1-225063452)
- CQC report example (corporate contrast): mydentist Hanley Road, Sneyd Green, Stoke, announced off-site inspection 17 July 2026, NHS and private, four treatment rooms — [CQC location 1-1254470479](https://www.cqc.org.uk/location/1-1254470479)
- GDC website requirements (Guidance on advertising, effective 30 September 2013): a practice website must show the name and geographic address of the service; contact details including email and phone; the GDC's address and contact details or a link; details of the complaints procedure and who to contact if dissatisfied (the NHS body for NHS care, the Dental Complaints Service for private care); the date the website was last updated; it must be updated regularly to reflect current personnel and services; it must not compare skills or qualifications with other professionals; every dental professional named as providing care must have their professional qualification and country of qualification and their GDC registration number displayed — [GDC, Guidance on advertising](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)
- Specialist titles: only dentists on a GDC specialist list may use "Specialist" or "specialist in"; others must not use Orthodontist, Periodontist, Endodontist or "specialising in", and DCPs must not use "Smile specialist" or "Denture specialist"; permitted forms are "special interest in", "experienced in", "practice limited to" — [GDC, Guidance on advertising](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)
- GDC advertising checklist asks whether the GDC registration number is included — [GDC Advertising Checklist](https://standards.gdc-uk.org/pdf/Advertising%20Checklist.pdf)
- IR(ME)R (CQC page, updated 28 August 2024): applies to independent and NHS providers; duty holders are employer, referrer, IR(ME)R practitioner and operator; each exposure must be justified and optimised "as low as reasonably practicable"; the April 2023 version added a detailed investigation and report when notifying a significant accidental or unintended exposure (SAUE) — [CQC IR(ME)R guidance](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/ionising-radiation/ionising-radiation-medical-exposure-regulations-irmer)
- IRR17 roles as described in a study guide: the Radiation Protection Adviser is an external certified expert appointed in writing; the Radiation Protection Supervisor is an internal staff member responsible for day-to-day compliance with the Local Rules — [open-exam-prep study guide (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/uk-lds-part1/law-ethics-health-safety/health-safety-clinical-governance)
- HTM 01-05 record-keeping as described by a dental supplier: each piece of decontamination equipment should have a dedicated logbook; test records are completed daily, weekly, quarterly or annually depending on the test; validation, testing and servicing records must be up to date and available for inspection; each practice must have a nominated infection-control/decontamination lead — [Kent Express blog (secondary)](https://kentexpress.co.uk/blog/the-importance-of-dental-decontamination-record-keeping); the gov.uk HTM 01-05 publication now redirects to the NHS England HTM library at https://www.england.nhs.uk/estates/health-technical-memoranda/ (not fetched)

## Key question 5: Recruitment struggles (dental nurses, hygienists, associates) and how they hit the schedule

### Takeaway
In the BDA's 2025 survey 77% of practice owners who tried to recruit dental nurses had difficulty, 63% of owners with vacancies had posts open for more than six months (76% for high-NHS practices), only 34% filled all posts, and the NHS dental vacancy rate is 19%; because every dentist appointment needs a nurse, an empty nurse post directly removes surgery sessions.

### Cited Findings
- 73% of practice owners sought to recruit dental nurses in 2024-25 and 77% of them had difficulty; of those, 40% said candidates were reluctant to work in NHS dentistry; 35% of high-NHS owners said the vacancy arose because staff were unwilling to continue working in dentistry (27% for others) — [BDA DDRB 2026-27 evidence](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- 34% of high-NHS practice owners could not appoint because they lacked capacity to train a less-experienced candidate (18% for others); "insufficient nurse capacity can act as a brake on the delivery of dentistry that would otherwise take place" — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- 70% had difficulty recruiting dental therapists (71% in 2023-24, 62% in 2022-23) and 63% hygienists; high-NHS owners were more likely to cite inability to offer an acceptable salary — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- 63% of owners reporting vacancies had posts open more than six months, rising to 76% for high-NHS practices; just 34% of owners with vacancies in 2024-25 filled all the posts they sought — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- NHS England dental workforce survey: NHS vacancy rate 19% versus 15% non-NHS and 6.9% for the NHS as a whole; 1,823 unsuccessful recruitment processes for NHS-delivering posts in England between April and December 2024 — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Associates: 22% sought a new role in 2024-25; 46% of those did so to deliver more private activity, 29% for geography, 6% for flexible hours; 59% of associates who felt unable to ask for a pay rise cited rising practice costs including laboratory bills and materials — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Intentions: 44% of practice owners and 41% of associates intend to reduce NHS commitment within five years; 42% of respondents intend to retire or leave within five years; 19% are aged 55+ — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Morale and stress: 56% of owners and 42% of associates report low or very low morale; 72% of owners and 47% of associates say stress rose in the last 12 months; owners' stress drivers are increased practice costs (92%) and staffing/recruitment/retention (70%); associates' are patient complaints and legal issues (62%) and NHS underfunding (50%) — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Nearly three-quarters of England respondents felt staff recruitment had deteriorated over the last year — [BDA DDRB 2026-27](https://www.bda.org/media/lm1nw32p/ddrb-26-27-round-bda-submission.pdf)
- Public Accounts Committee (4 April 2025, data to March 2024): more than 5,500 unfilled NHS dental positions, about 2,700 of them dentist roles, many vacant for at least 180 days; the average practice loses about £42 on a set of NHS dentures (BDA evidence); under 20% of the expected 240 £20,000 "golden hello" posts had been filled by February 2025; the mobile dental unit plan was dropped; the £28 minimum UDA uplift produced no identifiable improvement; ADG's Neil Carmichael: every dentist appointment requires a dental nurse — [Dentistry.co.uk, 4 Apr 2025](https://dentistry.co.uk/2025/04/04/gap-of-5500-professionals-found-in-nhs-dental-workforce/)
- NHS England's own analysis: the Dental Recovery Plan did not increase UDA delivery (excluding new-patient-premium UDAs) in 2024/25; total new patients per working day fell 5% over 2024/25; raising the minimum UDA value to £28 did not increase delivery compared with other low-value contracts; the plan's four measures were the new patient premium (£15 or £50 UDA credits), golden hellos, the £28 minimum UDA value and dental vans, of which only the first and third were fully rolled out — [NHS England, Data analysis: impact of the Dental Recovery Plan](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/data-analysis-impact-of-the-dental-recovery-plan/) (page empty to fetch; from search snippet)

### Inferences
- A one-to-three-dentist practice that loses a nurse cannot run that surgery; with 63% of vacancies open beyond six months, the schedule loss is measured in months, and the practice's UDA delivery (and clawback exposure) falls with it.
- Associate churn (22% looking, mostly toward private work) means mixed practices must re-plan NHS UDA allocations between performers mid-year, which feeds the Compass performer-level reporting work.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave nurse pay rates or agency/locum costs; the BDA document contains cost-index tables that were not extracted.
- Regional recruitment data for the specific sweep cities was not found.

## Key question 6: Private and mixed practice marketing, new-patient flow, and the rules constraining it

### Takeaway
Private and mixed practices lean on Invisalign, implants, whitening, membership plans and finance offers, but GDC advertising guidance, the ASA/CAP Code (Section 12) and an ASA enforcement notice on braces constrain claims, titles and testimonials; a peer-reviewed audit found only 1.8% of practice websites in North East England and North Cumbria fully compliant with GDC advertising guidance.

### Cited Findings
- GDC: all publicity must be "legal, decent, honest and truthful"; see Q4 for the mandatory website items and title rules — [GDC, Guidance on advertising](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)
- ASA/CAP enforcement notice on advertising of braces and dental devices (hosted by the GDC): claims such as "less painful" and "faster treatment" must not be used without sufficient substantiation; the notice cites ASA rulings against Perfect Smile Surgery Ltd (A16-348857), 15 Dental (A16-365575) and OrthoAccel Technologies Inc (A17-393373) — [GDC-hosted ASA enforcement notice PDF](https://www.gdc-uk.org/docs/default-source/information-standards-and-guidance/guidance-documents/asa-enforcement-notice.pdf?sfvrsn=c28bb3a0_2) (PDF body not machine-readable; the ruling references were readable in link annotations)
- CAP advice on the "Dr" title: dentists using "Dr" should be on the GDC register and make clear it is a courtesy title — [ASA advice online, Use of the term Dr: Dentists](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/use-of-the-term-dr-dentists.html)
- CAP also publishes specific advice on teeth-whitening claims — [ASA advice online, Teeth whitening](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/teeth-whitening.html)
- Peer-reviewed audit (BDJ 2021) of practice websites and social media in North East England and North Cumbria: only seven websites (1.8%) were fully compliant with GDC advertising guidance; the authors attribute this to lack of registrant knowledge of the guidance — [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33767368/); full text at [BDJ](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41415-021-2718-4) (redirected to a login wall)

### Inferences
- Longer NHS recall intervals push practices to sell private hygiene and membership plans to keep patients attending between NHS check-ups; the website sweep (Q7) records how many practices present plans and hygiene as separate private offers.
- Compliance exposure is highest on exactly the pages that drive new private patients (Invisalign, implants, whitening, "Dr" bylines, before/after galleries).

### Gaps

- Denplan or other plan-provider practice statistics were not reached.
- No source quantified typical new-patient enquiry volumes or conversion rates for UK independents.

### Inferences
