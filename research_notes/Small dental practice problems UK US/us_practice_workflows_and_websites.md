# US small independent dental practices: day-to-day workflows, operational problems and website sweep

Researcher notes, 16 October 2026. Scope: independent US practices (1-5 dentists, 1-2 locations, non-DSO) outside the coastal tech hubs. DSOs appear only as contrast. No solutions are proposed; this file records workflows, documents, manual steps, delays, regulatory burdens and what practice websites show.

Method note: web search was blocked for part of the run (shared budget exhausted, then a tool outage), so the website sweep relied on direct fetches of practice sites, New Patients Inc practice-finder pages (a marketing vendor's directory), WebMD/DentalPlans listings and search snippets. Several practice sites were unreachable from this environment (DNS failures or JavaScript-only pages). Where a finding comes from a directory or a vendor rather than the practice's own site, that is stated. All URLs were accessed on 16 Oct 2026.

## Key question 1: What does the ADA Health Policy Institute say about practice ownership, DSO share, staffing shortages and solo practice?

### Takeaway
HPI's own numbers show a long slide in ownership and solo practice, DSO affiliation rising every year (about 13-14% of all dentists, more than 1 in 4 of dentists under 10 years out), and staffing (hygienists above all) as the top self-reported challenge for 2025, with roughly 90% of recruiting dentists calling hygienist hiring very or extremely hard.

### Cited Findings
- 205,088 professionally active US dentists in 2025 (60.0 per 100,000); 39.4% female; 21.2% in an ADA-recognised specialty; average retirement age 68.7 in 2024 (up from 64.7 in 2001). BLS (May 2026): 222,740 dental hygienists and 387,790 dental assistants. — [ADA HPI, The dentist workforce](https://www.ada.org/en/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dentist-workforce)
- HPI "The U.S. Dentist Workforce" (September 2025, ~200,000 dentists): in 2024 more than 1 in 4 dentists up to 10 years out of dental school were DSO-affiliated, "much higher" than older cohorts. HPI's definition: a practice is DSO-affiliated if an outside entity manages some or all non-clinical functions (billing, marketing, HR). — [ADA News, 17 Nov 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/new-dentist/2025/november/hpi-more-new-dentists-affiliated-with-dsos/)
- Earlier HPI releases: 13% of US dentists DSO-affiliated in 2022 (10.4% in 2019, 8.8% in 2017); by career stage 23% (<=10 years out), 11% (11-25 years), 7% (>25 years); Nevada highest at 23%, Alaska lowest at 2%; orthodontics 18%, general practice 13%, periodontics 10%. — [Becker's Dental summarising HPI](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/41051-13-of-dentists-are-affiliated-with-dsos-7-things-to-know.html) (search snippet; page returned 403 on direct fetch)
- Among private-practice dentists the solo share fell from 66.5% (2001) to 46.2% (2021); ownership fell from 84.7% (2005) to 73% (2021); DSO affiliation ~13% in 2022 and ~14% in 2023, up every year since ~7% in 2015; dental-school seniors planning to join a DSO rose from 12% (2015) to 30% (2020). — [DrBicuspid summarising HPI](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/office-management/practice-trends/article/15683050/how-dentistrys-practice-models-continue-to-evolve) (search snippet)
- HPI Annual Dental Industry Report 2020: ownership 83% (2007) to 78% (2017); DSO 7% (2017) to 8.8%; owner income $210,711 vs non-owner $141,768 (2016-18 average); owner/non-owner gap shrank 26% over 15 years. — [DrBicuspid, 26 Feb 2020](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-business/dso/article/15376090/numbers-show-steep-drop-of-dentists-in-solo-practices)
- Staffing: about 62% of dentists named staffing shortages as their biggest expected challenge for 2025 (HPI poll, late 2024); 30-40% of dentists reported recruiting hygienists or assistants in the prior three months, little changed in recent years; among those recruiting, hygienist hiring rated "very or extremely challenging" by ~95% (late 2022) and ~90% (late 2024); assistants ~87% to just over 70%; first-year dental hygiene enrolment up nearly 20% from 2020-21 to 2024-25. Anecdotes: one practice went ~18 months without finding a hygienist; another had two applicants in three months; candidates repeatedly no-showed interviews. — [ADA News, 27 Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/five-years-later-staffing-shortages-infection-control-since-the-covid-19-pandemic/)
- HPI Q3 2025 Economic Outlook (per Becker's): 770 respondents; 462 had recruited in the prior three months; 74% of those rated hygienist hiring extremely challenging; 32.5% had recruited hygienists and 37.5% assistants. — [Becker's Dental](https://www.beckersdental.com/staffing-issues/how-dentists-are-faring-against-workforce-shortages/) (search snippet; fetch 403)
- Medicaid: 41% of US dentists participated in Medicaid or CHIP in 2024, about the same as 2015, despite a decade of adult-benefit expansions; Medicaid fee-for-service reimbursement in most states is below 50% of dentist charges and below 60% of private-insurance reimbursement; 49% of Medicaid/CHIP enrollees aged 1-20 saw a dentist in 2024 (44% in 2020, 52% in 2019). An earlier HPI study put participation at 73% for pediatric dentists vs 43% for general dentists. — [ADA News, Dec 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/december/dental-care-utilization-stagnant-among-medicaid-beneficiaries/); [ADA HPI, Dental care in Medicaid programs](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/coverage-access-outcomes/dental-care-in-medicaid-programs)
- Uninsured: a vendor blog attributes to HPI (2025) the figure of 74 million Americans (22%) without dental insurance. — [Clerri (vendor, citing HPI)](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-membership-plan-statistics/)

### Inferences
- The "typical" independent target practice is run by an owner in their 40s-60s who is older than the DSO-affiliated cohort, with fewer staff than they want, and with a hygiene schedule limited by who they can hire rather than by chair capacity.
- Because HPI defines DSO affiliation by outsourcing non-clinical functions (billing, marketing, HR), the independent practice by definition carries those functions in-house, usually on one or two front-desk people.

### Gaps
- The HPI page listing "U.S. dentist affiliation by practice size and DSO status" could not be fetched, so the 2024-2025 overall DSO share (as opposed to the under-10-years cohort) is not confirmed from a primary page.
- No HPI figure found for front-office (non-clinical) staffing difficulty specifically.

## Key question 2: What does a US front-desk team do each day and where does time go, especially on insurance verification and claims?

### Takeaway
The day is a loop of pre-visit verification, huddle, check-in/check-out with balance collection, same-day claim submission and an end-of-day deposit reconciliation; the single biggest documented time sink is insurance eligibility and benefits verification, variously estimated at 10-30 minutes per patient or about two hours a day, nearly all from phone queues and inconsistent payer portals.

### Cited Findings
Daily routine and documents (practice-management guidance, mostly consultant or vendor):
- Before the morning huddle the front office checks the answering service/machine, email and practice-management portals for schedule changes, and arrives with open slots in upcoming days to fill; huddle inputs from the front desk include scheduling gaps, overdue accounts, production and new-patient goals and referral candidates; a sign asks waiting patients to sit while the team meets. — [Burkhart Dental, Front office task flow guidelines](https://www.burkhartdental.com/practice-guide/front-office-systems/front-office-task-flow-guidelines/)
- Daily manager checklist items: verify lab cases have been received for today's patients; identify outstanding balances for today's appointments for collection at check-out; review and submit the previous day's insurance claims; change the previous day's procedures from "planned" to "completed" in the system; reconcile the daily deposit against the practice-management ledger. — [GoAudits, Dental office manager daily checklist](https://goaudits.com/checklist/dental-office-manager-daily-checklist/918/156/)
- End-of-day in practice software: print a day sheet, a transaction detail report, a credit-card settlement report and a bank deposit slip; build an end-of-day checklist for the practice. — [Carestream Dental WinOMS help](https://help.carestreamdental.com/rh/web/server/WinOMS/projects_responsive/WinOMS_CS_OnlineHelp/Managing_Your_Practice/Optimizing_Your_Practice/Performing_End_of_Day_Procedures.htm)
- Deposit timing: the deposit may be prepared at day's end but not closed until the next morning because card transactions can still be pending. — [DentistryIQ, end of day for the business coordinator](https://www.dentistryiq.com/front-office/collections/article/16353145/what-happens-at-the-end-of-the-day-in-the-life-of-your-business-coordinator)
- End-of-day reconciliation: confirming every payment collected is accounted for is "the first and most critical step", followed by balancing the daily deposit and verifying insurance and claims activity. — [Dental ClaimSupport, end-of-day checklist](https://www.dentalclaimsupport.com/blog/dental-office-managers-end-of-day-checklist)

Insurance verification time (all vendor or consultant sources; none independently measured):
- Manual verification 15-30 minutes of staff time per patient; 30 patients a day works out to five to seven staff hours. — [Overjet (vendor)](https://www.overjet.com/blog/verify-dental-insurance)
- 10-15 minutes per patient. — [Overjet (vendor)](https://www.overjet.com/blog/average-time-savings-ai-dental-insurance-verification-for-modern-dental-practices)
- About 12 minutes per patient cited as industry data; forum users report up to 20. — [RevenueWell (vendor)](https://www.revenuewell.com/article/dental-ai-insurance-verification-vs-manual-checks-which-saves-more-time)
- 5-30 minutes per patient depending on whether the payer has a usable portal or forces a phone call (attributed to Curve Dental); phone verifications "regularly stretch 15 to 30 minutes"; 25 appointments a day = 6-12 hours of verification work. — [Lassie (vendor)](https://www.lassie.ai/blog/verify-dental-insurance-before-appointment) (search snippet; page 404 on fetch)
- Average 23 minutes per patient. — [Ainora (vendor)](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-insurance-verification-ai-statistics)
- Outsourced-verification vendor on Dentaltown (28 Jun 2017): checking eligibility before appointments takes "an average of two hours a day", at least 10 hours a week and 40 a month; one call can run "20+ minutes" through automated prompts; insurer websites range from comprehensive to "extremely limited and inaccurate"; staff put eligibility last because it is the most time-consuming task, so "easy" payers get checked first and difficult ones slip; the vendor checks three days ahead to leave time for hard cases. — [Dentaltown post 7458](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/7458/is-dental-insurance-eligibility-taking-too-much-time)
- One practice reported cutting verification time from 20 to 5 hours a week with a vendor tool (vendor case study, not independent). — [Pearl case study (vendor)](https://hellopearl.com/case-studies/case-study-how-pearl-freed-a-busy-practice-from-the-insurance-hold-queue)

New-patient intake documents seen in the sweep:
- A pediatric practice's online new-patient form (Jotform) bundles demographics, treatment authorisation and financial agreement, appointment and cancellation policy ("$25 charge for broken appointments without 24-hour notice"), one-parent-in-clinical-area rule, and medical history. — [Pediatric Dentistry of Hamburg, Lexington KY, Jotform](https://form.jotform.com/230374684869168)
- A Lexington general practice tells new patients to "save time at your first visit by printing out and completing the patient forms in advance, or following the link sent by its automated system". — [TLC Dentistry Lexington (search snippet; officite host returned 403)](https://my.officite.com/0045748/new-patients)
- Tulsa: "Complete the below form and our Mose Family Dentistry team will confirm the details with you shortly" (request, then manual confirmation). — [Mose Family Dentistry](https://www.mosefamilydentistry.com/)
- Albuquerque: "Call or message us to schedule your first visit" and "Call us today to verify your benefits". — [Rainbow Dental](http://www.rainbowdentalabq.com/)

### Inferences
- Verification is done per appointment, per payer, often by phone; it is deadline-driven (must be done before the visit), repetitive (same questions each time), and document-heavy (the breakdown must be recorded in the chart). This makes it the step most likely to be skipped or done partially for "difficult" payers, which then surfaces later as a denial or patient balance.
- The end-of-day close depends on paper (day sheet, deposit slip) and on card settlements that lag, so reconciliation routinely spills into the next morning.
- Small practices with online "request" forms still confirm by phone, so the web form does not remove a front-desk step; it adds one.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) time-and-motion study of a US dental front desk was found; all per-patient minutes above come from firms selling verification software.
- No source found on hygiene recall and reactivation benchmarks (share of active patients unscheduled, recall effectiveness) or on treatment-plan presentation and case-acceptance follow-up practices; these could not be searched before the budget ran out.
- No source found on lab-case tracking failures (lost cases, slips) or on pre-authorisation/predetermination turnaround times.
- No source found on CareCredit/Sunbit merchant fees and workflow; the sweep only shows which practices advertise them.

## Key question 3: What is the claims and verification cycle (clearinghouses, payer portals, phone calls, attachments, AR buckets) and where do denials and delays come from?

### Takeaway
Claims go out daily, often with x-rays, perio charts and narratives attached; denials cluster around bundling, frequency limits, missing narratives/attachments, coordination-of-benefits and wrong identifiers; appeals must be filed within 90-180 days and only a small share of claims is ever appealed. Consultants want 90+ day AR under 5-10% of total, which practices find hard to hit.

### Cited Findings
- Denial causes: bundling (e.g. D0274 four bitewings billed with a comprehensive exam), missing or insufficient narratives (periodontal procedures, crown replacement within the frequency limit), frequency limits (cleanings, x-rays, fluoride, sealants; mid-year insurer switch; calendar-year vs policy-year mismatch), coordination-of-benefits errors (wrong primary/secondary, missing primary EOB, miscalculated secondary liability), incorrect patient data (DOB mismatch, wrong subscriber ID, missing NPI). Appeals require the original claim, the EOB with the denial reason highlighted, clinical notes, radiographs or photos, and a cover letter; most carriers require appeals within 90-180 days of denial, some shorter; a simple denial takes about 30 minutes to rework, COB or narrative cases "significantly longer"; example of three hours chasing a $28 reimbursement. — [Curve Dental, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.curvedental.com/dental-blog/dental-claim-denials-whats-causing-them-and-how-to-stop-the-cycle)
- Appeals: about 5% of claims reach appeal and about 50% of those are reversed, mostly periodontal because attachments are required more than for other CDT categories; each successive appeal needs a new, more detailed narrative; odds of reversal fall with time. — [dentalbilling.com (search snippet; fetch 403)](https://dentalbilling.com/?p=31917)
- Reviewers decide only from what was submitted; send an enlarged x-ray of the affected area rather than a full series, have the dentist mark decay on the image, include the perio chart for perio claims; keep narratives factual. — [Periospectives, "Managing the maze of appeals"](https://onlinedigeditions.com/article/Dental+Insurance%3A+Managing+The+Maze+Of+Appeals/3263094/551201/article.html) (search snippet)
- Medicaid example (California Medi-Cal Dental): secondary claims must attach the primary carrier's EOB/RA; clerical denials include missing treating-provider NPI; denials for failure to provide radiographs/attachments where required. — [Medi-Cal Dental billing tips](https://www.dental.dhcs.ca.gov/Providers/Medi_Cal_Dental/Provider_Training/BillingTips); [Medi-Cal FAQ on claim denials](https://www.dental.dhcs.ca.gov/DC_documents/providers/FAQ_claim_denials.pdf)
- AR benchmarks (consultant/vendor): 90+ day balances under 10% of total AR, net collections 98-100% after contractual write-offs — [Burkhart Dental](https://www.burkhartdental.com/is-your-ar-healthy/); 91+ days 5% or less, 0-30 days ~65% or less, 31-60 no more than 20%, 61-90 no more than 10%, acknowledging 5% "could seem unrealistic for a lot of us" — [ACT Dental](https://www.actdental.com/blog/data-snapshot-accounts-receivable-percentage); 90+ days about 2-3% of goal AR and total AR no more than about half a month's production — [Dental Products Report](https://dentalproductsreport.com/does-your-accounts-receivable-need-cpr); 90-day collection percent = collected / production, "paid for most work within 45 days" is a good sign — [Dental Intelligence (vendor)](https://learn.dentalintel.com/en/articles/6078844-collections-board-90-day-collection-percent)
- Credentialing (prerequisite to being paid in-network): documents a new dentist must assemble include dental licence, DEA registration, NPI, CAQH login, CV, diploma/residency certificates, board certification, malpractice information, government ID, immunisation records, references; timelines "vary by payer and state"; "Medicaid enrollment in particular can take months"; one dentist's start date slipped nearly three months; CAQH must be re-attested "every 120 days when required"; in-network status is tied to a tax ID, so job, location or ownership changes reset it; 85% of surveyed new dentists were not enrolled in (or unsure about) the ADA Credentialing Service and ~43% did not know it existed. — [ADA News, 6 Jul 2026](https://adanews.ada.org/new-dentist/2026/june/credentialing-101-what-new-dentists-need-to-know)
- Per-payer credentialing estimates from vendors: 30-45 days (Neolytix), 60-120 days per carrier (Pearl), 90-120 days after application (eAssist); delay causes: incomplete CAQH profile, missing documents, malpractice gaps, slow responses to carrier requests. — [Pearl (vendor)](https://hellopearl.com/blog/dental-credentialing-a-step-by-step-guide); [Neolytix (vendor)](https://neolytix.com/articles/dental-credentialing-guide/)
- CDT 2026: 60 code changes including 31 additions, effective 1 Jan 2026 (new codes for point-of-care saliva testing, cracked-tooth testing, duplicate dentures, implant debridement for peri-implantitis, occlusal-guard cleaning); ADA sells the CDT 2026 kit and an 8-course CE series. — [ADA News, 29 Sep 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/september/new-cdt-codes-you-should-know-for-2026/). Delta Dental of Kansas lists 31 new, 14 revised, 9 editorial, 6 deleted (including D1352 preventive resin restoration and D9248 non-IV conscious sedation, gone after 31 Dec 2025) and payer-specific policy changes for D0180, D4341/D4342, D4355, D7953. — [Delta Dental of Kansas PDF (search snippet; fetch 403)](https://deltadentalks.com/uploads/media/DDKS_CDT_2026_Code_Changes.pdf)

### Inferences
- The cycle is: verify (phone/portal) -> treat -> code (CDT, updated every January) -> submit via clearinghouse with attachments -> wait -> post EOB -> bill patient balance -> chase denials within a 90-180 day window -> write off small amounts. Each step has a document (breakdown of benefits, claim form, x-ray/perio chart/narrative, EOB, statement, appeal letter) and most have a deadline.
- Because only ~5% of claims are appealed and small denials cost more to rework than they return, independent practices quietly absorb a stream of small write-offs; the vendor AR targets (90+ days under 5-10%) describe an aspiration rather than the norm.
- Credentialing is a recurring, document-heavy, multi-month burden every time a dentist joins, leaves, moves or the practice changes tax ID, and Medicaid is the slowest payer to enrol with.

### Gaps
- No dental-specific, independent denial-rate statistic was found (sources give causes, not rates).
- Clearinghouse mechanics (DentalXChange, Vyne/NEA FastAttach, payer-portal uploads) could not be researched before the search budget ran out; no source is cited for them here.
- No source on predetermination turnaround or on EOB-posting error rates.

## Key question 4: What do state boards and HIPAA impose in paperwork, and what gets fined?

### Takeaway
Federal rules (HIPAA privacy, security and breach; OSHA bloodborne pathogens; No Surprises Act estimates; TCPA; FTC review rule; DEA) stack on state rules (records retention that varies from 5 to 10 years, x-ray registration and inspection fees, specialty-advertising rules). OCR's dental enforcement is dominated by right-of-access failures and basic privacy lapses, with penalties of $25,000-$140,000 against small practices.

### Cited Findings
HIPAA privacy and enforcement:
- Shen Smiles (Pennsylvania): OCR proposed a $140,000 civil money penalty in July 2024; the practice challenged it; settled 11 Aug 2026 without admitting liability; announced 8 Oct 2026. Origin: an April 2020 complaint that repeated requests for a former patient's records went unfulfilled (the practice said a workforce member stole the documents). OCR found records stored in plastic boxes, on the floor and in a private bathroom; files "regularly missing when patients arrived"; no formal HIPAA Privacy Rule training; no evidence of privacy or breach-notification policies. — [Paubox, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.paubox.com/blog/dental-practice-agrees-to-140k-settlement-after-records-complaint); HHS release at https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-office-civil-rights-settles-hipaa-privacy-rule-investigation-shen-smiles.html (hhs.gov blocked direct fetch)
- OCR imposed a $70,000 civil money penalty on a solo family dental practice, announced 21 Oct 2024 (search snippet; details not fetched). — [JD Supra topic page](https://www.jdsupra.com/topics/settlement/data-breach/ocr)
- Right-of-access settlements with dental practices: Great Expressions Dental Center of Georgia $80,000 (patient refused copies unless she paid a $170 copying fee; corrective action plan); Paradise Family Dental $25,000 (CAP with implementation reports, annual reports, updated access policies). — [HealthITSecurity (search snippet; fetch 403)](https://healthitsecurity.com/news/ocr-settles-three-hipaa-right-of-access-cases-with-dental-practices)
- First Choice Dental (Wisconsin, multi-location) paid $1.2 million to settle a private class action over an October 2023 hack (not an OCR action). — [JD Supra (search snippet)](https://www.jdsupra.com/topics/data-breach/ocr)

HIPAA Security Rule update (proposed, not final):
- OCR proposed the Security Rule overhaul on 27 Dec 2024 (Federal Register 6 Jan 2025). It would remove the "addressable vs required" distinction so nearly all specifications become mandatory; make encryption and multi-factor authentication required; require a written inventory of systems handling ePHI; require procedures to restore critical systems within 72 hours. The ADA joined a 17 Feb 2025 coalition letter urging OCR to rescind the rule. OCR's agenda targeted May 2026 for final action. — [Washington State Dental Association, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.wsda.org/news/blog/2026/04/29/hipaa-security-rule---proposed-rulemaking-update)
- Georgia Dental Association (updated 26 May 2026): final action now expected July 2027; once published, 60 days to effect plus 180 days to comply (~8 months). Proposed duties: asset inventory of all ePHI technology, regular risk assessments; MFA for remote access, email, cloud apps, admin logins, systems with ePHI and desktop logins; encryption at rest and in transit including email, portable devices, cloud, backups, file transfers, internal networks; written and tested incident-response and disaster-recovery plans; business associates directly bound. GDA advises practices to prepare now and to question vendors. — [GDA, 26 May 2026](https://gadental.org/news/what-dental-practices-need-to-know-about-the-proposed-hipaa-security-rule-changes)
- Trade coverage adds mandatory vulnerability scanning and penetration testing and annual documentation review to the proposal; risk analysis is already required but currently has no set timeframe. — [DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/patient-communication/privacy/article/15820763/the-hipaa-security-shakeup-is-your-dental-practice-ready) (search snippet); [CDA](https://cda.org/newsroom/endorsed-services/is-your-dental-practice-prepped-for-the-upcoming-hipaa-rule-changes) (search snippet)

OSHA:
- Bloodborne Pathogens Standard 29 CFR 1910.1030: written exposure-control plan identifying tasks and job classifications with exposure, with a schedule for implementing training, hepatitis B vaccination, post-exposure follow-up, record-keeping and infection-control procedures; made available to employees and updated at least annually; engineering controls first (high-volume evacuation, rubber dams, sharps containers); sharps injury log protecting confidentiality; annual training including safer sharps and free HBV vaccine. — [RDH Magazine](https://rdhmag.com/infection-control/personal-protective-equipment/article/16403665/review-of-bloodborne-pathogens-standard-clarifies-oshas-expectations-of-dental-offices); [AccountableHQ (compliance vendor)](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/osha-standards-for-dentistry-bloodborne-pathogens-hazard-communication-ppe-and-more). osha.gov/dentistry and the 1910.1030 text returned 403 on fetch; retention periods (training records 3 years, medical/exposure records employment plus 30 years) come from the vendor and are unverified against the regulation.

No Surprises Act:
- Private dental offices must give a written good-faith estimate to uninsured or self-pay patients within three business days of a request (not triggered for services scheduled or requested fewer than three days ahead or for emergencies); balance-billing protections largely do not reach dental offices because dental benefits are "excepted benefits" (CMS); patients may start dispute resolution if billed charges exceed the estimate by at least $400; providers are generally not required to give a GFE to someone enrolled in a limited-scope dental plan. — [ADA News, Mar 2022](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2022/march/ada-addresses-no-surprises-act-questions/); [Becker's (search snippet)](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/37665-dentists-now-required-to-give-good-faith-estimates-7-things-to-know.html); [Michigan Dental Association resource page (sample GFE forms dated Feb 2022)](https://www.michigandental.org/practice-resources/regulatory-compliance/no-surprises-act/); [Pennsylvania Dental Association tip sheet](https://www.padental.org/docs/Website/Advocacy/No_Surprises_Act_Tip_Sheet.pdf)

TCPA (texting and automated calls):
- Informational appointment reminders need "prior express consent" (oral or written; the patient gives the number knowing it will be used for appointment communications); marketing texts need "prior express written consent" (signed agreement disclosing automated messaging, naming the number, not a condition of service); the 2015 FCC healthcare exception applies to existing patients who gave their number, healthcare-related content only, with easy opt-out, and the guide says it is limited to voice calls with texting rules possibly differing; damages $500 per message, $1,500 if willful, no cap, no need to prove actual damages; messages after a STOP reply or any verbal/written revocation must cease. Page last updated March 2026. — [Appointment Reminder, TCPA compliance guide (vendor)](https://appointmentreminder.com/guides/tcpa-compliance)
- Vendor guides describe a healthcare-message exemption with limits of 160 characters, one message a day and three a week, and a 2025-26 change under which a universal opt-out could require stopping all other automated communications to that number, including reminders. These were not confirmed against FCC text. — [Solum (vendor)](https://getsolum.com/tcpa-healthcare-texting-rules); [ActiveProspect (vendor)](https://activeprospect.com/blog/tcpa-rules/)

DEA:
- A three-year DEA registration costs $888 (raised from $731 in 2020, the first increase since 2012); renewal is possible up to 60 days before expiry using form 224a; after expiry there are 30 days to renew before applying as a new registrant; federal law prohibits handling controlled substances under an expired registration; dentists who prescribe only non-controlled drugs (fluoride, antibiotics) do not need to register. — [Ohio Dental Association](https://www.oda.org/?p=75428); [Becker's Dental, 2020 fee increase](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/dea-prescriber-registration-fees-to-increase-oct-1); [Pennsylvania Dental Association QuickTips](https://www.padental.org/docs/website/advocacy/sbod/QuickTips_DEA_Registration.pdf); [Becker's citing CDA on not registering](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/not-registering-with-dea-may-be-an-option-for-dentists-says-cda/)

Radiation safety (state programmes, each different):
- Virginia: dental x-ray machines on a 3-year inspection cycle; $100 registration fee invoiced every three years. — [Virginia Department of Health](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/radiological-health/radiological-health/x-ray-machine-program/dental-x-ray-machines/)
- Utah: dentists inspected every five years; $65 plus $30 per additional tube; annual registration; the Division must be notified within 14 days of acquiring, transferring or disposing of an x-ray system. — [Utah DEQ](https://deq.utah.gov/waste-management-and-radiation-control/inspections-registrations-and-fees-x-ray-program)
- Kansas: annual registration due by 1 March; $200 per facility plus $50 per tube; records of equipment purchase, transfer and disposal kept for the life of the practice. — [Kansas Department of Health and Environment](https://www.kdhe.ks.gov/DocumentCenter/View/10065/Dental-Regulations-and-Explanations-PDF)
- Minnesota: $100 base facility fee plus $40 per radiation source annually. — [Minnesota statute 144.121](https://revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2019/cite/144.121/pdf)
- California: $118 per tube per year collected every two years; each source must be registered by the owner, not the vendor. — [California Dental Association](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/environment-radiation/radiation-machine-inspections-in-the-dental-office-what-to-expect-how-to-prepare/)

State dental board records and advertising rules:
- No national retention standard. A Hawaii board compilation (2016) lists California 7 years, Illinois 10, Texas 5, New York 6; Michigan 10 years from last treatment; Minnesota 7 years after last treatment plus 7 years past majority for minors; Iowa minimum 6 years after last exam, prescription or treatment; Wisconsin rulemaking cites 10 years. — [Hawaii board minutes (2016)](https://cca.hawaii.gov/pvl/files/2013/06/dental_160919_R.doc.pdf); [Ricoh blog (secondary)](https://www.pfu-us.ricoh.com/blog/how-long-to-keep-dental-records); [Wisconsin CR 14-011](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/ruletext/CR%2014-011)
- Specialty advertising: Texas board rule 22 TAC 108.54 tied "specialist" advertising to ADA-recognised specialties; the American Academy of Implant Dentistry sued; a federal district judge and then the Fifth Circuit held the rule unconstitutional ("completely prohibits the plaintiffs from advertising as specialists in their fields solely because the ADA has not recognized their practice areas"); the Texas board adopted replacement specialty-advertising rules on 12 March 2021 after a rulemaking begun in March 2016. — [DrBicuspid, AAID ruling](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-business/regulatory-updates/article/15371674/us-judge-rules-for-aaid-over-texas-board-on-specialist-ads); [Dental Tribune, appeal upheld](https://us.dental-tribune.com/news/court-of-appeals-upholds-aaid-victory); [Dentistry Today, 2021 Texas rule](https://dentistrytoday.com/?p=63710); [American Association of Orthodontists (interested party)](https://www2.aaoinfo.org/specialty-advertising-success-in-texas/)

FTC rule on fake reviews (see also Key question 5):
- Announced 14 Aug 2024, effective 60 days after Federal Register publication (reported as 21 Oct 2024). Prohibits fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials, compensation conditioned on review sentiment, undisclosed insider (officer/manager/employee/relative) reviews, company-controlled sites posing as independent, suppression of negative reviews by threats or false accusations, and buying fake social-media indicators; FTC can seek civil penalties against knowing violators. — [FTC press release, 14 Aug 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [CDA, 17 Apr 2025 (cites up to $51,744 per violation; a prize draw for 4-5 star ratings violates the rule)](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/practice-management/promoting-a-fake-or-purchased-review-youre-violating-the-ftc-rule/)

### Inferences
- The documents a small practice must keep current at all times: Notice of Privacy Practices and acknowledgements, HIPAA policies and training logs, security risk analysis, business associate agreements, OSHA exposure-control plan (annual), HBV vaccination/declination records, sharps log, SDS binder, x-ray registration and inspection certificates, DEA certificate (3-year), state licences and CE, GFE templates and copies, TCPA consent records, records-retention schedule by patient age.
