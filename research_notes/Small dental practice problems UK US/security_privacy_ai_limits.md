## Q1. US rules: HIPAA as applied to dental practices, BAAs, consumer ChatGPT, the 2025 Security Rule update

### Takeaway
A dental practice is a HIPAA covered entity; any AI vendor that touches PHI is a business associate and must sign a BAA. Consumer ChatGPT (Free, Plus, Team/Business) has no BAA, so pasting patient data into it is an impermissible disclosure. The January 2025 proposed Security Rule rewrite would make MFA, encryption, asset inventories, six-monthly vulnerability scans and annual penetration tests mandatory with no small-practice carve-out; as of March 2026 it was still not final.

### Cited Findings
- The California Dental Association's endorsed HIPAA partner Abyde (vendor) wrote on 27 Aug 2025: "AI companies are considered business associates if they access protected health information (PHI)"; "Dentists should never use the free, public version of ChatGPT with patient data" because inputs could train the model; ChatGPT "has offered Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) case by case to paying API customers"; dentists should "avoid vendors that refuse to sign a BAA, including medical AI companies"; and "guidance from the Office for Civil Rights on AI is forthcoming, and state AI laws are being developed." — [CDA / Abyde, 27 Aug 2025](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/endorsed-services/ai-in-dentistry-what-are-the-hipaa-violation-risks/)
- OpenAI's help centre (quoted via IntuitionLabs, page updated 9 Aug 2026): "we don't offer a BAA for ChatGPT Business"; "only ChatGPT Enterprise or Edu customers that have a sales-managed account are eligible for a BAA for ChatGPT at this time"; the API is BAA-eligible with modified retention and "an enterprise agreement is not required to sign a BAA" for API services. Free, Plus and Team are not BAA-eligible. — [IntuitionLabs, updated 9 Aug 2026](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/is-chatgpt-hipaa-compliant). Note: HIPAA Journal (quoted in the same article) says OpenAI "will not enter into a Business Associate Agreement for the Free, Plus, Team, or Enterprise versions" — a conflict on Enterprise; the OpenAI help page itself returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch on 16 Oct 2026.
- OpenAI launched "ChatGPT for Healthcare" on 8 Jan 2026 (Boston Children's, Cedars-Sinai, HCA, UCSF); HIPAA Journal says it "is not HIPAA compliant 'out of the box'"; there is no self-serve option and BAAs are prepared case by case, previewed only under NDA. — [IntuitionLabs, 2026](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/is-chatgpt-hipaa-compliant)
- USC Schaeffer Center, quoted: "once you enter something into ChatGPT, it is on OpenAI servers and they are not HIPAA compliant ... That's the real issue, and that is, technically, a data breach." Civil penalties range "$145 to $2,190,294 per violation" by culpability tier. — [IntuitionLabs, 2026](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/is-chatgpt-hipaa-compliant)
- The proposed HIPAA Security Rule was published in the Federal Register on 6 Jan 2025; it would eliminate the "addressable" designation and mandate encryption of ePHI, MFA for all access to ePHI systems with "no size exceptions for smaller organizations", network segmentation, bi-annual vulnerability scanning and annual penetration testing; compliance would be 60 days to effective date plus 180 days (240 days total) if finalised. — [D3RX guide (vendor)](https://d3rx.com/sra/guides/hipaa-mfa-requirements); [Medcurity (vendor)](https://medcurity.com/new-proposed-updates-to-the-hipaa-security-rule/). The Federal Register page itself redirected to an "unblock" page on direct fetch (16 Oct 2026) so the primary text could not be read.
- Status as of 10 Mar 2026 (Physicians Practice): the rule "was not yet final"; HHS "expected to finalize ... by May"; implementation "180 days to one year after publication"; every control would be mandatory "regardless of practice size or revenue". Quote: "For a two-physician family practice or a solo mental health provider, they represent a sea change." Compliance costs "can reach tens of thousands of dollars for practices with thin margins." OCR position: "the size of a practice is irrelevant when it comes to compliance." "In 2022, more than half of OCR financial penalties targeted small medical practices." — [Physicians Practice, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/small-practices-face-growing-cybersecurity-burden-as-federal-rules-tighten)
- Conflicting status reports: one vendor post (Infonaligy) states "The HIPAA Security Rule overhaul is final"; another (Outsource Accelerator, Aug 2026) says it is still proposed with OMB's Unified Agenda targeting July 2027 for final action. — [Infonaligy (vendor)](https://www.infonaligy.com/blog/hipaa-final-rule-small-practice-action-plan); [Outsource Accelerator, Aug 2026](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/hipaa-security-rule-delay/). Unresolved; the HHS fact sheet (hhs.gov) returned 403.
- Senate HELP Committee voted 22-1 in late Feb 2026 to advance the Health Care Cybersecurity and Resiliency Act, which would codify MFA and encryption mandates and fund grants for under-resourced practices. — [Physicians Practice, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/small-practices-face-growing-cybersecurity-burden-as-federal-rules-tighten)
- Context (large-org): "In 2024, more than 276 million patient records were compromised, a 64% increase"; Change Healthcare affected an estimated 190 million people; healthcare has had the highest breach cost for 14 consecutive years at about $10 million per incident. — [Physicians Practice, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/small-practices-face-growing-cybersecurity-burden-as-federal-rules-tighten)

### Inferences
- A solo or two-chair practice with no IT staff faces the same mandatory control list as a hospital if the rule is finalised; the cost burden is proportionally heavier.
- Because the only BAA routes for ChatGPT are sales-managed Enterprise/Edu or API contracts, a small practice using the consumer app is outside any BAA by default.

### Gaps

## Q2. HHS OCR enforcement against dental practices (names, fines, dates, causes)

### Takeaway
OCR has penalised at least 12 dental practices since 2019, mostly small ones, for three recurring causes: refusing or delaying patient record requests, disclosing PHI when replying to online reviews, and having no privacy officer, policies or training. Penalties range from $5,000 to $140,000; the largest dental-related amount ($350,000, Westend Dental, Dec 2024) came from a state attorney general over a concealed ransomware attack.

### Cited Findings
- HIPAA Journal's penalty list shows these dental entries: 2019 Elite Dental Associates $10,000 (social media disclosure, notice of privacy practices); 2022 New Vision Dental $23,000 (PHI released on social media); 2022 Great Expressions Dental Center of Georgia $80,000 (right of access, delay/fee); 2022 Family Dental Care $30,000 (right of access); 2022 B. Steven L. Hardy DDS / Paradise Family Dental $25,000 (right of access); 2022 Lawrence Bell Jr DDS $5,000 (right of access); 2022 Dr U. Phillip Igbinadolor DMD & Associates $50,000 civil money penalty (social media disclosure); 2022 Northcutt Dental-Fairhope $62,500 (marketing disclosure, no privacy officer); 2022 Dr Brockley $30,000 (right of access); 2024 Gums Dental Care $70,000 civil money penalty (failure to provide timely access); 2026 Dr Linda L. Shen dba Shen Smiles $140,000 (lack of Privacy Rule policies, procedures and safeguards). No dental cases listed for 2020, 2021, 2023 or 2025. — [HIPAA Journal penalty list](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-violation-fines/)
- Shen Smiles (Pennsylvania): HHS announced the $140,000 settlement on 8 Oct 2026; OCR had proposed the penalty in July 2024; resolved 11 Aug 2026 without admission. Origin: April 2020 complaint about unfulfilled repeated record requests. OCR found records kept "in plastic boxes, the floor, and a private bathroom", files often missing at appointments, "no formal HIPAA Privacy Rule training", and no evidence of privacy or breach-notification policies. OCR said the absence of harm "cannot be attributed to any actions by Shen Smiles." — [Paubox, 12 Oct 2026 (vendor)](https://www.paubox.com/blog/dental-practice-agrees-to-140k-settlement-after-records-complaint)
- Northcutt Dental-Fairhope (Alabama), $62,500: shared patient names and contact details with a campaign manager and marketing firm for the owner's state senate race; OCR found no designated privacy official and no HIPAA policies. — [search summary citing OCR roundup](https://natlawreview.com/node/166110/printable/pdf)
- Family Dental Care, PC (Chicago) paid $30,000 and adopted a corrective action plan in one of three dental right-of-access cases resolved together in Sept 2022. — [HealthITSecurity](https://healthitsecurity.com/news/ocr-settles-three-hipaa-right-of-access-cases-with-dental-practices)
- Westend Dental LLC (Indianapolis; six entities, about 17,000 patients): MedusaLocker ransomware on or about 20 Oct 2020; breach form not filed until 28 Oct 2022 ("more than two years after"); the practice told the state "This was not a ransomware attack. We did not receive any ransom demand" while written communications showed it knew; no forensic investigation; usernames and passwords stored in plain text on the compromised server and reused across servers; no risk analysis; no HIPAA training before Nov 2023; no password policy until at least Jan 2024; no BAA with the non-employee who helped manage the practices; servers "located, unprotected, in employee break rooms and bathrooms"; incomplete third-party backups; no RDP entry-point assessment. The investigation started from a patient's complaint that their records "could not be provided due to a hacking incident." $350,000 consent order with the Indiana Attorney General, lawsuit filed 23 Dec 2024; the state called it "an absolute horror show as far as privacy, security, and HIPAA compliance are concerned." HIPAA Journal notes OCR could still pursue penalties. — [HIPAA Journal, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.hipaajournal.com/indiana-ag-westend-dental-hipaa-violations/); [IBJ](https://www.ibj.com/articles/dental-office-agrees-to-pay-state-350k-after-data-breach-privacy-investigation)
- First Choice Dental (Wisconsin) agreed a $1.2 million private class-action settlement after an October 2023 hack (not an OCR action). — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/node/166110/printable/pdf)
- The online-review-response case on HIPAA Journal's list for 2023 (Manasa Health Center, $30,000) is a psychiatric practice, not dental; dental review cases are Elite Dental (2019), Igbinadolor (2022, Google review) and New Vision Dental (2022, Yelp). — [HIPAA Journal penalty list](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-violation-fines/)

### Inferences
- Patient record requests are the most common trigger of federal action against small dental practices: they are cheap for patients to file and easy for OCR to prove.
- A ransomware event in a small practice tends to surface indirectly (a records request that cannot be met), which then exposes every missing control.

### Gaps
- hhs.gov resolution-agreement page returned 403; dates beyond the year for 2022 cases were not confirmed from the primary source.
- Phoenix Healthcare-style 2024 right-of-access cases could not be checked for dental status.

## Q3. HHS tracking-technology guidance and the 2024 court ruling; FTC on health data and reviews

### Takeaway
OCR's December 2022 bulletin treating website trackers as HIPAA disclosures was partly vacated on 20 June 2024 (AHA v. Becerra, N.D. Texas); HHS withdrew its appeal on 29 Aug 2024. Guidance on authenticated pages (patient portals) still stands, and private wiretap class actions over pixels continue.

### Cited Findings
- On 20 June 2024 Judge Mark Pittman held the March 2024 revised guidance for unauthenticated pages "was promulgated in clear excess of HHS's authority under HIPAA"; the ruling "does not change OCR's guidance on tracking technologies on authenticated web pages" such as patient portals. Timeline: original bulletin Dec 2022; revised Mar 2024; vacated June 2024. — [HIPAA Journal, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.hipaajournal.com/texas-judge-vacates-ocr-website-tracking-technology-guidance/)
- HHS filed a Fifth Circuit appeal then withdrew it on 29 Aug 2024, about ten days later. Commentators: "OCR's decision to abandon its appeal is unlikely to stop the current wave of litigation." — [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/hhs-ocr-withdraws-tracking-technologies-appeal-in-aha-v-becerra); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/hhs-reverses-course-will-no-longer-appeal-federal-courts-hospital-web-tracker-decision); [MoFo, 18 Jul 2024](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/240718-federal-judge-vacates-ocr-guidance)

- FTC Health Breach Notification Rule: amendments finalised 26 Apr 2024 (3-2 vote), published 30 May 2024, effective 29 Jul 2024; covers health apps and online services not subject to HIPAA; civil penalties cited at $51,744 per violation (inflation-adjusted). — [Jones Day, Jul 2024](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2024/07/ftc-issues-final-health-breach-notification-rule); [MoFo, 14 May 2024](https://www.mofo.com/pdf/resources/insights/240514-ftc-issues-final-rule-to-expand)
- FTC fake-reviews rule: final rule bans buying or selling fake reviews and lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators; reported effective 21 Oct 2024 (one dental source says Sept 2024 — conflict); applies to reviews a real person did not write, misrepresented or compensated patient reviews, and insider reviews without disclosure, "wherever they are used, including websites, social media, and advertising". The ADA had asked the FTC in Jan 2023 to help protect dental practices from misleading online reviews. — [CDA](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/practice-management/promoting-a-fake-or-purchased-review-youre-violating-the-ftc-rule/); [ADA News, Jan 2023](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2023/january/ada-asks-ftc-to-help-protect-dental-practices-from-misleading-online-reviews/); [DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/what-healthcare-businesses-need-to-know-about-the-ftcs-final-rule-on-fake-reviews/); a Minnesota dentist's 5-star ratings came under investigation for fake reviews — [Fox 9](https://fox9.com/news/fake-reviews-fox-9-investigators)

### Inferences
- A practice website with an appointment-booking form behind a login is still covered by the surviving guidance; most small-practice sites mix public and authenticated pages.
- Reviews are a two-sided trap for a small practice: replying with any patient detail is a HIPAA/GDC breach, while soliciting or seeding reviews is now an FTC rule violation.

### Gaps
- No FTC enforcement action against a dental practice found; the fake-reviews effective date conflicts between sources.

## Q4. ADA AI guidance/policy; state dental board rules; TCPA; FDA status of AI dental imaging tools; malpractice insurer guidance

### Takeaway
The ADA has issued technical standards and a technical report on AI (not a House of Delegates policy found); the AGD adopted an AI statement in 2023. FDA has cleared several radiograph AI tools from Overjet, VideaHealth and Pearl as 510(k) devices. State AI disclosure laws (California AB 3030, effective 1 Jan 2025) and TCPA texting rules are in scope but not yet verified from primary text.

### Cited Findings
- The AGD House of Delegates adopted a statement "Artificial Intelligence in Dentistry" in 2023 holding that AI should not supersede or replace the dentist in clinical decisions. — [AGD policy](https://www.agd.org/advocacy/agd-priorities/agd-policies/agd-policies/artificial-intelligence-in-dentistry)
- ADA released two draft AI standards for comment in Oct 2024, one on "criteria for annotating and collecting data from 2D radiographs"; it "does not prescribe or endorse any specific AI implementation methodology". A Jan 2025 ADA technical report "calls for external validation of AI algorithms in dentistry". — [ADA News Oct 2024 index](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2024/october/); [Oregon Dental, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.oregondental.org/news-details/2025/01/16/ADA-technical-report-calls-for-external-validation-of-AI-algorithms-in-dentistry) (page returned 404 on direct fetch; summary from search snippet only)
- ASDA policy H-15 (2024) asks the ADA Council on Ethics to develop AI guidelines "emphasizing transparency, fairness, and patient privacy". — [ASDA H-15](https://www.asdanet.org/about-asda/leaders-and-governance/current-statements-of-position-or-policy/health-care-delivery/h-15-ethical-use-of-ai-in-dental-settings-(2024))
- FDI adopted an AI policy statement at its Sept 2024 General Assembly (Istanbul). — [FDI policy PDF](https://fdiworlddental.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/DPS4-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Dentistry_0.pdf)
- FDA: Overjet Dental Assist (K210187) cleared May 2021 for bone-level measurement; Overjet Caries Assist and a fourth clearance for younger patients; VideaHealth K232384 announced 3 Jan 2024 covering "30+ detections" for ages 3 and up. — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/fda-plays-tooth-fairy-awarding-clearance-videahealths-cavity-spotting-ai); [VideaHealth, 4 Jan 2024](https://app.videahealth.io/news/videa-dental-assist-clearance); [Becker's Dental 2023](https://www.beckersdental.com/ai-teledentistry/41476-4-dental-ai-companies-earning-fda-clearance-patents-in-2023.html)
- FDA "List of AI-enabled medical devices" (first 100,000 characters read; most recent visible decision 29 Jun 2026) shows dental radiograph 510(k) clearances: Overjet Iris Intelligent Imaging System K260082 (10 Apr 2026); Overjet CBCT Assist K251514 (5 Dec 2025); Pearl Second Opinion Panoramic K250525 (14 Nov 2025), Second Opinion CS K243234 (12 Jun 2025), Second Opinion 3D K243989 (23 May 2025), Second Opinion Pediatric K243893 (5 May 2025), Second Opinion BLE K243230 (9 May 2025), Second Opinion Periapical Radiolucency Contours K243145 (10 Apr 2025), Second Opinion CC K242522 (16 Jan 2025); Videa Dental AI K251002 (19 Sep 2025); also Diagnocat K252934 (15 Jan 2026), DS Core Detect K253009 (7 Jan 2026), DTX Studio Assist K252086 (17 Nov 2025), Better Diagnostics Caries Assist K242338 (7 Mar 2025). The list's intro describes devices as "authorized for marketing in the United States" and does not label them decision-support only. — [FDA AI-enabled device list](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/list-artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices)
- ADA standards on AI (ADA News, 3 Feb 2025): ADA White Paper No. 1106:2022 (overview of AI uses); ADA Technical Report No. 1109:2025 (evaluation of dental image analysis systems), which "describes the need for data and methods to be private and secure to avoid bias" and for an independent validation dataset "kept by a third party that is not an AI manufacturer"; ANSI/ADA Standard No. 1110-1:2025 (validation dataset guidance, image annotation and data collection), "the first U.S. standard on AI in dentistry approved by ANSI"; proposed ISO 18374 on 2D radiograph AI data. Robert Faiella DMD, chair of ADA Standards Working Group 12.7: "the need for guidance on the responsible use of AI in dentistry is greater than ever"; "Standards and regulatory frameworks are essential in guiding dentists toward responsible AI use." The article states no specific privacy duties for dentists. — [ADA News, 3 Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/what-are-the-standards-for-ai-use-in-dentistry/)

- Malpractice insurer guidance: The Dentists Insurance Company (TDIC) Risk Management, 27 Jan 2025 (reposted by NJDA 9 Mar 2026): "AI systems require vast amounts of patient data to learn and function effectively"; "Dentists must remember that AI is a tool to assist — not replace — their professional judgment"; "liability in cases of malpractice involving AI decisions is a legal gray area"; dentists should "Obtain informed consents that explicitly cover AI use", set "protocols for AI use, including decision-making and documentation", and verify AI diagnostic output against the standard of care; risks listed: data privacy/security, diagnostic errors, algorithmic bias, lack of human oversight, unclear liability, integration. Chatbots are listed as a common use but not analysed; AI notes not mentioned; BAAs not mentioned. — [CDA/TDIC, 27 Jan 2025](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/practice-management/ai-in-dentistry-strategies-for-managing-risks-and-potential-for-malpractice-claims); [NJDA/TDIC, 9 Mar 2026](https://www.njda.org/news-information/news-details/2026/03/09/navigating-the-risks-of-ai-enhanced-dentistry)
- American Association of Endodontists warns general AI tools "lack knowledge of carrier claims histories, state rules, and policy exclusions" and can give confident but unreliable coverage answers. — [AAE](https://www.aae.org/specialty/why-dentists-should-be-careful-when-using-ai-to-make-insurance-decisions/)
- State AI law: California AB 3030 effective 1 Jan 2025 requires a disclaimer on generative-AI patient communications about clinical information (written: at the start of each message; audio: verbally at start and end); exempt if "read and reviewed by a human licensed or certified health care provider"; administrative messages (scheduling, billing reminders) are not covered; violations go to the provider's licensing board; covered entities are "health facilities, clinics, physician's offices and offices of group practices" — dental offices are not named in any source found. — [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/california-requires-disclaimers-health-care-providers-ai-generated-patient); [Sidley, 28 Sep 2024](https://www.sidley.com/en/-/media/resource-pages/ai-monitor/laws-and-regulations/20240928-california-ab-3030-artificial-intelligence-in-health-care-services.pdf?la=en); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/california_passes_novel_law_governing_generative_ai_healthcare_1224.html)
- TCPA: the FCC's one-to-one consent rule for marketing calls/texts was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit on 24 Jan 2025, three days before its 27 Jan 2025 effective date, for conflicting with the TCPA's "prior express consent"; healthcare organisations can keep a single consent covering related providers; state laws can be stricter. No source found addressing dental appointment-reminder texts specifically. — [MoFo, 30 Jan 2025](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule); [Convoso (vendor)](https://www.convoso.com/news/paul-st-clair-on-what-the-overturned-1-1-consent-rule-means-for-healthcare-contact-centers/)

### Inferences
- Imaging AI is the one dental AI category with a clear regulatory status (510(k)); note-taking, chatbots and phone AI sit outside device regulation and under HIPAA/BAA contract rules only.
- The main dental malpractice insurer's advice is consent-and-oversight focused; it does not tell dentists which AI tools are safe, which leaves a solo owner to judge BAAs and data flows alone.

## Q5. UK rules: UK GDPR/DPA, ICO, DSPT, GDC, CQC, MHRA, NHS England, Caldicott, indemnifiers on AI

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 the GDC has no AI-specific guidance; it has only signed a joint statement of intent with other UK healthcare regulators to develop "high-level principles". Dental Protection has publicly asked the GDC for clear AI rules. The MHRA (with NHS England) has said transcription-only AI scribes are not medical devices but anything supporting diagnosis or acting without clinician review is. Clinicians remain responsible for checking AI output.

### Cited Findings
- GDC AI report: analysed 45 international studies published since 2020, "primarily from China (18) and the United States (13). No UK-based studies were included." — [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/5054) (404 on direct fetch; from search snippet)
- GDC "Guidance on using social media" (undated PDF): "Your professional responsibilities, such as patient confidentiality and professional courtesy, are still fundamental when you use social media"; you must "maintain and protect patients' information by not publishing any information which could identify them on social media without their explicit consent"; Standards for the Dental Team 4.2.3: "you must not post any information or comments about patients on social networking or blogging sites"; anonymised case discussion allowed only if "the patient or patients cannot be identified"; Standard 9.1.3: "must not make personal, inaccurate or derogatory comments about patients or colleagues"; posting under another username "does not guarantee your confidentiality". — [GDC social media guidance PDF](https://www.gdc-uk.org/docs/default-source/guidance-documents/guidance-on-using-social-media); UK dento-legal advice: "A dentist should never respond to patient's complaint directly on line because of the duty of confidentiality." — [SD Magazine, 11 Apr 2019](https://www.sdmag.co.uk/2019/04/11/social-media-a-blessing-or-a-curse); [Denplan practice advice](https://www.denplan.co.uk/the-core/practice-advice/running-your-practice/navigating-social-media-comments-and-complaints-denplan); [MDDUS, Q3 2022](https://mddus.com/resources/publications/publications-library/insight-dental/q3-2022/professional-pitfalls-in-social-media-use)
- 17 Sept 2026: UK regulators for dentistry, medicine, nursing/midwifery and social work signed a joint statement of intent to create "high-level principles" for AI regulation; "This statement represents the beginning of a formal collaboration across professional regulation." No timeline given. The article also notes a parliamentary report warning clinicians using AI diagnostic software "could face negligence claims even when a defect originated in the AI system." — [Dentistry.co.uk, 17 Sep 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/09/17/gdc-to-develop-rules-for-ethical-ai-use-in-healthcare/)
- 9 Sept 2026: Dental Protection (Dr George Wright, dental director) asked the GDC to "consider clarifying expectations concerning professional accountability" for AI; "Our concern is that open-ended expectations may create uncertainty for dental professionals." Concerns: accountability, independent judgement, scrutiny of AI output, understanding system limits. — [GDPUK, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.gdpuk.com/top-news/dental-protection-calls-on-regulator-to-deliver-clear-guidance-on-ai-use/)
- MHRA guidance (with NHS England), reported 2 Aug 2026: tools used only for "transcription, consultation summaries, draft referral letters or suggested clinical codes" for clinician review "are not medical devices under current law"; products supporting "diagnosis, treatment or prevention" or that "take automated action without clinician review" are regulated as medical devices; "clinicians remain responsible for checking AI-generated transcripts, summaries or outputs". — [Dentistry.co.uk, 2 Aug 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/08/02/ai-scribes-guidance-dental-practices/)

## Q6. Incidents: ransomware and breaches at dental practices and vendors

### Takeaway
Small practices are hit through their vendors as much as directly: the 2019 DDS Safe/PerCSoft ransomware locked about 400 practices' backups; the Feb 2024 Change Healthcare attack blocked dental claims for 10+ weeks; Henry Schein (2023) and Delta Dental (2023 MOVEit, ~7 million people) exposed supplier and insurer channels.

### Cited Findings
- DDS Safe / PerCSoft (West Allis, Wisconsin): ransomware on 26 Aug 2019 encrypted files for "approximate 400 dental practices" (Wisconsin Dental Association); 80-100 restored at publication; strain reportedly REvil/Sodinokibi; ransom reportedly paid (amount unknown); some practices feared missing payroll; decryptor did not unlock all files. — [KrebsOnSecurity, 29 Aug 2019](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2019/08/ransomware-bites-dental-data-backup-firm/)
- Henry Schein: incident 27 Sept 2023, discovered 14 Oct 2023; AlphV/BlackCat claimed responsibility; 29,112 individuals notified (Maine AG); second e-commerce disruption 22 Nov 2023, US e-commerce restored 27 Nov; Goldman Sachs estimated 10-15% of customers "went elsewhere"; $0.55-$0.75 per-share earnings hit; practice management software not disrupted. — [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/henry-schein-cyberattack-29k-affected/702036/); BlackCat claimed 35TB stolen — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechsecurity/news/366594164/Henry-Schein-Hit-By-BlackCat-Ransomware-Gang)

## Q7. Shadow AI in practice: staff use, AI notes, AI receptionists, website chatbots, review responses

### Takeaway
The ADA Health Policy Institute (mid-2026) finds 43.3% of US dentists use AI for at least one task, led by imaging (22.8%), insurance verification (13.6%) and explaining findings to patients (13.2%), with charting/note-taking the top planned use (34.8%); no survey measuring staff pasting patient data into consumer ChatGPT was found. Privacy is the dominant stated worry in proxy surveys (67% in one industry poll; 91.8% of faculty in an Indian study), and every vendor and insurer source treats AI receptionists, chatbots and review replies as PHI-disclosure paths.

### Cited Findings
- ADA Health Policy Institute, HPI Perspectives (July 2026, data "as of mid-2026", quarterly panel of US dentists, sample size not stated): 43.3% use AI for at least one task; 26.4% plan to; current uses: imaging and diagnostics 22.8%, insurance verification 13.6%, explaining clinical findings to patients 13.2%, social media 10.7%, business analytics 10.1%, reception/front-desk check-in 10.1%, treatment recommendations under 5%; planned: charting and note taking 34.8%, insurance verification 32.6%, imaging 25.4%; 82.6% do not plan to use AI for treatment recommendations; hesitancy "driven in part by dentists' fears of AI replacing human judgment and the potential for errors leading to overtreatment and misdiagnoses"; 32.4% of dentists report being overworked. — [ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dentists-ai-usage-and-attitudes)

- Study of 384 dental students and faculty (Maharashtra, India): data-privacy concerns prominent, 91.8% among teaching faculty. — [PMC11914850](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11914850/)
- Cross-sectional study of dentists and patients: "actual utilization remained limited, primarily due to concerns about reliability, cost, and ethical implications." — [PMC12107015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12107015/)
- 2023 review (Journal of Dental Research, PubMed 37017291): large language models "pose challenges for patient data confidentiality and cybersecurity that must be tackled"; ChatGPT "occasionally giving incorrect answers". — [PubMed 37017291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37017291/)
- Review responses (UK): MDDUS advises that "responding directly to a negative review online could breach confidentiality"; keep the reply to an acknowledgement with a link to the complaints policy "without going into any specific detail about the patient making the comment"; MDDUS case study of a dentist advised against posting a detailed rebuttal. — [MDDUS case study, Apr 2022](https://www.mddus.com/resources/resource-library/case-files/2022/april/case-study-adverse-comment)

## Q8. Everyday pains and costs

### Takeaway
The best quantified picture of day-to-day security in small practices is a UK IT provider's analysis of 192 on-site audits at 184 dental and orthodontic practices (typical site 3-4 surgeries): 48% ran out-of-date Windows/server software, 25% used generic shared logins or weak passwords, 20% had exposed or unmanaged remote access, 20% had only a consumer router, 17% had failing or absent backups and 9% shared Wi-Fi with patients. Enforcement files (Westend, Shen Smiles) show the same pattern in the US. Cyber insurance now gates on MFA, encryption and offline backups, at roughly $1,000-$2,500 a year for a solo US practice.

### Cited Findings
