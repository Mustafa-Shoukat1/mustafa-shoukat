## Key question 1: US front desk / insurance coordinator, from new-patient call to posted EOB, step by step, with timings and costs

### Takeaway
A small US practice runs a six-stage loop (collect insurance details -> verify eligibility and benefits 1-3 days ahead -> optional predetermination -> submit claim with attachments via a clearinghouse -> post the EOB/ERA -> work denials and bill the patient). The verification stage is the most expensive and still largely manual or portal-based: the 2024 CAQH Index (2023 data) puts dental eligibility/benefit verification spending at $2.1 billion (up 15%) with a $580 million savings opportunity, and manual checks (phone, fax, email, mail) average about 12 minutes each versus 4 minutes fully electronic.

### Cited Findings

Step 1-2: collect and verify insurance (eligibility and benefits)
- The 2024 CAQH Index (2023 data) reports dental eligibility and benefit verification spending "increased by 15% to $2.1 billion", the largest rise in dental administrative spending; potential savings from switching portal/manual checks to automated electronic checks rose 7% to $580 million. CAQH classes checks as fully electronic (HIPAA 270/271 transaction), partially electronic (plan portal) or manual (phone, fax, email, mail). Dental providers told CAQH that automated transactions "often don't return data robust enough to be reliable", which keeps them on portals; portals "vary in requirements and formats". — ADA News, 24 Mar 2025, [Benefit verification drives increased administrative spending in dental offices](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/march/benefit-verification-drives-increased-administrative-spending-in-dental-offices/)

### Inferences
- The 12-vs-4 minute CAQH differential, combined with ADA's note that 270/271 responses are often not robust, explains why small practices keep a hybrid of portal plus phone even when a clearinghouse eligibility feed is turned on in the PMS.
- Per-claim clearinghouse cost (about $0.50) is trivial next to verification labour; the cost centre a small practice feels is staff hours on phone/portal, not transaction fees.

## Key question 2: UK receptionist / practice manager, from patient arrival to accepted FP17 claim, step by step, with timings

### Takeaway
The UK NHS flow is: confirm identity and NHS exemption status at reception (patient signs the declaration; staff tick "evidence seen"), take the band charge or record the exemption, clinician completes the course of treatment and the FP17 clinical data set in the PMS, the claim is transmitted by EDI to NHS BSA Compass (or keyed on Compass directly) within two months of completion, Compass validates it against the Clinical Data Set band rules, and it appears on the monthly schedule, counting toward the contract's UDA target. Exemption declarations are later sample-checked against DWP data and unconfirmed ones generate a Penalty Charge Notice to the patient.

### Cited Findings

Step 1: exemption and eligibility at reception
- FP17 completion guidance: "If an exemption or remission is claimed, then one of the 'evidence seen' boxes must be ticked - including a prison exemption"; for under-18s both the "Patient under 18" and "Evidence of Exemption or Remission seen - Yes/No" boxes must be ticked; if Regulation 11 is ticked a patient charge must be entered; "Tax Credit Exemption is no longer accepted on a course of treatment with the Date of Acceptance on or after 6 April 2025". — [NHSBSA FP17 completion guidance England V14, Aug 2025](https://cms.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/Completion_of_form_guidance_FP17-England_20250801_%28V14%29.pdf); latest version is V15.1 dated 23 Jun 2026 — [NHSBSA FP17 completion guidance V15.1 (docx)](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/Completion_of_form_guidance_FP17_England_20260623_%28V15.1%29.docx)
- NHS dental charges England from 1 Apr 2026: Band 1 £27.90, Band 2 £76.60, Band 3 £332.10, Unscheduled care £27.90 (2025/26 was £27.40 / £75.30 / £326.70). — [NHSBSA charges FAQ KA-02003](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-02003); [Oral Health Foundation on April 2025 charges](https://www.dentalhealth.org/blog/new-nhs-dental-charges-from-april-2025)
- Exemption checking after the event: patients who declare an exemption "are sample checked"; NHSBSA first checks its own databases by NHS number, then DWP benefit records via an API (response within 5 seconds); about 1.2 million checks per month (prescriptions and dental combined) plus ~15,000 patient-initiated checks via "Respond to your letter"; if not confirmed, an enquiry letter is posted, the patient can respond online/email/post, and if still unverified a PCN is issued: original dental charge plus a penalty of five times that charge, capped at £100, with a 50% surcharge on the penalty if unpaid after 28 days. Patients confirmed through Real Time Exemption Checking (RTEC) are not subject to this check. Data sharing with DWP sits under SI 2025/124. — [gov.uk Algorithmic Transparency Record: NHSBSA prescriptions and dental exemption check](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/nhsbsa-prescriptions-and-dental-exemption-check)
- PCN volumes (dental, Universal Credit claimants only): 96,489 PCNs issued 23 Feb 2024-Jan 2025 (NHSBSA adapted its UC checks in late 2023, none issued before 23 Feb 2024); 115,463 issued 21 May 2024-16 May 2025, of which 60,677 had made a payment and 0 taken to court. Income-based JSA dental PCNs: 8,232 in the 12 months to Oct 2025. — [NHSBSA FOI-02518](https://opendata.nhsbsa.net/dataset/foi-02518); [NHSBSA FOI-02776, 22 May 2025](https://opendata.nhsbsa.net/dataset/foi-02776); [NHSBSA FOI-03279](https://opendata.nhsbsa.net/dataset/foi-03279)
- NAO (2019): around 1.7 million PCNs (30% of those issued, £188m) were later withdrawn because a valid exemption was confirmed after challenge; the penalty element may be removed on contact but the treatment cost remains payable. — [NAO investigation into healthcare PCNs, 2019](https://www.nao.org.uk/report/investigation-into-healthcare-penalty-charge-notices/)
- Dental Exemption Checking Service (DECS): NHS England directly awarded NHSBSA a contract valued £7,589,216 incl. VAT for 1 Oct 2025-31 Mar 2026 to "investigate dental claims from patients who have claimed exemption from payment to verify the accuracy of the exemption declared". — [Find a Tender notice 081407-2025](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/081407-2025)
- BDA position: "Those eligible for free dentistry shouldn't face £100 penalties by default just for ticking the wrong box." — [GDPUK report](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/3201-bsa-slated-for-fining-disabled-person-with-mental-age-of-two)

- Exemption/remission categories on the FP17 (England, Oct 2022 guidance V11.4, still the structure in use): Patient under 18; Full remission HC2 certificate; Partial remission HC3 certificate; Expectant mother; Nursing mother (baby in last 12 months); Aged 18 in full-time education; Income Support; NHS tax credit exemption (withdrawn for acceptances on/after 6 Apr 2025, see V14 above); Income-based JSA; Pension Credit Guarantee Credit; Prisoner; Income-related ESA; Universal Credit. "Evidence of Exemption or Remission seen - Select either the Yes or No box to indicate whether the patient provided evidence". "Patient Charge Collected - Enter any NHS patient charge that has been collected for this course of treatment." — [NHSBSA, Guidance for the completion of FP17 activity submissions in England (October 2022) V11.4, PDF](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-11/Completion_of_form_guidance-FP17-England_202210_V11.4_Final.pdf)
- Patient declaration (PR form): "The patient must sign a paper PR form or electronic equivalent. The signed PR form must be retained by the practice as part of the patient record for a period of two years" (ProvClin & Practice staff guidance V5.0, April 2026; same wording in Clinician guidance V9.0 and the Compass FAQ). - Free NHS treatment entitlement is listed for patients on nhs.uk (under-18s, 18 in full-time education, pregnant or had a baby in previous 12 months, NHS hospital in-patients, named on HC2/HC3, qualifying benefits); patients who claim wrongly "could get a penalty charge notice". — [NHS.uk: Who is entitled to free NHS dental treatment in England](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/dentists/who-is-entitled-to-free-nhs-dental-treatment-in-england/)
- PCN mechanics on NHSBSA's own page: "We carry out checks on patient claims"; if entitlement cannot be confirmed "they'll be sent an enquiry letter asking them to confirm their entitlement"; no response within 28 days -> PCN; "The penalty charge is 5 times the original amount owed, up to a maximum of £100"; "if no payment has been made within 28 days, a surcharge may be added"; basis is the NHS (Penalty Charge) Regulations 1999. Dental payment line 0300 330 1293; Dental Exemption Checking Team, Newcastle. — [NHSBSA: Understanding penalty charges](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/understanding-penalty-charges); [NHSBSA: Penalty charges hub](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/penalty-charges)

Step 2-3: treatment, band assignment and FP17 completion
- FP17 business rules from the NHSBSA completion guidance (Oct 2022 V11.4, PDF text extracted): "All forms should be submitted within two months of the date of completion"; Incomplete treatment: for banded courses "commenced but not completed" the charge is calculated against the incomplete band box, and the Treatment Category band "must be the same as, or higher than" it; Urgent treatment box "for treatment that falls in the band 1 urgent treatment" category; Regulation 11 replacement appliance requires a patient charge "which should be 30% of the band 3 charge per appliance"; Prescription only, denture repairs, bridge repairs, arrest of bleeding and removal of sutures carry no patient charge; "Free repair/replacement - Select this box if a restoration ... has to be repaired or replaced within 12 months"; "Further treatment within 2 months - Select this box if this course of treatment was required within two months of the completion of a previous course of treatment and is in the same or lower band ... No patient charge will be deducted"; this "does not apply if the original course of treatment was either 'urgent' treatment or was incomplete treatment", and an urgent course within two months "must be claimed as a separate course of treatment. A patient charge will be deducted if applicable"; Highest BPE sextant score and NICE recall interval are mandatory on adult banded claims accepted on/after 1 Oct 2022; "Dentist's Declaration - The declaration must be completed by a qualified dentist on every form. This would normally be the performer responsible for the course of treatment"; all three declarations selected except discontinued courses (first and last only). CDS band-matching rule for acceptances on/after 1 Apr 2022: Band 1 claims may contain only Band 1 items; Band 2 may not contain Band 3 items and needs at least one Band 2 item; Band 3 needs at least one Band 3 item; Urgent may contain only Band 1/2 items; the check is skipped for incomplete-band or advanced-mandatory-services referrals. — [NHSBSA FP17 completion guidance V11.4, Oct 2022](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-11/Completion_of_form_guidance-FP17-England_202210_V11.4_Final.pdf)
- Validation error codes (Wales FP17W document, same CDS framework and same comment codes 011-014 as England): 115 "Treatment code not valid according to the claim date, the region, the patient's age, the claim type or in relation to other treatment/data items" (commonly discontinued exam/endo/extraction codes); 125 unacceptable combination of treatments; 131, 134, 135, 136, 137 Wales-specific (ACORN/Free Exam/prescription-only/'Patient Presented With' mandatory). Suggested fixes are to correct dates or remove/replace the item; NHSBSA Customer Contact Centre 0300 330 1348, 8am-6pm Mon-Fri. — [NHSBSA Common Errors FP17W from 1 April 2022](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-09/Common_Errors_FP17W_from_1_April_2022_FINAL.pdf)
- Baseline UDA values: examination = 1 UDA (Band 1); fillings, root canal, extractions typically 3 UDAs (Band 2); dentures and crowns 12 UDAs (Band 3); urgent courses were 1.2 UDAs before April 2026. — [Dentistry.co.uk, 21 Jul 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/07/20/nhs-dental-contract-reform-whats-changed-and-where-do-we-go-from-here/)
- April 2026 contract changes (England): unscheduled care paid a nationally set £70 per course plus £5 per mandated urgent course whether or not the patient attends; urgent cases to be seen within 24 hours, non-urgent unscheduled within a week; complex-care pathways with standardised fees (5+ carious teeth £284 ~6 months; 5+ carious teeth plus unstable periodontal disease £709 up to 12 months; new grade C periodontal diagnosis £248 ~6 months; extra for lab-made restorations); fluoride varnish course for EDDNs without a full check-up; fissure sealants re-banded to Band 2 worth 3 or 5 UDAs (previously 1); new denture sub-band worth 2 UDAs for modifications/repairs/relines claimable alongside Band 2; quality improvement £3,400 per practice per year; appraisals valued at 6 UDAs per eligible person. Consultation ran 8 Jul-19 Aug 2025. — [Dentistry.co.uk, 21 Jul 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/07/20/nhs-dental-contract-reform-whats-changed-and-where-do-we-go-from-here/)
- Other summaries give slightly different urgent-care figures ("average payment rises by 76% to £75 per patient", "£15 is paid up front"), rollout "in two waves across April and June 2026", fixed cash values converting back to UDAs at each practice's own rate for reconciliation, and the Dental Access Portal becoming the main route for allocating new patients. — [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/5127); [Swansea Bay UHB dental update, 28 May 2026](https://swanseabay.nhs.wales/about-us/our-health-board/our-board-business/board-meetings-and-papers/28-may-2026/4-7-dental-update/); [Denplan](https://www.denplan.co.uk/the-core/industry-and-company-news/nhs-dental-contract-what-the-latest-reforms-mean-for-your-practice)
- Resolution of the £70/£5 vs £75/£15 conflict: the £70 per course plus £5 per mandated course were the July 2025 consultation proposals; the arrangement in force from 1 April 2026 is £75 per urgent course, split into a "£15 fixed credit per required course of treatment" and a "£60 activity credit per delivered course of treatment", replacing 1.2 UDAs; a minimum urgent-care requirement of 8.2% of contract value in 2026/27 (11 courses per £10,000 of negotiated annual contract value); extra courses earn £75 credit converted to UDAs at the provider's nominal UDA value. Same source: complex-care pathways from June 2026 at £284 / £709 / £248 as monthly credits; fluoride varnish new sub-band 1 at 0.5 UDAs; fissure sealants moved from Band 1 to Band 2 at 3 or 5 UDAs; denture modifications new Band 2a at 3 UDAs and denture repairs increased to 2 UDAs; quality-improvement programme £3,400 per year with registration by 8 May 2026; appraisal funding £213 per eligible clinician; described as "no new investment ... a redistribution of the current contract base". (Accountancy-firm summary; it does not name the NHS England source letter.) — [Price Bailey, Kirsty Beck](https://www.pricebailey.co.uk/blog/nhs-contract-changes-cma-review-dental-practices/); consultation figures in [Dentistry.co.uk, 8 Jul 2025](https://dentistry.co.uk/2025/07/08/nhs-dental-contract-changes-announced-by-government/)
- FP17O (orthodontics) counting: an assessment-only claim (Part 5 Box 1 "Assessment and Review", used where the patient is not ready, is wait-listed or is referred to secondary care) carries 1 UOA; the overseas-patient assessment/de-bond item added in Oct 2022 also yields 1 UOA once validated; in EXACT, selecting "Assess & Accept for Treatment" shows the maximum UOAs for the case in the form summary. Case-start UOA values by age band were not confirmed from a reachable primary page (see Gaps). Orthodontic claims are not subject to the two-month rule. — [NHSBSA node 11067](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/node/11067); [NHSBSA quick reference guide, Oct 2022 (docx)](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-10/Changes%20to%20the%20FP17%20and%20FP17O%20from%201%20October%202022%20%E2%80%93%20a%20quick%20reference%20guide%20for%20dental%20teams.docx); [SOE help: NHS Orthodontic Treatment](https://help.softwareofexcellence.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4404424483604-NHS-Orthodontic-Treatment); [NHSBSA schedule programme 2026](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/Schedule-programme-April-2026-to-December-2026.pdf)
- 2024 Dental Recovery Plan: minimum UDA value raised to £28 (reported frozen since); New Patient Premium £15 (Band 1) or £50 (Band 2/3) per new patient not seen by an NHS dentist in the previous two years, time-limited. — [Denplan summary](https://www.denplan.co.uk/the-core/industry-and-company-news/nhs-dental-contract-what-the-latest-reforms-mean-for-your-practice); [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/5127)

Step 4: transmission to Compass and validation
- Two-month rule: "You have 2 months from the date of completion of a course of NHS treatment"; late claims have UDAs disallowed and are marked "Late Submitted Claim" on the schedule; the FP17 must still be submitted after the cut-off because the treatment must be recorded; FTR (incomplete) submissions are exempt; an FP17 received after the Year End report is published does not count toward that year's activity. — [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01820](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01820/en-us)
- Schedule programme: electronic claims need at least 48 hours to be received and processed; "Compass on-line forms MUST be authorised before they will be received and processed"; corrected (previously rejected) claims must also be received within two months of completion; orthodontic claims are not subject to the two-month rule. — [NHSBSA schedule programme Apr-Dec 2026](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/Schedule-programme-April-2026-to-December-2026.pdf)
- EDI transmissions "may not show on Compass for a number of hours"; check receipt in Compass "Activity Search Detail"; "NHS Dental Services are unable to advise on EDI transmission issues" (contact the software vendor); EDI users can also key FP17s directly on Compass. — [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01797](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01797/en-us); [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01849](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01849/en-us)
- Validation: from 1 April 2022 Clinical Data Set items must match the band claimed; failures initially produce comment codes on schedules (011 no treatment to justify band; 012 treatment entered higher than band; 013 Reg 11 claim without appliance; 014 free repair without repairable item) and NHSBSA said it would warn before these become rejecting error codes. — [NHSBSA: Changes to FP17s for dental teams in England](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/changes-fp17s-dental-teams-england)
- In EXACT (Software of Excellence), the FP17 "is sent to the NHS electronically via EXACT as part of every claim and can viewed at any point" from the course-of-treatment screen. — [Henry Schein One / SOE knowledge base](https://soeidental.atlassian.net/wiki/x/JgHsVQ)
- Dentally: an FP17 can be invalidated from the NHS tab and reappears as a task, except "if a claim has already been submitted with the exemption linked to it, you will not be able to mark it as invalid". — [Dentally community thread](https://community.dentally.com/practice-admin-45/wrongly-completed-fp17-683)
- NHS WebEDI acknowledgement messages: a replacement or deletion submission rejects if the original FP17 with the same contract and claim reference cannot be found (DentalPlus knowledge base, a different PMS). — [DentalPlus KB](https://desk.zoho.com/portal/dentalplus/en/kb/articles/nhs-acknowledgement-messages)

Step 5: year end, UDA counting, clawback
- Year-end reconciliation is "completed by September each year"; UDA/UOA delivery below 96% = financial clawback up to 100% of the contract's activity target value plus a breach notice; 96-100% = shortfall carried forward; 100-102% = over-delivery carried forward; above 102% not carried forward; carried-over activity must be completed before any activity counts toward the next year. — [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01672](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01672/en-us)
- One ICB (BNSSG) reported clawback of about £15.5m (2022/23), £17.0m (2023/24) and £14.4m (2024/25), noting the figures include other financial adjustments. — [BNSSG ICB FOI 2526-428, Mar 2026](https://bnssghealthiertogether.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FOI.ICB-2526_428-NHS-Dental-Commissioning-Final-Response.pdf)
- NASDAL benchmarking (reported May 2026): NHS practice profits rose partly because clawback fell alongside higher UDA rates. — [Dentistry.co.uk, 1 May 2026](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/05/01/asdal-benchmarking-report-2025-dental-practice-profits/)

Step 6: private estimates, consent and medical history
- GDC Standards (effective 30 Sep 2013, PDF updated 28 Jun 2019): standard 1.6 "Whenever a patient is returning for treatment following an examination or assessment, give them a written treatment plan and cost estimate"; 1.7 requires fresh consent and an amended plan/estimate if the plan changes. Principle 3 requires discussing options, risks, benefits, prognosis, cost and what happens without treatment, and documenting that discussions took place and that the patient understood. (Wording taken from an NHS England reproduction and secondary sources; GDC page itself only offers the PDF.) — [GDC Standards for the dental team](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/standards-for-the-dental-team); [HEE Thames Valley reproduction of GDC Standards](https://thamesvalley.hee.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2025/04/GDC-Standards-for-the-Dental-Team.pdf)
- Consent forms: a signed written consent form is required for sedation or general anaesthetic; for ordinary conscious treatment no specific form is needed but verbal consent must be noted in the records; a signed form is advisable for long, complex or expensive treatment. — [MDDUS Soundbite](https://www.mddus.com/resources/publications/publications-library/soundbite/soundbite-issue-09/whats-new-with-the-gdc)
- Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board (UK Supreme Court, 11 Mar 2015) moved the disclosure test to a patient-centred one: material risks and reasonable alternatives; a signature on a plan "does not indicate valid consent". — [Dentists Closeby consumer guide](https://dentistscloseby.com/blog/consent-to-dental-treatment-your-rights); [Dentistry.co.uk, Feb 2021](https://dentistry.co.uk/2021/02/15/informed-patient-consent/)
- Medical history: GDC "You must make and keep complete and accurate patient records, including an up-to-date medical history, each time that you treat patients"; CQC requires "an assessment of the patient which must include a current or updated medical history"; FGDP guidance (cited by CQC) expects the history to be updated as needed and signed and dated by patient and clinician. — [Dental Update: Medical histories for the computerized practice](https://dental-update.co.uk/content/general-dental-practice/medical-histories-for-the-computerized-practice)

### Inferences
- Because UDAs are disallowed after two months and corrected claims must also land within that window, a rejected claim found on a monthly schedule late in the window can be unrecoverable; this makes early transmission and daily schedule checking the practical control.
- The 2026 cash-value items (urgent £70, complex-care fees) are converted to UDAs for reconciliation, so the UDA target, 96% clawback floor and two-month rule remain the binding constraints on the admin side.

### Gaps
- The 50% surcharge figure appears only in the gov.uk transparency record; NHSBSA's own page says "a surcharge may be added" without a percentage.
- FP17 clinician/provider guidance V9.0/V5.0 (Apr 2026) and completion guidance V14/V15.1 returned 404; band and declaration rules are quoted from the Oct 2022 V11.4 PDF, which predates the 2026 items (urgent £75 credit, fluoride varnish 0.5 UDA, Band 2a, complex-care pathways).
- FP17O case-start and completion UOA values by age band were not confirmed from a reachable primary page.
- No NHS England primary document for the April 2026 changes was reached (BDA blog 403); the final figures rest on an accountancy-firm summary and trade press.
- Software of Excellence banding page returned 403; Carestream R4 FP17 help page not located; Dentally's own FP17 rejection guide not found (only a community thread).
- Real Time Exemption Checking: no NHSBSA page describing which dental exemptions RTEC confirms at the chairside was reached; only the transparency record's mention that RTEC-confirmed patients are excluded from the sample check.
- No timing data (minutes per FP17, minutes per exemption check) was found for UK reception workflows; UK sources give rules and deadlines, not labour times.
- No NHSBSA statistic for the share of FP17s rejected, late, or carrying comment codes was found.

## Key question 3: Which products automate which steps, what they cost, and what users say

### Takeaway
Automation clusters into (a) clearinghouses bundled into the PMS (DentalXChange, Vyne, EDS; Change Healthcare largely abandoned after 2024), (b) AI/RPA verification add-ons (Zuub, Pearl Precheck, Curve Eligibility+, Verrific, Overjet) that scrape payer portals and compile breakdowns, and (c) outsourced human billing/verification services priced per check or per percentage of collections. ## Key question 4: What remains manual or legally non-delegable

### Takeaway
On both sides of the Atlantic the human-only steps are: interpreting and importing eligibility data (Open Dental says a person must interpret it), the clinician's completion of the clinical data set and the patient's signed declaration on the FP17, obtaining consent (GDC principle 3; Montgomery), medical-history confirmation at each treatment, and challenging denials or PCNs with evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Open Dental: a person "must still interpret the data before importing", choose patient vs insurer share, and select in/out-of-network. — [Open Dental manual](https://opendental.com/manual/ebenefits.html)
- FP17 patient declaration and "evidence seen" boxes must be completed; a PCN falls on the patient when NHSBSA cannot confirm the exemption. — [NHSBSA FP17 guidance V14](https://cms.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/Completion_of_form_guidance_FP17-England_20250801_%28V14%29.pdf); [gov.uk transparency record](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/nhsbsa-prescriptions-and-dental-exemption-check)
- GDC: written plan and estimate before treatment (1.6/1.7); consent discussion and documentation (principle 3); up-to-date medical history each time you treat. — [GDC Standards](https://www.gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/standards-for-the-dental-team); [Dental Update](https://dental-update.co.uk/content/general-dental-practice/medical-histories-for-the-computerized-practice)
- NHSBSA will not help with EDI transmission errors; the practice must work with its software vendor. — [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01797](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01797/en-us)
- FP17 signing rules: "Dentist's Declaration - The declaration must be completed by a qualified dentist on every form. This would normally be the performer responsible for the course of treatment"; the patient must sign a paper PR form or compliant electronic equivalent, retained two years; Compass online forms "MUST be authorised before they will be received and processed". — [NHSBSA FP17 completion guidance V11.4](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-11/Completion_of_form_guidance-FP17-England_202210_V11.4_Final.pdf); [NHSBSA FAQ KA-01916](https://faq.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/knowledgebase/article/KA-01916/en-us); [NHSBSA schedule programme 2026](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/Schedule-programme-April-2026-to-December-2026.pdf)
- Exemption liability sits with the patient, not the practice: NHSBSA's sample check, enquiry letter and PCN are addressed to the patient; the practice's duty is to tick "evidence seen" Yes/No truthfully. — [gov.uk transparency record](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/nhsbsa-prescriptions-and-dental-exemption-check); [NHSBSA: Understanding penalty charges](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/understanding-penalty-charges)
- US: eligibility responses are not a guarantee of payment, so the practice still decides what to tell the patient and what to collect at the visit. — [EDI study guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/cbcs/electronic-claims-edi-and-clearinghouses/edi-transaction-map-837-835-270-271-277ca-999)
- US clearinghouse outage fallback remains manual: print-and-mail claims, which "may not work if claims require electronic attachments", and portal-by-portal eligibility checks. — [CDA, Mar 2024](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/newsroom-archives/recommended-actions-for-practices-affected-by-change-healthcare-cyberattack/)

### Inferences
- Automation vendors can draft breakdowns and pre-fill claims but cannot sign the FP17 declaration, obtain Montgomery-standard consent, or decide the clinical band; those remain with the clinician and patient.
- Because NHSBSA accepts only electronic PR signatures captured by a compliant system and forbids scan-and-destroy, UK "paperless" exemption capture is gated by the PMS vendor's certification rather than by the practice.

### Gaps
- HIPAA minimum-necessary (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and business-associate text could not be quoted from a primary source: hhs.gov returned 403 and ecfr.gov redirected to a block page.
- The NHSBSA "Guidance for ProvClin & Practice staff" documents (which define provider vs performer vs practice-staff responsibilities on Compass) returned 404 at every indexed URL; only the PR-form retention sentence is quoted, via search snippet.
