# Dental marketing market and leakage: small independent practices and the agencies serving them (UK and US)

Status: research notes completed 16 Oct 2026 (with gaps listed per question). Method: ~45 web searches and ~40 direct fetches; the shared search budget ran out once mid-way and Reddit, Upwork job pages, Becker's, Dental Economics, DentistryIQ and some agency pricing pages blocked direct fetches (403/404), which is recorded in the Gaps. Vendor-published figures are marked "(vendor)". Search-engine snippets not verified against the page are marked "(snippet)". Scope: small independent practices and the agencies serving them, UK and US, 2024-2026 preferred. This is problem and market research only; no solution is recommended.

Crowded vs thin (cross-cutting inference from the evidence below): crowded = AI phone receptionists and call-intelligence (Peerlogic, Patient Prism, VoiceStack, Viva, Convirza, Weave all publishing near-identical "missed call" statistics; Google autocomplete already carries country and cost modifiers for "ai dental receptionist"), dental SEO and websites (dozens of agencies on DesignRush and UpCity; Fiverr gigs from $10), review-generation tools (My Social Practice claims 375,000 reviews; Upwork gigs for buying, recovering and removing reviews), and agency-written "front desk is the problem" content. Thin = independent (non-vendor) measurement of leakage in either country; any UK primary data newer than 2017-2019 on enquiry handling; any public, comparable data on agency contract terms, churn or client-reported ROI; treatment-level cost per seated patient (every dataset stops at cost per lead); and documented enforcement against small practices (the only US pixel case and the only UK ASA dental ruling found are against a DSO and a treatment facilitator respectively).

## Key question 1: Monthly marketing spend and cost per new patient, UK and US

### Takeaway
No independent, recent survey gives a clean "cost per new patient" for either country; the only survey-based spend data found are the ProSites "State of Dental Marketing" (US, 110 practices, Oct 2021) and vendor benchmark ranges. Agency and vendor ranges cluster at US $150-300 per general-dentistry patient, $400-800 for implant/cosmetic, retainers $1,000-5,000/month; UK vendor ranges put blended cost per patient around £30-300 and monthly budgets £500-15,000 by practice size.

### Cited Findings
- ProSites "2022 State of Dental Marketing" survey (110 US practices, surveyed Oct 2021, reported 21 Mar 2022): 61% spent $501+/month in 2021; 35% spent more than $1,000/month (51% of top performers); just under 4% spent more than $5,000/month; nearly half of $1M+ practices spent $1,000-5,000/month; ~75% planned to keep budget flat, 20% to spend more. Channels rated important: word of mouth 80%, online reviews 57%, website 46-51%, SEO 46%; least important: TV (69% said unimportant), radio 67%, mailers/postcards 45%. (vendor survey) — [DrBicuspid, 21 Mar 2022](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/patient-communication/marketing/article/15379233/marketing-spending-reaches-new-high-in-2022-survey)
- Dental Economics 2020 marketing survey (snippet; page returned HTTP 403 on fetch): in the prior year 33% of practices spent less than 1% of monthly revenue on marketing; share spending under 1% later fell to 11% — [Dental Economics](https://dentaleconomics.com/practice/marketing/article/14173977/results-of-the-2020-dental-marketing-survey)
- Recommended share of revenue (all vendor/consultant guidance, not surveys): HIP Agency 3-6% of gross revenue, 4-6% for "winning" practices — [HIP Agency](https://hip.agency/dental-marketing-budget-guide-investing-the-right-amount-in-growth/); Bulletproof Dental Practice 3-5% established, 5-8% growth mode, 8-12%+ startups — [Bulletproof Dental Practice](https://www.bulletproofdentalpractice.com/dental-marketing-budget-percentage/); Oral Health Group 7-10% for growth-focused — [Oral Health Group](https://www.oralhealthgroup.com/features/how-much-should-dental-practices-spend-on-marketing/) (snippets)
- US cost per new patient (vendor ranges, snippets): general dentistry $150-300; implant/cosmetic $400-800; SEO-sourced $50-150 vs Google Ads $150-350 — [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-patient-acquisition-cost-statistics), [Redefine Web](https://redefineweb.com/blog/dental-marketing-cost/), [Best Results Dental Marketing](https://bestresultsdentalmarketing.com/2026/13/how-much-should-dentists-spend-on-marketing-2026-guide/); Colorado agency says $200-400 per new patient is supportable — [Colorado Web Impressions](https://coloradowebimpressions.com/learn/dental-marketing-cost-colorado); one vendor reports groups with a $14,800 monthly median spend get 52 new patients per location per month at $74 cost per new patient (vendor, group-level, snippet) — [Emulent](https://emulent.com/resources/trends/dental-practice-marketing-trends-and-projections/)
- Lifetime value (vendor, snippets): $7,000-10,000 per patient in one source; $700-1,250 first-year spend in another; rule of thumb that CPNP/LTV should be under 4% — [Emulent](https://emulent.com/resources/trends/dental-practice-marketing-trends-and-projections/), [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-patient-acquisition-cost-statistics)
- Viva (AI lead-response vendor, 2026, unsourced vendor claims): Meta cost per lead $8-15 general dental, $25-60 implants; Google high-intent dental CPC $80-150; implant case value $4,000-30,000 — [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/lead-response-time-statistics-dental-2026/)
- US agency retainers (vendor pricing guides, snippets): SEO $1,000-2,500/month typical, $750-5,000 range; solo practice $1,500-3,000, growing single location $2,500-5,000, small DSO $5,000-15,000; ad spend billed separately; quotes under $750-800/month flagged as a sign of offshore subcontracting or no reporting; directory listings show $500-1,500+ minimum retainers — [Digitalis Medical](https://www.digitalismedical.com/blog/dental-seo-pricing), [Redefine Web](https://redefineweb.com/blog/dental-marketing-agency-pricing-costly-red-flags/), [Gartner Digital Markets agency directory](https://agencyapp.dmbx.aws.gartner.com/digital-marketing/dental); UK SEO retainer £995-5,000+ — [Dominate Dental](https://cms.dominatedental.com/?p=1834)
- UK (Whito "UK Dentist Marketing Data", May 2026 report, updated 2 Jul / 12 Oct 2026; self-described independent research publisher that sells research; "all figures are estimates ... directional benchmarks"; figures not attributed to named sources): marketing spend 5-10% of revenue private-focused, 3-5% mixed, under 2% NHS-heavy; monthly budgets: sole dentist £500-1,500, 2-3 dentists £1,500-4,000, 4-6 dentists £3,000-8,000, multi-site £4,000-15,000; cost to acquire: Google search £30-150, referrals £0-50, social £20-80; new-patient source mix Google 35%, referrals 30%, walk-ins 15%, social 10%; annual new patient value NHS-heavy £300-500, mixed £600-1,200, private £1,000-2,500, cosmetic £2,000-5,000+; retention 70-80% "good", under 50% "poor"; UK search volumes Q1 2026: "dentist near me" 165,000+/month, "emergency dentist" 90,000+, "NHS dentist accepting patients" 74,000+, "teeth whitening [city]" 40,000+, "dental implants [city]" 33,000+, "Invisalign [city]" 27,000+ — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-dentist-marketing-data/)
- UK paid-search costs (all agency blogs, 2025-2026, figures do not reconcile): general dental CPC £1.50-4.50 with 5-12% landing-page conversion; competitive metro terms £2.50-8.00 ("dental implants London" ~£6-8, "Invisalign near me" ~£4-6); other agencies cite £11-18 for "Invisalign London" and £8-15 for implant/Invisalign search ads; cost per lead: emergency £25-40, cosmetic consult £60-120, implants £100-180 / £120-250 / £180-350 (three different agencies), Invisalign £80-150 / £120-200; one agency's 2025 campaign average £62.01 per lead at 9.08% conversion; Central London 40-60% dearer than Birmingham or Manchester for identical keywords — [Dominate Dental advertising cost guide](https://www.dominatedental.com/dental-advertising-cost-uk-guide/), [Dominate Dental Google Ads 2026](https://cms.dominatedental.com/?p=625), [Whitehat SEO](https://whitehat-seo.co.uk/blog/dental-google-ads-ppc) (vendor, snippets)
- UK NHS-overflow context: BDA analysis put unmet dental need in England at over 11 million adults (2022); of people without an NHS dentist who failed to get care, 11% went private and 78.5% did nothing (BDA, Oct 2024); BDA 2022 poll of 2,200 dentists: 45% had reduced NHS commitment and 45% likely to go fully private; Denplan Oral Health Survey 2024: 20% of Britons now use private dental care and 29% of non-regular attenders cite inability to find an NHS dentist; Healthwatch poll of 2,026 adults: 17% felt pressured to pay privately when booking, 24% had to pay privately for all required treatment, only 7% in northern England could afford private care vs 20% in the south — [The Dentist](https://the-dentist.co.uk/content/news/survey-indicates-half-of-dentists-have-cut-back-on-nhs-work), [The Dentist (BDA)](https://the-dentist.co.uk/content/news/dental-association-urges-the-government-to-support-nhs-dental-services), [Healthcare & Protection](https://healthcareandprotection.com/patients-pressured-to-go-private-as-dentists-ditch-nhs-work/), [GDPUK](https://www.gdpuk.com/news/latest-news/4858-61-of-nhs-patients-would-consider-private-alternatives) (snippets)
- UK agency claims (vendor, snippets): overall average £295 per new patient, SEO ~£170, Google Ads ~£270 — [Dominate Dental](https://www.dominatedental.com/dental-marketing-case-study-examples/); implant leads £180-300, Invisalign leads £80-200; £150-400 per qualified lead for £3,000-8,000 treatments; private practice with £5,000+ LTV can afford £100-200 per new patient — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/?p=13840), [Whitehat SEO](https://whitehat-seo.co.uk/blog/dental-marketing)
- UK anecdote: a principal with 2,000+ active adult patients spending £500/month on SEO plus £1,000/month on digital ads (£18,000/year) with "no idea" of ROI and unable to get clear information from the agency — Chris Barrow, [Dentistry.co.uk, 29 Dec 2021](https://dentistry.co.uk/2021/12/29/marketing-money-down-the-drain/)

### Inferences
- The figures mix cost per lead, cost per enquiry and cost per seated patient without consistent definitions; every quantitative source found is a vendor or agency, so the spread ($50-800; £30-400) reflects definitional and channel differences more than real benchmarks.
- The only survey data (ProSites 2022) implies the median small US practice spent well under $1,000/month in 2021, far below the $1,500-5,000 retainers agencies quote, which suggests most agencies' addressable market is the top third of practices by spend.

### Gaps
- No ADA Health Policy Institute figure on marketing spend found (search budget exhausted before this could be checked).
- Dental Economics 2020 survey page returned 403; its numbers are from snippets only. No newer (2023-2026) Dental Economics marketing survey was located.
- No independent UK survey of marketing spend or cost per patient; Denplan/BDA reports not reached.

## Key question 2: Documented leakage rates (missed calls, lead response, no-shows, recall, case acceptance) and who measured them

### Takeaway
All leakage numbers come from vendors (call-tracking, AI-phone, practice-management software). The largest datasets are Patient Prism (11.55M calls, 8,280 US locations, 2025 data: 31 of every 100 callers hang up before reaching anyone; 21 book) and Henry Schein One (thousands of practices: average case acceptance 42-46%, no-show 4%, retention 70%). UK primary data are old (DenGro 2017 survey, Converso 2011 call test).

### Cited Findings
- Patient Prism "Dental Patient Access Report" (published 2 Jul 2026, 2025 data, vendor platform data): 11,552,668 calls across 8,280 dental practice and DSO locations; 1,445,814 patients walked away without an appointment; 890,621 never got a callback. Per 100 calls: 69 connected with front desk, 31 hung up before reaching an agent; 34 real booking opportunities, 35 not bookable; 21 booked; 13 walked away without booking; 4.8 got a follow-up call; 0.7 came back and booked. Reasons for not booking (1,163,398 categorised opportunities): consideration/timing 45.2%, financial barrier (insurance, price) 34.4%, service fit 10.3%, connection failed (voicemail, disconnect, hang-up on hold) 9.3%; insurance outweighs price "by more than 11 to 1"; no after-hours split and no new vs existing split given — [Patient Prism](https://www.patientprism.com/report/dental-patient-access-report/)
- Peerlogic (AI call follow-up vendor) case study, 26 practices in one dental group, Feb 2026: 4,280 calls, 38% unanswered; calls-to-booking 40% average; new-patient conversion 25.24% vs existing 55.77%; top reason for non-booking was calls disconnecting before a real conversation; revenue framing inconsistent across the vendor's pages ($47,088 one-month total vs "per location" elsewhere) — [Peerlogic](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study)
- Pain-Free Dental Marketing (agency) via Dentaltown, 5 Aug 2026: across 55,000+ tracked calls for its clients, 37% went unanswered; no conversion figures given — [Dentaltown](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/26399/your-marketing-isnt-broken-your-front-desk-is)
- Convirza (call tracking vendor) claims about three-quarters of missed dental calls happen after hours (snippet) — [Convirza](https://www.convirza.com/content-hub/missed-dental-calls-after-hours/); Peerlogic elsewhere cites ~30% of calls arriving after hours with no methodology — [Peerlogic](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study)
- Secondary blog estimates of 20-35% of business-hours calls missed, one attributed to Dental Economics without a link (snippets) — [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-practice-phone-call-statistics-2026), [Dentaltown/Dental Marketing Guy](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/26052/why-your-front-desk-is-losing-new-patients-before-they-ever-book-and-the-best-way-to-fix-it)
- Henry Schein One "2026 Catalyst Index" (22 Apr 2026, 1-7 location practices, "thousands of offices", sample size and definitions not given): average vs top 10%: new-patient appointment lead time 25 days vs 4.5 days; case acceptance 42% vs 75%; patient retention 70% vs 94%; no-show 4% vs under 1%; new patients per month 30 vs 81; collection rate 80% vs 97% — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index)
- Henry Schein One 2024 Benchmark Study (20 Aug 2024): case acceptance 46% average, 83% top 10%; unscheduled treatment worth about $1-1.5M a year for the average practice; no-show fell from 7% (2022) to 4% (2023), 1% for top 10% (snippet for the no-show trend) — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/blogs/average-providers-are-losing-hundreds-of-thousands-in-unscheduled-treatment), [Becker's](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/how-dental-practices-fare-in-case-acceptance-collection-rates-and-more/)
- 2025 Dental Industry Outlook (snippet): 49% of practices had case acceptance between 40% and 70% — [BusinessWire, 13 Mar 2025](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20250313922618/en/2025-Dental-Industry-Outlook-60-of-Practices-Report-Same-Store-Growth-Defying-Economic-Uncertainty)
- Clerri (membership-plan vendor) claims only 34% of diagnosed treatment is accepted (snippet, unverified) — [Clerri](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-case-acceptance-statistics/)
- Hygiene re-appointment targets: ACT Dental recommends at least 85% rebooked before leaving — [ACT Dental](https://www.actdental.com/blog/940-miranda-beeson); Dental Intelligence sets 90%+ — [Dental Intelligence](https://educate.dentalintel.com/en/articles/6070096-hygiene-re-appointment-what-is-this). Recall-rate ranges of 60-70% average, 85-95% well-managed, attributed by a blog to Dental Intel/ADA but unverified (snippet) — [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-recall-reactivation-statistics-benchmarks)

- UK, VoiceStack (AI phone vendor, 2025) claims one-third of UK dental appointments are missed due to communication gaps, £100,000-1.5M a year cost; no primary source (snippet) — [VoiceStack](https://voicestack.com/resources/en-GB/article/the-hidden-cost-of-missed-calls-in-uk-dental-practices-what-you-re-losing-and-how-to-reclaim-it)
- Front-desk anecdote (DentistryIQ, Sept 2018, author sells call tracking/training; page 403 on fetch): a practice's new-patient count fell from 53 to 4 after only the front-desk staff changed (snippet) — [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/practice-management/marketing/article/16367594/all-dental-marketing-goes-through-the-front-desk)
- Becker's/Henry Schein One survey "no-shows and cancellations still biggest hurdle" (page 403; snippet only) — [Becker's](https://beckersdental.com/dentists/40950-patient-no-shows-cancellations-still-biggest-hurdle-for-dental-practice-schedules.html)

### Inferences
- Using Patient Prism's funnel, roughly a fifth of inbound calls become bookings and under 1% of lost opportunities are recovered by callbacks, which is the quantitative core of the "leads delivered vs patients seated" gap.
- Case acceptance in the mid-40s means more than half of diagnosed treatment value (the thing marketing was meant to feed) is lost after the patient is already in the chair.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or regulator-measured missed-call, speed-to-lead or recall rate for dental practices in either country.
- Weave's 2024 benchmark report was not reached; the 26-hour figure is second-hand.
- No UK no-show (DNA) rate for dental practices found; NHS dental statistics do not publish it in the sources reached.
- CallRail dental study not reached.

## Key question 3: What practice owners say about agencies, and what agencies say about practices

### Takeaway
Owners' complaints centre on unreadable or unobtainable ROI, "give it more time", lead volume without bookings, and wrong-fit patients; agencies' counter-narrative is that the front desk loses the leads (37-38% of calls unanswered in their own call-tracking data) and that owners fire agencies for a staffing problem.

### Cited Findings
- Chris Barrow (UK coach), Dentistry.co.uk, 29 Dec 2021: principal spending £18,000/year cannot get ROI data; spending is justified by "incomprehensible excuses and explanations"; the predicted agency line is "You need to give it more time" — [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2021/12/29/marketing-money-down-the-drain/)
- Trustpilot review of UK lead-gen agency Dentology (2 stars): leads were poor, prospects "turned up not even knowing the cost or what they wanted"; agency replied that ROI fell below the median across its client base and that 103 of 108 bookings were screened successfully (snippet) — [Trustpilot](https://se.trustpilot.com/review/dentology.ai)
- Agency blog claim that 68% of UK practices are dissatisfied with agency leads: no primary source found (snippet) — [Dominate Dental](https://cms.dominatedental.com/category/blog/page/26/)
- Practice-owner anecdote: after firing an agency charging $4,200/month, "the week after, our lead flow went up" (single unverified story) — [socialperks](https://socialperks.onrender.com/stories/why-i-fired-my-marketing-agency-and-results-improved)
- Agency side: "owners decide the ads aren't working and start shopping for a new agency" when the real issue is a staffing or process gap — [Dental Marketing newsletter](https://dental-marketing.beehiiv.com/p/i-ll-admit-the-unsexy-truth-about-dental-marketing); Pain-Free Dental Marketing: 37% of 55,000+ client calls unanswered; "your marketing isn't broken, your front desk is" — [Dentaltown, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/26399/your-marketing-isnt-broken-your-front-desk-is); RevUp Dental: answering phones "requires a special set of skills that has to be taught", firing the receptionist alone does not fix it — [Dentaltown](https://www.dentaltown.com/channel/post/26075/what-it-takes-for-dental-staff-training-to-actually-work)

- LinkedIn commentary: cost per lead is the wrong metric; lead forms produce many submissions but most never register; "cost per registered patient" is what matters — [LinkedIn](https://lt.linkedin.com/in/laurynas-mickus)
