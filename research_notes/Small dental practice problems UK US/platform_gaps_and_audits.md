## A. US practice management systems: who buys, cost, what is solved, what is not

### Takeaway
Every US PMS in scope has the same four recurring complaint clusters in 2024-2026 reviews: (1) support hold times and inconsistent answers, (2) rigid reporting that will not export or that disagrees with the analytics add-on, (3) opaque, rising pricing with add-ons for features buyers assumed were included, and (4) painful migration (images in proprietary formats, in-flight claims, ledger history). Open Dental is the only vendor with fully published pricing; every other vendor prices behind a demo.

### Inferences
- A 2-dentist practice's real monthly software spend is driven more by bolt-ons than by the PMS licence: Open Dental's own price list shows a $199 base becoming roughly $400-$500 with web scheduling ($150), eConfirmations ($40 + per text), eClipboard ($45) and hosting, before any comms layer.
- "Reporting" complaints across Dentrix, Ascend, Fuse, Sensei and Denticon have the same shape: the data exists but cannot be exported, filtered or trusted without a paid analytics layer (Jarvis, DI, PbN) or a custom query ($110/hour at Open Dental).
- Lock-in is structural rather than contractual for Dentrix and Eaglesoft: proprietary imaging formats, paid API access, payment processing tied to the vendor, and server hardware already sunk.

## B. Patient-communication, marketing, analytics layers and AI receptionists

### Takeaway
The comms layer is where contract and billing complaints concentrate: auto-renewal, 12-month lock-ins, refused cancellations and feature unbundling appear for Weave, Solutionreach, RevenueWell, NexHealth and Dental Intelligence. Sync with the PMS is the second theme (Curve + NexHealth; DI "numbers always inaccurate"; DI's own help centre says its claims-aging will not match Dentrix). AI receptionists have no public pricing and no independent reviews yet.

### Inferences
- The audit market is unpriced because it is unbundled: vendors give the checklist away, analytics vendors sell the dashboard, offshore labour sells the keystrokes, and nobody sells the validation step (do the recall types, fee schedules, insurance plans and claim statuses in this database actually reconcile with the analytics layer and, in the UK, with Compass).
- Price anchors that exist ($110/hour Open Dental queries, $500-$700 small-practice analysis, £600-£1,500 UK training day, $4-$7/hour offshore posting) bracket a one-off practice-level audit at low hundreds to low thousands, well below the $1-1.5m unscheduled-treatment figure vendors use to sell follow-up tools.

### Where automation and reporting stop, and what needs an add-on, API work or a consultant

#### Takeaway
Automation stops at the edge of each product's own tables: the PMS reminds and confirms, but unscheduled-treatment follow-up, lead nurture, insurance eligibility, review collection and multi-product reporting are all add-ons; export, custom queries and cross-system reconciliation need a consultant or paid API access.

#### Cited Findings
- Reporting: Dentrix "most don't allow importing them to Excel"; Ascend Power Reporting "clumsy", Jarvis "comes with an additional cost"; Fuse reporting misses adjustments/refunds; Sensei "reporting not comprehensive yet"; Open Dental custom queries $110/hour [review/vendor] — sections A
- Recall/online booking: Open Dental Web Sched Recall and New are $75/month each add-ons; Sensei Cloud lacks online booking; Dentally Starter has no Portal; Ascend has no online intake form [vendor/review] — sections A, C
- Insurance: Ascend preauth to primary only; Curve Eligibility Plus and Weave verification are add-ons; verification otherwise manual at ~2 hours/day [review/vendor/consultant] — sections A, B, D
- Follow-up: DI, Adit, Planet DDS analytics expose unscheduled lists; execution remains a human task; "AI Recare" is "coming soon" at Adit [vendor] — section B
- Integration: Dentrix API $1,750-$5,000 + per-site fees (user-reported); Sensei API requires Carestream approval; Zapier apps absent or unreachable; Dentally is the only in-scope PMS with its own Zapier page [estimate/vendor] — sections A, B, C
- UK: FP17 rejections must be filtered and fixed manually in Dentally; Compass cannot show the unscheduled split; GP17 DMF hand-counted [vendor/press/forum] — section C

#### Inferences
- The consultant-shaped work is reconciliation, not configuration: aging report vs analytics, Compass vs PMS, lead CRM vs PMS patient list, recall types vs continuing-care lists.

#### Gaps
- No Zapier trigger/action counts; no documented Eaglesoft or Fuse API terms.

### Which gaps are common across products

#### Takeaway
Seven gaps recur regardless of vendor or country.

#### Cited Findings (cross-references)
1. Reporting that cannot be exported or trusted without a paid analytics layer: Dentrix, Ascend, Fuse, Sensei, Denticon, DI-vs-Dentrix aging — section A, B
2. Unscheduled treatment follow-up left to a human list: HSO 46% acceptance, DI/Adit/Planet DDS boards, "60-70% no systematic follow-up" — section D
3. Recall lists unworked and corrupted by duplicate continuing-care types: Dentrix Enterprise checklist, Hello Patient, reactivation 10-25% — sections A, D
4. Insurance eligibility and claims aging handled outside the PMS at hours per day: Dentaltown 2017, Overjet, Dental Claim Support, Weave/NexHealth/Curve add-ons — sections B, D
5. Contract lock-in and opaque pricing with add-on creep: Weave, Solutionreach, RevenueWell, NexHealth, DI, Sensei, Eaglesoft subscription, Dentrix API fees — sections A, B
6. Migration loss of images and in-flight claims, with conversion fees non-refundable: Curve, RevUp, Open Dental, Sensei CSV export — section F
7. UK-specific: NHS claim validation and UDA/Compass reconciliation left to the practice; lead handling on paper: Dentally community, GDPUK, DenGro survey — section C

#### Gaps
- Multi-site reporting, stock control and lab tracking gaps were only lightly evidenced (CareStack lab module note only); no practitioner complaints found on these.
