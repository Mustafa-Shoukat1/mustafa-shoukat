# Quantified operational and financial metrics: small dental practices, UK and US

Researcher notes, 16 Oct 2026. Problem research only: numbers, sources, dates, short context. No solutions.
Conventions: "VENDOR" = figure published by a software, billing, marketing or phone vendor or agency with a commercial interest; "secondary" = trade press or aggregator reporting a primary source. Where a figure covers all practices (including DSOs), that is stated. Years are the data year where the source gives one, otherwise the publication date.
Research conditions: the shared web-search budget ran out part-way through; the work continued with direct fetches. Several primary pages were rate-limited or returned 403/404 (listed under Gaps); where that happened the figure is taken from a search summary or secondary coverage and the primary URL is still given.

## US: practice counts, ownership, DSO share and dentist income

### Takeaway
US dentistry is still mostly owner-run (72.5% of dentists owned a practice in 2023, down from 84.7% in 2005) but DSO affiliation has doubled in a decade (7.2% in 2015 to 16.1% in 2024) and solo practice has slipped to about a third of dentists; average general-dentist net income was $215,320 in 2025, about $60,000 lower than 2010 in real terms.

### Cited Findings
- Average net income of private-practice general dentists: $207,980 (2024) and $215,320 (2025); specialists $338,900 (2024) and $346,520 (2025). ADA HPI Survey of Dental Practice (all private practices, incl. DSO-affiliated). — [ADA HPI dental practice trends (page returned 404 on direct fetch; figures from search summary)](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dental-practice-trends)
- 2024 net income splits: male $222,158 vs female $181,194; owners $217,781 vs non-owners $160,891; inflation-adjusted average net income down nearly $60,000 since 2010 and median down more than $64,000 over 14 years. — [Becker's Dental (secondary, citing ADA HPI)](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/good-news-bad-news-for-dentists/)
- BLS puts general dentists' average annual income at $196,100 (different methodology; ADA says its figure includes more compensation types). — [Dental Economics (secondary; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/macro-op-ed/industry-news/news/55332389/ada-study-reveals-dentists-revenue-net-income-and-hours-worked-in-2025is-the-profession-struggling)
- DSO-affiliated dentists: ~7% (2015), nearly 13% (2022), nearly 14% (2023); by career stage in 2023: 0-5 years ~29%, 6-10 years ~20%, 11-25 years ~12%, 25+ years ~8%; Colorado, Nevada, Georgia ~25%. Solo practice: 36% (2022) to 35% (2023). Practices with 100+ locations: 9% (2022) to 10% (2023). — [DrBicuspid summarising ADA HPI](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/office-management/practice-trends/article/15683050/how-dentistrys-practice-models-continue-to-evolve)
- DSO affiliation 16.1% in 2024 (7.2% in 2015); 26.5% of dentists up to 10 years out of school affiliated (2024); ~34% of dentists practising in a single location with no other dentist (2024); 84.7% of dentists owned a practice in 2005 vs 72.5% in 2023. — [Becker's Dental summarising ADA HPI 2024 workforce report (search summary; fetch rate-limited)](https://www.beckersdental.com/?p=21392)
- Professionally active dentists: 202,485 (KFF, 2024 data); 159,562 licensed general practitioners and 10,830 orthodontists (ADA via Statista, 2024). — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/total-dentists), [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1114666/number-of-active-dentists-by-practice-area-us)
- Number of dental businesses: 175,947 (2024, +1.1% on 2023) per one IBISWorld page; 183,403 enterprises (+1.07%) per another IBISWorld-sourced page (definitions differ). — [IBISWorld](https://img3.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/dentists/1557)
- Top three DSOs supported more than 3,800 practices and 7,800 dentists combined heading into 2024. — [Becker's Dental](https://beckersdental.com/dso-dpms/42468-where-the-top-3-largest-dsos-stand-heading-into-2024.html)

### Inferences
- Roughly 85% of US dentists remained outside DSOs in 2024, so the small independent practice is still the dominant unit; the DSO share is concentrated in early-career dentists (one in four to one in three).
- Real net income for general dentists has fallen about 20% since 2010 while overhead has risen ~5% a year (see next section), which is the financial squeeze behind most of the operational problems below.

### Gaps
- Exact count of dental offices (NAICS 621210 establishments) not found; IBISWorld figures conflict and no Census County Business Patterns figure was retrieved.
- ADA HPI pages for 2025 practice-model data (solo share, ownership) not retrieved; latest ownership figure is 2023 and latest solo/DSO figure is 2024.
- Share of practices (as opposed to dentists) that are solo vs small group not found.

## US: overhead, production, collections and new patients

### Takeaway
Overhead is rising about 5% a year (65% of practices reported higher overhead in the latest Dental Economics/Levin survey), vendor benchmarks put the average practice at 30 new patients a month with an 80% collection rate, and third-party estimates put overhead at roughly 60-65% of collections.

### Cited Findings
- Dental Economics/Levin Group Annual Practice Survey: 65% of practices reported higher overhead, 14% lower, 21% unchanged; overhead rose about 5.1%; ~5% a year described as the new normal. (Survey year not stated in the summary; the May 2024 survey covered full-year 2023.) — [Dental Economics (page 403 on fetch; from search summary)](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/practice/overhead-and-profitability/article/55280684/higher-production-helps-offset-chronic-overhead-increases), [2024 DE/Levin survey](https://dentaleconomics.com/practice/article/55016675/higher-production-and-much-needed-stability-the-2024-de-levin-group-annual-practice-survey)
- Overhead 60-65% of collections at the average practice; general practices operated at 61.8% overhead in 2024 based on an analysis of 100+ practices (VENDOR blog; methodology not verifiable). — [Clerri (VENDOR; fetch rate-limited)](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-practice-overhead-statistics/)
- Henry Schein One 2026 Catalyst Index (VENDOR; 1-7 location practices; "thousands of offices"; published 22 Apr 2026; data period not stated): new patients per month average 30 / top 10% 81; collection rate average 80% / top 10% 97% (target 90%+); patient retention 70% / 94%; new-patient appointment lead time 25 days / 4.5 days. — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index)
- Dentists expecting staffing shortages to be their top challenge in 2025: ~62% (ADA HPI late-2024 survey). — [ADA News Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/five-years-later-staffing-shortages-infection-control-since-the-covid-19-pandemic/)

### Inferences
- With overhead rising ~5% a year and net income flat in real terms, every missed call, no-show or denied claim lands on a thinner margin than a decade ago.

### Gaps
- Overhead component breakdown (staff, lab, supplies, rent, marketing, software) from a primary survey not retrieved; Dental Economics pages returned 403 and the Dentimax/Clerri aggregator pages were rate-limited.
- Dental Economics survey new-patient and collections figures for 2024/2025 not retrieved.

## US: staffing vacancies, turnover and pay

### Takeaway
Hygienist recruitment is the most persistent staffing problem: only 60% of dentists have enough hygienists (HPI, April 2026), ~90% of those recruiting call it very or extremely challenging, and dental-office staff employment has been flat for 18 months despite record hygiene graduations.

### Cited Findings
- Only 60% of dentists have an adequate number of hygienists on staff; 91% of dentists recruiting hygienists said it was very or extremely challenging; hygiene first-year enrollment +16% 2020-2025 and 2025 had the most hygiene graduates ever; dental-office staff employment flat for at least 18 months; inflation-adjusted average wages for dental office staff lower than a few years ago. — [ADA HPI, April 2026](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dentist-workforce/dental-hygienist-shortage)
- Recruiting difficulty (very/extremely challenging): hygienists ~95% (end 2022) to ~90% (late 2024); assistants ~87% (late 2022) to just above 70% (end 2024); 30-40% of dentists recruited hygienists or assistants in the prior three months (little change over several years); hygiene first-year enrollment up nearly 20% from 2020-21 to 2024-25. — [ADA News, Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/five-years-later-staffing-shortages-infection-control-since-the-covid-19-pandemic/)
- Q1 2025: 87.9% of dentists recruiting hygienists found it extremely/very challenging (96% in Q1 2023); 72% for assistants. Q3 2025 Economic Outlook (n=816): 32.5% of recruiting dentists were recruiting hygienists. — [Becker's Dental (search summary; fetch rate-limited)](https://www.beckersdental.com/?p=19716), [Becker's 37% recruiting hygienists (earlier)](https://www.beckersdental.com/?p=9127)
- BLS May 2024 (via secondary sites citing BLS OEWS): dental assistants median $47,300 a year ($22.74/hr), 371,600 jobs in 2024, +6% projected 2024-34; receptionists and information clerks (all industries) median $38,210. — [Stealth Agents (secondary; cites BLS)](https://stealthagents.com/research/dental-front-desk-staffing-statistics-2026). Primary BLS pages: [Dental assistants OES](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes319091.htm), [Dental hygienists OES](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes292021.htm), [Receptionists OES](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes434171.htm) (all rate-limited on fetch).

### Inferences
- Because staff employment is flat while graduations rise, the shortage is now about retention and exits rather than supply of new graduates; HPI itself says exit data are lacking.

### Gaps
- Turnover rates for dental assistants, hygienists and front-desk staff not found from a primary source.
- Dental hygienist May 2024 BLS median not confirmed (secondary claim ~$87,000); dental-specific receptionist pay not found from BLS.
- DentalPost/ADA salary survey figures not retrieved.

## US: phones, missed calls, lead response, no-shows, recall and case acceptance

### Takeaway
No public or ADA dataset measures call handling; vendor data say roughly 35-38% of calls to dental practices go unanswered and only 38% of non-booking callers get a follow-up. No-show rates range from 4% (vendor benchmark) to 9-14% (peer-reviewed), case acceptance averages ~42%, and hygiene reappointment can be as low as 42%.

### Cited Findings
- Average dental practice misses 35% of incoming calls; 65% of missed calls come from potential new patients (VENDOR, 2025). — [DentaMax blog](https://www.dentemax.com/dentists/blog-articles/2025/Why_missed_phone_calls_are_dental_offices_largest_revenue_loss)
- Peerlogic analysis of 26 practices (Feb 2026, VENDOR): 38% of callers get no response. — [Peerlogic](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study)
- Patient Prism 2026 report across 8,280 dental locations (VENDOR): 38% average follow-up rate for callers who did not book; top reason for not booking was the call disconnecting prematurely. — [cited via Viva (VENDOR)](https://www.getviva.ai/lead-response-time-statistics-dental-2026/)
- ~75% of missed dental calls happen after hours; only ~14% of new patients leave a voicemail if unanswered (VENDOR). — [Convirza](https://www.convirza.com/content-hub/missed-dental-calls-after-hours/)
- Average practice converts ~23% of new-patient calls into appointments (VENDOR, unsourced); no federal or ADA dataset publishes a national dental call-abandonment rate. — [VoiceStack (VENDOR; fetch rate-limited)](https://resources.voicestack.com/article/dental-phone-analytics-what-call-data-reveals)
- Lead response (cross-industry, not dental): HBR audit of 2,241 US companies: average first response 42 hours, 37% responded within an hour; MIT/InsideSales: contact within 5 minutes is 9x more likely to convert than within 30 minutes. — [cited via Viva (VENDOR)](https://www.getviva.ai/speed-to-lead-dental-practices/)
- No-show rate: average 4%, top 10% under 1% (Henry Schein One 2026 Catalyst Index, VENDOR). — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index)
- No-show rate: peer-reviewed 2025 University at Buffalo pediatric study 14.3%, with published dental estimates ranging 5-38%; 2023 Saudi dental college study 9.14% (1,389 of 15,193 appointments). Vendor blogs cite ~15% average, hygiene 18-25%, new patients 20-30% (unverified). — [Ainora (VENDOR summary)](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-no-show-statistics-ai-reduction), [Clerri (VENDOR; rate-limited)](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-patient-no-show-statistics/)
- Case acceptance: average 42%, top 10% 75% (Catalyst Index; its FAQ says "mid-40% range"). — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index)
- Case acceptance (Dental Intelligence, VENDOR, via AACD): top 10% of practices get 90% of patients to accept some part of a plan; 85-90% target. — [AACD](https://aacd.com/top-key-performance-indicators-for-dentists)
- Hygiene reappointment: Dental Intelligence CEO says practices often find it far lower than expected, some as low as 42%. — [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/practice-management/financial/article/16366041/illusory-superiority-why-its-important-to-measure-your-dental-practice-performance); an agency page cites "63% average hygiene reappointment across platform data" (platform unnamed). — [Webtonic (agency)](https://www.webtonic.io/sub-services/growth-advisory-dental)
- Patient retention: average 70%, top 10% 94% (Catalyst Index, VENDOR). — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index)

### Inferences
- The 35-38% missed-call figures come from three different vendors with different denominators and should be quoted as a vendor range, not an industry statistic.
- The spread in no-show rates (4% vendor vs 9-14% academic) likely reflects definitions (cancellation within 24h counted or not) and payer mix.

### Gaps
- No ADA or academic benchmark for missed calls, lead response time or hygiene recall rate exists; Dental Intelligence's own benchmark article returned 404.
- Reactivation rates (dormant patients recovered) not found.
- Treatment plan values (average unscheduled treatment per patient) not found.

## US: insurance participation, PPO write-offs, claims, denials, AR and collections

### Takeaway
Roughly two-thirds of US dentists participate in PPO networks, write-offs of 21-30% of fees are common, first-submission denial rates are quoted at 8-20% (vendor), days in AR typically 32-45, and the February 2024 Change Healthcare attack hit 190 million people and stopped claim submission for a third of small medical practices.

### Cited Findings
- ADA counts cited by a PPO vendor: 181,709 professionally active dentists, 116,978 participating in dental PPOs (~64%); date not given. — [DenteMax FAQ (VENDOR, secondhand ADA figure)](https://www.dentemax.com/clients/resources/faq)
- Among PPO participants, each dentist participated with ~14 carriers on average (NADP, 2017). — [Indianapolis IU archive citing NADP](https://archives.indianapolis.iu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/98d6ef16-6d58-435c-b1e1-da1ded8618b6/content)
- Fee-for-service practices ~16% in 2024, up from ~6% attributed to the ADA three years earlier (podcast guest; unverified). — [ACT Dental podcast](https://www.actdental.com/blog/698-shelley)
- PPO write-offs: 21-29% of fees for heavy-PPO practices (consultant); ~30% common (Dental Economics columnist); 20% threshold advised. — [DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/office-management/insurance/article/15379863/can-you-negotiate-a-better-ppo-dental-fee-schedule), [Dental Economics](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/practice/patient-communication-and-patient-financing/article/14039815/dropping-dental-insurance-plans-could-your-practice-survive)
- Denial rates (VENDOR/billing firms): industry benchmark 4% or below, most practices 8-15% (DentistryIQ, page 403 on fetch); first-submission denials quoted at 10%, 15%, 19.3% and "nearly 1 in 5" by different vendors with no primary source; well-managed practices 3-5%. — [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/revenue-cycle-management/article/55409403/the-7-revenue-cycle-gaps-most-dental-practices-dont-know-they-have), [Clerri (VENDOR)](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-insurance-claim-denial-statistics), [DentalAIAssist (VENDOR)](https://dentalaiassist.com/blog/why-dental-claims-denied-prevention/), [Dental Billing Assist (VENDOR)](https://dentalbillingassist.com/blog/posts/dental-billing-kpis-benchmarks)
- Days in AR: industry average 32-45 days, best practice under 30 (billing firm); solo practices often 40-60 days (2026 vendor piece); AR over 90 days: 18% of practices' AR on average (vendor), 10% ceiling / <3% top performers. — [Dental Billing Assist (VENDOR)](https://dentalbillingassist.com/blog/posts/dental-billing-kpis-benchmarks), [Arini (VENDOR)](https://www.arini.ai/blog/days-sales-outstanding-benchmarks-dental-practices), [Outsource Strategies (VENDOR)](https://www.outsourcestrategies.com/blog/improving-financial-health-guide-ar-collection-dental-practices/)
- Clean claim rate: leading practices 95%+, majority 75-85% (VENDOR). — [Outsource Strategies](https://outsourcestrategies.com/blog/rcm-reporting-metrics-dentists-track)
- Resubmissions (Premier 2024 survey, medical, cited by DentistryIQ): 54% of denied claims eventually paid, after an average of three resubmission cycles of 45-60 days each. — [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/revenue-cycle-management/article/55409403/the-7-revenue-cycle-gaps-most-dental-practices-dont-know-they-have)
- Collection rate: average 80%, top 10% 97% (Catalyst Index, VENDOR); vendor targets 95-98%. — [Henry Schein One](https://henryscheinone.com/insights/ebook/2026-catalyst-index)
- Change Healthcare attack (21 Feb 2024): ~190 million people affected (UnitedHealth, Jan 2025). — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/190-million-people-affected-change-healthcare-cyberattack)
- AMA survey of physician practices (26 Mar-3 Apr 2024; mostly practices under 10 physicians): 36% suspended claim payments, 32% could not submit claims, 22% could not verify eligibility, 39% no electronic remittance advice, 80% lost revenue from unpaid claims, 85% committed extra staff time. — [AMA survey PDF](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/change-healthcare-survey-results.pdf), [AMA follow-up (590+ responses)](https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/change-healthcare-follow-up-survey-results.pdf)

### Inferences
- Denial-rate claims of "19-20% on first submission" circulate only in vendor marketing; the more defensible statement is a 4% benchmark versus 8-15% typical, with the source labelled as billing-vendor data.

### Gaps
- No ADA HPI figure found for PPO participation share or network write-off percentages; no dentist-specific Change Healthcare survey found (AMA physician data used as proxy).
- Time spent on insurance verification and claims per day: no sourced figure retrieved (Clerri billing-statistics page rate-limited).
- CareCredit usage or approval figures not retrieved.

## US: marketing spend, cost per new patient, reviews

### Takeaway
Agencies put cost per new general-dentistry patient at $150-$300 and marketing budgets at 4-7% of revenue; all figures are vendor-sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Cost per new patient: general dentistry ~$150-$300; implant/cosmetic cases $400-$800; organic search $50-$150 vs Google Ads $150-$350; budgets 4-7% of revenue (one agency ~5% moving to 5.7% by 2028; another cites ~15% of gross for established practices); lifetime value $7,000-$10,000 (agency) (all VENDOR/agency, 2025-26). — [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-patient-acquisition-cost-statistics), [Emulent](https://emulent.com/resources/trends/dental-practice-marketing-trends-and-projections/), [Best Results Dental Marketing](https://bestresultsdentalmarketing.com/2026/13/how-much-should-dentists-spend-on-marketing-2026-guide/), [Redefine Web](https://redefineweb.com/blog/dental-marketing-cost/)

### Inferences
- At 30 new patients a month (Catalyst Index) and $150-$300 each, a typical practice's new-patient marketing spend is on the order of $4,500-$9,000 a month, which is why missed calls (which waste paid leads) are framed by vendors as the largest leak.

### Gaps
- Review counts and star-rating expectations: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey page was rate-limited; no figure retrieved. — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)
- No independent (non-agency) measurement of dental cost per new patient found.

## UK: NHS activity, access, UDA delivery, clawback, hand-backs and fees

### Takeaway
NHS England delivered 73m UDAs and 35m courses of treatment in 2024/25, but only 40% of adults were seen in 24 months, about one pound in seven paid to NHS dentists was clawed back over two years (£479m in 2024, £457m in 2025), 78% of people who tried to get an NHS appointment succeeded, and the average contract pays about £36 per UDA.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- At ~£36 per UDA and 96% delivery threshold, a typical contract loses money on any Band 2/3 course that overruns, which is the mechanism behind both the clawback totals and hand-backs.
- The 40% adult 24-month coverage figure has been flat two years running despite UDA growth, so added activity is not reaching new patients.

## UK: workforce, vacancies, pay and dentist income

### Takeaway
The GDC register grew to 131,680 in 2025 (47,916 dentists, 68,472 dental nurses) with international joiners now outnumbering UK-qualified dentists; NHS general-dentist vacancy rate fell from 19% to 10% in the year to December 2025 while dental nurse vacancies were 8% in March 2024; average NHS-active dentist taxable income in England was £83,000 in 2024/25 and dental nurses average £14.73 an hour.

### Cited Findings
- GDC register end-2025: 131,680 total; dentists 47,916 (+3.4%); DCPs 83,764 (+5.5%); dental nurses 68,472 (+5.4%, over half the register); therapists 8,661 (+21%); hygienists 11,292 (+11%); technicians below 5,000 (sixth consecutive fall); 53% of dentists joining were internationally qualified vs 47% UK; 1,069 dentists removed in 2025 (-16% on 2024). End-2024: 125,736 total, 46,362 dentists, 79,374 DCPs. — [GDC news 7 May 2026 (fetch rate-limited; from search summary)](https://www.gdc-uk.org/news-blogs/news/detail/2026/05/07/internationally-qualified-dentists-outnumber-uk-qualified-dentists-joining-gdc-register-for-first-time), [GDC 2024 registration report PDF](https://www.gdc-uk.org/docs/default-source/reports-and-publications/gdc-registration-statistics-report-2024.pdf)
- NHS England dental workforce survey: December 2025 general-dentist NHS vacancy rate 10% (19% a year earlier); 84% of providers responded. March 2024: 1,161 unfilled NHS dental nurse posts = 8% vacancy rate; one fifth of NHS general dentist positions unfilled (Nov 2024 report). — [NHS England Dec 2025 (fetch 403)](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/publications/statistical/mi-nhse-dental-workforce/december-2025), [Dentistry.co.uk 15 Nov 2024](https://dentistry.co.uk/2024/11/15/one-fifth-of-nhs-general-dentist-positions-unfilled-data-shows/)

## UK: complaints, regulation, practice sales and software prices

### Takeaway
Complaints and regulatory pressure rose sharply: DCS cases doubled to 985 in 2024 and GDC fitness-to-practise concerns rose 26% to 1,766 in 2025 with a 78-week average assessment stage; practice goodwill sits at 109% of gross fees (NHS 135%, private 95%) and UK practice software is mostly quote-only apart from Dentally (£195-£415 a month) and Dengro (£199-£249 a month).
